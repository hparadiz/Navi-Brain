**Central judgment.** GNW supplies a demanding hypothesis about selective, recurrent information access. Navi-Brain already implements useful workspace selection and scoped delivery, but these mechanisms do not establish neuronal ignition or its proposed relationship to consciousness. The strongest next step is to measure whether selected content causally improves distinct downstream computations, then determine whether reciprocal processing adds value beyond ordinary context provision.

**Core idea and mechanism.** The paper principally addresses *conscious access*: information becomes available to numerous processors, including memory, evaluation, planning, and report. It distinguishes the current content from the overall state permitting access, and explicitly allows access without overt report (p. 1, Introduction). Its treatment of phenomenal consciousness is a theoretical position; functional accessibility does not independently settle the disputed relationship to subjective experience.

GNW adds a proposed neural implementation to Baars's psychological workspace. Specialized processors exchange signals through distributed, densely interconnected neurons with long-range reciprocal projections. A feedforward wave may decay without access. With sufficient input and favorable network state, recurrent excitation produces nonlinear **ignition**: selected representations amplify and persist while competitors are inhibited. Simulations distinguish fast AMPA-mediated propagation from slower NMDA-mediated feedback; balanced excitation and inhibition matter to stable propagation (pp. 2–4, “Simulations”; Figs. 1–2). Endogenous activity can also initiate ignition. This is a dynamical explanation, not simply a shared storage location or thresholded priority score.

Prefrontal cortex is an important hub, not the exclusive location of consciousness. Parietal, cingulate, temporal, thalamic, and other contributions matter; participation by primary sensory regions depends partly on task resolution (pp. 2, 6–9). Co-selection of an object's distributed features is an explicit integration hypothesis (Fig. 4). Bayesian prediction and error exchange are offered as a possible computational interpretation of broadcasting, rather than a uniquely derived GNW algorithm (p. 4).

The working-memory distinction is especially relevant to Navi. The authors associate actively manipulated contents with distributed firing, while allowing unattended items to persist through weaker local activity or temporary synaptic changes. Transforming an activity-silent item is proposed to require reactivation (pp. 9–10, “Conscious Representations and Working Memory”). This supports distinguishing storage, active availability, and manipulation; it provides no engineering requirement to run model inference continuously merely to preserve state.

**Evidence and limitations.** This is a narrative synthesis by GNW proponents, including its originators, rather than a new controlled experiment or systematic meta-analysis. Its simulations establish that selected architectures can generate relevant dynamics under their assumptions. They do not establish that those dynamics are unique to GNW, that biological consciousness has been reproduced, or that an LLM executive will benefit from a corresponding design.

The strongest content-level example is van Vugt et al.'s macaque detection experiment, summarized on pp. 5–6 and Fig. 2. Recordings in V1, V4, and dorsolateral PFC distinguished hits, misses, correct rejections, and false alarms across stimulus strengths. Missed stimuli could produce substantial early visual activity; hits produced late sustained PFC activity, with smaller late visual effects. **False alarms also produced PFC ignition.** The paper relates propagation efficiency to signal-detection sensitivity and ignition threshold to response criterion. This supports distinguishing local encoding from sustained access, while showing that strong access is not evidence of external truth. These were report-linked recordings, so perceptual, decision, and response-related contributions remain difficult to separate. Separate no-report rivalry findings described on p. 5 mitigate one confound without resolving all of them.

Evidence for recurrence includes mouse experiments where optogenetically silencing feedback from secondary motor to somatosensory cortex attenuated late amplification and impaired perception (p. 7, “The Role of Recurrent Activity”). Such interventions strengthen causal evidence for the affected circuit. They do not selectively manipulate the entire GNW or distinguish every theory requiring recurrence. Figure-ground results further show why identical early encoding can coexist with different later perceptual outcomes (Fig. 3).

State-level evidence spans anesthesia, brain injury, and sleep (pp. 10–13). Propofol, sevoflurane, and ketamine are associated with disrupted long-range interactions and reduced diversity of functional configurations despite distinct molecular effects (Fig. 5). Early sensory processing can survive while late activity is lost. However, unresponsiveness can coexist with disconnected experience; lesion populations are heterogeneous; connectivity estimates do not directly identify message content or causal direction. The review itself reports anesthetic hypersynchrony in some circuits and restoration of wakefulness without restoration of particular EEG connectivity measures (pp. 11, 13). Therefore neither “more synchrony” nor “more connectivity” is an adequate adoption metric. Thalamic stimulation findings support causal network involvement but also involve arousal and broad circuit changes.

The P3b dispute is consequential. Task-irrelevant stimuli reported as perceived can retain an earlier negativity while losing P3b; the paper explicitly leaves awareness versus post-perceptual interpretations unresolved (pp. 4–5). Its suggestion that some such stimuli were merely accessible rather than actually accessed needs independent operational support. Similarly, perturbational complexity and anesthesia findings are compatible with multiple theories (pp. 12, 15; Table 1), rather than uniquely validating GNW. Developmental and self-consciousness sections are research directions: infant latency differences resist a universal 300-ms rule, and the authors acknowledge the absence of a good theory of self-representation (pp. 15–16). The adversarial comparison mentioned on p. 17 was underway in this **2020 version**; no later outcome is assumed here.

**Prioritized recommendations.** These are proposed offline evaluations and possible later changes, not work performed for this review.

1. **P1 — Establish causal workspace uptake before expanding broadcasting.** At `WorkingMemory::contextForWork` and `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork`, extend existing checksum/role provenance into an evaluation ledger joining selected record, delivered content, work item, and independently scored outcome. Use held-out tasks requiring a selected fact to affect both a decision proposal and a separately queued mind-stream inference; score structured outcomes without requiring a verbal claim of awareness. Compare intact delivery, targeted removal, irrelevant-content substitution, and direct delivery of the same evidence. Control all duplicate routes documented in Q14 and preserve intentional evidence exclusions. Measure content-specific effects, task success, unsupported conclusions, and actual input/output tokens. Dependencies are labeled tasks, isolated replay data, and additional inference cost; input-budget matching must use the actual backend tokenizer, given Q11. **Failure criterion:** no reliable selective effect on the intended consumers, or an apparent advantage explained by extra evidence/tokens or leakage through an unablated route. A benefit equal to direct delivery supports useful routing, not a special ignition mechanism.

2. **P2 — Experiment with bounded, content-directed recurrence.** If P1 establishes useful access, add an experimental evidence-request transition around `DecisionStateMachine::integrate`: a proposal identifies a specific missing or conflicting fact, requests a typed memory query, and receives at most one additional evidence-integration pass. Bind requests and responses to source IDs and workspace version; revalidate materially changed dependencies before using the result, addressing Q02's freshness limitation. This is a **speculative software analogue** of top-down mobilization and subsequent bottom-up correction (pp. 4, 7), beyond the one-pass path verified in Q03. Keep a fixed total compute budget and preserve terminal-adapter validation. Compare against both a single pass with a larger initial retrieval and an equally costly second pass without new evidence. **Failure criterion:** no held-out accuracy gain at matched compute, increased unsupported confidence, or failure to reject a deliberately stale/conflicting response. Costs include another queued call, larger state-machine surface, and latency; no neural equivalence is implied.

3. **P3 — Calibrate admission against false alarms, not apparent ignition.** Use `CapsuleAssembler::contest/score` to study evidence strength, distractor load, and existing hysteresis on ordinary contested roles. Include absent-target and misleading-high-confidence trials, motivated directly by Fig. 2. Distinguish a relevant record being available, selected, delivered, and correctly used; report hits, misses, false admissions, retention after distraction, and displacement of better-supported content. Compare existing selection with a simple non-hysteretic ranking baseline. If proposing an admission floor, choose it on separate calibration data and retain reserved safety behavior. Dependencies are independent relevance labels and P1's uptake measurements; token-memory query learning must be isolated between conditions because ranking is not globally mutation-free (QMEM). **Failure criterion:** any sensitivity gain disappears at matched false-admission rate, or longer persistence merely preserves errors. A programmed discontinuity in a score is not evidence of emergent ignition. This experiment should decide whether selection needs changing at all.

The principal adoption risk is self-confirming amplification: an initially mistaken representation can gain persistence, influence later retrieval, and appear corroborated by its own repetitions. The paper's false alarms make this a direct concern. Navi's existing workspace-entry usefulness credit adds a concrete reason to separate delivery from independent benefit. Conversely, the paper does not justify forcing every token association or verified action-learning event through a global acceptance gate; Q01 already verifies distinct learning routes. Requiring all modules to consume all workspace contents would also discard useful task and privacy boundaries without evidence of benefit.

**Verdict: experiment.** Adopt GNW as a source of falsifiable coordination hypotheses; retain existing persistent memory and scoped context machinery. Prioritize P1's causal uptake evaluation, with P2 conditional on a demonstrated coordination bottleneck. Do not adopt consciousness claims, biological timing targets, or continuous inference requirements from this review. No consequential implementation question remained unanswered by the shared reference; live effectiveness, provider neural dynamics, and the proposed experiments remain unverified.
