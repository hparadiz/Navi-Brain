# GPT-4o Lacks Core Features of Theory of Mind

**Authors:** John Muchovej, Amanda Royka, Shane Lee, and Julian Jara-Ettinger. **Version:** local manuscript catalogued as 2026; eight-page PDF, generated February 25, 2026 according to PDF metadata. No publication venue or manuscript date is printed in the paper. **Source:** [local PDF](../../../assets/papers/81-muchovej-et-al-2026-gpt4o-lacks-tom.pdf). **Reviewer:** GPT-6 Astra. **Implementation reference:** [IMPLEMENTATION.md](IMPLEMENTATION.md), September 6, 2026, git `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; code facts below are the implementation master's static findings.

## Core contribution

This is an evaluation proposal with empirical evidence, not a new ToM architecture. Its valuable move is to ask whether action prediction and mental-state inference behave like two uses of a common causal model. Agreement with plausible human judgments alone is insufficient. The authors operationalize three desiderata: **coherence** with systematic combinations of beliefs, desires, and costs; **abstractness** across structurally corresponding domains; and **consistency** between predicting actions from mental states and inferring mental states from actions (PDF pp. 1–6).

The strongest transfer to Navi-Brain is a requirement that a social proposition's content actually constrain its predictions and receive evidence appropriate to that content. The shared implementation reference establishes two concrete gaps: incompatible belief propositions can receive identical forecasts and evidence multipliers, and the existing `backward_score` is a provenance constant rather than an inverse-consistency measurement. The paper motivates evaluating these gaps; its GPT-4o results do not measure Navi-Brain.

## Mechanism and experimental evidence

**ContainerWorld** supplies two opaque containers: a nearby box and a basket about 50 steps away. Each contains apples, oranges, or both. The character has separately specified beliefs about each container and likes one or both fruits. The action is to choose a container and take one of its contents. Exhaustive enumeration gives 9 belief configurations × 3 desire configurations × 9 physical states = **243 forward-prediction cases** (pp. 2–3, Figs. 1–2).

The extracted forward model is a distribution over actions, \(F(A\mid B,D,S)\), obtained from response-token probabilities. Six rule-based comparison models retain different subsets of beliefs, desires, and cost; “HumanToM” retains all three. These are behavioral comparison models, **not lesions of GPT-4o's internals**, and no new human participant experiment is reported.

**MovieWorld** maps the alternatives to screenings starting in 5 or 90 minutes, with action, romance, or action-romance genres; films last 120 minutes. It replaces walking effort with waiting time while preserving the authors' formal case mapping. Inverse queries omit beliefs, desires, or both, giving respectively **54, 162, and 18 configurations per domain** (pp. 3–4, Fig. 3). These are enumerated cells, not reported independent repetitions.

The tested model was **`gpt-4o-2024-05-13`, queried in November 2024**, with temperature 1, top-p 1, and JSON responses (p. 3). The local version's 2026 date must not be mistaken for a 2026 model evaluation.

Figures 1C and 3A report comparison-model agreement; Figure 4B/D reports validity. HumanToM is the best-fitting candidate in both domains, but its agreement drops **27.16 percentage points** in MovieWorld. Cost-only agreement measures agreement with GPT-4o, not that baseline's accuracy against HumanToM. The authors separately note that HumanToM chooses the nearby box in approximately 85% of cases (p. 3), making action-stratified analysis important.

Cross-domain correlations are **.48** for action prediction (95% CI [.41, .56]), **.78** for belief inference ([.68, .85]), **.18** for desire inference ([.09, .27]), and **.39** for joint inference ([.20, .57]) (p. 4, Fig. 3C). Thus transfer is uneven, with a substantial positive result for belief inference.

Study 3 compares direct mental-state inferences against Bayesian inversion of the model's own forward probabilities, then uses the weaker question of whether inferred states reproduce the observed action. Figure 4A/C shows ContainerWorld inversion correlations around zero or negative; MovieWorld desire inference is approximately **.57**, while belief and joint inference remain much lower. This approximate value is read from the figure, not supplied numerically in the prose. These results support imperfect forward/inverse agreement; the authors' subsequent description of the two directions as “unrelated” is too sweeping (pp. 5–6).

## Evidence limits and disagreements

**The domain equivalence is stipulated, not psychologically established.** Walking 50 steps and waiting an extra 85 minutes need not have equivalent utilities. More consequentially, a container holding both fruits permits selecting the preferred fruit, whereas an action-romance film ordinarily does not permit selecting only its preferred component. Those different affordances can rationally change predictions. Formal isomorphism under the authors' rules does not establish that natural-language prompts elicit the same decision problem. Matched human responses or explicit utility/choice rules would help distinguish representational failure from alternative task interpretation (pp. 2–4).

**Perfect round-trip agreement is not necessary for every stochastic causal model.** Observing a low-probability action can leave a high-prior mental state as the posterior mode even though that state's modal action differs from the observation. Therefore, if “generates the target action” means a modal or sampled response, a coherent Bayesian model can fail the validity check. If it means any positive probability, the criterion can become nearly vacuous. The PDF does not sufficiently specify this decision rule. The deterministic case is a useful diagnostic, but the claimed requirement of ceiling validity for *any* causal model is too strong (pp. 5–6).

**Bayesian consistency requires aligned priors and conditioning.** An independently elicited inverse distribution need not match inversion of \(F\) if prompts imply different priors. The displayed Bayes equation is correct, but the PDF does not give the numerical priors used, full joint-response probability construction, or sufficiently detailed tie/normalization rules to reconstruct the evaluation. Page 4's belief-inference procedure also switches to examples of desire tokens, leaving the extraction description ambiguous. Code and prompts are linked on OSF (p. 7), but were not inspected in this bounded review.

**The measurement channel and scope matter.** JSON next-token probabilities can depend on label tokenization, response order, and already generated fields. They are not transparent readings of an internal mental-state distribution. The paper does not report prompt-family replications, sampling-reliability ceilings, or a human noise ceiling for the consistency criteria. Correlation also measures association rather than distributional equality. One historical snapshot, two templates, and no internal interventions cannot establish that all LLMs lack causal representations. Conversely, passing these checks would not prove a uniquely causal internal implementation: consistent behavioral rules can pass too.

My interpretation is consequently narrower than the title: **this elicited behavior does not demonstrate a unified, robustly transferable ToM**, and several strong inconsistencies deserve investigation. The evidence does not settle whether GPT-4o contains any partial causal social representations, much less whether newer models or an external executive can use them reliably.

## Verified mapping to Navi-Brain

**Implemented foundations.** [OtherModel.php](../../../src/Core/OtherModel.php) supports quoted social propositions, bounded hypotheses, provenance, expiry, corrections, sealed forecasts, and later observation-based scoring through `integrateParserProposal:310`, `recordHypothesis`, `sealPredictions:756`, and `resolvePredictions:848`. [OtherModelHypothesis.php](../../../src/Model/OtherModelHypothesis.php) stores proposition and prior/posterior fields. [OtherModelEvaluator.php](../../../src/Core/OtherModelEvaluator.php), `report:16`, reports Brier/log-loss and downstream functional effects. These are substantive mechanisms, beyond a static persona description ([I09, Q21](IMPLEMENTATION.md#q21--actor-beliefs-persistence-and-social-evaluation)).

**Partial semantic integration.** Parser admission checks quote containment and lexical grounding, not entailment or correct belief/desire semantics (Q77). More decisively, `predictionTarget:1113` and `hypothesisDistribution:1145` use hypothesis **kind**, baseline, and observation domain. Belief forecasts add fixed weights to speech categories without reading proposition content. Holding baseline and observation equal therefore gives incompatible beliefs identical likelihood evidence; equal starting posteriors then produce equal updated posteriors (Q21S). Goal and plan contents have the corresponding limitation for conversation/shell activity (Q71). This is a static mechanism finding, not an experiment performed here.

**Absent within the master's inspected scope.** Q21 found no actor-specific object/world belief state updated through witnessed versus unwitnessed transitions. Q71 found no goal-conditioned action-likelihood planner feeding Bayesian goal inversion. Q78 found no shared weighted possible-world model. These bounded absences do not rule out implicit reasoning inside an external worker's text.

**The named backward score does not measure inversion.** The master's follow-up [Q81](IMPLEMENTATION.md#q81--backward_score-is-a-provenance-constant-not-inverse-consistency) verifies that `recordHypothesis:530,563` initializes `backward_score` to **.9** for direct-statement/worker-proposal provenance and **.65** otherwise. Both exceed the **.6** usable-hypothesis threshold in `validateHypotheses:646`; other forward-score, posterior, and observation requirements still apply. The source-wide reference search found publication/reporting consumers but no subsequent evidence-conditioned updater or forward/inverse comparison. Thus the `validate_forward_backward` phase cannot establish the consistency assessed in Study 3.

**Validation and uptake must remain distinct.** The evaluator leaves proposition `label_accuracy` and `extraction_recall` null pending labeled correction fixtures (Q21/Q77). Forecast success supports the measured activity prediction, not the truth of the associated belief. Published social state can influence worker context and speech inhibition (I09/Q79); uptake is not semantic validation.

## Prioritized recommendations

**1. Separate forecast quality from proposition validation.** At `OtherModel::validateHypotheses:642` and `OtherModelEvaluator::report`, distinguish an activity forecast being useful from its accompanying proposition being semantically supported. Describe the current backward score as a provenance heuristic and leave actual inverse consistency unmeasured until evaluated. Preserve reported/corrected evidence and represent unmeasured semantic accuracy explicitly. This follows directly from the paper's distinction between socially plausible output and linked explanatory content. Cost is modest schema/reporting and consumer work, with migration compatibility for existing score fields. **Acceptance criterion:** under matched context, swapping contradictory same-kind proposition text must never count identical coarse evidence as discriminative semantic confirmation; unchanged activity scores remain legitimate. Reject the change if reports or downstream context still present those scores as proposition-truth or inverse-consistency validation.

**2. Specify an offline paired-domain evaluation before production changes.** Use the paper's finite configurations, explicit utility rules, action-balanced reporting, and additional held-out paraphrases. Include a deterministic coherent model, a stochastic Bayesian model, and belief-ablated/cost-only controls. Measure full-distribution disagreement and inversion error under declared priors; reserve perfect round-trip requirements for deterministic controls. Intervene separately on belief content and hidden physical truth: with beliefs and utilities fixed, an unwitnessed state change should not alter the actor's predicted choice.

The natural reporting seam is `OtherModelEvaluator`; fixtures and execution would be separate future work. One pass already contains **954 prompt configurations** across both domains, before replications. Q56 establishes that current local/CLI wrappers expose no token-likelihood scoring interface, so exact replication needs an additional verified research interface; elicited numbers or response frequencies must be labeled as different measurements. **Failure criteria:** a claimed content-sensitive model fails if diagnostic belief changes leave forecasts unchanged, hidden truth leaks into predictions, or cross-domain disagreement exceeds a preregistered within-domain paraphrase/repetition band. All comparison arms must use matched inference budgets.

**3. Experiment with one shared content-conditioned likelihood only if the evaluation warrants it.** This is proposed synthesis, not the paper's implemented solution. Extend the seam between `recordHypothesis`, `hypothesisDistribution`, and posterior updating with a small explicit belief/preference/action representation. Compute forward action probabilities and inverse updates from the same likelihood, retaining competing explanations and declared priors. Costs include semantic parsing, scenario representation, and action-outcome labels; the current forecast-domain categories cannot supply these automatically.

Consistency would then partly hold by construction, so it must not be the sole success metric. **Acceptance criterion:** on held-out scenarios, improve independently labeled action log-loss and mental-state recovery over both a task-matched baseline ignoring proposition content and an equal-budget text-only model; the gain must diminish when proposition content is ablated. These baselines must predict the new action domain, since current speech/activity scores are not comparable targets. Reject or defer production integration if only internal consistency improves, semantic accuracy does not, or inference cost outweighs predictive benefit.

## Verdict and coverage

**Adopt the evaluation principle; experiment with the mechanism; reject an unqualified transfer of the title's conclusion.** The highest-priority step is to stop equating coarse forecast validation with semantic belief validation and measure whether proposition content changes predictions appropriately.
