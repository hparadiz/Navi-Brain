# BDI Agents: From Theory to Practice

Anand S. Rao and Michael P. Georgeff (1995). *Proceedings of the First International Conference on Multiagent Systems (ICMAS-95)*, pp. 312–319. Version reviewed: the local eight-page proceedings scan, [source PDF](../../../assets/papers/28-rao-georgeff-1995-bdi-agents.pdf). Reviewer: GPT-6 Astra. Implementation evidence: [shared implementation reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, git HEAD `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; all code claims below come from the implementation master’s static inspection.

**Central finding.** Adopt BDI as a discipline for maintaining and reconsidering commitments. Navi-Brain already has substantial executive machinery to support that discipline. The immediate deficiency is the operational meaning of commitment transitions: automatic completion writes `released` while reporting `completed`, and dependency readiness accepts either. This conflates achieving a prerequisite with abandoning it. Adding a richer planner before resolving that distinction would amplify an existing ambiguity.

**Core idea and mechanism.** The paper connects decision theory, modal descriptions of rational agency, a practical interpreter, and deployed application designs. Its central argument concerns bounded computation: recomputing a preferred action after every environmental change can consume the time needed to act, while executing an old decision unconditionally can make action inappropriate. An intention preserves the output of earlier deliberation so action can continue between reconsiderations (PDF pp. 2–3, printed pp. 313–314, “The System and its Environment”).

The motivating domain has uncertain environmental evolution, alternative actions, competing objectives, context-dependent means, partial sensing, and environmental change on a timescale comparable to computation and action. Beliefs retain information gathered through partial observations; desires represent objectives or payoffs; intentions retain chosen courses of action. Crucially, the compromise assumes potentially significant changes can be identified “instantaneously” at the granularity of primitive events/actions (PDF p. 3, footnote 3). Efficient detection of relevance is therefore a prerequisite, not an achievement demonstrated by merely storing intentions.

The decision-theoretic bridge replaces chance branches with separate possible-world trees, retaining decision alternatives within each tree. Probabilities concern belief-accessible worlds, payoffs concern desire-accessible paths, and deliberation restricts intention-accessible worlds to selected courses of action (PDF pp. 3–4, “Decision Trees to Possible Worlds”). This is a representational account of a supplied decision problem and deliberation function; it does not infer reliable probabilities, utilities, or models from experience. The transformation algorithm is referenced to earlier work rather than fully presented here.

The symbolic treatment then abstracts probabilities and payoffs to dichotomous attitudes. Belief uses KD45; desire and intention use K and D with necessitation. The authors emphasize a family of static relationships and commitment policies rather than one mandatory BDI axiom system (PDF pp. 4–5, “BDI Logics”). Commitment has both a condition maintained and a termination condition. In this paper’s wording, a blindly committed agent rejects belief/desire changes conflicting with its commitments; a single-minded agent accepts belief changes that can terminate them; an open-minded agent also permits desire changes to do so. OASIS illustrates single-minded commitment: preserve a landing sequence until the aircraft have landed or the next aircraft is no longer believed able to meet its assigned arrival time (PDF p. 6).

The abstract interpreter generates options from an event queue, deliberates, updates intentions, executes, receives external events, and drops successful or impossible attitudes. Its update/query operations must enforce the chosen compatibility constraints; the loop alone provides no such guarantee. The authors explicitly say the ideal architecture is impractical because of logical closure and noncomputable provability procedures (PDF p. 5, “Abstract Architecture”).

The practical PRS/dMARS approximation makes three consequential restrictions: represent current beliefs as ground literals without disjunction or implication; supply plans with invocation events, context preconditions, and bodies of actions/subgoals; represent adopted plans through hierarchical intention stacks that can run, suspend, or be scheduled. A `post-intention-status` step controls which intention changes reach subsequent option generation (PDF p. 6). Much of the tractability comes from supplied procedural knowledge and restricted alternatives. In OASIS, the plan library already excludes trajectories outside its fuel-efficient options (PDF p. 7, “Abstract Interpreter”).

**Evidence and limits.** This is an architectural synthesis with application reports, not a new controlled benchmark or a self-contained verification proof. Model-checking results and several algorithmic details are referred to earlier publications. OASIS is described as comprising up to seventy or eighty concurrent aircraft/global agents and undergoing parallel evaluation at Sydney airport using live radar data (PDF pp. 1, 6). That supports practical engineering relevance. It does not establish operational certification, superior safety, measured sequencing optimality, or a quantified advantage over competing architectures.

The most concrete productivity claim is that replacing FORTRAN pilot-reasoning rules with plans shortened tactical-change turnaround from two months to less than one day (PDF p. 7, “Essential Features”). No sample size, matched task comparison, variability, or separation of language/tooling and BDI effects is provided. The paper supplies no latency distribution, commitment-policy ablation, or experimental measurement of the cost of detecting relevant changes. Its report of broad application experience should not be converted into a causal performance estimate.

I also find the necessity argument narrower than its rhetoric. Remembering a selected policy is useful under expensive deliberation, but this does not prove every successful agent needs three separately named structures. The paper itself allows a BDI-specified system without identifiable BDI data structures (PDF p. 5). Likewise, decision theory can in principle value computation and switching costs; the critique targets the simplified recompute-on-change scheme discussed here, not every decision-theoretic controller.

Persistent intentions, action contracts, and asynchronous recovery already distinguish Navi from a stateless prompt wrapper. Nevertheless, a durable record of a chosen next action does not establish PRS plan-stack semantics, and procedure provenance dependencies differ from dependencies expressing why a plan remains appropriate. Existing interruption records also do not constitute universal action revocation ([Q23/Q22](IMPLEMENTATION.md), `ExecutiveCore::claimActionDispatch:797`).

The master’s [Q28](IMPLEMENTATION.md) clarification establishes heterogeneous reconsideration: `ExecutiveCore::prepareHighBrainMoment` can queue a completion check for a title-matched held intention in its hourly-consolidation branch; sensory events wake cognitive threads; parent-authorization and procedure-generation checks can invalidate particular work. These mechanisms do not guarantee periodic evaluation of every intention or textual release condition. `releaseFocus:9786` restricts reasons to achieved/impossible/reason_lapsed but does not prove their truth. No general belief-change-to-plan dependency monitor was found in the inspected completion/focus/lifecycle/thread, compiler, and procedural paths. Thus existing responsiveness should be preserved while adding selective plan invalidation.

**Prioritized recommendations.** These are proposed engineering experiments and acceptance criteria; none was performed for this review.

**P0 — Make commitment outcomes unambiguous before expanding planning.** In `ExecutiveCore::closeIntention` and `integrateCompletionCheck`, introduce one canonical transition representation that distinguishes verified achievement, authorized withdrawal, infeasibility, and temporary blockage. Model-generated achievement should remain a proposal until the applicable observable predicate/evidence check succeeds; genuinely semantic goals may need explicit human judgment. Use the same transition meaning in persisted state, events, return values, and `IntentionCompiler::dependencyStates`. Dependencies should specify whether they require achievement or merely termination. This implements the paper’s distinct success/impossibility termination conditions and compatibility-preserving updates (PDF pp. 4–5).

Cost/dependencies: status migration, evidence contracts, and caller updates. Historical `released` records cannot safely be relabeled successful en masse. Evaluate a labeled offline trace set containing achieved, predicted-only, impossible, withdrawn, and blocked goals; include direct and automatic closure. Acceptance requires agreement between record/event/API outcome and zero achievement-dependent launches following mere withdrawal or unverified predicted success. A falsely completed prerequisite or divergent automatic/direct transition fails the change. Recheck required commitment predicates at the dispatch boundary while retaining existing exact-action ownership and procedure-generation checks.

**P1 — Experiment with selective reconsideration tied to explicit plan assumptions.** This is a synthesis of the paper’s event-controlled commitment and Navi’s asynchronous workers. Associate a chosen plan with typed, versioned assumptions, a commitment policy, and a bounded review deadline. Relevant changes should request reconsideration; irrelevant changes should leave the plan intact. New instructions or withdrawn authority must be able to terminate commitment. Validate assumptions when integrating a delayed proposal; make the revision check atomic with the dispatch claim to exclude a recorded-invalidation/admission race. Reuse decision evidence and `ExecutiveCore::claimActionDispatch`; a whole-workspace checksum alone would invalidate plans for unrelated changes (PDF pp. 3, 5–6; [Q02/Q05/Q22/Q28](IMPLEMENTATION.md)).

Dependencies/cost: explicit predicates, event-to-assumption indexing, revision handling, and monitoring overhead. Compare the existing policy, every-event reconsideration, and dependency-based reconsideration on identical scripted goals with irrelevant churn, infeasibility, goal withdrawal, and changes during model latency. Proposed acceptance targets are at least 20% fewer model calls than every-event reconsideration, task success within two percentage points, and zero dispatches after a known required assumption has become false. Include detector compute and notification-to-invalidation latency; reject a policy whose monitoring costs erase the savings. Measure actual end-to-end timestamps: the existing `total_ms` is a sum of stage durations and can omit asynchronous waiting ([Q02](IMPLEMENTATION.md)). These targets are review proposals, not paper results.

**P2 — Pilot a small declarative plan layer only after P0/P1.** Extend the existing procedure library around `ProceduralMemory` with invocation-event matching, explicit context guard, terminal goal predicate, bounded body, and failure disposition. Applicability matching and alternative-plan choice would be new machinery under the master’s bounded search ([Q28](IMPLEMENTATION.md)). Start with a few authored plans using installed adapters; defer unrestricted hierarchical planning. Keep procedural action success separate from context suitability: repeated successful execution of a shape does not show when it should be chosen. This follows the practical approximation on PDF pp. 6–7, not the ideal possible-worlds construction.

Costs include authoring/maintaining guards and controlling overlapping applicable plans. Compare against the current proposer with the same adapters and task budget. Use contexts in which identical event names require different responses and failure requires a different applicable plan. Reject the pilot if it selects a plan with a false guard, loops indefinitely among alternatives, or fails to reduce deliberation cost while preserving verified goal outcomes. This is an optional capability experiment, not justification for replacing the executive wholesale.

**Risks and disagreements.** OASIS has unusually explicit feasibility constraints; open-ended language tasks often cannot distinguish impossible, unsupported, and merely difficult goals cheaply. Unknown evidence should usually trigger bounded investigation or suspension rather than a false impossibility judgment. The paper’s informal allowance for mutually incompatible desires (PDF p. 3, footnote 2) also should not be casually equated with a globally consistent modal desire set: the representation and chosen axioms matter. Blind commitment’s protection of commitments from inconvenient belief updates is a poor policy for an evidence-oriented assistant. Finally, grounding all plans in an authored library buys predictable execution by restricting coverage; it cannot establish general intelligence or consciousness.

**Verdict: adopt commitment semantics; experiment with selective reconsideration; defer a general BDI planner.** First resolve the achievement/release/dependency mismatch and make those meanings enforceable where actions are admitted. The paper’s durable contribution is a way to spend deliberation selectively while preserving coherent action. Its strongest transfer is therefore to Navi’s transition and scheduling contracts, not to the labels on its mental-state records.
