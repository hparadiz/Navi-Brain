# Goal-Driven Autonomy in a Navy Strategy Simulation

**Central finding.** This paper makes a useful architectural distinction: adapting the plan for an unchanged goal can remain inadequate even after correctly explaining a surprise. An agent may need a different goal, justified by the explanation and standing mission knowledge. ARTUE demonstrates incremental benefits from that capability in three authored naval scenarios. It does not demonstrate unrestricted goal invention, learned mission priorities, or robust causal diagnosis under arbitrary model error. For Navi, the valuable transfer is an auditable connection from discrepancies through explanation to justified intention changes. The master's Q32 confirms that this complete bridge is not implemented in the traced paths; installing an HTN planner or copying ARTUE's world-state explanation search is not yet warranted.

**Core idea and mechanism.** GDA is a controller architecture, while ARTUE is one concrete implementation. The framework leaves its planner and reasoning algorithms open. A planner supplies both an action sequence and corresponding expected state constraints. During execution the controller detects violated expectations, generates explanations, proposes goals from the explanations and current state, and manages the pending goal set before invoking planning again (pp. 2–3, §3, Fig. 1). Goal management can retain the current goal: surprise does not logically imply replacement. Newly formulated goals can address concerns outside the original task and need not be instrumental subgoals of it.

ARTUE extends SHOP2 with a wait action and projection of continuous processes and exogenous events using PDDL+. It checks discrepancies when waits finish and at intervals during longer waits. Its example begins with transporting a ship: observing unexpected lightning supports an approaching-storm explanation, which activates a shelter goal before the storm becomes directly observable and escape becomes impossible (pp. 4–5, §§5.1–5.5). This separates prediction, hidden-state inference, and choosing what should be achieved.

Explanation uses an assumption-based truth maintenance system. PDDL+ processes and events are compiled into rules for periods without non-wait actions. Candidate hidden facts instantiate predefined hidden predicates over known objects and Skolem objects representing potentially unseen entities. The ATMS searches consistent possible worlds; assumptions shared by all surviving explanations become beliefs (pp. 4–5, §5.3). This is logical abduction relative to the authored model, not probabilistic identification of the actual cause. Openness to new entities does not establish discovery of new predicates or causal laws.

Goal generation applies authored **principles** containing typed participants, a condition, a fixed intensity, and a goal template. `stormApproaching(?vehicle)` instantiates `DirectShipToShelter(?vehicle)` with high intensity. The manager tries the highest-intensity pending goal, falling back to the next if planning fails; ARTUE executes only one plan at a time (p. 5, §§5.4–5.5, Table 1). The goal instance is generated autonomously, but its motivational justification and priority come from supplied domain knowledge. Neither learned utility nor globally optimal scheduling is established.

**Evidence and limits.** The evaluation compares PLAN1, which never revises its initial plan; REPLAN, which detects discrepancies and replans without explanations or new goals; EXPLAIN, which additionally revises beliefs through explanation but retains its goals; and complete ARTUE. Each ran 25 times per scenario with randomized starting locations and prearranged events held constant across versions (pp. 5–6, §6).

These are Table 2's scenario-specific mean scores on a 0–1 scale; their units do not support direct cross-scenario comparisons. ARTUE improves over EXPLAIN by 0.16, 0.09, and 0.24 respectively. The authors report (p<.0001) for ARTUE versus each ablation within every scenario, and (p<.01) for successive ablations. The paper provides neither the statistical test nor dispersion, confidence intervals, or raw trial scores, limiting independent assessment of those significance claims.

Scouting rewards identification plus dealing with an unexpected submarine; Iceberg rewards destination progress, survival, and timely rescue beyond the transport assignment; SubHunt adds mine sweeping to finding and destroying a submarine (p. 6, §6). These are suitable demonstrations that a narrow supplied goal can omit mission-relevant responses. However, the score explicitly rewards those additional concerns, and the authored principles enable them. The experiment establishes performance under that broader objective, not independent discovery of what the evaluator values.

The nested ablations support incremental contributions along this particular integration sequence. They do not isolate goal generation from goal management, establish that explanation is necessary for every useful new goal, or rule out a direct condition-to-goal controller. The abstract's claim that each component is “necessary” is stronger than this design supports. Three scenarios in one simulator also leave transfer to another ontology or sensor regime untested.

Knowledge engineering is substantial: 22 types, 115 predicate forms including 17 hidden forms, eight fluent forms, 33 action models, 37 event models, seven process models, 42 tasks, 148 HTN methods, nine principles, and 28 goal-generation rules (p. 6). Domain-independent algorithms therefore do not imply inexpensive domain transfer. Table 3 reports mean clock times of 10, 15, and 3.5 minutes, with maxima 22, 28, and 6 minutes. These are scenario-run times, not per-decision latencies. Most time goes into explanation, whose world-state search is described as exponential in the number of discrepancies; no latency distribution or scaling experiment establishes suitability for Navi's executive deadlines.

**Risks and disagreements.** ARTUE distinguishes hidden causes, wrong dynamics, and wrong perception, but primarily explains the first. When its model cannot explain an observed successor fact even individually, it discards that fact from the explanation problem; model-learning goals are future work (pp. 4–5, §5.3). Transferring this literally could turn a model's limitations into apparent evidence against a real observation. Shared assumptions across enumerated worlds remain conditional on the hypothesis vocabulary and retained evidence; agreement is not causal truth.

There is also a source-level threshold inconsistency. The authoritative PDF says continuous discrepancies occur when expected and observed values differ by **less than 0.1%** of the absolute observed value (p. 4, §5.2), while p. 5, §5.3 treats values **within 0.1%** as equivalent. Both passages were visually verified. This likely reflects an inequality error in the description, but the executed comparator is unverified. Even the likely intended relative tolerance needs treatment near zero and under heterogeneous sensor noise. It should not become a copied implementation constant.

Finally, fixed intensity and single-plan execution leave unresolved starvation, repeated interruption, retraction after a hypothesis fails, and preservation of existing commitments. GDA permits richer management, but ARTUE's experiment does not validate it. There is no evidence here about consciousness, personal identity, or the desirability of allowing an assistant to expand its authority.

**Prioritized recommendations.** These are proposed documentation-driven experiments, not changes or experiments performed during this review.

1. **P0 — Establish explicit, bounded discrepancy-to-explanation records.** At the interfaces around `ForwardModel::observe`, `ExecutiveCore::finishAction`, and decision proposal integration, prototype a record linking the prediction, observation, action/intention, timestamps, and model version to a small set of candidate explanations. Keep observations intact and distinguish hidden cause, model error, sensor error, and unresolved status. Attach predicted discriminating observations to hypotheses rather than accepting explanatory prose as verification. This follows §5.3 while deliberately correcting its evidence-discarding weakness. Cost is an explicit event schema, domain hypotheses, and a bounded reasoning budget; begin with one known operational domain. On labeled offline episodes containing each cause class, compare against an unclassified surprise baseline. Fail adoption if explanations do not improve subsequent prediction or diagnosis, if wrong-model cases are consistently labeled hidden causes, or if evidence is silently dropped. Inject delayed observations to verify that obsolete explanations cannot be treated as current facts.

2. **P1 — Prototype a principle-to-intention bridge in shadow mode.** Add a proposed goal-formulation component beside `ExecutiveCore`, initially emitting reviewable proposals without creating active intentions. Use a small registry of typed conditions, evidence requirements, goal templates, declared scope, priority, deduplication key, and release predicates, with eventual admission through `createIntention`. For example, an explained unavailable evidence source could propose an authorized alternative-evidence goal with a checkable completion condition. Preserve the original commitment and record whether the proposal supplements, suspends, or replaces it. Dependencies include P0, explicit scheduling/dispatch invalidation semantics, and an executable scope check; an authority string alone is insufficient (I05/Q05/Q22/Q32). Compare proposed goals with independently labeled appropriate interventions, including harmless surprises, conflicting hypotheses, and repeated events. Fail if unsupported goals are admitted, duplicates accumulate, or invalidated premises continue to justify dispatch. This is a bounded synthesis inspired by §§5.4–5.5, not an existing ARTUE feature set.

3. **P2 — Require a stronger ablation before production adoption.** In an isolated scenario model, hold replanning and available actions constant and cross explanation on/off with goal formulation on/off; include fixed-plan and direct condition-to-goal comparators. Use paired scenario seeds, held-out combinations, sensor corruption, and incorrect domain rules. Equalize external knowledge and record both inference cost and authoring effort. Score original-task completion separately from independently specified incident response, inappropriate goal changes, switching frequency, and end-to-end delay. Existing decision/action records are useful provenance, but Q02's summed stage timing is not complete asynchronous elapsed time. Predeclare a success criterion: positive paired incident-response improvement with a confidence interval excluding zero, original-task completion within a chosen five-percentage-point noninferiority margin, and no scope violations. Reject the added explanation machinery if a direct-rule controller achieves comparable outcomes more cheaply. This addresses the causal and transfer limits of Table 2 without claiming the paper already resolves them.

**Verdict: experiment.** Adopt GDA's separation of explanation, goal formulation, and goal management as an architectural review lens. The verified gap makes a small, auditable explanation-to-goal experiment the consequential next step. Defer full ARTUE replication: its demonstrated advantage is useful, but its knowledge cost, explanation complexity, and handling of model error make wholesale transfer poorly justified.
