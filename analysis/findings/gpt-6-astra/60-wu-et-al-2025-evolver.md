**Verdict: experiment with outcome-linked strategic memory; defer policy training.** EvolveR provides useful evidence that explicit principles and a policy trained to retrieve them can improve search-based QA. Navi already has several experience-learning mechanisms. The transferable addition is a separately evaluated relationship between abstract advice, its actual exposure to decisions, and independently measured task outcomes. The paper does not establish that self-distillation automatically improves with experience, or that its principle score measures causal usefulness.

## Mechanism and assumptions

EvolveR alternates three processes (§§3.1–3.3, pp. 3–7). During offline distillation, the current policy model, with weights frozen, summarizes successful trajectories into guiding principles and failed trajectories into cautionary principles. Each contains a sentence and subject–predicate–object triples. These are structured descriptions, not executable rules or a demonstrated logical inference system.

New principles from the same problem undergo pairwise semantic deduplication. Against the existing experience base, embedding retrieval supplies candidates and the model judges semantic equivalence; matching trajectories attach to an existing principle. Appendix A.1 specifies BGE-M3 embeddings, Milvus storage, a similarity threshold of 0.85, and retrieval of three principles per request (p. 16). Equation 1 only expresses threshold-based insertion/merging, omitting the LLM-equivalence condition described in prose; the prose and appendix therefore matter for reproduction.

Each principle receives the score

\[
s(p)=\frac{c_{\mathrm{succ}}(p)+1}{c_{\mathrm{use}}(p)+2},
\]

and periodic sweeps remove scores below 0.3 (Eq. 2, p. 5; A.1). Under a stationary Bernoulli interpretation this is a Beta(1,1)-smoothed success-rate estimate. It is not an estimate of the improvement caused by supplying that principle. The paper does not precisely operationalize retrieval versus application in its usage counter or explain credit allocation among co-retrieved principles.

Online, the model can search its experience base, search external factual knowledge, or answer. GRPO updates its policy using eight sampled trajectories per prompt and 128 prompts per RL step. Reward is normalized-answer exact match plus 0.1 times a structural reward for reasoning/search patterns; clipping and KL regularization constrain updates (§3.3; A.1). Retrieved experience and document tokens are normally masked from the training loss, while generated behavior conditioned on that context is optimized (A.3, p. 19). This distinction separates learning to use advice from directly training on advice text.

The lifecycle assumes task outcomes can be reliably labeled, trajectories contain enough evidence to diagnose strategy, and relevance/equivalence judgments preserve conditions of applicability. It also needs trainable model access. “Self-contained” applies imperfectly: approximately 700 cold-start examples receive LoRA SFT, and Appendix A.4.1 identifies GPT-4o as their trajectory generator (pp. 7, 19–20). Self-distillation does not mean an entirely teacher-free origin.

## Evidence and disagreements

Training/experience construction uses NQ and HotpotQA training splits; five other QA datasets assess transfer within search-based QA (§4.1, p. 7). Main results are benchmark-macro-average exact match:

| Comparison | Reported result | Supported inference |
|---|---:|---|
| 3B EvolveR vs Search-R1-instruct | 0.382 vs 0.325 | Combined system improves by 5.7 percentage points (Table 1, p. 8). |
| 7B EvolveR vs Search-R1-instruct | 0.417 vs 0.385 | Improvement persists at 7B; it loses on 2Wiki (0.395 vs 0.414). |
| Same experience-trained 3B policy, retrieval enabled/disabled | 0.382 vs 0.340 | Access to memory matters after experience-conditioned training (Table 3, p. 10). |
| 3B self-distillation vs GPT-4o-mini distillation | 0.382 vs 0.370 | A small advantage in this comparison, not a general same-model advantage (Table 2, p. 9). |
| 3B GRPO vs successful-trajectory SFT | 0.382 vs 0.357 | RL improves on this SFT configuration by 2.5 points (Table 8, p. 17). |

The ablations are valuable but narrower than several interpretations in the text. Disabling retrieval after training creates a changed input/tool regime; it does not isolate the benefit of adding memory to an otherwise unchanged agent. Table 4's non-RL retrieval score, 0.357, matches the explicitly SFT-trained result in Table 8. Consequently, it should not be cited as demonstrated zero-parameter-update memory performance. The SFT comparison also differs in negative-trajectory access and optimization, so it cannot establish that SFT merely imitates “surface-level” behavior while RL understands utility.

Teacher distillation wins at 0.5B (0.220 versus 0.150) and 1.5B (0.290 versus 0.270). The reversal at 3B is interesting, but there are no reported repeated-seed uncertainty estimates establishing the 0.012 difference. “Cognitive alignment” is an explanation proposed by the authors, not an independently manipulated or measured variable. Nor is the superiority of principles over raw trajectories isolated by a matched raw-trajectory retrieval baseline.

**Curation quality is associated with score, not causally established by it.** Manual annotation of 100 principles finds 82% ideal and 8% incorrect in the high-score tier, versus 26% and 24% in the low tier (Table 6, p. 11). This supports score as a potentially useful screening signal. It does not show that pruning caused the difference: task difficulty, policy competence, selection frequency, and co-retrieved advice can all affect the score. Annotation agreement and uncertainty are not reported. Table 10's later-created surviving principles score 0.500 versus 0.462 for the earliest cohort (pp. 18–19), but survival selection, unequal exposure, and changing policy competence prevent attribution to improved distillation skill.

The score also has concrete retention problems. With zero recorded uses, a principle scores 0.5 and survives a 0.3 sweep indefinitely; zero successes in two uses yields 0.25. Thus pruning alone neither bounds storage nor protects a rare useful warning encountered on difficult tasks. Threshold sensitivity gives 0.265–0.276 average EM across thresholds 0.1–0.9, with the default 0.3 scoring 0.270 (Table 9, p. 17). This supports robustness over those runs more strongly than the necessity of the chosen threshold.

**Efficiency claims need a reward ablation.** Appendix A.1 rewards six reasoning steps and increases the search bonus through six searches. Figure 5's convergence to approximately six reasoning steps, four knowledge searches, and two experience searches therefore tracks explicitly favored counts (pp. 16, 18). It is not independent evidence of efficient reasoning. The displayed successful rollout issues the same factual query three times, including two repetitions after obtaining relevant documents (Table 18, p. 24). That example does not establish widespread reward exploitation, but it undermines using action frequency alone as evidence of strategic improvement.

Scalability measurements span 4,213–49,934 principles and 0.04–0.20 seconds for top-three search (Table 7, p. 11). EM peaks at 0.410 around 45,000 entries and falls to 0.387 around 50,000. Because the base grows during training, this is not a fixed-policy memory-size intervention or a long-term forgetting test. Retrieval latency excludes the full generation/curation burden. The reported 3B training lifecycle takes 39.4 hours on eight A100 80GB GPUs, approximately 315 GPU-hours (A.1).

Reproduction also needs clarification of offline update/sweep scheduling and merge decisions. The printed successful-distillation prompt asks for a “pitfall” and “failure pattern” and gives non-JSON tuple syntax despite requesting JSON (Table 14, p. 21). This is a documentation inconsistency, not verified evidence about the released code. Appendix A.6 appropriately acknowledges limited task breadth and unresolved lifelong efficiency. These results support bounded QA transfer, not open-ended competence, embodiment, or a claim about consciousness.

## Prioritized proposals and falsifiable criteria

These are proposed experiments, not performed work. Numerical acceptance thresholds below are suggested preregistration choices, not results from the paper.

**P0 — Establish the benefit with a frozen-policy offline evaluation.** Before production changes, use independently scored, resettable knowledge tasks with splits by source/entity and task pattern. Compare existing retrieval, a token-matched raw-episode condition, and distilled principles; include a generic-advice control to detect gains from extra instruction alone. Freeze model, source snapshots, and inference budgets; isolate native memory state per condition because retrieval itself learns. Q58 identifies no existing chronological frozen-memory task evaluator; `DecisionStateMachine::compare:682` aggregates stored operational cycles rather than running this experiment. The cost is task construction and repeated inference. Advance only if principles improve held-out success by at least three percentage points with a paired 95% interval excluding zero, old-task regression stays below two points, and end-to-end cost rises no more than 20%. Otherwise retain current memory behavior.

**P1 — If P0 succeeds, trial a separate principle artifact and curation path.** Add a proposed strategic-principle model beside the existing memory records, with applicability conditions, guiding/cautionary type, source action/episode IDs and hashes, model revision, and versioned evidence links. Reuse the queue and consolidation admission structure around `enqueueConsolidation`/`integrateConsolidation`, while keeping heuristic proposals distinct from established factual claims. Native retrieval is a legitimate candidate-generation baseline; embedding infrastructure is a dependency to justify experimentally. Merge only when conditions and advice agree, retaining distinct warnings when conditions differ. Evaluate human-labeled paraphrase, contradiction, and scope-shift pairs; reject automatic merging if incompatible-condition merges exceed 1% or any adversarial warning is silently converted into contrary guidance. Include a self-versus-teacher comparison without assuming self-distillation wins.

**P2 — Measure incremental utility before allowing outcome-based pruning.** At `DecisionStateMachine::start`, record exactly which principle IDs entered the prompt. Link later `finishAction` evidence to a separately adjudicated task result; distinguish retrieved, displayed, model-claimed application, verified adapter completion, and task success. Randomly withhold selected eligible advice in isolated trials, holding policy and task mix fixed, to estimate incremental benefit. This extends beyond Eq. 2 and is explicitly a proposed causal evaluation. Compare raw smoothed scores with sample-aware retention plus a fixed storage budget, preserving unsupported rare warnings for review. Reject pruning if it removes warnings with positive controlled benefit or causes old-task regression above two points. This requires outcome labels and attribution plumbing, not renaming access counts as rewards.

**P3 — Defer RL; isolate retrieval control first.** Q03 supports a bounded adaptive retrieval/re-proposal experiment at the decision-cycle boundary, with a strict call budget and existing adapter validation. Compare it against initial-only retrieval under equal total tokens and wall-time limits; reject it if extra calls do not improve success within those limits. Full EvolveR reproduction additionally needs a trainable backend, likelihoods, audited trajectories, outcome labels, and substantial compute. If later attempted, ablate the search/think-count reward and measure duplicate queries and success per cost. Table 11's slight degradation from directly training on retrieved experience (0.371 versus 0.382, p. 19) supplies no reason to prioritize that absorption variant.

**Most consequential next step:** measure whether conditions-bearing strategic advice adds held-out task value beyond Navi's existing episodic/semantic retrieval. Storage growth, accepted summaries, and successful adapter execution cannot answer that question.
