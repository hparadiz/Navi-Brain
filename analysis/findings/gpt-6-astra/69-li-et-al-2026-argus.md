## Core contribution and mechanism

Argus treats a long research effort as bounded missions over durable campaign state. Its distinctive proposal is **evidence-backed revision of the operational objective while preserving standing user intent**. Persistence alone can perpetuate an impossible route; unrestricted revision can redefine failure as success. The proposed remedy couples revision to evidence, explicit ownership, and a retained explanation (pp. 1–5, §§1, 3.1).

The report summarizes the working contract as \(K_t=(\iota,o_t,c_t,v_t)\): standing intent, current objective, constraints, and verification criteria. A proposed revision is admitted with evidence, a recorded verdict, and the required user or Manager authority. Crucially, `ManagerAdmit` is an **analytical projection over distributed implementation surfaces**, not an atomic transaction or proved invariant-preserving algorithm (p. 5, §3.1; p. 10, Algorithm 1). A stage may hold, advance one step, or roll back; rollback changes the research route while retaining useful results and rejected branches (p. 6, §3.2).

Manager owns campaign/stage decisions; Planner supplies bounded tasks; Engineer executes; Reviewer can return `done`, `continue`, or `blocked`. Fresh Engineer/Reviewer sessions share artifacts and a bounded `CHECKPOINT.md`, backed by a separate event record. When invoked, Reviewer is the checkpoint's final editor for that round. Allowed self-review leaves Engineer's version as the handoff (pp. 9–11, §§4.1–4.4).

This is not universal independent certification. Under permitted low-risk policy, Engineer can select `review=skip`; its prompt requires a passing verifier, but the runtime accepts that explicit judgment without an additional validator (§4.3). Table 2 also assigns tools/procedures to system configuration, verification checklists to Planner, routing to Manager, and task admission to Scheduler. The umbrella phrase “verification-gated” therefore covers several materially different admission strengths.

“Self-evolution” means changes to persistent memory, skills, verification guidance, and routing with fixed backbone weights. It is a useful description of runtime adaptation; it establishes neither unrestricted self-modifying code nor a theory of consciousness. The mechanism assumes useful evaluators, reliable evidence capture, meaningful authority boundaries, and later retrieval of the retained state.

## Evidence and limits of inference

The main comparative result is whole-system SWE-Bench Pro: approximately 78% accuracy versus 59% for Direct Copilot on 731 tasks, both using GPT-5.5/xhigh through Copilot, at approximately 1.41 times aggregate tokens (pp. 12–13, §§5.2, 6.1; Table 4). This supports the complete harness under the reported protocol. It does not isolate persistence, pivoting, or review, and there is no equal-compute baseline or retained raw Copilot token accounting. Six other arenas show breadth rather than a common effect size: 63/82 AARRI tasks, competitive GPU kernels and training runs, and a 28.0 pass@4-minus-pass@1 synthesis gap. That gap is a dataset-design objective, not general mathematical accuracy. Small advantages over historical human references, such as 79.77 versus 80.18 seconds for nanoGPT, lack uncertainty estimates sufficient to establish robust superiority.

Review routing is usefully explicit: 466 tasks invoke Reviewer and 265 self-review; among 43 revision-requested tasks, 34 subsequently pass the official verifier and 22 also obtain later Reviewer `done`; 35 are blocked (pp. 14–15, §6.2; p. 24, Table 11). These are observed correction trajectories, not 34 causally attributable rescues. Extra execution or verifier feedback might also repair them without Reviewer. Adaptive routing prevents a straightforward group comparison, and the 2.75-times token/1.80-times time difference can reflect review overhead as well as harder selected tasks. It does not independently establish task difficulty.

The headline reuse result is observational: startup uses 2.95M solve input tokens and 8.52 active minutes per task; mature waves use 2.33M and 7.25 minutes, reductions of roughly 21% and 15%. Late waves rebound to 3.72M tokens (pp. 12–15; Appendix A). My arithmetic check of Table 9 finds **618 represented tasks, not 731**. Two incomplete waves are explicitly omitted; the table does not provide the remaining 113 tasks' resource outcomes. Consequently, its grouped means do not describe the entire benchmark, and omission may affect inference. Task/repository mix changes, no frozen-state replay exists, and active time excludes preparation, external verification, infrastructure recovery, and post-task knowledge maintenance. The comparison cannot demonstrate net amortized savings from memory.

The vertical cases provide stronger mechanism illustrations than causal estimates. One mathematical campaign retains a falsified route and six proof-backed deltas, including re-derived baselines; internal acceptance does not establish novelty. Appendix C's counting argument is conditional on omitted pair-occurrence and path/set correspondence details, so the excerpt cannot independently validate the full theorem package (pp. 16–17, 25). Six manuscript campaigns span 254 missions, 640 aggregate campaign-hours, and 16 rollbacks; submission-stage completion is not venue acceptance or a measured zero-touch rate. One final PDF coexists with a stale `BLOCKED` assurance object—an especially relevant consistency failure (pp. 17–18, 21, §§6.7, 7.3).

The external cases have distinct evidential boundaries. Appendix E reports human-reviewed upstream kernel adoption, including a maintainer-requested numerical-stability repair. ACE-2 demonstrates scoped simulation, synthesis, and static timing; it is not fabricated silicon, and generation stops at two tokens (pp. 18–19). Materials best-of-K improves MOFChecker outcomes at matched budget, but uses the same 989-crystal subset involved in score design, lacks independent physical validation, and has an incomplete larger evaluation (pp. 19–20, Table 7). Optimizing an external validator does not make it an independent holdout. These cases justify examining the control discipline, not importing their scientific success claims into Navi.

## Theory: useful definitions, limited measurements

Proposition 1 is sound under its stated assumption: if the final artifact is a deterministic projection of a process record, an unrestricted optimal policy can ignore the extra information, so process access cannot increase minimum decision risk (p. 8, §3.7). It does not imply a finite-context model benefits from raw logs. The compression objective in Equation 12 appropriately introduces reading cost and downstream risk.

Equation 13 gives the most useful evaluation target: a state update compounds only if matched future tasks have lower loss with the update than without it; negative transfer is allowed. Table 13's startup/mature difference is not an estimate of that counterfactual quantity. Similarly, the 118,633-times event-history/checkpoint byte ratio demonstrates size disparity, not preservation of decision-relevant information. The strict/inclusive density scores, 5.95 and 28.06 effective token-equivalents/second, are reproducible arithmetic over coarse mission-purpose labels. Their multiplicative construction is not independently calibrated intelligence or causal efficiency. Reviewer precision improves under Equation 15 only when sensitivity exceeds false acceptance; neither parameter is measured by the recovery funnel (pp. 8, 22, 25).

## Prioritized recommendations and falsifiable evaluations

These are proposed follow-up experiments and design changes, not work performed in this review.

**P1 — Introduce a versioned operational contract and explicit pivot record.** At `Intention`, `ExecutiveCore::advanceIntention`, and `DecisionStateMachine::start/integrate`, preserve the originating request/standing intent and attach an immutable revision identifying objective, constraints, acceptance criteria, evidence, approving authority, and parent revision. A rejected route should retain a scoped falsification record; a failed attempt is not automatically proof of impossibility. Bind queued proposals and closure evidence to that revision, rejecting or re-evaluating stale work before plan publication and at `ExecutiveCore::startAction:1904`/`claimActionDispatch:797` (Q69). Reuse action/procedure fences but do not confuse them with contract freshness. This adds schema, migration, and commit/recovery work; an eight-stage research workflow is unnecessary for ordinary Navi tasks. In an isolated evaluation, inject justified revisions, unsupported criterion weakening, and delayed proposals from prior revisions. Fail the design if any stale proposal commits under incompatible criteria or an unauthorized weakening becomes canonical; also measure false rejection of legitimate revisions.

**P2 — Bind completion to scoped, versioned evidence and reconcile its projections.** Extend the existing completion producer/integrator and direct-close path to reference contract revision, artifact identity, verifier version/result, verdict source, and unresolved exclusions. Determine review requirements from task policy; a fresh model role alone does not establish evidence independence. Resolve `released`/`completed` semantics and make event/UI/assurance projections agree with the canonical verdict. Argus's stale assurance incident directly motivates this consistency invariant. Dependencies include task-specific evaluators and reliable evidence capture; low-cost self-review may remain appropriate where its narrower guarantee is explicit. Evaluate seeded implementation faults, faulty specifications, stale successful verifier outputs, and interruption between verdict and projection. Fail on any falsely certified artifact or surviving contradictory completion state after recovery. Compare independent review with equal-budget additional execution to separate review value from extra computation.

**P3 — Measure retained-state value before expanding the knowledge machinery.** Using the linkage points in Q53/Q58, design an offline paired evaluation with identical future tasks, backbone, budgets, and reset environments: frozen state, admitted updates, and admitted updates with rejected-route records ablated. Record externally scored success, repeated failed-route attempts, obsolete exclusions, all model/maintenance costs, and elapsed time. Keep negative knowledge conditional on assumptions and verifier version so environmental change can reopen a route. Isolate token-memory stores: native ranking can reinforce query links, and counted retrieval changes access priority (I02, QMEM), so nominally identical repeated reads can contaminate controls. Prespecify a task-loss/cost criterion; reject the compounding claim if accepted updates do not improve paired outcomes or cause unacceptable old-task regression. No monotone skill-count or startup/mature proxy should substitute for this comparison.

## Verdict

**Experiment with the contract and admission discipline; adopt explicit evidence scope as a documentation rule.** Argus supplies a compelling architectural case for retaining failed routes while allowing accountable pivots. It does not establish that four roles, larger logs, or fixed-model state accumulation cause its headline gains. Navi's most consequential next step is a revision-bound completion/pivot prototype with adversarial stale-state evaluation, built around its existing durable executive rather than a wholesale runtime replacement.
