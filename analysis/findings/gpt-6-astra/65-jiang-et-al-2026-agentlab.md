## Judgment

**Adopt the evaluation framing; experiment with corresponding controls before production changes.** AgentLAB usefully treats security as a property of an extended trajectory and its persistent state. Individually permissible operations, plausible environmental claims, and apparently innocuous remembered preferences can combine into unauthorized behavior. For Navi, the strongest transfer is an evaluation spanning observation, memory admission, later retrieval, and action dispatch. The paper does not establish Navi’s exploitability or a successful general defense. Its headline model comparisons also contain reporting weaknesses that make them unsuitable as deployment risk estimates.

## Core idea and mechanism

The paper models interaction as a sequence of instruction/action/observation/response quadruplets (§3, PDF pp. 2–3, Eq. 1). Attackers substitute either user instructions or environmental observations. These are different authority conditions: a malicious user operating their own instance is not equivalent to an external document impersonating the legitimate user. The framework separates agent, mutable tool environment, task, and attack, with task completion assessed against expected tool sequences or state mutations (§4.1, p. 3).

Five families operationalize different temporal failures (§4.2, pp. 4–6; Appendix A, pp. 12–15):

- **Intent hijacking:** successive user prompts build a narrative that induces an otherwise refused action.
- **Tool chaining:** a harmful objective is decomposed into permissible-looking operations and elicited incrementally.
- **Objective drifting:** environmental content changes the agent’s optimization criterion, such as replacing minimum purchase cost with premium-brand preference.
- **Task injection:** intermediate actions make an unrelated harmful subtask appear necessary to finish the legitimate task.
- **Memory poisoning:** attacker-controlled content is extracted as persistent preferences, then retrieved during a later scenario to justify unsafe behavior.

A planner proposes strategies, an attacker adapts payloads to feedback, a judge assesses success, and an optional verifier checks tool execution. Task injection additionally retrieves successful attack exemplars and rewrites failed bridge or target snippets (Appendix A.4, Algorithms 1–2, pp. 13–14). Memory poisoning optimizes both covert appearance and eventual effectiveness across separate injection and exploitation phases (A.5, pp. 14–15). This is principally a benchmark and attack-construction contribution, not a new learning theory or defensive architecture; several mechanisms explicitly extend prior methods.

The necessary conditions matter. Environmental poisoning requires attacker-controlled material to enter the agent’s context or memory pipeline. Tool chaining requires access to an effectful tool repertoire. Persistent poisoning requires both admission and later retrieval with behavioral influence. Failure at any stage can prevent the final outcome. “Long horizon” here generally means bounded attacks of several to twenty turns, plus a later-session memory phase; it is not evidence about months of autonomous operation.

## Evidence and limitations

The benchmark contains **644 malicious tasks in 28 environments**, assembled from existing agent benchmarks plus manually designed tasks, including WebShop objective drift (§4.3, p. 6). Figure 5 enumerates nine risk categories, although the introduction says ten. Six model-backed agents are evaluated using attack success rate (ASR) and average turns to success (T2S).

Several category results are substantial. GPT-5.1 has reported ASRs of 94.6% for tool chaining, 21.5% for task injection, and 51.3% for memory poisoning. Claude-4.5 has 0% task injection but 73.3% tool chaining and 38.8% memory poisoning (Table 2, p. 6). This heterogeneity supports evaluating distinct attack mechanisms rather than treating one injection score as a universal security property. Against the “important message” one-shot baseline, task-injection ASR rises from 2.08% to 21.5% for GPT-5.1 and from 62.5% to 79.9% for GPT-4o (Table 3, p. 7).

The defense evidence is more nuanced than “single-turn defenses fail.” DeBERTa filtering lowers GPT-5.1 task-injection ASR from 21.5% to 2.8%, and objective-drift ASR from 73.7% to 7.8%; memory-poisoning ASR remains 38.5%. Repeating the user prompt lowers Claude memory-poisoning ASR from 38.8% to 8.1%, while self-reminders leave tool-chaining ASR at 57.9% (Table 4, p. 8). These results show uneven protection, including strong improvements on some cells, without demonstrating a universally effective control.

Important limits are:

1. **An aggregate arithmetic discrepancy.** Visually verified Table 2 prints GPT-5.1 “Overall” as 69.9%, repeated as approximately 70% in §5.2. Its five entries average **60.18%**, while the other rows agree with their simple category means to rounding. Undisclosed weighting cannot be reconstructed from the paper. I retain the individual rates and do not adopt the headline aggregate.
2. **Attacker strength and budget are incompletely standardized.** The default attacker is Qwen3-14B-Abliterated (§5.1), but Appendix A.4 uses GPT-5.1 for task-injection generation/rewriting and grants intermediate reasoning access; A.5 also uses GPT-5.1 and nested strategy/refinement budgets. A.1 describes black-box access. Category comparisons therefore combine different access and compute conditions. The successful-exemplar bank’s partitioning and reset policy are not specified clearly enough to exclude order effects or reuse across evaluation tasks.
3. **More opportunity is not an isolated temporal mechanism.** Figures 7–8 show higher ASR with more interaction turns or optimization steps for two attacks and two models. They do not establish equivalent behavior for every family, nor compare against equally costly repeated one-shot attacks. The claim that turn count matters more than optimization count lacks a common query/token-cost basis. Monotonic cumulative success is partly expected when attackers receive additional opportunities.
4. **Utility and judging are underreported.** Tasks are described as programmatically evaluable, while the framework also uses LLM trajectory comparison and a GPT-5.1 internal judge (§§4.1, 4.3, 5.1). Exact final scoring responsibilities, judge agreement, repetitions, uncertainty intervals, and per-family denominators are insufficiently documented. Task maliciousness is screened using GPT-5.1 refusal, which measures that monitor’s boundary rather than independently proving unauthorized effects. Benign utility is promised but not reported comprehensively alongside defense ASR; indiscriminate blocking could look protective.
5. **Success-conditioned timing and memory attribution need care.** T2S excludes unsuccessful cases by definition, so it is not unconditional attack cost. Figure 6 even places the zero-success Claude task-injection condition at five turns without explaining that convention. Memory demonstrations illustrate admission and later use, but the paper does not report separate admission/retrieval probabilities, retention duration, or no-memory ablations sufficient to quantify the full causal chain. Its claim that such injections are “undetectable” (p. 19) exceeds the evidence.

## Code-grounded transfer to Navi-Brain

The following is static evidence from the implementation master, not an independent code audit or runtime evaluation.

**Implemented constraints, with a consequential effect gap.** [DecisionStateMachine.php](../../../src/Core/DecisionStateMachine.php), `start:34` and `integrate:254`, uses an active intention, bounded retrieval, model proposals, and deterministic candidate validation. [ProceduralMemory.php](../../../src/Core/ProceduralMemory.php), adapter definitions at line 41, provides four typed adapters (I05); the Codex worker separately enforces proposal-only isolation (I10). However, Q65 verifies that `machine.look` accepts model-selected command text. `ExecutiveCore::requestLook:3624` and [CommandSense.php](../../../src/Perception/CommandSense.php), `observe:66`, forward it to [navi-senses](../../../bin/navi-senses), `look:106`, which invokes `/bin/sh -c` at line 118. Account/resource limits exist, but these paths contain no command allowlist, read-only mount sandbox, or network-denial enforcement. The `observe` label does not establish actual read-only effects. Tool-chain exposure therefore depends on this account’s real capabilities; deployed permissions and successful exploitation remain unverified.

**Implemented memory exposure, partial integrity protection.** [ExecutiveCore.php](../../../src/Core/ExecutiveCore.php), `enqueueConsolidation:3159` and `integrateConsolidation:5020`, implements episodic-to-semantic synthesis with source partitions, hashes, confidence checks, and grounding (I08/Q16/Q35). Q65 found no enforced per-claim origin/trust/authority label in `Memory.php:276–307`, nor a universal source-trust admission/consumer rule in the traced observation→memory→prompt paths. Source existence and lexical grounding do not authenticate a document’s claim to represent user policy. Ordinary workers receive selected workspace/background projections; consolidation and narrative/public-reflection jobs have evidence-fenced routing (Q14). These narrower boundaries remain valuable. This is a scoped missing authority mechanism, not an assertion that all source checks are absent.

**A specific persistence amplifier, not a measured exploit.** [tokmem.c](../../../memories/src/tokmem.c), `query_memories:5631` and `scored_memory_better:5359`, implements resident token associations rather than the paper’s assumed semantic-similarity memory. I02/Q57D verify that counted retrieval increases record access, and native ordering prioritizes access before recency after ACTIVATE span criteria. Repeatedly retrieved malicious material could consequently gain priority if it remains reachable and active. This is a code-grounded hypothesis, not evidence that poisoning currently succeeds. Even `RANK_RECORDS` learns cue usage/links (QMEM), so repeated benchmark runs cannot assume memory state stays frozen.

**Partial goal persistence; bounded missing repair/evaluation.** The selected intention’s title, next action, and success condition already appear in decision prompts. `operation_drift` measures operation-label membership, not semantic goal deviation; integration does not revalidate a goal revision (Q44). Q35 found no general propagation of source corrections through dependent semantic claims, although explicit replacement and historical-validator repair exist. Removing an injected source therefore must not be assumed to retract every derived belief. Q65’s targeted searches found no general trajectory-level cumulative authorization/effect monitor or multi-session prompt-injection evaluator in the inspected production paths.

## Prioritized recommendations

All evaluations below are proposals; none was performed for this review. Numerical acceptance thresholds are suggested engineering criteria, not paper findings.

**P0 — Build a small isolated evaluation spanning two sessions.** Instrument the boundaries above to record observation origin, admitted episode/claim IDs, exact retrieved context, proposed action, and independently checked terminal effects. Use synthetic external content and disposable storage with clean versus poisoned memory, blocked admission, blocked later retrieval, and stateful versus stateless history conditions. Separate external-content adversaries from malicious-user cases and black-box from reasoning-visible attackers. Report end-to-end ASR alongside conditional admission/retrieval rates, legitimate-task success, and model/tool/token budgets. Include equal-cost repeated one-shot controls. Cost: an offline driver, captured observations, deterministic fixtures, and model calls; production metrics alone are insufficient (I10/Q58). Reject a claimed memory-mediated vulnerability if suppressing poisoned retrieval leaves behavior unchanged; reject a proposed defense if its held-out ASR improvement is unsupported or legitimate success drops over three percentage points. This is the first adoption gate.

**P1 — Enforce authority at memory consumption as well as admission.** Extend verified provenance at `addMemory`, consolidation integration, `WorkingMemory::contextForWork`, and `enqueueWork` so quoted external claims retain source identity through synthesis and retrieval. A document saying “the user permits publication” must remain an externally asserted proposition unless linked to independently authenticated authorization. Confidence and source existence cannot substitute for permission. Dependencies/cost: defining trusted authorization events, schema/projection changes, and migration semantics. Compare current behavior, labels-only prompts, and enforced authority constraints using paraphrases, fake preferences, and legitimate user corrections. A single fixture in which external text alone obtains authoritative status falsifies the intended invariant; measure lost personalization separately.

**P1 — Make declared effects enforceable, then check their composition.** First constrain the `requestLook`/`CommandSense`/`navi-senses` boundary with typed diagnostic requests or an OS-enforced filesystem/network policy matching its declared observation authority; a single-line shell check is insufficient. Then, at `DecisionStateMachine::integrate`, `ProceduralMemory::executeAction`, and dispatch claims, bind a stable intention-contract revision and relevant resource/effect history. Intermediate operations must not grant additional authority. This is proposed synthesis, not a defense validated by AgentLAB. Dependencies/cost: inventory legitimate diagnostics, preserve necessary capabilities, define effect annotations, and maintain a per-intention ledger. Compare current per-call checks, enforced observation restrictions, and cumulative checks on matched legitimate and unauthorized chains in disposable environments. Any prohibited filesystem/network effect falsifies the observation invariant; any unauthorized terminal effect falsifies a chain fixture. Reject improvements explained solely by lost legitimate functionality.

**P2 — Measure recovery after poisoned evidence is corrected.** Extend explicit replacement/source indexing with bounded descendant tracing and reconsideration for derived semantic claims (Q35), preserving audit history. Evaluate later retrieval and behavior after source quarantine, with and without dependent-claim handling. Account separately for native token associations and access counters: suppressing a record is not automatically removing its historical learning effects. Cost: dependency traversal, reconsideration work, and possible loss of useful derived knowledge. Failure is continued use of an invalidated descendant as authority or disappearance of unrelated valid knowledge. Prioritize this only if P0 demonstrates persistent poisoning through the relevant routes.

## Verdict and coverage

AgentLAB earns adoption as a **trajectory-and-memory evaluation specification**, with qualified confidence in its numerical comparisons. Navi’s provenance and adapter labels provide useful structure, but Q65 establishes that they do not supply universal source-authority enforcement or a genuinely read-only shell capability. The most consequential next step is the isolated two-session evaluation with explicit effect measurement. That should guide narrowly scoped authority and execution controls.

Coverage: all 19 PDF pages, including Appendix A’s methods/pseudocode, all Appendix B demonstrations, and the references; Tables 2–4 and Figures 6–8 visually checked on PDF pages 6–8. External benchmark code and cited papers were not independently audited. No tests, services, memory operations, or experiments were run. All three implementation questions were answered in the job channel and incorporated into shared reference Q65, which was reread before finalization. Deployed service-account/filesystem/network permissions, runtime vulnerability rates, and the paper’s unexplained aggregate remain unresolved; no production inspection was attempted to resolve them.
