## Core contribution

PEPA makes personality an input to goal generation and action preferences, then uses experience to revise those preferences. Its useful contribution is an embodied integration and allocation of computation across timescales: occasional expensive reflection produces guidance for frequent inexpensive decisions. The evidence supports a bounded adaptation experiment, considerably less than the claimed demonstration of open-ended evolution.

The three systems have specific responsibilities (PDF pp. 3–4, §§III-A–C, Fig. 1):

- **Sys3:** A large language model receives a personality description, capability/internal-state descriptions, and memories. It generates an ultimate goal and daily goals. Daily reflection summarizes outcomes and proposes incremental adjustments to the existing intrinsic reward, including trait preferences, goal incentives, constraint penalties, and experience-specific adjustments.
- **Sys2:** During training-data collection, an LLM supplies candidate actions and simulated outcomes for Monte Carlo tree search. Selection uses UCT, `Q(s,a) + c sqrt(log N(s)/N(s,a))`, and the most-visited action supplies a training label. Deployment uses a distilled dual-head BERT: multi-label action classification from `[CLS]` and BIO slot tagging for navigation parameters. This is supervised policy distillation from search outputs, not online reinforcement learning demonstrated on the robot.
- **Sys1:** Perception and installed movement/expression skills execute commands with outcome reporting. Episodic records contain pre/post observations, outcomes, resource use, and context; daily summaries and longer-term traces support later reflection.

The formal reward is `R_total = R_intrinsic + R_extrinsic`, with the intrinsic component generated from personality, memory, capabilities, and state (§II-A, pp. 2–3). This is an organizational specification rather than a fully specified POMDP solver: belief updates, reward encoding/scaling, search budgets, and the policy's conditioning interface are not supplied. Because memories and rewards change, a stationary formalization would need to incorporate that evolving context into state, or explicitly model nonstationarity.

The experimental personalities are **three-sentence natural-language descriptions**. Big Five coordinates are analytical labels, not numerical inputs to Sys3 (§IV-B, p. 6). Consequently, the results concern prompted behavioral prototypes, not identification of a continuous psychometric trait model. Personality remains a user-specified anchor; the demonstrated changes principally concern goals and resource-management strategies.

## Evidence and what it establishes

**Physical integration is real but narrow.** A Unitree Go2-W, Piper arm, Jetson Orin NX, LiDAR, and RGB-D camera implement office navigation, elevator interaction, stairs, expression, and charging (§IV-A, pp. 5–6). Navigation uses prebuilt maps and engineered controllers. The staircase ablation reports 10/10 successful traversals with a height-aligned costmap versus 0/10 with fixed-height slicing, split into five ascents and five descents for each table row (Table I). This supports the local navigation modification. It does not isolate personality, reflection, or persistent goal generation. Deployment snapshots show selected skills and charging; the paper supplies no comparable quantitative unattended multi-day physical survival study.

**The longitudinal results are simulated.** Due to cost and safety concerns, the three-day personality experiments use hand-crafted rules calibrated from hardware: fixed action battery costs, temperature accumulation/recovery, fixed-rate charging, and failure at zero battery (§IV-B, pp. 6–7). All five personalities fail through battery depletion on Day 1; all reach the 24-hour evaluation ceiling on Day 3, reportedly with 72%–100% battery remaining (Fig. 5). Lazy's rest fraction rises from 14.5% to 49.8%; Playful's exploration declines from 29.2% to 17.1% (Fig. 4 and p. 7). Generated goals include raising Playful's exploration battery threshold from 35% to 40% (Table II).

These results are consistent with useful experience-conditioned energy management. They do not distinguish reflection from simpler charging rules, fixed conservative rewards, or additional prompting: there is no frozen-reflection, shuffled-memory, fixed-reward, or non-personality energy-controller ablation. Independent rollout counts, uncertainty intervals, and alignment-scoring procedures are not reported for these longitudinal results. Twenty-four hours is a censored survival endpoint, not an estimated lifetime. Action shares also come from days with different survival durations; without matched observation windows they can change because later-surviving agents experience different states. High remaining battery must be assessed alongside accomplished activity to exclude trivial resource preservation.

**Architecture comparison favors hybrid deployment, with confounds.** Table IV (p. 8) reports:

| Measure | All-cloud Qwen3-235B | All-edge Qwen2.5-1.5B | PEPA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cloud calls/day | 320.6 | 0 | 1 |
| Inference latency | 25.64 s | 3.54 s | 0.15 s |
| Day-3 average simulated survival | 24 h | 2.85 h | 24 h |

The measured tradeoff supports amortizing expensive inference. It does not isolate a necessary three-system architecture: model size, training/distillation, inference placement, and invocation frequency change together. Nor does survival equivalence establish general decision-quality equivalence. Missing details include distillation dataset size, train/evaluation separation, search compute, retraining schedule, and latency variability. Most consequentially, the PDF does not specify how newly revised goals/rewards reach the deployed BERT policy: conditioning inputs, renewed distillation, or another mechanism. The claimed single daily cloud call does not resolve that interface or account for offline teacher-search cost. This is underdocumentation, not evidence that no working implementation exists.

**Controlled personality probes show differences, with an unresolved tension.** Table III uses ten repetitions per personality in each of three identical-input scenarios (§IV-D, pp. 7–8). That is evidence of condition-dependent action distributions, not longitudinal personality stability. Scenario B explicitly requests exploration under low battery, yet Lazy records 100% in the combined Explore/Affection row; Playful and Curious record 80% exploration-associated choices. The table's grouped labels and missing precise battery/policy-day details prevent a definitive violation claim. Nevertheless, these observations do not establish reliable low-energy self-preservation and require reconciliation with the prose emphasizing conservation. There is no separately measured cross-context personality fidelity or human assessment.

## Main disagreements and limits

The open-endedness argument (§II-B, p. 3) assumes an unbounded sequence of distinct goals with pairwise distinct goal-conditioned trajectories, then concludes nonrecurrence. PEPA neither proves that its mechanism produces that sequence nor measures the relevant recurrence horizon. Distinct finite episodes can also revisit states; textual goal novelty does not establish new competence. Three iterations cannot validate an infinite-horizon premise. The conclusion acknowledges the fixed skill repertoire (p. 8), but merely adding primitives would not establish unbounded evolution either. Conversely, a finite action alphabet alone does not mathematically prohibit unbounded sequences.

The stronger defensible claim is **memory-conditioned revision of a bounded policy under externally supplied personality preferences**. No evidence here establishes evolving psychological identity, consciousness, or self-originating values. The natural-language personality, installed skills, simulation physics, and initial design still organize the agent's opportunities. Autonomously choosing daily objectives within that design is meaningful; it does not remove those dependencies.

Resource constraints represented as mutable reward penalties are also different from enforced physical limits. Sys1 is described as aborting unsafe commands (§III-C), but the paper does not characterize coverage of that enforcement. Daily reflection cannot substitute for immediate resource checks. This distinction matters when transferring the design to an executive that can revise its own guidance.

## Prioritized recommendations

**P0 — Evaluate a bounded reflection-to-goal proposal loop before changing production selection.** In a future isolated evaluator, reuse `enqueueWork`, `IntentionCompiler`, and the `createIntention` schema boundary (§§I05/I10/Q32), but collect proposals without activating intentions. Each proposal should cite episode IDs, capability preconditions, a measurable outcome, a budget, and a release condition. Compare frozen guidance, reflection on relevant episodes, shuffled-episode reflection, and removal of personality conditioning under equal model budgets and matched horizons. This directly tests PEPA's missing causal attribution. It requires curated capability/outcome labels and held-out changed-cost/failure scenarios. A proposed acceptance threshold is a ≥10-percentage-point gain in independently judged useful, achievable proposals over frozen guidance, with a confidence interval excluding zero and no forbidden proposals. Failure to beat shuffled evidence rejects the claimed memory contribution; increased activity without verified benefit does not count as improvement.

**P1 — If P0 succeeds, represent preference changes as bounded, versioned proposals.** Add an explicit proposed delta beside `MotivationCompiler` inputs, with evidence, old/new values, scope, expiry, and predicted behavioral effect. Keep trait anchoring distinct from adaptive resource thresholds, following PEPA's incremental-update mechanism. A future application boundary near `DecisionStateMachine::integrate` must revalidate current resource and authority constraints independently of preference scores. This is a **speculative transfer** to Navi's executive: §Q02 documents missing general workspace-freshness comparison, and §Q23 documents that recorded interrupts are not a universal dispatch veto. Compare bounded updates with fixed preferences and unconstrained updates under resource shocks, contradictory memories, and pending-stop cases. Reject on any forbidden dispatch, unbounded drift, or apparent gain explained solely by idling; require improved useful outcomes per budget on held-out scenarios. Costs include constraint semantics, correlated outcome instrumentation, and update review/versioning.

**P2 — Consider distillation only after measuring a repeated decision bottleneck.** PEPA motivates amortizing expensive reasoning for a bounded action space. Profile queue, inference, and integration separately before selecting a narrow classifier target; §Q02 warns that existing `reason_model_ms` includes queue/integration delay. Compare a prospective compact model with the existing deterministic typed-procedure reuse (§I08), a lookup/rule baseline, and the full proposal model. Include unseen preference versions and changed preconditions, with abstention on unsupported inputs. A proposed gate is at least a fivefold reduction in measured inference latency with verified task success within two percentage points of the teacher and no added constraint failures. Count dataset generation and refresh costs. Reject distillation if rules match it, teacher behavior does not transfer under revised goals, or inference is not the actual bottleneck.

## Verdict and coverage

**Experiment; do not adopt PEPA wholesale.** Its strongest contribution is slow experience-conditioned guidance paired with fast bounded execution. Its weakest consequential link is the undocumented propagation of changing rewards into the distilled policy. Navi's next step should be the P0 causal evaluation.
