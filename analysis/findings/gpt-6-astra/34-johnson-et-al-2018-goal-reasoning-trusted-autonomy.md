## Core idea and mechanisms

Goal reasoning changes which objectives an agent pursues, including introducing or preempting goals; replanning changes how an existing objective is pursued. The chapter compares two models and connects them to human trust. It is an architectural synthesis of the authors' research, with summaries of earlier experiments and clearly identified future directions.

**Goal-Driven Autonomy (GDA).** Planner-generated expectations are compared with observations; discrepancies motivate possible causal explanations; explanations nominate goals; a goal manager reconciles nominations with pending goals before planning resumes (§3.2.1, PDF pp. 3–4 / printed pp. 49–50, Fig. 3.1). These are distinct inferential and control steps. A surprising sensor reading is neither an explanation nor sufficient justification for changing an objective. GDA assumes usable expectations, an environment model, and mechanisms for explanation and nomination; the diagram does not itself provide them.

M-ARTUE replaces some engineered goal-selection knowledge with three motivators: social responsiveness to assigned goals, opportunity through resources and available actions, and exploration of previously unvisited states. It ranks candidate goal plans by

\[
F(X_g,s_c)=\sum_m u_m(s_c)f_m(X_g),
\]

where urgency depends on current state and fitness evaluates a predicted sequence of states (§3.2.2, PDF pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 51–52). Social urgency increases with time since an operator goal was achieved; opportunity urgency rises as resources/options shrink; exploration urgency declines with elapsed time. The functions are intended to transfer across planning domains, but still require an adequate domain planning model. Novel state visitation is explicitly distinguished from predictability, competence gain, and skill acquisition. Nor is a weighted social preference a hard authority constraint.

**Goal refinement.** The Goal Lifecycle progressively constrains a goal through formulate, select, expand, commit, and dispatch. Monitoring and evaluation can finish it or invoke repair, re-expansion, deferment, and reformulation (§3.3.1, PDF pp. 9–10 / printed pp. 55–56, Fig. 3.3). Selection checks prerequisites/resources; expansion generates alternatives and expectations; commitment chooses an expansion. These distinctions matter because discovering a bad plan need not invalidate the goal, while an invalid goal should not survive merely because an executable plan remains available.

The Situated Decision Process separates mission-level goal changes from vehicle controllers synthesized from temporal specifications. Commands are gated by health sensors; unsatisfied health conditions trigger contingency behaviors. Equation 3.1 specifies \(\phi=\varphi_e\rightarrow\varphi_s\), with initial, safety, and recurring-goal components (§3.3.2, PDF pp. 10–12 / printed pp. 56–58, Fig. 3.4). The guarantee is conditional on environmental assumptions and the Boolean abstraction faithfully representing relevant behavior. If assumptions fail, the implication alone supplies no unconditional guarantee. Synthesizing a controller that activates specified behaviors also does not establish that arbitrary real-world or natural-language goals will be achieved.

GRIM makes lifecycle decisions legible through a common progress measure. In its two-vehicle, three-region survey example, “uncertainty” means **remaining search-path length**, measured in kilometers—not Shannon entropy or confidence in beliefs. Goal constraints, predicted progress, and execution bounds are distinct curves. Crossing an execution bound can trigger repair before a hard goal constraint is violated (§3.3.3, PDF pp. 12–14 / printed pp. 58–60, Figs. 3.5–3.6). This distinction between impending underperformance and actual failure transfers particularly well to asynchronous cognition.

The Autonomous Squad Member additionally infers teammates' actions, plans, and goals, adapting its own goal when theirs change. Its proposed Situation awareness-based Agent Transparency interface separates status, reasoning, and projections (§3.2.3, PDF pp. 7–9 / printed pp. 53–55). Inferred teammate goals provide context; they are not automatically authoritative instructions.

The proposed “rebel agents” can object to assignments because of divergent information, capability mismatch, moral conflict, team diversity, or narrative believability (§3.4.2, PDF pp. 15–17 / printed pp. 61–63). Their useful engineering interpretation is reasoned objection and negotiation. The authors distinguish rebellion from awareness of its social consequences; this is not a demonstrated mechanism of self-awareness.

## Evidence and limits

The chapter supports architectural plausibility more strongly than quantified effectiveness:

| Component | Evidence reported locally | Limit of inference |
|---|---|---|
| GDA/ARTUE | Earlier work is reported to outperform an online planner under unexpected events (PDF p. 3). | This chapter gives no corresponding numerical comparison or experimental protocol. |
| M-ARTUE | Comparable performance to ARTUE in a simulated Mars-rover domain, up to three rovers and three hazard levels, without additional goal-selection domain engineering (PDF p. 6). | No sample sizes, effect sizes, uncertainty intervals, or statistical equivalence analysis. “Comparable” is not established equivalence or broad out-of-distribution robustness. |
| GRIM | Illustrated simulated disaster-relief execution; a cited resolve-strategy ablation reportedly increases completed goals under changing conditions (PDF pp. 12–14). | The plotted execution is an illustration, not an aggregate ablation result. Detailed study data are in reference [23], not reproduced here. |
| ASM transparency | Interface integration and intended benefits are described (PDF pp. 8–9). | No local human-subject evidence that transparency improves calibrated reliance or lowers workload. |
| Inverse trust/rebellion | Preliminary limited simulations are cited for inverse trust with static goals; rebellion is a research agenda (PDF pp. 14–17). | Inverse trust had **not been integrated with goal reasoning**. Neither extension demonstrates reliable autonomous goal revision here. |

The underwater-vehicle incident is especially instructive: low-level software failed to report a trajectory deviation (§3.2.2, PDF p. 5). Replacing the goal selector alone supplies no remedy when the information needed to detect deviation never reaches it. Independent observability and assumptions about sensor failure remain necessary.

Trustworthiness, perceived trust, and appropriate reliance must also remain separate. The chapter sometimes proposes increasing trust as a goal; my disagreement is that optimizing perceived trust can reward reassuring explanations without better decisions. Performance-based inverse trust is a hypothesis about another agent's assessment, not direct measurement of that assessment. The chapter itself acknowledges unobservable influences on trust and limited validation (§3.4.1).

## Mapping to the verified Navi-Brain implementation

**Commitments and execution are implemented; the full refinement semantics are partial.** [ExecutiveCore.php](../../../src/Core/ExecutiveCore.php) `createIntention:1385`, `advanceIntention:1449`, and `closeIntention:1471` maintain durable intentions with success/release text and dependencies. [DecisionStateMachine.php](../../../src/Core/DecisionStateMachine.php) `start:34` and `integrate:254` implement proposal, evaluation, selection, dispatch, and verification. [ProceduralMemory.php](../../../src/Core/ProceduralMemory.php) supplies typed adapters, generation checks, sequential composition, and asynchronous continuation. These are genuine mechanisms, but adapter success is narrower than goal achievement (reference §§I04–I05, Q28–Q29).

A consequential inconsistency is already verified: automatic completion in `ExecutiveCore::integrateCompletionCheck:9877` writes canonical status `released` while emitting `intention.completed`. Direct completed closure writes `completed`. Meanwhile, [IntentionCompiler.php](../../../src/Core/IntentionCompiler.php) `dependencyStates:103` treats both completed and released dependencies as ready; dispatch does not recursively enforce the intention dependency graph (§Q05). This undermines the Lifecycle's distinction between prerequisites satisfied and a goal merely dropped. Model confidence ≥0.7 in the automatic path supplies no independently checked completion evidence.

**GDA has substrates but lacks the complete bridge in traced paths.** [ForwardModel.php](../../../src/Perception/ForwardModel.php) `observe:35` and [SensoryCortex.php](../../../src/Perception/SensoryCortex.php) `evaluate:311` forecast, detect errors, publish percepts, and wake threads. `ExecutiveCore::createRepairArtifacts:12474` creates proposed diagnostic/counterfactual records. The master's searches found no consumer implementing discrepancy → causal explanation → goal nomination → internal intention creation; the only discovered caller of `createIntention` is the explicit CLI route (§Q32). This is a bounded negative, not a denial that an external agent can submit goals.

**Current gates are narrower than SDP's behavioral contract.** `ExecutiveCore::claimActionDispatch:797` enforces action ownership and procedure/run validity. It does not check pending executive interrupts or the intention dependency graph. `raiseInterrupt:5793` records an interrupt without revoking queued dispatch; ordinary decision integration has no interrupt veto (§§Q22–Q23). Exact-intention and specialized thread/speech guards should be retained, but these findings preclude claiming a universal enforced stop boundary from interrupt records alone. The shared evidence does not establish an LTL synthesis implementation.

**Motivation and social modeling are related, not equivalent.** [MotivationCompiler.php](../../../src/Core/MotivationCompiler.php) `rankCandidates:200` uses fixed drive/authority/context/recency/investment/actionability weights, influencing ordinary model prompts indirectly. Terminal selection uses confidence, adapter simulation, action class, and lexical grounding, not M-ARTUE's predicted-state fitness (§§Q03, Q31). [OtherModel.php](../../../src/Core/OtherModel.php) supports hypotheses, delayed forecast evaluation, and speech inhibition (§I09); this does not establish ASM plan recognition or an estimate of the operator's trust in Navi.

**A complete GRIM-style monitor was not found in the inspected intention/thread lifecycle paths.** The master's follow-up (§Q34) verifies that `Intention` lacks quantitative trajectory, observation-series, and separate execution-bound/deadline fields. Narrower controls are real: `ExecutiveCore::evaluateEpistemicAdvanceThread:9075` completes at an accepted-refinement target and blocks on stagnation/carryover limits; curation at `:11151` sets progress to accepted refinements divided by the target, capped at one. Stored uncertainty changes through operation/confidence formulas, rather than measured goal-outcome progress. The search covered Intention/IntentionCompiler and ExecutiveCore progress, uncertainty, stagnation, deadline, and trajectory references. Numeric thread progress is not a time-indexed prediction-and-repair monitor.

## Prioritized recommendations and falsifiable evaluations

These are proposed changes and evaluations; none was implemented or run for this review.

1. **P0 — Specify and enforce commitment transitions before expanding autonomous goal choice.** Unify direct and model-mediated closure in `ExecutiveCore`, separating achieved, failed, withdrawn, and deferred outcomes from record lifecycle status. Require goal-specific completion evidence and explicit dependency satisfaction semantics. Extend the relevant dispatch boundary, including `ExecutiveCore::claimActionDispatch`, to consume current goal/constraint versions and an explicitly defined interrupt policy. This follows §§3.3.1–3.3.2: selecting goals and enforcing execution conditions are separate obligations. Cost includes record migration, compatibility with existing released intentions, and defining revocation ordering for asynchronous actions. Evaluate offline transition/interleaving cases covering released prerequisites, closed parents, delayed proposals, interrupts, and already-started effects. **Failure:** any newly admitted action violates the specified ordering/prerequisite policy, or any completion path emits an achievement inconsistent with its canonical state/evidence. Do not claim retroactive cancellation of effects already performed.

2. **P1 — Pilot GRIM-style progress envelopes on one measurable goal class.** Attach a monitor to a bounded inspection procedure whose required observations have independently checkable acceptance conditions. Define remaining obligations, a deadline, an expected trajectory, and a separate tolerable execution envelope. Crossing the envelope requests evaluation; it must not automatically mark completion, failure, or invent a new goal. Reuse `ProcedureRun`/`ActionExecution` evidence and timestamps (§Q29), adding correlated timing where needed; decision `total_ms` omits some asynchronous waits (§Q02). This adds semantics beyond the verified refinement-count controls (§Q34). Cost is a goal-specific measurement model and bounded repair/defer policy. Compare unchanged scheduling, deadline-only monitoring, and projected-bound monitoring on matched simulated delays, missing sources, and harmless sensory noise. **Failure:** no improvement in independently verified completion before deadline at equal compute budget, or false repair on more than 5% of healthy scenarios. The 5% threshold is a proposed engineering criterion, not a paper result.

3. **P2 — Build SAT explanations from recorded decisions.** Extend decision/lifecycle projections with current goal/expansion, the exact evidence or constraint motivating a transition, and its timestamped forecast. Use existing `DecisionCycle` and action records, preserving hypothesis-versus-observation labels. This applies §3.2.3 while avoiding post-hoc rationales mistaken for executed reasoning. Cost is provenance joins, freshness handling, and a small presentation layer. Compare status-only, status-plus-reason, and full projection views in offline decision-reconstruction tasks and, subsequently, consented operator evaluation. **Failure:** unsupported causal claims in the view, no improvement in identifying warranted versus unwarranted goal changes, or increased unjustified reliance. Pleasantness or higher self-reported trust alone does not count as success.

4. **P3 — Experiment with explanation-backed nominations, preserving authority.** Between sensory/action discrepancies and `createIntention`, prototype a proposal record carrying competing explanations, discriminating evidence, a nominated goal, and an explicit relation to an existing authorized commitment. Deduplicate recurring discrepancies and bound nomination frequency. This supplies the missing GDA transition (§3.2.1; reference §Q32), at the cost of a nomination policy and independent scenario labels. Compare discrepancy-only nomination against explanation-conditioned nomination at equal model budget, including sensor faults and irrelevant novelty. **Failure:** no reduction in unnecessary goal changes, missed necessary changes increasing, or any proposal acquiring authority merely from its own explanation. Defer deployment of M-ARTUE resource/option preservation and inverse-trust maximization until these semantics are sound.

## Verdict and coverage

**Adopt lifecycle semantics; experiment with bounded progress monitoring and evidence-linked transparency; monitor the trust/rebellion proposals.** The most consequential next step is consistent, enforceable goal completion and dependency semantics. Motivational scoring and proactive objection merit evaluation only with clearly defined constraints and observable outcomes; increasing perceived trust is an insufficient objective.
