# AutoToM: Scaling Model-based Mental Inference via Automated Agent Modeling

**Authors:** Zhining Zhang, Chuanyang Jin, Mung Yao Jia, Shunchi Zhang, Tianmin Shu. **Venue/year:** NeurIPS 2025, Spotlight. **Local version:** arXiv:2502.15676v3, 14 January 2026; the filename's 2025 date does not identify the revision reviewed. **Source:** [local PDF](../../../assets/papers/80-zhang-et-al-2025-autotom.pdf). **Reviewer:** GPT-6 Astra. **Implementation evidence:** [shared implementation reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, HEAD `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; code claims below come exclusively from that master reference.

## Core contribution and mechanism

AutoToM makes the *structure and temporal extent of a mental-state model* adjustable at inference time. Its contribution is more specific than maintaining competing textual hypotheses: it constructs a probabilistic dependency model, proposes values for its latent variables, evaluates local factors, and explicitly marginalizes nonqueried variables. A belief question can initially use observation-to-belief inference, then introduce a goal and goal-conditioned action likelihood when observed behavior helps distinguish competing beliefs (PDF pp. 4–8, §§3.1–3.4; Fig. 5, p. 11).

The pipeline extracts states, actions, and utterances into a target-agent timeline; proposes a small initial model beginning at the latest timestep; samples natural-language hypotheses with an LLM; prunes hypotheses with low local likelihood; and computes the query posterior from the retained joint distribution. Uncertainty triggers greedy additions of mental variables and subsequently earlier timesteps. Previously extracted information, hypotheses, and local conditionals can be reused (pp. 18–20, §§A1.1–A1.3).

The search objective is

\[
U(M,q)=-H[P(q\mid X^{t_s:t},M)]-\alpha|M|,
\]

where complexity counts latent mental variables. The reported settings are \(\alpha=0.02\) and \(U_{\min}=-0.693\) (p. 7, Eq. 5; p. 19, Algorithm 1). This trades query concentration against a complexity proxy; it is neither Bayesian model evidence nor a measured expected improvement in answer accuracy. Algorithm 1 ultimately returns an argmax even when exhausting the available history fails to achieve the utility threshold.

The implementation also qualifies the paper's “any model/mental state/recursion” language. Its ordinary model family has 30 configurations per timestep built from state, action/utterance, observation, belief, goal, and interactive belief, with restricted dependency transformations (pp. 20–21, Table A1 and §A1.5). Recursive inference samples **one** state at each descent through nested beliefs, rather than integrating every possible nested world (p. 20, §A1.4). The affective extension adds attitude and preference to the causal structure (§A3.3.2). These are useful extensibility demonstrations, not unrestricted discovery of psychological ontologies.

## Evidence and what it establishes

The strongest result is improved reasoning with the same GPT-4o backend and, for multimodal datasets, the same fused textual inputs. Table 1 reports a five-benchmark mean of **82.43%**, versus **63.39%** for GPT-4o and **74.18%** for Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking. MMToM-QA shows the largest clear improvement: **83.00% versus 44.00%** for GPT-4o. Three MMToM-QA seeds yield **82.56% ± 0.45 percentage points standard error**, compared with **65.94% ± 0.59** for o3-mini-high (pp. 9–10). This supports a substantial scaffolding benefit on the evaluated tasks.

It does not win every benchmark: SymbolicToM reaches 98.60% on its native ToMi versus AutoToM's 88.30%; Gemini Thinking is slightly higher on MuMA-ToM; o3-mini-high is higher on Hi-ToM. Hi-ToM coverage is only the length-one subset, 200 questions across orders zero through four; AutoToM scores 55% at order four. “Arbitrary recursion” is an algorithmic interface claim, not demonstrated depth-independent reliability (pp. 25, 29–30, Tables A7/A11). Additional coverage includes 200 short-context, first-order FANToM questions and 596 OpenToM attitude questions; OpenToM improves over GPT-4o but remains below o3-mini-high (pp. 22–24).

The ablations support adaptation rather than indiscriminate model growth. Removing variable adjustment reduces the five-benchmark mean to 77.49%; using only the last timestep gives 69.11%; using every timestep gives 77.92% and raises mean tokens from approximately 10,000 to 19,000. Removing hypothesis reduction gives 81.15% at approximately 15,300 tokens (pp. 25–26, Tables A8–A10). These results establish a favorable empirical allocation tradeoff, although they do not isolate entropy from alternative search objectives. There is no corresponding ablation of the stopping score against held-out predictive evidence.

Cost is material. AutoToM averages **27.03 API calls per question** across these benchmarks. On MMToM-QA it reports 8,000 tokens and 8.5 seconds, versus o3-mini-high's 10,900 tokens and 21.6 seconds, but Gemini Thinking is faster at 6.1 seconds (pp. 22, 26, Tables A2/A10). These provider-dependent measurements do not establish suitable latency for Navi's local workers. Alternative backends improve substantially under AutoToM, but MMToM-QA scores remain 67.5–71.7%, below GPT-4o-backed 83% (p. 12, Table 2).

Human-judgment correlations are encouraging but distinct from calibration: AutoToM obtains Pearson correlations 0.93 for fully observed goal inference, 0.88 for partially observed desire inference, and 0.73 for belief inference. o3-mini-high is better on the first task at 0.97 (pp. 11–12, Table 3). The stimuli comprise six usable goal scenarios and one representative from each of seven food-truck scenario types, translated into captions and compared with original human judgments. Correlation with these judgments does not measure whether nominal 90% predictions are correct 90% of the time (pp. 30–32, §A4).

The embodied study supplies a useful downstream result: with the same uncertainty-aware planner, AutoToM gives **27.7% speedup**, versus GPT-4o's 6.8%, across 20 household simulation episodes, three runs each (pp. 12–13). Speedup is \(L_{main}/L_{helper}-1\), where lengths count task steps, **not inference-inclusive elapsed time** (p. 31, §A5.1). This supports goal inference helping a supplied planner within four task categories; it does not demonstrate open-ended human assistance or improved real-world interaction.

## Main disagreements and adoption risks

**Concentration is not correctness.** A missing true hypothesis or incorrectly estimated observation can yield low entropy and stop model expansion before the decisive earlier evidence is used. Repeatedly selecting structures for sharper posteriors can select overconfidence. Numerically, with three latent variables, a binary posterior of approximately (0.68, 0.32) already passes the published utility threshold. The threshold is therefore not a general high-confidence guarantee. Human correlation and answer accuracy do not resolve this structural problem.

**Explicit arithmetic does not validate the factors.** Appendix A6.3 supplies “Likely/Unlikely” prompts containing substantive rules about perception, helping, hindering, and belief persistence (pp. 38–41). These encode useful assumptions, but the paper does not fully specify a calibrated, normalized conditional-probability construction for all proposed values. Posterior arithmetic is explicit; likelihood validity and hypothesis coverage remain learned/modeling assumptions. In particular, prompt rules such as treating possible actions as likely are weaker than a normalized rational policy over alternatives. The model is inspectable, but its causal graphs are hypotheses rather than experimentally established human mechanisms.

**The online filter needs clarification before copying.** Algorithm 2 samples new goals from an action-conditioned distribution called a “prior,” then multiplies their weights by action likelihood without specifying the proposal correction or initial weights. It also filters normalized weights below 10% with 20 particles: a uniform 20-particle set would lose every particle, and at most ten can survive. No empty-set fallback is shown (p. 33). These are limitations of the published specification, not claims about unseen author code. The assistance result remains empirical evidence, but it does not prove exact Bayesian SMC or robustness under sustained ambiguity.

## Prioritized recommendations and falsifiable criteria

1. **P0 — Establish content-sensitive inference in an isolated study.** At the conceptual seam of `OtherModel::hypothesisDistribution`, prototype one narrow actor/object-location domain with explicit state, exposure, belief, goal, and action factors. Preserve reported evidence separately from inferred values. Compare direct prompting and this fixed factor model using identical inputs/backend budgets; use the existing kind-conditioned forecast as a negative control for content sensitivity. Include matched scenarios differing only in witnessed versus unwitnessed change, goal identity, and action slips. Dependency: independently labeled trajectories and a defined action domain; current activity categories are insufficient. **Failure:** no held-out belief/action accuracy and log-loss improvement over direct prompting, or unchanged predictions when the relevant belief/goal is counterfactually changed. No production replacement follows merely from better narrative explanations.

2. **P1 — Evaluate structure and history selection against correctness, not confidence alone.** Around a proposed model-search controller, cache factors by evidence and model revision, bound calls/history, and record every accepted/rejected expansion. Compare the paper's entropy-minus-size rule with fixed-window baselines and a calibrated stopping rule allowing “unresolved.” This alternative is **speculative synthesis**, not an AutoToM result. Inject confidently wrong initial models, omitted true hypotheses, and decisive evidence far back in the timeline. Extend the evaluator's separate semantic-label reporting rather than relabeling existing activity metrics. **Acceptance:** predeclare a target such as at least 25% fewer calls than full-history inference with no more than one percentage-point accuracy loss and no worse held-out log-loss. **Failure:** reduced entropy accompanying increased error, or saving calls primarily by missing early evidence.

3. **P1 — Audit probability support before enabling online filtering.** Specify normalized conditional tables, likelihood calibration, proposal weights, duplicate-goal handling, and an empty-particle fallback. Retain an explicit unresolved/other alternative and track effective sample size. These additions address §§A1/A5's approximation boundaries and should initially remain outside live hypothesis updates. Dependency: the bounded generative domain from P0; cost grows with retained support and factor combinations. **Failure:** a finite ambiguous example eliminates all valid goals, duplicate paraphrases change the semantic posterior materially, or computed posteriors disagree with exhaustive inference using the same factors. Compare pruning thresholds and proposal correction under goal changes before interpreting filter sharpness as reliable certainty.

4. **P2 — Measure helping value only after inference passes.** Reuse existing social-state publication and functional-uptake logging as observation points for an offline assistance simulation with fixed action authority and planner. Compare assistance, abstention, and clarification under identical traces; include mistaken goals and unavailable help actions. **Acceptance:** better independently scored task completion after including inference latency, unnecessary interventions, and correction burden. **Failure:** simulation-step gains disappear in elapsed time or increase mistaken assistance. The paper's 27.7% result motivates this comparison, not a broadening of Navi's present speech gate or action authority.

## Verdict and coverage

**Experiment; defer production adoption.** AutoToM provides credible evidence that selecting variables and history can outperform both direct prompting and oversized fixed models. Navi's most consequential prerequisite is content-distinguishing forward predictions: adaptive search cannot repair hypotheses whose contents never affect their likelihoods. The entropy objective and online-filter specification need independent validation before their confidence can govern assistance.
