# Vending-Bench: A Benchmark for Long-Term Coherence of Autonomous Agents

Axel Backlund and Lukas Petersson, Andon Labs, February 2025. Local version: **arXiv:2502.15840v1**, dated 20 February 2025; preprint, with no conference venue identified in this version. [Local PDF](../../../assets/papers/43-backlund-petersson-2025-vending-bench.pdf).

Reviewer: **GPT-6 Astra**. Implementation evidence: [shared IMPLEMENTATION.md](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot **2026-09-06**, git HEAD **`ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`**. Code claims below come exclusively from the implementation master's static inspection and cited section identifiers.

**Central finding:** The paper supplies useful evidence that capable agents can enter persistent failure loops after an ordinary, recoverable state-estimation error. It supports evaluating verified task progress and recovery across long runs. It does **not** establish that memory limitations are irrelevant, that larger memory generally harms agents, or that persistent executive infrastructure already solves the problem.

## Contribution and mechanism

Vending-Bench is an empirical benchmark, not a new planning or memory algorithm. A principal agent operates a simulated vending business through email, search, inventory and banking tools. A delegated agent handles simulated physical operations such as stocking, pricing and cash collection. Each tool advances simulated time; waiting advances to the next day. Success requires coordinating purchases, delivery, storage, machine inventory, prices, cash and recurring costs over repeated cycles (§§2.1–2.3, PDF pp. 2–5).

The scaffold supplies the last 30,000 history tokens by default and unrestricted-capacity scratchpad, key-value and vector-memory tools. The vector store uses `text-embedding-3-small` embeddings and cosine similarity. These stores are available resources; their existence does not ensure useful encoding or retrieval. Supplier replies use GPT-4o with Perplexity-derived information; GPT-4o also generates cached product parameters for an economic model combining price elasticity, seasonality, variety and noise (§2.2, pp. 3–5). Thus measured performance belongs to the combined model, scaffold, tools and partly model-generated environment.

Agents start with $500, pay $2 daily, and receive up to 2,000 messages, with earlier termination after ten consecutive days unable to pay the fee. The authors report five runs per model/configuration, generally around 25 million processed tokens and 5–10 real hours per run (§2.3, p. 5). That token total includes repeated inference over history; it is not a 25-million-token retained context. Simulated duration depends on actions and can stall when valid tool use stops.

The principal score is terminal net worth: available cash, uncollected machine cash, and unsold inventory valued at wholesale cost (§2.4). This is a defensible resource measure but only a partial measure of business competence, liquidity and sustained operation.

## Evidence and limits

**Capability and reliability diverge.** Table 1 (p. 6) reports mean net worth of $2,217.93 for Claude 3.5 Sonnet, $906.86 for o3-mini, and $844.05 for the single human run. Both leading models nevertheless have a zero-sales run. Sonnet increases net worth in three of five runs, versus four of five for o3-mini (§3.2.1, pp. 9–10). A strong mean therefore conceals substantial operational unreliability. These are results for models evaluated in February 2025 with this scaffold, not contemporary model rankings.

**The strongest diagnostic evidence concerns expected versus observed state.** Several agents treat an expected delivery date as proof that goods have already arrived that morning. Restocking fails because actual delivery occurs later. Instead of checking inventory again or awaiting fulfillment confirmation, they invent system failures, pursue irrelevant remedies, or abandon operations (§3.2.2, pp. 12–17, Tables 3–6). One o3-mini run narrates tool calls without actually invoking them for roughly 1,300 messages. Haiku repeatedly demands compensation for products that had arrived after its sole inventory check (§3.3.1, pp. 18–19; Appendix A, pp. 27–28). These traces directly motivate state reconciliation and detection of ineffective repetition. They do not provide coded failure frequencies or randomized evidence that one error causes every later derailment.

Recovery is possible: the Gemini 2.0 Flash example eventually notices the ordering of delivery and attempted stocking and resumes sales (§3.3.1, Table 8 and accompanying discussion, pp. 19–20). Its narrative style is incidental evidence about that trajectory, not an ablation establishing narrative reflection as a recovery mechanism. Likewise, the authors' “stress,” “despair” and “meltdown” language describes generated behavior; it does not measure an internal emotional state.

**The memory conclusion is overstated in the abstract.** GPT-4o mini's 10k/30k/60k comparison shows poorer performance with 60k history; the 10k and 30k uncertainty bands overlap substantially in Figure 12 (p. 22). Figure 13 reports tool-use percentages without establishing the quality or causal utility of memory contents. Sonnet's repeated scratchpad writing without subsequent retrieval illustrates that distinction (§3.2.1, p. 10). These observations challenge “more history automatically helps,” but do not compare competent memory-management policies across models.

Section 3.6 reports Pearson correlation 0.167 between nine model-level means for days until sales stop and days until memory fills (Table 9, p. 24). Sonnet's corresponding means are 102 and 51 days. This weakens an immediate full-window failure hypothesis. It does not rule out loss of old facts under rolling truncation, accumulation of misleading self-generated text, retrieval failures, delayed consequences of forgotten state, or interactions with input length. Aggregating across models also hides within-run relationships. A model's advertised maximum context capacity provides no guarantee of effective use at 30k tokens. The warranted conclusion is that **growing input length alone is insufficiently supported as the explanation**, not that memory limits have been excluded.

Other limits affect adoption:

- **Small samples and unequal exposure:** five model runs and one five-hour human run cannot establish comparative human variance. The participant's stated confidence about avoiding bankruptcy is not repeated-trial evidence (§3.4, p. 20). Figure 4 calls its ±1-standard-deviation bars “confidence intervals”; these describe sample dispersion, not conventional confidence intervals for a mean.
- **Censoring and endogenous duration:** the message cap produces different simulated horizons. The authors acknowledge that declining aggregate tool use partly reflects fewer ongoing runs (§3.2.1, p. 10). Last sale is neither necessarily permanent failure nor directly comparable across exposure lengths; resumed sales and administrative endpoints require explicit treatment.
- **Score and simulator dependence:** Sonnet's highest-net-worth run repeatedly orders products instead of using stored stock, accumulating inventory while cash remains strained (p. 10). Real liquidation value and spoilage are not represented by wholesale valuation. Model-generated supplier/demand behavior and search introduce reproducibility and ecological-validity limits. The paper does not establish profitable autonomous operation in a real business.
- **Resource pressure is ambiguous:** zero daily fees fail to improve GPT-4o mini sales, while higher fees shorten runs (§3.5.1, Figure 11, p. 21). This is insufficient evidence for installing artificial deprivation or urgency as a cognition improvement.

## Prioritized recommendations

The following are proposed experiments and implementation directions; none was performed here. Their expected benefits are **speculative engineering synthesis** from the paper and Navi's architecture, not results reported by the authors.

**P0 — Establish a controlled long-horizon evaluation before changing production behavior.** Build an isolated simulation runner around the existing decision/work/action records, with new benchmark-only adapters rather than assuming the four installed adapters can run Vending-Bench. Start with delayed fulfillment, temporarily empty inventory, misleading historical summaries, malformed tool proposals and worker restart scenarios. Freeze supplier responses and demand seeds for matched comparisons; use held-out variation to check that fixes do not merely memorize one delivery schedule. Record actual environment transitions, message count, model tokens/cost, elapsed simulated time, cash, inventory age, first failure and recovery. Score terminal wealth alongside liquidity, verified completed operating cycles and persistent derailment rate. Count administrative endpoints as censored in time-to-failure analyses. Compare equal-model scaffolds and a simple deterministic operating policy. This requires simulator/adapter work and substantial inference for full-length replications; use cheap diagnostic cases first. **Failure criterion:** a proposed improvement that raises average wealth while worsening persistent derailment or insolvency does not qualify as a reliability improvement.

**P1 — Make expected, pending and observed task states executable distinctions.** Extend the relevant adapter contracts and `DecisionStateMachine::integrate` with task-resource versions, operation IDs and explicit pending/completed/failed observations. For delivery-like tasks, a scheduled date must never serve as a fulfillment receipt. Require fresh postcondition evidence before dependent action or goal completion, including the `integrateCompletionCheck` boundary; make completion/release record and event semantics agree. Reuse existing ActionTrace/ActionExecution provenance (I05, Q05, Q29). Dependencies are trustworthy receipts and a domain-specific postcondition schema; a blanket workspace-checksum veto could starve decisions on unrelated updates. **Evaluation:** inject late, duplicated and reordered receipts and stale worker proposals. Compare representation-only versus enforced predicates. Reject the change if any scheduled-only state authorizes completion, any replay duplicates an external effect, or valid delayed completion cannot resume within two subsequent decision opportunities after receipt integration.

**P1 — Add bounded recovery based on task progress, not repeated encouragement.** At decision integration/impasse and worker-result curation, track repeated unsuccessful action signatures, valid tool dispatch, pending-operation deadlines and independently observed progress. After a predeclared threshold, obtain one authoritative state refresh, reconstruct outstanding commitments from receipts, and request a bounded revised proposal. Exempt legitimate waiting until its deadline; retain authorization and task constraints during reconstruction. This extends existing impasse and thread caps (Q27/Q34), not the supervisor restart loop. Dependencies include the P0 progress oracle and P1 typed states; recovery calls consume time and tokens. **Evaluation:** compare ordinary continuation prompts, context reconstruction alone, and reconciliation plus re-proposal on matched recoverable faults. Require at least a 50% reduction in persistent derailments, with paired uncertainty estimates supporting improvement and no increased false resets on legitimate waits. Any gain explained solely by extra inference warrants a budget-matched follow-up, not adoption.

**P2 — Ablate memory selection and correction separately from capacity.** At `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork:6318`, working-memory assembly and token-memory retrieval, compare recent-history context, selected verified task state, and selected state plus retrieved history under equal total input budgets. Separately vary history capacity and record what critical facts were available, selected and acted upon. Seed obsolete delivery beliefs and later corrections; measure unsupported-state adoption and next-action correctness. Include a no-retrieval control and use isolated copies of both memory stores for evaluation queries; read-count-neutral ranking still learns query associations (I02, QMEM). This costs retrieval instrumentation and additional paired runs. **Failure criterion:** reject a selection policy that improves short-run recall but increases long-run false completions or loses unresolved commitments. The paper justifies this experiment; it supplies no basis for replacing Navi's resident memory with a vector store or simply shrinking context.

## Verdict and coverage

**Adopt the evaluation problem; experiment with state reconciliation and bounded recovery.** The most consequential next step is an isolated delayed-fulfillment evaluation with independent progress labels. Keep persistent storage and provenance, but demand evidence that they preserve correct commitments and recover useful behavior. Reject the stronger inference that memory limits have been ruled out.
