# Attention Schema in Neural Agents

**Authors:** Dianbo Liu, Samuele Bolotta, He Zhu, Yoshua Bengio, Guillaume Dumas. **Year/version:** 2023; local arXiv:2305.17375v3, 14 July 2023. **Venue provenance:** PDF metadata names the Proceedings of ICML 2023; the review relies on this identified local version, without independently establishing its proceedings status. **Source:** [local PDF](../../../assets/papers/49-liu-et-al-2023-attention-schema-neural-agents.pdf). **Reviewer:** GPT-6 Astra. **Implementation reference:** [IMPLEMENTATION.md](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, commit `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; section identifiers below refer to the implementation master's static inspection.

## Core idea and mechanism

The useful contribution is a specific experiment in controlling selective information access: train a recurrent module to predict an attended representation and let it suppress components of attention before the policy consumes that representation. The evidence supports investigating this architecture, but does not establish that its advantage comes from a rich attention schema, or that modeling one's own attention enables inference about another agent's attention.

The paper distinguishes five relationships between attention and internal control (§§2, 4; PDF pp. 2–5, Figures 1–2). Hypothesis 1 uses attention alone. Hypothesis 2 places recurrent processing before attention. Hypothesis 3 places it after attention. Hypothesis 4 sends independent attention and recurrent branches to the policy. Hypothesis 5 uses recurrent internal control to modify attention itself. Shared components use the same architectural designs across conditions, with separately learned parameters; all policies train using PPO (§4.1, p. 3). This is architectural consistency, not a documented match of total parameter count or training cost.

Attention uses image patches, projected keys and values, and a query derived from an average over observations. The control module is a GRU. Figure 2(e), visually checked on p. 4, resolves the important wiring: the initial attention output enters the GRU; its output drives both a representation predictor and activator/suppressor MLPs. Gumbel-softmax produces a binary mask, which modulates attention scores; those scores combine with values to produce the policy input. Schematically:

\[
A_t=\operatorname{softmax}(Q_tK_t^\top/\sqrt{d_k}),\quad
u_t=A_tV_t,\quad z_t=\operatorname{GRU}(u_t,z_{t-1}),
\]
\[
u_t^m=(A_t\odot M(z_t))V_t,\qquad
\widehat u_t^m=P(z_t).
\]

Section 4.6 (p. 5) calls the prediction objective “ContrastiveLoss,” but displays only squared prediction error, \((h1'_m-h1_m)^2\). No negative examples or contrastive normalization appear. The source does not specify the vector reduction. Also, the displayed target is the **current masked representation**, not an explicitly future attention state or an action-conditioned counterfactual. The GRU supplies temporal memory; that alone does not demonstrate predictive knowledge of attention dynamics. Hypothesis 3 also contains a recurrent module, so the key distinction is where that state influences processing.

Architecture 5.4 is the main attention-score mask. The finer ablations remove masking (5.1), mask the policy's action distribution (5.2), mask the attention output (5.3), or feed the predicted representation into the policy (5.5; §5, p. 5). This comparison of intervention locations is the paper's most transferable experimental idea.

## Evidence and limitations

The experiments use cooperative agents with partial RGB observations and discrete movement (§5.1, pp. 5–6). GhostRun penalizes ghosts visible across the team, plus a movement cost. MazeCleaners rewards cooperative cleaning in a 13×13 maze with two agents; its described OOD change randomizes spawning locations. A GhostRun continual-learning condition adds a ghost every 50 episodes (§6.2, p. 6).

Figure 6 reports GhostRun IID/OOD reward distributions over the final 100 episodes, labeled 900–1000, from ten random seeds (p. 6). Hypothesis 5 is favored in the main comparison, and the authors describe 5.4 and 5.5 as the strongest variants. Figure 7 extends the comparison to increasing difficulty. These are useful multi-seed, multi-condition observations, although reward distributions overlap and the paper itself calls the advantages “marginal” and results “preliminary” (§5).

The MazeCleaners replication deserves a narrower reading than §6.3's statement that 5.4 “gave the best performance among all architectures.” Visual inspection of Figure 8 (p. 7) shows 5.4 highest in the learning-reward bar chart, while **architecture 4 is slightly higher in the bar chart captioned OOD evaluation**. The test distributions overlap substantially. Thus “uniformly best across tasks and evaluation conditions” is unsupported. Figure 7 has a separate reporting inconsistency: its caption assigns learning to the first two panels and OOD evaluation to the last two, whereas the first two axes say `rewards(OOD)` and the last two say `rewards`. The relative architectural curves remain visible, but precise condition-specific numerical conclusions would require clarification or underlying results.

Four limitations materially weaken causal attribution:

1. **The social mechanism remains untested.** Cooperative reward can improve through better individual perception or temporal memory. The experiments do not measure predictions of another agent's attention, transfer a self-attention representation to an other-agent prediction task, or isolate coordination gains from single-agent competence. Section 7 explicitly leaves representing and predicting others' attention to future work (p. 8), despite the stronger motivation in the introduction.
2. **Several mechanisms change together.** Hypothesis 5 adds gating and an auxiliary prediction objective. The reported variants do not provide a complete factorial separation of recurrence, prediction supervision, and attention control, including a matched recurrent gate without prediction loss. Better optimization or regularization remains an alternative explanation.
3. **The prediction target is endogenous.** The control state helps construct the masked target it learns to predict. Low error could reflect making that target easier rather than understanding useful attention. A suppress-everything solution illustrates this pressure, although PPO's task objective could oppose it; collapse is a risk inferred from the objective, not a reported observation. Prediction quality, mask occupancy, and target variance are not reported.
4. **Reproduction details are sparse.** The local paper supplies no appendix, parameter-count table, detailed PPO/GRU configuration, auxiliary-loss weight, mask-temperature schedule, or explicit target-gradient treatment. Renormalization after score masking is not specified. The basic GhostRun OOD shift is not clearly defined. Seed-level confidence intervals, effect-size tables, and statistical tests are absent; ten seeds are explicit for Figure 6, not independently specified for every subsequent result.

These limitations do not erase the experimental signal. They make it evidence for a promising neural control arrangement, rather than decisive validation of Attention Schema Theory. The authors themselves acknowledge that their GRU is far from a rich, coherent descriptive and predictive model (§7, p. 8). No linguistic-access, consciousness, or subjective-awareness result follows from these rewards.

Two distinctions prevent overclaiming. First, the master's Q13 describes future, intervention-sensitive allocation prediction, which is a stronger target than this paper's displayed same-step representation loss. Importing the paper would not automatically satisfy that stronger criterion. Second, capsule selection is deterministic given its complete inputs. Relearning its formula with a GRU may add cost without knowledge; exact replay is the natural baseline. The unresolved engineering question is whether a compact history-dependent controller improves **which evidence becomes available and useful** under changing distractions and budgets.

Existing feedback also supplies a caution: workspace entry itself can mark a sensory edge useful before downstream task success is established (Q04). Training a schema on that label could reinforce the selector's existing preferences. A controller needs an outcome measure independent of its own selections.

## Prioritized recommendations

**P0 — Establish an offline selection-and-use benchmark before production changes.** At the `CapsuleAssembler::gatherCandidates/contest/persist` and `WorkingMemory::contextForWork` boundaries, design a replay bundle containing candidate identities/features, incumbents, ordered exclusions, query, time, budget, selected records, and actual worker-visible content. Keep this separate from live memory access; **I02/QMEM** establish that ranking and retrieval can learn or update counters. Use controlled tasks with independently specified evidence requirements, including distractor floods and stale-but-confident incumbents. Capture complete inputs deliberately rather than assuming existing provenance already contains them.

The paper motivates measuring the attended representation before assigning it a model. Dependencies are an isolated replay adapter and annotated tasks; cost is instrumentation and annotation, with no need for a new model initially. Require exact selection reproduction for fully captured deterministic cases and measure downstream correctness at a fixed context budget. Failure to reproduce selection invalidates the benchmark; failure to connect selection changes to task outcomes blocks a control claim. No such benchmark was executed for this review.

**P1 — Test whether prediction adds value beyond a recurrent gate.** If P0 establishes a meaningful bottleneck, add an experimental controller immediately before optional supporting-evidence competition in `CapsuleAssembler::contest`. Preserve reserved and mandatory context. Evaluate a matched recurrent controller with/without prediction supervision and with/without gate application, alongside the unchanged selector and a random gate matched for retained context. A post-selection filter provides the paper's 5.3-style location comparison. Keep the worker model, training allowance, and context budget fixed.

The paper motivates gating allocation rather than only filtering actions; the factorial design repairs its attribution gap. Dependencies include an isolated learning/evaluation path absent from the verified PHP selector and enough held-out task sequences to measure uncertainty. Start with same-step selection/representation prediction; label next-step or intervention-conditioned forecasting as an extension. Require the combined condition to beat both gate-only and prediction-only conditions on independently scored OOD task success. Illustrative preregistered deployment thresholds are at least five percentage points improvement with a paired confidence interval excluding zero, under 10% added end-to-end latency, and no increase in required-evidence omissions. These are proposed engineering thresholds, not paper results. Reject the schema-specific benefit if it disappears against matched gating or only reduces prediction loss by suppressing input.

**P2 — Keep self-to-other transfer as a separate, lower-priority hypothesis.** Only after P1, use an isolated cooperative-agent task with observable partner selections. At a proposed adapter boundary beside `OtherModel`/`OtherModelEvaluator::report:16` (**I09**), compare a frozen self-attention representation with a matched generic recurrent representation for predicting a partner's attended region or missed evidence. Hold partner histories and training data constant; vary observation occlusion and partner policy. Score held-out prediction with a proper scoring rule and score coordination separately from individual competence.

This directly tests the paper's deferred theoretical claim, but requires partner-attention labels and a simulator, making it substantially more expensive than workspace replay. Reject transfer if the matched representation predicts equally well or coordination gains vanish after controlling individual performance. Applying such transfer to Aku's private attentional state would be speculative synthesis; this paper does not establish that correspondence.

## Verdict and coverage

**Experiment; do not adopt a production neural schema on this evidence.** The consequential next step is an isolated benchmark that separates evidence selection from verified usefulness, followed by a prediction-versus-gating ablation if warranted. The strongest reusable idea is the location-specific control experiment. The weakest inference is treating cooperative reward as evidence of another-agent attention modeling.
