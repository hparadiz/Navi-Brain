# Design and evaluation of a global workspace agent embodied in a realistic multimodal environment

**Rousslan Fernand Julien Dossa, Kai Arulkumaran, Arthur Juliani, Shuntaro Sasai, and Ryota Kanai (2024).** Published version, 14 June 2024, *Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience* 18:1352685; DOI: 10.3389/fncom.2024.1352685. [Local PDF](../../../assets/papers/04-dossa-et-al-2024-global-workspace-agent.pdf). Reviewer: **GPT-6 Astra**. Implementation evidence: [shared master reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, git `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`. **Central judgment.** This is a useful empirical demonstration that a particular recurrent audiovisual architecture can improve navigation under small working-memory dimensions. It does not isolate global broadcast as the cause, demonstrate reliable long-delay memory, or establish consciousness. For Navi, its strongest contribution is an experimental question: does a bounded workspace improve coordination because selected information changes subsequent processing, and under what resource constraints? **Experiment with existing mechanisms; do not adopt the neural architecture wholesale.**

**Mechanism and assumptions.** The authors operationalize four GWT indicators: parallel specialists, a selective capacity bottleneck, broadcast to modules, and state-dependent attention. They add recurrent perceptual processing and organized representations as RPT indicators (Table 1, p. 3). These are design criteria derived from a functional theory; satisfying their architectural interpretation is distinct from demonstrating every hypothesized cognitive capacity.

Each modality has a CNN and its own GRU. In the GW agent, the preceding workspace state enters both sensory recurrent encoders alongside their local features and histories. Cross-attention then uses current audio, current vision, and previous workspace as queries, with those three representations plus a null input as keys/values. A central GRU receives the attended modality features and attended previous workspace, producing the next workspace for the action policy (Sections 2.4–2.5, pp. 4–6; Figure 1, p. 5). Thus temporal information has several routes: local modality recurrence, workspace recurrence, and workspace-to-encoder feedback. Little attention to the explicit workspace key cannot establish little memory use, because current modality features already contain history.

The null input permits allocating attention away from substantive inputs, but is mostly unused in aggregate results. Crucially, the previous workspace is detached on its input path to modality encoders, stopping their gradients from flowing back into that workspace; the forward information path remains intact (Section 2.7, p. 8). This is a specific architecture and optimization recipe. The baseline retains recurrent sensory specialists and a bottlenecked central GRU, removing both broadcast feedback and the attention mechanism. It therefore tests a package of changes, not broadcast alone.

**What was measured.** SAVi extends SoundSpaces with audiovisual navigation to objects from 21 categories in scanned indoor environments. Sound ends after a variable initial interval, nominally requiring remembered acoustic information combined with subsequent vision. Inputs are RGB images and binaural spectrograms; actions are forward, left, right, and stop (Sections 2.6–2.7, pp. 6–7). Virtual embodiment is real task interaction, but this remains a narrow navigation problem with limited action semantics.

Both architectures use behavioral cloning on the same 500,000 oracle observation/action/termination tuples. Training samples ten contiguous sequences of length 150. The authors compare workspace sizes 32, 64, 128, 256, and 512, tune learning rate, entropy coefficient, and clipping over ten runs per search, and train final configurations for 50,000 iterations with five seeds. Tuning stops at 25,000 iterations. Reported final experiments cost over 8,616 RTX 3090 GPU-hours (Section 2.7, pp. 7–9; Table 2). The controlled training distribution is a strength, although it does not remove deployment distribution shift when cloned policies make mistakes.

Navigation is evaluated throughout training using deterministic argmax actions, with rolling episode results and IQM estimates over five seeds plus bootstrap 95% intervals (Section 2.8.1, p. 8). GW generally reaches roughly 80% success and is more stable at small workspaces; larger baseline models become competitive. GW-256 is an exception with poor runs, and late deterioration sometimes occurs after the tuning horizon (Section 3.1; Figure 3, pp. 9–10). The evidence supports a conditional advantage, not universal superiority. The cited original SAVi result of 25% uses RL and other architectural assumptions; the 25-to-80 comparison cannot quantify a workspace effect.

The representation results are less favorable to a strong memory interpretation. Two-layer nonlinear probes classify target category and scene from each modality and the workspace. There is no consistent architecture-wide advantage or clear relationship between aggregate probe accuracy and navigation performance (Sections 2.8.2 and 3.2; Figure 4). When sound stops, target-category decoding drops sharply for both architectures (Figure 5, p. 11). This does not prove all useful information was erased: a different encoding or an already useful location estimate could suffice. It does show that success on this task is weak evidence for maintaining the tested semantic representation. The authors explicitly conclude that SAVi is inadequate for testing working-memory capacity (p. 9).

Smaller GW models exhibit more mixed, changing attention; large ones often settle into nearly binary patterns early while maintaining similar task performance (Section 3.3, pp. 11–14; Figures 7–8). Therefore attention dynamics and performance are separable observables. A normalized input-weight magnitude analysis is interpreted as evidence that broadcast propagates information through sensory modules (Sections 2.8.4 and 3.4; Figure 9). This is suggestive, not a causal intervention. Gates, signs, feature correlations, and alternate recurrent paths can change functional influence without proportional changes in weight magnitude. Even the blanket claim that workspace inputs dominate both encoders has an exception: at size 128, the audio encoder's plotted sensory-input value exceeds the workspace value (Figure 9, p. 14).

**Limits and disagreements.** Removing attention and broadcast together leaves their separate contributions unidentified; matching workspace width also does not establish matched parameter count or computation. Five seeds and conspicuous configuration failures warrant retaining the unfavorable runs, rather than treating GW-256 as a resolved outlier. Different selected hyperparameters and the short tuning horizon further qualify attribution to bottleneck size alone.

Probe evaluation covers 150 held-out trajectories spanning only six categories and five scenes, compared with training vocabularies of 21 and 56 (p. 9). The paper's full-vocabulary uniform chance rates are legitimate uniform-random references, but insufficient controls for this restricted evaluation distribution; prevalence-based and shuffled-label baselines would sharpen the interpretation. Nonlinear decodability is not evidence the policy uses a feature, and scene identification is not a demonstrated spatial world model.

There is also a reproducibility ambiguity: the visually checked Equation 1 prints cross-entropy **plus** positive policy entropy in an objective to minimize, while the surrounding account motivates entropy regularization against overfitting (pp. 7–8). Under the stated ordinary entropy definition this penalizes entropy. The actual training-code sign is unverified here; one should resolve it before reproducing the training recipe. The claim that regularization is essential is not established by a clean regularization ablation in this paper.

Finally, checklist compliance is not a consciousness assay. The study does not separately demonstrate flexible sequential module querying on complex tasks merely by providing state-dependent queries. Its modest behavioral advantage is compatible with an ordinary inductive-bias explanation. The discussion's alternative that indicators need refinement is reasonable; evolutionary arguments about cortical energy expenditure do not resolve these experimental ambiguities (Section 4, pp. 13–14).

The sensory feedback deserves particular attention. `ExecutiveCore::evaluateMindStreamThread:9287–9306` marks selected sense edges “used”; `SensoryCortex::recordOutcome:860` counts used/accepted events as useful. `tune:945` can then change refractory intervals or pause a sense. An already-recorded edge does not receive another update when later thought integration attempts acceptance. Workspace admission can therefore become the usefulness judgment without independently verified benefit (Q04). This is a real causal feedback loop, with a potentially self-reinforcing credit proxy.

[DecisionStateMachine](../../../src/Core/DecisionStateMachine.php), `start:34` and `integrate:254`, snapshots workspace, retrieves records, requests proposals, and integrates actions (I04). Its inspected flow has one initial retrieval and no model-directed retrieval/re-proposal loop within a cycle (Q03). Observation/retrieval text also creates duplicate information routes, while saved workspace checksums are not a general freshness veto at integration (Q14, Q02). Removing one context route need not remove access to a fact. Native token-memory ranking is a distinct bounded associative mechanism whose queries can learn; experimental replays must isolate that changing state (I02, QMEM).

**Prioritized recommendations — proposed, not performed.**

1. **P1: Establish causal workspace use before extending broadcast.** Add experimental routing controls and per-job provenance at `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork`, `WorkingMemory::contextForWork`, and decision input assembly. Compare current selection and a recency-based control, crossed with relevant workspace delivery enabled/ablated, at matched prompt budgets and fixed task inputs. Preserve evidence-fenced jobs, account for duplicate observation/retrieval facts, and freeze sensory tuning across conditions. Include stale or irrelevant workspace substitutions to test content specificity. Dependencies are an isolated replay runner, exact input records, and repeated model calls. Preregister a material effect, for example a five-percentage-point increase in externally scored success with a paired 95% interval above zero, without higher contradiction rates. No improvement, or equal benefit from irrelevant state, fails the claimed coordination mechanism. This directly addresses the paper's bundled ablation and weight-proxy limitations.

2. **P2: Measure capacity where retained information is indispensable.** In the same isolated evaluation, vary nonreserved capsule slots and actual delivered tokens at `CapsuleAssembler::assemble` and `WorkingMemory::contextForWork`; hold mandatory constraints fixed. Present a transient cue, intervening distractors, and a delayed decision requiring that cue plus a new observation. Balance cue/action associations, prevent the correct answer from being inferred from the last observation, and include a full-history ceiling. Vary delay and distractor load, measuring decision accuracy, cue retention, unsupported assertions, token cost, and end-to-end latency. A provisional acceptance criterion is maintaining accuracy within two percentage points of the ceiling while halving workspace tokens. Failure at delays relevant to Navi, or gains explained by accidental cue duplication, rejects that budget. Do not infer improved cognition from increased slot turnover: unlike the paper's trained networks, shrinking a fixed selector does not automatically train better attention.

3. **P3: Separate sensory admission credit from demonstrated usefulness.** This is a paper-inspired synthesis, not an algorithm tested by Dossa et al. At `ExecutiveCore::evaluateMindStreamThread`, `SensoryCortex::recordOutcome`, and `tune`, first shadow-log distinct admission, downstream consumption, and independently judged task contribution. Later acceptance must remain recordable after admission without double-counting utility. In isolated replay, compare current tuning against tuning credited by those separate outcomes, including frequent irrelevant events and rare decisive cues. Dependencies include labeled event sequences and enough judged events to cross tuning thresholds; delayed credit can otherwise starve adaptation. Adopt revised credit only if it reduces irrelevant processing while keeping relevant-cue recall within a preregistered two-percentage-point noninferiority margin and avoiding inappropriate sense pauses. Lower event volume alone is failure, not success. The paper's distinction between attention weights and causal contribution makes this existing loop a better immediate target than adding content-conditioned neural feedback.

**Verdict and coverage.** Adopt the paper's demand for resource-sensitive, mechanism-specific evaluation; experiment with Navi's existing workspace before adding neural machinery or wider feedback. The next consequential step is a controlled transient-cue task with explicit broadcast-route ablations.
