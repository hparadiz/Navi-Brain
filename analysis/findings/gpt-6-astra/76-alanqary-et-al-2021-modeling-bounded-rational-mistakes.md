## Core contribution

The useful result is that an unsuccessful action can remain evidence for the goal it unsuccessfully pursues. An observer who assumes globally competent planning can infer the wrong intention precisely when someone needs assistance: after an agent wastes its only key, an optimal planner favors the remaining reachable gem, while people still recognize the now-unreachable intended gem (p. 4, Fig. 3a).

Alanqary et al. explain this through a generative agent model with distinct latent **original goal, temporary goal, partial plan, and executed action**. Bayesian inversion estimates the original goal from observed trajectories, marginalizing over mistakes. This extends an existing bounded-planning/SIPS framework; the contribution is the combined account of goal, plan, and action mistakes and its comparison with human judgments, rather than a newly invented particle filter (pp. 1–3).

Three mechanisms make different predictions:

- **Goal confusion:** with probability εg, the current goal changes from the original goal to a corrupted version, or returns from a corrupted version to the original. Otherwise it persists. In Block Words, corruption randomly permutes the target word's letters. This is a domain-defined corruption process, not a learned semantic similarity model (p. 2, Fig. 2i).
- **Planning mistakes:** the agent retains a usable partial plan and extends it when exhausted or inconsistent with the current state. A negative-binomial budget bounds probabilistic A* search. Search expands a frontier state with probability proportional to `exp(−[c(s)+h(s,g)]/γ)` and returns a path to the last-expanded state when the budget runs out. The operative budget counts **state expansions**, not necessarily plan depth. An optimistic heuristic can consequently produce coherent, multi-action mistakes: the maze heuristic ignores doors, while the block heuristic relaxes constraints on accessing covered blocks (pp. 2–3, Fig. 2ii; p. 8).
- **Execution slips:** with probability εa, the agent executes an alternative available action sampled uniformly instead of its planned action. This can produce brief deviations without changing its intended goal or making its whole plan irrational (p. 3, Fig. 2iii).

The observer assumes a shared PDDL environment model and a fixed, context-provided candidate-goal set. Symbolic observations have Boolean corruption and, where applicable, Gaussian numeric noise. Sequential Inverse Plan Search approximates `P(g₀ | o₁:t)` through sequential Monte Carlo (p. 3). These assumptions do substantial work: goal discovery, interpreting raw video, learning dynamics, and recognizing arbitrary mistaken world models are outside the demonstrated mechanism.

The Boltzmann baseline instead computes values across the state space and noisily selects reward-favoring actions, using action cost −1 and terminal goal states (p. 3, Eq. 7). Adding noise to an optimal policy can explain a slip; it need not assign sensible likelihood to an extended commitment based on a bad partial plan. Conversely, the proposed model's finite search does not prove resource optimality: its search-budget distribution is assumed/fitted, not derived from an optimization of computation cost against task reward.

## Evidence and its limits

Both studies used deliberately constructed trajectories that emphasized particular mistakes. Participants selected one or more likely goals; selections became uniform distributions over the chosen alternatives, with “I don't know” uniform over all candidates. This measures elicited choices aggregated across people, rather than directly elicited continuous probabilities from each person (pp. 3–5, p. 8).

| Experiment | Design and retained participants | Overall human–model Pearson correlation |
|---|---|---|
| Doors, Keys & Gems | 16 trajectories; three candidate gems; 18 of 20 recruited participants retained | Full plan/action model .87 ± .04; either lesion .72 ± .04; Boltzmann .77 ± .04 |
| Block Words | 16 trajectories; five candidate words; each participant saw 10; 27 of 32 retained | Full goal/plan/action model .86 ± .02; goal lesion .84 ± .02; plan lesion .73 ± .02; action lesion .68 ± .03; Boltzmann .80 ± .03 |

Values are as reported in Figs. 3b and 4b (pp. 4–5), including the authors' reported uncertainty summaries. The strongest evidence is the **pattern across mistake types**, not merely the overall ranking. On irreversible maze failures, full-model correlation is .94 versus .71 for Boltzmann; on backtracking, .92 versus .58. On action slips, however, Boltzmann does better: .93 versus .76. In Block Words, goal-mistake correlation is .84 for the full model versus .68 after removing goal confusion, but removing that component improves the plan- and action-mistake subsets. Short block-planning errors can resemble action noise; the authors explicitly acknowledge this ambiguity (pp. 4–6).

Model parameters were grid-searched to maximize correlation on these same judgments. SIPS predictions averaged ten runs, with 300 particles per run for gems and 500 for words. Sensitivity plots indicate useful performance across multiple parameter settings, but **do not substitute for held-out model comparison**. Models have unequal flexibility; no participant/trajectory holdout or paired significance analysis establishes that the small overall .86-versus-.84 gap is reliable. Cross-domain generality means the model family fits two domains after domain-specific choices and refitting: Table 1 changes full-model search noise from .5 to .02, and goal corruption appears only in the word domain (pp. 4–6, p. 8).

Several qualifications materially affect interpretation:

1. Aggregate correlation is not posterior calibration, ground-truth goal accuracy, or proof that individual observers implement these latent computations. Correlated time points and probabilities constrained to sum to one also complicate uncertainty estimates.
2. Exclusions partly depended on low task scores, and participants received performance-based incentives. Results therefore characterize engaged, selected observers in a small task family, not an unfiltered population or naturalistic assistance.
3. The appendix calls its 500-resample confidence procedure sampling “without replacement” without specifying the resample size. That is insufficient to reconstruct a conventional bootstrap. Figure 5's maze full-model curve also reaches roughly .91, whereas Figure 3 reports .87; the text does not explain the numerical difference. Treat reported intervals and sensitivity rankings as reported evidence, not a fully reproducible significance analysis.
4. Mistake recognition was queried only after each participant's final two stimuli. The appendix reports mistake detection of 81% for gems and 70% for words, with action mistakes identified only 20% of the time. This supports separating goal inference from explicit error descriptions; it does not establish accurate classification of every latent mistake (p. 8).

The evidence supports structured error models as useful explanations of human goal judgments. It does not demonstrate intervention benefits, real-time inference at engineering scale, or general access to another person's mental state.

## Mapping to Navi-Brain

The principal transfer is to **other-agent inference**, with self-diagnosis a secondary, speculative extension. “Failed to achieve X” should not automatically become “did not intend X.” This does not authorize Navi to dismiss a user's correction as confusion.

**Already implemented:** [OtherModel.php](../../../src/Core/OtherModel.php) has observation cycles, parser proposals, grounded hypotheses, TTLs, corrections, sealed predictions, and later observation-based resolution (`observeReading:52`, `integrateParserProposal:310`, `sealPredictions:756`, `resolvePredictions:848`; master **I09, Q21**). [OtherModelHypothesis.php](../../../src/Model/OtherModelHypothesis.php), lines 38–72, stores kinds including goal, plan, belief, and error, with proposition/structured content, priors/posteriors, and provenance. This is substantive machinery, not merely a profile prompt.

**Partially implemented substrate, missing semantic discrimination:** the master's **Q71** traces `OtherModel::predictionTarget:1113` and `hypothesisDistribution:1145`. Goals forecast conversation activity and plans forecast shell activity. Each kind adds fixed weights to recent/cooling categories; the proposition and structured goal contents do not enter the forecast. Consequently, incompatible same-kind goals under the same baseline produce identical predictions and likelihood evidence. A posterior number attached to those propositions is not yet a goal-conditioned inverse-planning posterior.

**Absent within the master's stated search:** Q71 found no goal-conditioned forward planner with alternative actions, transition/cost model, and trajectory likelihood feeding goal inversion in the inspected OtherModel routes. TTL/expiry does not implement the paper's original-goal/temporary-confusion latent process. No algorithmic equivalence follows from storing a plan or imposing worker token/time budgets.

**Already represented, but not generatively inferred:** the master's **Q76** confirms an explicit mixture of `action_slip`, `bounded_plan_failure`, `temporary_goal_confusion`, and `genuine_goal_change`. `OtherModel::shouldRepresentError:582` detects multiple sufficiently surprising prediction violations; `errorCandidate:593` assigns hand-coded weights from matched/violated counts, normalizes them, and stores the leading mode plus `error_likelihoods` through `recordHypothesis:538–548`. This is more than an unused error-kind name. However, every error hypothesis forecasts by taking square roots of baseline probabilities and renormalizing (`hypothesisDistribution:1145`). Its mode and mixture do not change that forward likelihood, and the inspected references contain no mode-specific Bayesian update. Better coarse prediction can validate this flattening without validating the proposed cause of error.

**Reusable evaluation:** [OtherModelEvaluator.php](../../../src/Core/OtherModelEvaluator.php), `report:16`, `onlineBaselines:221`, and `functionalUptake:305`, separates prediction scores from action-distribution changes. `OtherModel::resolvePredictions` already uses Brier and negative-log-probability scoring through [ProbabilityScorer.php](../../../src/Core/ProbabilityScorer.php), lines 39/50 (**Q21**). These score actual coarse observations, while proposition extraction recall and label accuracy remain null. They can support an extension but do not currently validate an inferred goal's semantic truth. Existing behavioral uptake mainly inhibits unprompted speech; answering bypasses that competition (**I09**).

## Prioritized recommendations

**P0 — Make existing goal and error contents affect falsifiable predictions.** At the extension seams `OtherModel::predictionTarget`, `hypothesisDistribution`, and the evaluator, define an offline observation contract containing known scene state, candidate goals, possible actions, timestamps, and provenance. Use bounded symbolic fixtures with stipulated goals initially; recognizing these variables from desktop activity is a separate dependency. Pair incompatible goals under identical activity baselines, and pair a transient slip with a persistent mistaken plan where they predict different subsequent actions. Score held-out goal and next-action probabilities separately from coarse activity prediction. **Failure criterion:** forecasts remain invariant when a causally relevant goal/error mode changes, or labels improve while proper scores do not. Current kind-based forecasts and Q76's heuristic mixture provide negative controls. This requires new semantic targets and labeled data, not additional names for mistakes.

**P1 — Experiment with explicit bounded-plan likelihoods in one small domain.** Add an isolated forward-model prototype feeding the same sealed-prediction/evaluation boundary, with latent original/temporary goal, retained plan, expansion budget, and action slip. Preserve unavailable-goal and unknown-goal possibilities instead of discarding them on failure. Compare full GPA, all three lesions, an optimal Boltzmann model, and a simpler sticky-goal/action-noise model. Hold out whole layouts and trajectory families, fit parameters only on training cases, and evaluate irreversible failures, extended detours, misspecifications, and slips separately. Record particle counts, expansions, inference variance, and p95 latency under a predeclared compute cap. **Proposed adoption threshold:** at least 5% lower held-out goal log loss than the strongest baseline, with a paired interval excluding no improvement, and compliance with that cap. Reject the added complexity if it only improves fitted correlation or cannot beat the simpler persistent-goal account. The symbolic transition model and observation contract are the major dependencies; the paper supplies no transferable production latency bound.

**P2 — Give the existing goal-change mode a distinct evidence model.** This is **speculative synthesis** addressing a limitation of the paper's fixed original goal and extending Navi's already stored `genuine_goal_change` mode. Extend the proposed latent transition model to distinguish temporary confusion from an actual goal change, preserving direct-statement provenance through `integrateParserProposal:310` and existing corrections (`correctHypothesis:1231`; **Q21/Q76**). Evaluate matched trajectories containing a slip, confusion followed by correction, and an explicit changed instruction. Keep an unknown/ambiguous outcome when histories do not identify the cause. **Failure criterion:** the model persists with the former goal after an authoritative explicit correction, or improves error attribution by systematically misclassifying genuine changes. This requires labeled revisions and additional model selection; it should precede any downstream speech or assistance policy experiment.

For later self-diagnosis, **Q06/Q48/Q76** verify proposal fields in DecisionCandidate, dispatched operation/outcome fields in ActionExecution, and expected/observed/match/repair records around `ExecutiveCore::finishAction:2281`. Those could support distinguishing bad plans from failed execution. They do not supply independently verified mental intent or a complete environment trajectory, and this paper did not test that transfer. Do not infer an execution slip merely from a failed postcondition. The master's clarification resolves the requested implementation facts; live behavior remains unverified.

## Verdict

**Experiment; adopt the distinction, defer production integration.** The paper gives persuasive qualitative and useful quantitative evidence that temporally structured mistakes preserve information about intention that optimal-policy action noise can lose. Its latent causes remain underidentified outside carefully chosen tasks, and the local statistical reporting leaves replication questions. Navi-Brain already names and weights these mistake types; its immediate bottleneck is a forecast that changes with the actual goal and error hypothesis. Build and falsify that bounded semantic prediction contract first; only then determine whether GPA inference earns its extra computation.
