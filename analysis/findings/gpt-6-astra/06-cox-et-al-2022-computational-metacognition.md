**Judgment: experiment with explicit cognitive expectations and diagnosis-linked repair; do not adopt the entire dual-cycle architecture on this evidence.** The useful contribution is a control chain from observable reasoning failure to a specific learning intervention. The experiments show that learning plus goal reasoning improves this task against a nonlearning planner. They do not establish that explicit metacognitive explanation or meta-level planning causes the improvement beyond simpler learning and conservative goal selection.

**Core idea and mechanism.** The paper distinguishes explanatory metacognition about past failures, immediate regulation of current cognition, and anticipatory judgments about future cognitive performance (§2, pp. 2–4). Only explanatory metacognition receives a detailed implementation and an experiment here. Anticipatory delegation is sketched and supported by a separate cited publication; immediate metacognition remains future work. This paper therefore supplies neither a demonstrated compute-allocation policy nor evidence for generally effective delegation.

MIDCA represents the cognitive system as a mental transition system, Ω = (Sᴹ, Aᴹ, ω). Its seven-component mental state contains the current goal, goal agenda, plan, world model/state, discrepancies, explanations, and last executed action. An alternating trace of states and mental actions makes phase inputs and outputs inspectable. Expectations constrain triples `(state_before, cognitive_operation, state_after)` (§§2.1, 3.1, pp. 3, 7). These are external symbolic process representations, not access to an agent's every internal computation.

The gardening example makes the level distinction concrete. Herbicide unexpectedly killing a neighboring native plant is an ordinary world-model discrepancy. The cognitive Explain operation subsequently failing to produce an explanation is a *metacognitive* expectation failure. MIDCA retrieves a meta-explanation pattern, checks its applicability, and attributes the failure to incomplete spray knowledge, conditional on circumstances including no other nearby agent (§3.1, pp. 7–8; Figure 2). A causal antecedent then becomes a repair target: learn a better spray operator. Detecting surprise alone does not instantiate this chain.

The meta-level chooses that learning goal and uses Fast Downward with PDDL operators to plan its achievement. The demonstrated plan has one `perform-learning` step, whose preconditions assert a discrepancy, an outdated operator, and that operator's causal involvement. FOIL uses positive/negative examples and supplied relational background knowledge to induce Horn clauses. MIDCA translates the learned adjacency relation into conditional effects of the spray operator, changing subsequent planning (§3.2, pp. 8–10; Table 2). This is an actual change to executable planning knowledge, stronger than storing a reflective narrative. Conversely, the planning effect `(learned ...)` is a symbolic success assertion, not independently sufficient evidence that an inferred operator generalizes.

The mechanism assumes usable traces, appropriate expectations, applicable causal explanation patterns, a suitable hypothesis language, and a repair learner that can express the missing effect. These are substantial supplied structures. The example does not discover a new sensor vocabulary, causal ontology, or learning algorithm. MIDCA runs a complete meta-cycle after every cognitive phase (§3, p. 5); its conclusion acknowledges singleton meta-goal sets and short, basic meta-plans (§6, p. 16). The authors also explicitly concede that metacognition need not occupy a separate module (§5, p. 15). Navi can adopt the functional interfaces without duplicating an entire executive.

**Evidence and limitations.** The experiment uses a 10×10 grid with plants in a central 6×6 garden, move/spray operators, static plants, and harmful neighboring spray effects. Problems contain 1–20 goals, with 100 randomized trials at each count: 2,000 trials for each of two native:invasive ratios, 75:25 and 60:40 (§§4.1–4.2, pp. 10–11). The baseline neither learns nor performs goal reasoning; the learning agent updates its action model and can reject harmful goals. Random placement supplies environmental variation, but does not isolate the contribution of each architectural component.

Figures 4 and 7 show higher aggregate achievement for the learning agent. At 20 goals, the plotted achievement rates are approximately 75% versus 52% in the 75:25 condition and 61% versus 53% in the 60:40 condition; these are visual readings, not published numerical tables. Figures 6 and 9 show learning-agent medians of 15 and 12 achieved goals respectively (pp. 12, 14). The authors say the operator is complete by the 20-goal problems and explain residual failures by physically incompatible preservation/removal goals (§4.3, p. 13).

An important alternative interpretation follows from those figures: **15/20 and 12/20 exactly match the native-preservation fractions.** Given one goal per plant as described, never spraying would preserve that many native goals without removing any invasives. This does not prove the learned policy never sprays; aggregate gains and individual trials could include successful removal. It does show that aggregate achievement and a tight distribution cannot establish useful removal beyond a simple conservative baseline. Report preservation, invasive removal, harmful actions, rejected goals, and achievable-goal performance separately. Score against the original requested goals so rejection cannot silently improve the denominator.

The comparison also bundles explanation, goal reasoning, and FOIL learning against none of these. Missing controls include the identical learner triggered directly by world-model error, conservative no-spray behavior, and a planner given the correct operator initially. The authors regard learning-without-metacognition comparisons as conceptually problematic (§6, p. 16); I disagree that terminology prevents the executable ablation. Keep examples and learner fixed, and remove explicit explanation/meta-planning from the trigger path.

The text describes differences as significant but reports no inferential test, confidence interval, or numerical effect-size table. Boxplots do not substitute for these. Training carryover and resets across increasing problem sizes are insufficiently specified, despite claims that the operator improves across trials; difficulty and accumulated experience may consequently be entangled. No compute, latency, or sample-efficiency comparison establishes net benefit under resource constraints. Explanations for the smaller gap at 60:40 also lack measured adjacency counts and separate goal-type outcomes. The evidence supports a useful integrated demonstration in a restricted symbolic domain, not broad causal superiority of metacognition.

**Prioritized proposals.** These are proposed experiments and design changes, not work performed during this review. P2's application to retrieval repair is speculative transfer of the paper's control pattern, not its demonstrated operator-learning algorithm.

1. **P1 — Extend existing expectations with typed failure classification.** At decision transition/result-integration boundaries, reuse Q06's pre/post and expected/observed fields, linking input snapshot, operation/version, artifact, and failure category. Begin with one expectation: a completed proposal must yield either an admissible candidate supported by referenced evidence or an explicit, classified abstention. Distinguish transport failure, valid lack of information, stale evidence, and a reasoning/contract failure; do not require a fabricated explanation merely to satisfy the monitor. This follows the paper's before/after triples. Cost is bounded trace storage and deterministic checking, plus independent labeling of semantic usefulness. In an offline set containing adequate evidence, genuinely missing evidence, and delayed results, require ≥90% precision for repair-worthy alerts and ≤5% false alerts on valid abstention. Failure means the monitor mostly rediscovers parser errors or confuses justified uncertainty with faulty cognition. These thresholds are suggested engineering gates, not paper results.

2. **P2 — Make one diagnosis produce one bounded, verifiable repair goal.** Add an explicit controller for enriched diagnostic artifacts, reusing `createRepairArtifacts` deduplication and intention/action provenance; Q06 establishes that proposed repair artifacts currently do not execute this loop. A causal-pattern record should identify applicable observations, competing explanations, a target knowledge gap, allowed intervention, and an independent success predicate. Pilot “insufficient retrieved support” with one revised retrieval/proposal attempt; that transition is new capability under Q03. A successful correction may produce a source-backed semantic lesson through guarded consolidation. Do not equate that lesson with a learned executable effect or let it redefine installed adapter authority. Cost includes the new control transition, snapshot freshness checks, and one capped model call; uncertain diagnosis must permit abstention. Compare against the same retry and consolidation operations triggered by a simple failure rule. Require higher held-out task recovery at matched total call budget and fewer repeated failures on later relevant tasks. Fail if explanations improve only their own plausibility, lessons lack downstream benefit, or retries oscillate.

3. **P3 — Establish the value of metacognitive control before adding general meta-planning.** Evaluate current behavior, monitor-only behavior, direct error-triggered repair, and diagnosis-selected repair with identical workers, intervention menus, and evidence. Include irrelevant failures and erroneous diagnoses, not only cases matching the pattern library. Measure externally adjudicated original-goal achievement, harmful/unauthorized effects, correct abstention, later recurrence, and wall-clock/token cost. Include a conservative abstention baseline analogous to never spraying. Use paired tasks and uncertainty intervals; reject the additional controller if gains disappear against direct repair at matched cost, or derive solely from abandoning goals. Instrument per-job timing first: Q02 establishes that `reason_model_ms` includes queue delay and existing totals may omit asynchronous waits. This experiment addresses the paper's central missing ablation and its admitted overhead problem (§6, pp. 15–16).

**Risks and final verdict.** A Meta-XP is a causal hypothesis, not causal identification. In Navi's asynchronous environment, stale evidence, tool failure, and incomplete observations can all resemble faulty reasoning; repairing the supposed knowledge deficit can entrench a wrong explanation. The gardening example's restricted alternatives and expressive relational background make attribution substantially easier. Likewise, goal rejection must remain distinguishable from satisfying an authorized goal: the master's Q05 already documents inconsistent automatic completion/release representation and limited evidence enforcement. Fixing labels alone would not validate repair success.

Adopt the distinction between world failure and cognitive-process failure; **experiment** with a small diagnosis-to-intervention loop. Defer FOIL, a second general planner, and unconditional reflection after every phase. The decisive next result is whether diagnosis-selected repair outperforms the same available repairs under a simpler trigger, preserving original-goal accounting and including computational cost.
