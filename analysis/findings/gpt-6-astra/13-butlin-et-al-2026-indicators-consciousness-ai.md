## Core contribution

This is an assessment methodology, not an architecture or a demonstrated consciousness detector. The target is **phenomenal consciousness**, distinguished from intelligence, humanlike concerns, and merely accessible information (Box 1, PDF p. 3). The proposal is to derive computational indicators from credible neuroscientific theories, investigate whether particular AI systems instantiate them, and update confidence in consciousness while retaining uncertainty about the theories and their transfer beyond humans.

The central distinction is between **narrow theories**, which explain conscious versus unconscious states in humans, and **broad theories**, which assert conditions across possible systems. An AI satisfying a narrow theory’s condition might lack an unmentioned biological or computational background condition. A broad theory permits a stronger conditional inference, but its broader scope is less directly supported by human evidence (PDF p. 5, “Deriving and interpreting indicators”). Computational functionalism makes this program tractable by locating relevant similarity at the algorithmic level. The authors do not establish functionalism; biological-substrate theories and IIT still matter to an overall assessment (Box 2, PDF pp. 4–5).

The four derivation guidelines are substantive: retain theories’ central explanatory commitments; balance unusual possible minds against implausibly simple implementations; include plausible background conditions; and clarify terminology without pretending current theories supply precise engineering specifications (PDF pp. 7–10). Table 1 contains 14 indicators across recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order theories, attention schema, predictive processing, and agency/embodiment. Background indicators have a different rationale from a theory’s allegedly sufficient conjunction.

The inferential rule is conditional: evidence E supports consciousness H under theory T when `P(H|E,T) > P(H|T)` (PDF p. 11). It supplies no numerical likelihoods. Dependencies matter: Table 1 says PP-1 entails RPT-1 and HOT-1, while GWT indicators build on one another. Counting these as independent confirmations would inflate evidence. A positive finding under one theory can also be outweighed by another theory or an omitted necessary condition (PDF pp. 11–12).

## Evidence and limitations

The paper presents conceptual arguments and a synthesis of theories, not a new experiment, systematic benchmark, estimated effect size, or validated classifier. References to AI studies testing AST/GWT illustrate a research program; this article does not supply their comparative results or establish that their systems are conscious (PDF p. 12, “Looking ahead”). The inherited indicator list is revisable, not a validated scale.

Three limitations particularly constrain engineering transfer:

- **Selection can destroy evidential value.** Box 3 explicitly extends the gaming problem from expressive behavior to computational features (PDF p. 6). Installing something called a workspace because it appears on the checklist changes why that feature is present. Architecture labels and first-person reports require causal corroboration; neither is privileged evidence.
- **Minimal implementations remain unresolved.** A shared buffer could satisfy a permissive workspace definition while challenging its intended explanatory significance. “Complex tasks” and “general belief-formation” make the conditions more demanding, but leave discretion over thresholds (PDF p. 9). These are useful research constraints, not settled pass/fail rules.
- **System boundaries change assessments.** A transformer pass and an autoregressive transformer-plus-context loop have different recurrence properties (PDF p. 11). Persistent orchestration likewise requires an explicit boundary and observation interval. Persistence alone does not settle recurrence in perceptual processing or consciousness between model invocations.

There is also a statistical wording issue: the specificity discussion on PDF p. 11 describes how often indicator-positive systems are conscious, approaching positive predictive value, whereas the glossary gives the conventional false-positive-based definition (PDF pp. 4–5). Any quantitative extension should write the conditional probabilities explicitly. Neither sensitivity nor specificity has been empirically calibrated here against AI consciousness ground truth.

My judgment is that the method organizes uncertainty better than behavioral impressionism, but does not establish reproducible, calibrated consciousness probabilities. Causal experiments can establish an indicator’s computational role; they cannot alone validate the indicator-to-phenomenology inference.

## Prioritized recommendations

**P0 — Adopt a theory-indexed evidence ledger before changing production.** Extend review documentation with each indicator’s operational definition, system boundary, relevant time interval, code evidence, causal evidence, dependencies, and alternative interpretations. Separate uncertainty about implementation from uncertainty about theory and background conditions. Reuse I03/Q14 routing evidence rather than treating “workspace present” as four independent GWT successes. Cost is documentation and independent assessment, with no runtime dependency. **Falsifiable check:** give assessors the same architecture with component names anonymized and include a minimal shared-buffer comparison. If attribution changes with naming, or indistinguishable mechanisms receive different classifications without an explicit criterion, revise the rubric. This measures assessment discipline, not consciousness ground truth.

**P1 — Experiment on the strongest near-term claim: workspace-mediated sequential coordination.** Instrument existing capsule provenance at `CapsuleAssembler::persist:677`, queue-time context at [ExecutiveCore.php](../../../src/Core/ExecutiveCore.php), `enqueueWork:6318`, and transitions in [DecisionStateMachine.php](../../../src/Core/DecisionStateMachine.php), `integrate:254`. Declare the cognitive modules in scope. Evaluate held-out tasks where one module’s newly computed result determines which module must be queried next. Compare live workspace with frozen or permuted workspace under matched model-call and context budgets. Audit direct observation/retrieval channels because Q14 identifies duplicated access; preserve deliberate evidence fences. Cost is correlated tracing and an isolated evaluation harness. **Failure criterion:** if intervention does not change the appropriate successor query, or live-workspace task success does not exceed both disrupted controls with a paired 95% confidence interval excluding zero, the proposed causal role remains unsupported on this task set. Performance gains would support coordination, not consciousness.

**P2 — Experiment on reliability-to-belief uptake before claiming higher-order monitoring.** This is an engineering synthesis from HOT-2/3, not an algorithm provided by the paper. Building on Q13’s confirmed forecast-quality consumers, prototype separate sensory-reliability and relevance signals at `SensoryCortex::evaluate`, carrying provenance into explicit belief acceptance or candidate handling at `DecisionStateMachine::integrate`. Existing significance-as-confidence should not silently become truth confidence. Use independently labeled clean, corrupted, and systematically misleading observations; predictable false signals are essential because forecast accuracy can coexist with false belief. Compare active monitoring with shuffled and disabled estimates. Dependencies are ground-truth labels and tracing beliefs to sensory evidence; costs include annotations and decision complexity. **Failure criterion:** require lower Brier score and false-acceptance rate than both controls, with no more than five percentage points lost in clean-task completion. Missing these preregistered targets defeats the proposed engineering benefit. More refusals alone do not count as improved discrimination.

Do not add an attention schema, recurrent network, or embodiment layer solely to accumulate indicators. Their costs need an independently specified functional objective and discriminating evaluation.

## Risks, unresolved facts, and verdict

The main adoption risk is turning an uncertainty-management method into a consciousness score optimized by design. Conversely, demanding biological timing, neural implementation, or a physical robot would exceed this paper’s functionalist working assumptions. Engineered affect and needs should not be equated with valenced experience: the paper explicitly identifies valence as a further research problem (PDF p. 13).

**Evidence limits at completion:** all three bounded implementation questions were answered and incorporated from shared sections Q13/Q13S. No code question remains pending. Backbone representations, live system behavior, and indicator-specific empirical performance remain unverified. The sufficiency of limited social feedback for AE-2 is a theoretical interpretation issue, not a missing source lookup.

**Verdict: adopt the assessment discipline; experiment on causal workspace coordination; defer consciousness attribution.** The consequential next step is a boundary-explicit, provenance-backed GWT-4 evaluation that distinguishes stored context from task-relevant sequential control. This paper supports that research question without predicting its outcome or establishing Navi’s consciousness.
