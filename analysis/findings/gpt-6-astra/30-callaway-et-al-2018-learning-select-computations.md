## Core idea and mechanism

Bayesian metalevel policy search (BMPS) learns which computation to perform next and when to stop, maximizing the quality of the eventual decision minus computational expenditure. Its useful contribution is a deliberately small policy class built from decision-relevant information values. It does not establish that longer deliberation, greater uncertainty reduction, or more elaborate self-reflection is inherently beneficial.

The metalevel MDP has belief states over environmental parameters, computations that update those beliefs, and a termination action that executes the object-level policy with highest expected utility. Computations have no external effects and incur cost; termination yields the expected utility of the chosen policy. The experiments generally assign the same cost to each computation. The optimal value of computation (VOC) includes the consequences and costs of continuing optimally afterward, making it expensive to calculate (§2.1, PDF p. 2).

BMPS substitutes three features: the expected decision improvement from one computation followed by stopping, `VOI₁(c,b)`; the value of perfect information about all parameters, `VPI(b)`; and perfect information about a designer-specified relevant subset, `VPI_sub(c,b)`. Equation 5 is:

`VOC_hat(c,b) = w₁ VOI₁(c,b) + w₂ VPI(b) + w₃ VPI_sub(c,b) − w₄ cost(c)`.

The information weights are nonnegative and sum to one; `w₄ ∈ [1,h]` approximates the cost of a possible continuation. Bayesian optimization searches the resulting three free parameters using simulated episode returns. A computation must beat termination's zero VOC to justify continuing (§3, PDF pp. 3–4). This is direct policy search using a supplied metalevel transition model, not online temporal-difference learning from unrestricted agent experience.

Two distinctions matter. First, the bounds motivate features; they do not certify that every learned mixture accurately estimates optimal VOC. Expected information benefits assume coherent belief updates and a decision maker able to exploit or ignore information. The subset-belief formula also uses a product of parameter marginals; correlated uncertainty needs additional treatment. Second, for fixed weights and equal computation costs, global `VPI(b)` is identical across candidate computations. It helps determine whether to continue but cannot itself decide which computation to select. The action-dependent myopic and subset features supply that discrimination. Replacing these with a single global uncertainty score would discard an essential mechanism.

The subset mapping is substantive domain engineering: a simulated bandit pull concerns one arm; a tree-node inspection concerns that node, its ancestors, and descendants (§§4.2–4.3, PDF pp. 6–7). “Domain-general” describes the framework, not automatically available features or a learned universal representation.

## Evidence and limitations

The paper provides simulation evidence across three controlled problems and a hypothetical application:

| Evaluation | Reported evidence | Scope of inference |
|---|---|---|
| Binary stopping, horizon 30 | BMPS was at most 5.19% below optimal performance and averaged 20.3% above meta-greedy; trained with 500 optimization iterations × 2,500 episodes and evaluated on 3,000 independent episodes (§4.1, PDF pp. 4–5, Fig. 2). | Known Beta–Bernoulli updates and one computation type; useful evidence for learned stopping, not general reasoning allocation. |
| Choosing among 2–5 Bernoulli options, horizon 25 | Mean return 0.6535 versus optimal 0.6596, blinkered 0.6559, meta-greedy 0.60, and DQN 0.58. Training used 10 × 1,000 episodes, followed by 25,000 additional candidate-selection episodes; DQN used five million steps (§4.2, PDF pp. 5–7, Fig. 3). | Strong sample-efficiency evidence for this structured policy class. The small 0.37% difference from blinkered performance limits any claim of uniform superiority over that baseline. |
| Planning in binary trees, heights 2–6 | At heights 2–3, BMPS achieved 98.4% of optimal return, 0.367 versus 0.373. Across heights/costs, normalized returns were 0.392 for BMPS, 0.368 recursively blinkered, and 0.307 meta-greedy (§4.3, PDF pp. 6–8, Fig. 4). | Exact optima were computed only for heights 2–3; larger instances establish baseline advantages, not measured near-optimality. Transitions are known and each inspection perfectly reveals a binary reward. |
| Tornado evacuation | A policy trained for 20 cities/50 simulations was reused for 10 or 30 cities, with 5,000 rollouts per setting. Reported selection time was approximately 1–3 ms; accounting conservatively for 0.001 hours per selection still favored BMPS over uniform allocation (§5, PDF pp. 8–9). | This is synthetic allocation with assumed losses and simulation durations, not a deployed weather simulator or validated emergency-management outcome. |

The tornado analysis explicitly budgets selection overhead: `n_sim = floor(T / (t_MR + t_sim))`. Training cost is excluded on the stated assumption that repeated use amortizes it. Supplementary experiments show metareasoning can lose when simulations become sufficiently cheap (PDF p. 11, Figs. 6–7). This makes the paper's practical claim conditional on operation cost, reuse, and task structure. Its favorable comparison with DQN does not isolate representation from optimizer: BMPS receives engineered information-value features and a tiny search space. The local supplement does not provide enough DQN configuration or independent-training-run analysis to establish broad optimizer superiority.

There are source inconsistencies worth preserving. On PDF p. 7, §4.3.4 reports `t(159998) = −2.87, p < 10⁻¹⁵`; those quantities are incompatible for an ordinary t-test. Figure 4(b)'s caption says costs were marginalized over `10⁻⁴–10⁻¹`, whereas §4.3's methods specify `2⁻⁷–2⁰`. I visually verified both printed passages; neither is an extraction artifact. Reported means remain useful, but that p-value and the exact pooling range should not be treated as settled. The referenced “Supplementary Figure 1” regression visualization and “Supplementary Figure 2” learning curve are not present in this local 12-page file.

The authors themselves identify exponentially expensive exact VPI integration and the assumption of an exact model of the agent's computational architecture as limitations (§6, PDF p. 9). Monte Carlo approximation does not supply a general cheap, accurate solution for arbitrary language-model beliefs. The work offers neither human-subject validation of BMPS nor evidence about consciousness.

Also, `machine.look` is an observation action with external execution costs, and consolidation changes durable knowledge; neither should silently inherit the paper's assumption that computation has no external effects.

## Prioritized recommendations

These are speculative engineering adaptations for Navi, not interventions evaluated in this review.

**P0 — Establish a measurable computation decision before selecting an algorithm.** For one narrow task family with independently adjudicable answers, propose collecting computation identity, pre/post evidence state, candidate decision, final correctness, input/output usage, and enqueue/claim/invocation/integration times at the existing decision and worker boundaries above. Distinguish quality improvement from model confidence, successful execution, or stored “uncertainty reduction”; the latter is partly updated by proposal-confidence formulas ([I10](IMPLEMENTATION.md#i10--workers-persistence-over-time-and-evaluation-limits), [Q13](IMPLEMENTATION.md#q13--attention-schema-and-sensory-monitor-uptake)).

Use isolated copies and paired branches to compare stopping, one additional retrieval or critique, and short sequences that could have complementary effects. Dependencies are an adjudicated task set, state-preserving replay, and added timing correlation; observational traces alone cannot identify unchosen-computation value. Evaluate held-out predicted versus realized utility gains and total cost. Do not progress to policy learning if no allowed operation or short sequence yields detectable net benefit, or held-out benefit estimates fail to improve over a constant predictor. This prevents optimizing a proxy for activity.

**P1 — Experiment with a small offline policy, including strong cheap baselines.** Using P0's task model, define a maximum of two extra operations and an explicit stop action. Estimate one-step decision improvement and a relevant-subproblem improvement ceiling; use exact VPI only where the state representation warrants it. Any learned “oracle improvement” surrogate must be labeled a proxy without the paper's bounds. Fit a small BMPS-style mixture by offline policy search, comparing it with immediate stop, fixed allocation, and a calibrated myopic policy. Include the cost of estimating features and amortized fitting cost over a declared reuse count.

The future integration location is an explicit additional transition around `DecisionStateMachine::start/integrate`, with existing work leasing and proposal validation retained. This is a proposed extension, not an available loop. Require a positive lower 95% confidence bound for net utility improvement over the best cheap baseline on held-out tasks, plus a predeclared correctness floor. Fail adoption if gains vanish after overhead or a global-uncertainty-only baseline matches it. Ablating `VPI_sub` directly checks whether computation-specific structure earns its complexity.

**P2 — Challenge the model on complementary computations and stale state.** Include tasks where retrieval alone or critique alone rarely changes the decision, but retrieval followed by critique does. The paper's objection to myopia predicts improvement specifically on this subset. Compare learned selection with myopic selection under the same hard budget, then separately vary queue delay, task distribution, and correlated evidence. Dependency: P1 plus explicit remaining-budget and evidence-version state. `DecisionStateMachine::integrate:254` currently checks proposal identity without comparing the saved workspace checksum to current state ([Q02](IMPLEMENTATION.md#q02--timing-freshness-and-serialization)); freshness handling would be additional machinery. Reject progression if the controller cannot exploit complementary operations, repeatedly chooses exhausted evidence, or exceeds its quality/deadline thresholds under these perturbations.

## Verdict and coverage

**Experiment; defer production adoption.** BMPS offers a credible low-dimensional approach to learning the marginal value of another computation. Navi already has useful execution boundaries, but the verified reference does not establish the required calibrated task utilities, computational transition model, or adaptive stop/continue loop. The most consequential next step is P0's isolated measurement of whether an extra operation actually improves a decision enough to pay for itself.
