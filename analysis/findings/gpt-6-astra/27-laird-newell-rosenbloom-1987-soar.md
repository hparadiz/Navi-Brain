## Core contribution

Soar's strongest contribution is the integration of **insufficient knowledge → impasse → problem solving in a subgoal → compiled control knowledge**. Learning can subsequently prevent the original impasse. This is considerably more specific than retaining successful action sequences or recording reflections about failure.

Every task is represented as search in a problem space. A context contains goal, problem space, state, and operator slots; contexts form a stack. Productions supply both task implementations and search-control knowledge. Their matched instantiations fire in simulated parallel, adding working-memory structures until quiescence, followed by a fixed decision procedure. Productions do not directly delete structures or manipulate the context stack; architectural mechanisms own those operations (§§2.1–2.3, pp. 10–18). Quiescence is operationally bounded: a typical limit of 100 elaboration iterations forces a decision if necessary (p. 17, footnote 4).

Control knowledge takes the form of explicit acceptability, rejection, ordering, and indifference preferences. Rejection overrides acceptability. Conflicting orderings remain representable; absence of a known preference does not establish indifference. The decision procedure can therefore expose **tie, conflict, no-change, and rejection** impasses instead of always forcing an arbitrary winner (§2.3.2, pp. 18–23, Fig. 2-8). A subgoal makes the deficient decision itself an object of further problem solving. Default knowledge supplies selection, lookahead, and backtracking behavior; without it or adequate task knowledge, automatic subgoaling can regress indefinitely (§§2.4–2.5, pp. 27–33).

Chunking turns a subgoal's results accessible to higher contexts into production actions. It traces backward through the productions that generated those results, retaining dependencies on working-memory elements predating the subgoal. Intermediate derivations disappear; identifiers become variables, while constants and relational constraints remain. Independent result groups can produce separate rules (§2.6.1, pp. 34–37). This is **derivational dependency analysis under the supplied rules**, not discovery of causal laws from observations. It is also not merely concatenation of actions: a chunk can cache an evaluation or emit a preference that avoids future deliberation.

The control and learning mechanisms depend on representation. Relationally factored tile/cell/binding structures let chunks ignore irrelevant identities; inspecting irrelevant constants makes chunks unnecessarily specific (§§2.2, 3.3.3, pp. 14–15, 53–55). This is a joint requirement on the data representation and the computations reading it.

## Evidence and limits

This is an implemented architectural proposal with demonstrations and selected comparative runs, not a controlled demonstration of general intelligence. Its broad task inventory is explicitly an unsystematic sample; several systems are small or incomplete demonstrations (pp. 1–3).

The eight-puzzle example examines 20 states without learning, 15 during the first learning run, and 10 on a subsequent run. A separate transfer example learns on a different initial/goal configuration, then evaluates the original task with learning disabled: 16 states instead of 20 (§3.3.1, pp. 46–51, Figs. 3-6–3-7). These distinguish within-run transfer, repeated-task caching, and transfer between related problems. They do not show generalization between unrelated task domains. Crucially, chunking caches the evaluation function's imperfections; this example does not improve that function or learn new primitive puzzle operators (p. 51).

R1-Soar implements approximately 25% of R1's functionality, including demanding module-to-backplane configuration. For one small configuration task, the base system takes 1,731 decisions without learning, 485 while learning, and seven after learning. Versions supplied additional search-control rules also reach seven on rerun (§§3.1, 3.3.2, pp. 40–43, 51; Fig. 3-8). This is strong evidence that compilation can eliminate substantial repeated computation. Seven decisions on an already encountered problem is not evidence of seven-decision competence on arbitrary new configurations, nor a corresponding wall-clock speedup after accounting for matching and compilation costs.

The more informative extended study uses 15 orders with accumulating productions across two learning passes and three assessment passes. The authors report average decision reductions of approximately 14% during the first learning pass, 49% at the next assessment, and 84% at the final assessment; productions grow from 314 to 444 (§3.3.2, pp. 52–53, Fig. 3-9). These are **not 15 independent learning experiments**. The second learning pass improves neither within-task nor across-task performance during that pass: its benefit appears in subsequent cached execution. Small positive and negative transfer occurs, with overly general chunks causing misguided search and recovery costs. No uncertainty intervals or broad held-out distribution establish the stability of these gains.

Representation experiments reinforce the mechanism: encoding 35 macro-operators takes 112 productions rather than 170 without sharing; a revised representation **plus an incremental goal test** reduces this to 61 (§3.3.3, pp. 54–56, Fig. 3-10). Attribution solely to representation would confound those two changes.

The limitations are explicit: automatic task acquisition, creation of representations, recovery from overgeneralized learning, and external-environment interaction remain missing or incomplete (pp. 2–3, 58). Memory-resource limits are largely assumed away (p. 14, footnote 2), and conflict-subgoal experience is too limited for confidence in its treatment (p. 30, footnote 10). The eleven concluding hypotheses remain architectural hypotheses, including the universal claims about productions and chunking; the demonstrations do not establish their necessity (§4, pp. 57–58).

## Prioritized recommendations

**1. P1 — Experiment with one bounded impasse-resolution continuation.** Extend the decision state machine's `start/integrate/impasse` flow with a diagnostic record containing the blocked decision, deficiency type, candidate identities, relevant evidence, and required resolving result. Begin with a missing-evidence case that can use the existing `memory.search` planning adapter, allowing one additional query and one revised proposal. Invalid authority or an unavailable adapter should remain a refusal, rather than trigger attempts to expand the action set. Preserve the distinction between insufficient evidence to compare candidates and positive evidence that either candidate is equally suitable (§§2.3.2–2.5).

This needs durable continuation identity, an explicit cumulative token/time budget, and the validity boundary in recommendation 2. Compare focused diagnostic retrieval against the current cycle-closure behavior, explicitly scheduled later cycles, and an equal-budget generic retry, using independently scored cases with known missing facts. Measure correct resolutions per model token, repeated identical impasses, total latency, and unnecessary retrieval on already decidable cases. Reject the extension if gains vanish against the equal-budget retry, task correctness declines, or any case exceeds its continuation bound. Soar's unbounded universality is not required for this experiment.

**2. P1 prerequisite — Give diagnostic descendants explicit validity and termination semantics.** In the proposed continuation lifecycle, distinguish resolved, failed, superseded, and budget-exhausted outcomes. Bind each result to the parent decision and the versions of the specific state/evidence on which it depends. Recheck these at result integration and immediately before dispatch, using the existing worker fencing and [ExecutiveCore.php](../../../src/Core/ExecutiveCore.php) `claimActionDispatch:797` boundary as integration points, not assuming they already enforce this dependency relation (I10, Q05, Q22–Q23).

This follows directly from Soar's top-down context reconsideration: changing a supporting higher context terminates subordinate work, while **termination itself is not success** (§2.4, pp. 28–29). The implementation cost is atomic validity bookkeeping across asynchronous results. In an isolated evaluation, vary whether parent resolution, withdrawal, or relevant evidence replacement occurs before, during, or after diagnostic work. Require zero stale dispatches and exactly one terminal continuation outcome; irrelevant workspace updates should not cancel valid work. Any stale dispatch fails adoption. Avoid interpreting a generic `released` status as proof that the diagnostic objective was achieved.

**3. P2 — Compile small verified control results after the continuation experiment succeeds.** This transfer design is speculative engineering synthesis. Add a separate conditional-control artifact alongside `ProceduralMemory::observe`, initially caching a machine-checkable candidate evaluation or preference. Record the typed premises, dependency derivation, installed-adapter generation, and conclusion. Retain conservative literal conditions first; generalize identifiers only where declared relational semantics justify it. An LLM rationale or an action's successful exit status cannot substitute for the derivation. Preserve runtime adapter checks and use existing generation invalidation to withdraw stale artifacts (I05, I08, Q06, Q27).

The paper motivates starting with small results near the bottom of the subgoal hierarchy, where reuse is broader and derivations shorter (§§2.6.1, 3.3.2). Instrumentation and a bounded predicate vocabulary are substantial dependencies; Navi's current heterogeneous context cannot simply become a Soar proof trace. Evaluate exact reruns separately from renamed entities, irrelevant-context perturbations, and cases changing one necessary premise. Ablate literal versus relational conditions and compilation versus deliberation. Require fewer model tokens and lower total latency at preserved independently judged correctness, with zero artifact firings after a declared prerequisite fails. Reject rules that save deliberation but increase total lookup cost or reproduce incorrect choices. These are proposed adoption criteria, not reported experimental results.

## Risks and verdict

**Experiment with the impasse/compilation mechanism; reject wholesale architectural replacement.** Soar offers a compelling explanation of how deliberation becomes routine execution under explicit symbolic assumptions. Its results do not justify treating every Navi intention as an impasse, converting all memory to productions, or equating architectural self-reference with consciousness.

The central transfer risk is compiling context-dependent model judgments as if they were valid derivations. In an asynchronous, changing environment, “the same action shape succeeded” is insufficient evidence that the same decision should recur. The most consequential next step is a bounded, validity-checked missing-evidence continuation whose result demonstrably resolves its originating decision. Only then is compilation of that resolution a concrete engineering target.
