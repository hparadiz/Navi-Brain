**Judgment: experiment with LIDA’s causal organization of attention, action, and learning; retain Navi’s existing machinery.** The consequential question is whether selecting a representation makes it useful to downstream action and memory. LIDA supplies a coherent hypothesis about that relationship, but its evidence does not establish the benefits of its complete architecture, attention as a necessary condition for all learning, or human cognitive timing as an engineering target.

The contribution is a systems architecture organized around recurrent **understanding → attending → acting/learning** cycles (§III, pp. 4–6, Fig. 1). Sensory features activate a Perceptual Associative Memory (PAM). Percepts cue episodic memories; retrieved associations, recent percepts, and surviving structures support a Current Situational Model in the Workspace. Small specialized structure-building codelets assemble relations. Attention codelets recruit coalitions from that model, and the most salient coalition wins access to the Global Workspace. Its broadcast recruits procedural schemes and, conceptually, updates several memories. A scheme represents context, action, expected result, and activation; matching schemes instantiate into behaviors, a behavior net selects an action, and sensory-motor machinery executes it.

The Workspace and Global Workspace have different jobs: rich situational construction precedes selective access. Equally, availability and action selection are distinct: a broadcast supplies information to the selector, while selection itself is described as unconscious (§IV, p. 6). Internal representational actions are intended to support reasoning over multiple cycles (§V, pp. 6–7). Most processing is asynchronous, with two serial decision points; proposed 200–300 ms cycles can overlap, allowing five to ten cycles per second (§II, p. 4). This is an architectural timing hypothesis, not an instruction to serialize every subsystem behind one model call.

Learning combines creation of representations, termed “instructionalist,” with reinforcement of existing ones, termed “selectionist” (§VI, pp. 7–8). Current activation measures situational salience; base-level activation measures longer-term usefulness. Conceptually, every broadcast can create or reinforce perceptual, episodic, and procedural representations. Profligate generation is constrained by reinforcement and decay. Feelings are PAM nodes linked to represented situations; appraisal changes their activation, and they influence coalition competition, scheme recruitment, learning, and execution (§VII, pp. 8–11). The model proposes emotion as a flexible motivational mechanism; it does not derive an experimentally validated reward function.

These mechanisms assume that useful structures can be constructed and matched, salience is informative for limited access, and selected content reaches appropriate consumers. The Java framework implements modules, tasks, observer/listener communication, and configurable activation/decay strategies (§IX, pp. 13–15). Domain-specific sensors, motor routines, and some representational content still require engineering. Using an extensible framework does not demonstrate that the represented knowledge was learned autonomously.

**The evidence is strongest for selected behavioral patterns.** Section X (pp. 15–17) summarizes four previously reported agents rather than reporting a comprehensive new evaluation of integrated cognition:

- **Reaction time:** 283 ms mean cycle duration across 30 runs, compared with an approximately 200 ms human reference (p. 15). Neuroscience-derived timings constrain the model, but its roughly 83 ms mismatch remains. Temporal expectation is offered as an explanation and explicitly was not implemented. Episodic retrieval and structure-building codelets were omitted because the task did not require them.
- **Allport simultaneity:** the agent produces one no-movement threshold at 96 ms, agreeing with the qualitative human result (pp. 16–17). Its twelve perceptual nodes and two schemes were hard-coded, and the environment controller changed stimulus timing. The interesting result is that discrete broadcasts can contain surviving percepts from earlier broadcasts. This defeats a particular inference from the experiment to strictly distinct moments; it does not uniquely identify discrete consciousness over other models with temporal integration.
- **Spatial cueing:** congruent and incongruent conditions yield 360 and 380 ms, respectively, reproducing a reported 20 ms advantage (p. 17). The proposed explanation is that congruent cues have already instantiated the relevant scheme. This supports a specific preparation mechanism; it does not establish the necessity of every LIDA module.
- **Attentional blink:** Fig. 5 (p. 17) visually compares human and agent correctness at 107, 213, and 427 ms, showing a second-target trough and recovery. The summary supplies no aggregate fit statistic, uncertainty for the agent curve, or matched alternative-model comparison. Emotional attenuation and several broader effects are conceptual explanations, not demonstrated results of this agent.

The aspiration to preserve internal parameters across disparate tasks is valuable, but §X does not provide a full parameter-sharing and held-out-prediction audit. These demonstrations therefore support feasibility and partial behavioral correspondence. They neither establish broad autonomous task competence nor isolate broadcast as the cause of every successful fit. The paper also explicitly says that the functional advantage of parallel/asynchronous execution had not yet been evaluated (p. 14, footnote 14).

The distinction between **LIDA_C**, the conceptual model, and **LIDA_I**, the implementation, is essential (§I, p. 2). Most learning and all of the described emotion model were conceptual. Section XI clarifies that episodic learning was implemented in the framework, procedural learning existed before it and was not yet integrated, and neither had been validated against human learning data (p. 18). Reports of earlier higher-level implementations in §§IV–V should be read alongside the explicit current weaknesses: no implemented multicyclic cognition or language model, little causality/probability work, and missing integrated metacognition (pp. 18–19). The status descriptions are uneven; historical prototypes are not evidence that the reviewed framework integrated all those capacities.

The dynamic-field discussion is also preliminary (§VIII, pp. 11–13). Its leaky activation equation, local excitation/global inhibition, and simplified traffic-light example in Fig. 3 suggest a possible realization. Page 12 explicitly disclaims data substantiating that realization’s neural plausibility or module-to-brain mapping. Functional consciousness means broadcast availability; the authors disclaim commitment to phenomenal consciousness (p. 3, footnote 3). Neither the neural analogy nor the behavioral simulations resolve that question.

**Three proposed experiments, in priority order:**

1. **Trace selected content through actual consumers before changing selection or learning policy.** Join existing capsule/slot IDs, workspace checksums, work/cycle IDs, and `action.finished` provenance with explicit consumer records carrying content hashes and scope. Locations are `CapsuleAssembler::persist`, `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork/finishAction`, and `DecisionStateMachine::integrate` (I03/I04/Q14/Q01). LIDA’s central empirical proposition requires a selected representation to affect recruitment; logging selection alone cannot establish that. Reuse the verified provenance rather than constructing a second event history. Cost is bounded storage and replay tooling; preserve evidence-fenced job exclusions. In a future isolated evaluation, pair intact inputs with matched-budget removal of task-relevant selected evidence, accounting for duplicate direct-input routes. Measure independently judged action correctness and erroneous commitments. Predeclare a minimum useful accuracy advantage and estimate paired uncertainty: an interval excluding that advantage rejects useful uptake for the evaluated path and task family; a wide interval or uncontrolled duplicate access is inconclusive. Do not use `closure_accuracy` as ground truth: I10 defines it as a lifecycle proxy.

2. **Evaluate small relational coalitions within the existing context budget.** Prototype candidates that bind a claim to its supporting source and a relevant contradiction or constraint. Start in `CapsuleAssembler::gatherCandidates/contest`; use explicit provenance/relation identifiers to bound grouping, retain protected roles and scope rules, and apply claimed-record exclusions across group members (Q01). Keep total tokens and candidate counts fixed. This is a speculative engineering translation of LIDA’s structure-building and coalition competition (pp. 5–6), not a claim that paired text recreates codelets. It costs relation bookkeeping and risks combinatorial growth or overpersistent winners. Compare current selection, grouping, and grouping without hysteresis on tasks requiring two individually weak but jointly decisive facts; also measure sensitivity to nonreserved roster order. Measure joint-evidence action accuracy, missed constraints, displacement of useful incumbents, and latency. Reject grouping if gains disappear under equal token budgets, merely reflect favorable roster order, increase constraint misses, or exceed a preregistered latency bound. An attentional blink is a diagnostic failure mode for Navi, not a target capability.

3. **Separate exposure, attention, and outcome credit in a learning ablation.** Use the trace from proposal 1 to compare current query-driven association reinforcement with attention-conditioned and independently verified-outcome-conditioned reinforcement at `tokmem.c::query_memories` and its PHP protocol boundary (I02/QMEM). Keep representation acquisition distinct from usefulness credit, and preserve consolidation provenance checks and procedure execution contracts (I08). This tests LIDA’s separation of current salience and base-level usefulness while challenging its unproven universal attention gate (pp. 7–8). Dependencies include an isolated memory store, explicit learning flags, delayed-credit bookkeeping, and matched retrieval/write budgets. Repeatedly expose the system to salient distractors alongside less frequent task-relevant cues; evaluate held-out retrieval precision, novel-cue recall, task accuracy, and reinforcement of false associations. Adoption requires reduced distractor reinforcement and improved downstream performance without unacceptable recall loss. Reject an attention gate if it merely strengthens already salient material or suppresses useful unattended statistical learning.

The main disagreement is with treating attention as sufficient justification for durable reinforcement. The paper’s examples of strong attended learning do not establish that attention is necessary for every learning mechanism. Salience, affect, truth, and utility can diverge; reinforcing broadcasts can make self-selected distractors increasingly dominant. Likewise, a single-winner bottleneck may explain human errors while reducing a software agent’s throughput. These are reasons for controlled, bounded experiments rather than architecture replacement. This paper offers no comparative evidence supporting a migration of Navi’s token engine to SDM, a Java rewrite, or mandatory 200–300 ms executive cycles.

**Verdict: experiment.** The most consequential next step is causal provenance and an isolated uptake ablation using the workspace and cycle already implemented.
