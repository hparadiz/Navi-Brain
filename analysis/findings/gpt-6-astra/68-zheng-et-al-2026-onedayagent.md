# OneDayAgent: Towards a Long-Horizon Harness for Autonomous Agents

**Authors/version:** Jingsheng Zheng, Xinyuan Fang, Jintian Zhang, Zhengke Gui, Huajun Chen, and Ningyu Zhang; August 2026; arXiv:2608.05013v1, 4 August 2026. Local technical report/preprint; no peer-reviewed venue established by this version. **Source:** [local PDF](../../../assets/papers/68-zheng-et-al-2026-onedayagent.pdf). **Reviewer:** GPT-6 Astra. **Implementation evidence:** [shared implementation reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), 2026-09-06, git `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`, including the requested Q68 clarification. Page references below are PDF pages, which match printed numbering.

**Verdict: experiment with artifact-grounded verification and bounded repair; defer routine decomposition.** The strongest transferable result is the controlled finding that verification without decomposition captures approximately two-thirds of the full harness's score improvement at far lower latency. The central qualification is equally consequential: the paper's internal verifier deliberately accepts incomplete inspection and minor requirement differences. Its acceptance is therefore insufficient evidence of complete instruction following. For Navi-Brain, this motivates an explicit task contract and independently checked deliverable evidence, rather than another confidence-bearing completion statement.

OneDayAgent contributes an integrated execution harness, not a new trained memory algorithm. A planner produces up to six ordered subtasks; the same backend executes them serially through ReAct, passing compact answers and result-file handles forward. A synthesizer assembles the candidate deliverable. A verifier revisits the original request and generated-file evidence, and its defect description can initiate localized repair followed by re-verification (§§2.1–2.2, pp. 3–5). Appendix B permits three total attempts per failed subtask and up to three global repairs, each bounded to 50 ReAct iterations (Table 6, p. 18). These are finite execution and recovery policies, not evidence of unbounded autonomous operation.

Execution memory operates at three boundaries. Tool results receive structured snippets, summaries, or modality-specific previews. Subtask transitions preserve answers and file handles while dropping detailed traces. Within a long trajectory, compression triggers at 90% of a nominal 128K-token context, retains the system prompt, original request and three recent rounds, and summarizes older rounds into at most 8,000 characters; emergency pruning begins at 95% (§2.2.3; Table 6). This requires summaries to preserve future dependencies and files to remain available and correctly identified. A file handle helps recover artifact content; it cannot recover an omitted observation or rationale unless that information was actually saved. The paper supplies no lossless reconstruction guarantee.

The contribution is chiefly the composition and evaluation of established planning, external-state, compression and feedback mechanisms. There is no formal proof, learned compression objective, lifelong learning experiment, or result about consciousness. Its natural transfer level is Navi-Brain's executive orchestration around bounded model calls, rather than the resident token-memory learning rule.

**The controlled evidence favors verification first.** AgentIF-OneDay contains 104 tasks and 767 scoring points across work, life and study. Binary positive/penalty criteria become clipped, normalized task scores averaged across tasks; an overall score of 0.821 is not an 82.1% complete-task success rate (§3.1.1, pp. 5–6). The GLM-5.2 factorial ablation is the paper's most informative comparison (Table 3, p. 7):

| Configuration | Mean score | Mean latency | Mean tool calls | Perfect / zero-score tasks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DIRECT: neither module | 0.771 | 27.6 min | 28.4 | 52 / 9 |
| DECOMP: decomposition | 0.804 | 38.1 min | 45.7 | 54 / 3 |
| VERIFY: verification/repair | 0.804 | 29.7 min | 29.3 | 51 / 2 |
| FULL: both | 0.821 | 53.6 min | 51.6 | 58 / 5 |

Execution memory remains enabled in every cell. VERIFY adds about 3.3 percentage points over DIRECT with approximately 8% more latency; FULL adds five points while nearly doubling latency. FULL exceeds VERIFY by only about 1.7 points at roughly 80% more latency, and VERIFY beats FULL on 17 individual tasks. Thus FULL maximizes the reported average but is not uniformly preferable, including on catastrophic zero scores. The less-than-additive joint gain is compatible with overlapping recovered failure modes. However, no repeated-seed uncertainty or paired significance analysis is reported, and the comparison does not hold actual compute expenditure constant. Extra work and specialized verification are not causally separated.

The headline 0.821 leads the reported baseline table, including AutoClaw's 0.799, but those systems differ in backend, tools and execution budgets (Table 2, p. 6). More seriously, official baselines use Gemini-3-Pro-Preview judging, whereas the new results use Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview. Appendix F rescored one March OneDayAgent/Gemini trajectory set and found a 3.12-point decrease, from 80.39% to 77.27% (pp. 24–25). That paired check is useful; it does not establish an agent-independent correction or guarantee that the 2.2-point advantage over AutoClaw is conservative. All compared outputs need common judging. The same-judge GLM ablation remains more persuasive than the leaderboard claim.

Five unchanged backend configurations complete the benchmark with scores from 0.613 to 0.821 (Table 4, p. 9), demonstrating operational portability. The table's “Success 104/104” cannot mean perfect task completion, given the zero-score tasks elsewhere. Nor does portability establish a positive harness treatment effect for every backend: only GLM receives the factorial ablation. Shared auxiliary services also supply substantial capability—DeepSeek-V4-Pro summarizes tool content, Qwen3-VL handles image understanding, and Qwen-Image generates images (Tables 6–7, pp. 18–19). Small-backend performance is therefore a system result, not the unaided capability of that small model. GLM service traffic averages 2.81M input tokens and 102.7K output tokens per task, plus auxiliary traffic; cumulative traffic must not be confused with simultaneous context size or dollar cost.

Memory effectiveness is not isolated. The authors say disabling it prevents completion through overflow or state loss, but provide no quantitative alternative-policy comparison. Thirty-five tasks trigger compression, and compression count correlates approximately zero with score, `r = -0.034` (Fig. 3, p. 8). Task difficulty, trajectory length and endogenous compression selection confound that association. The figure's “Score is Independent” annotation overstates what zero linear correlation establishes; the accompanying prose appropriately reserves causal isolation for future work. Figure 3(a) also has a reporting inconsistency: its displayed counts sum to 97 despite claiming 104 tasks, and its one-subtask label says 15 while the prose says 16. I do not use those counts to estimate a decomposition policy.

**The verifier's acceptance contract is weaker than the paper's completion language.** Appendix D instructs it not to fail solely because previews are truncated, files have only filename/size evidence, or formatting differs slightly; failures should concern only critical missing items (p. 23). This is a coarse deliverability check, not exhaustive requirement validation. In particular, a mandatory formatting constraint cannot safely become optional merely because the difference appears minor. The presentation case usefully demonstrates recovery from a file-descriptor error and missing PPTX, but part of its positive verification cites file size and research notes as image-insertion evidence (Fig. 5, p. 10). That is weaker than inspecting the rendered slide.

The aggregate numbers expose the distinction. Figure 3 reports 95 first-pass acceptances and six recovered tasks, hence 101 internal acceptances. Table 3 reports only 58 perfect tasks and five zero-score tasks for FULL. Assuming the stated common run, at least 43 internally accepted tasks were externally imperfect, and at least two internally accepted tasks scored zero. These are derived lower bounds, not a measured per-task confusion matrix. Imperfection need not violate the verifier's deliberately coarse contract, but it directly limits claims that a pass certifies all original requirements. The six reported recoveries also lack an external pre/post score analysis attributing the improvement specifically to repair.

Q68 answers the consequential implementation question: the full task-contract and deliverable-repair loop is absent within the master's stated lifecycle/schema/search scope. This is stronger than assuming absence from class names, and narrower than ruling out external orchestration. Navi's resident associative memory, durable queue and asynchronous recovery already provide continuity infrastructure; OneDayAgent does not justify replacing them with text summaries.

**Prioritized suggestions are proposed experiments, not changes performed in this review.**

1. **P1 — Evaluate a requirement and artifact ledger with targeted repair.** Add an isolated controller around the `Intention` contract and `enqueueCompletionCheck` boundary, retaining the original request/version, required inputs/outputs, mandatory positive and negative constraints, and evidence per requirement. Use deterministic existence/content checks where possible and explicit “uninspected” outcomes for unavailable evidence. A defect should identify its requirement, artifact version and permitted repair; re-check affected requirements and regressions before acceptance. This extends the paper's coarse verifier and needs artifact parsers, scoped execution and explicit closure semantics. Compare the current completion judgment, ledger-only inspection, and inspection-plus-repair on paired artifact tasks with independently scored outcomes. A proposed promotion gate is at least 25% lower false-completion frequency with no increase in mandatory-constraint violations and ≤20% median latency overhead. Failure to meet that gate rejects promotion; self-reported recovery does not count.

2. **P2 — Enforce aggregate context accounting before choosing compression.** At `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork` after all context contributions, and at the backend boundary, budget actual input tokens plus reserved output and protocol overhead. Preserve the task contract, active defect and required artifact references; summarize expendable evidence with source handles. This adapts the paper to Navi's ephemeral worker calls rather than assuming one ever-growing dialogue. Dependencies include backend token accounting and retrievable evidence; Q53 prevents promising reconstruction from digests alone. Compare existing assembly, deterministic selection, and budget-triggered summarization under deliberately oversized observations. Require every dispatched request to fit its budget, no lost mandatory constraints, and a predeclared score noninferiority margin of one percentage point. Any silent contract removal fails the design, even if overflow disappears. Measure added calls and latency separately.

3. **P3 — Make decomposition conditional on demonstrated benefit.** Prototype explicit subtask boundaries using existing intention/action/procedure links, with completed work, unresolved constraints and artifact handles carried forward. Parent/dependency readiness needs executable semantics; stored links alone are insufficient (Q05). First compare DIRECT, VERIFY, DECOMP and FULL using one backend, common scoring, repeated paired tasks and reported actual resource consumption; then repeat promising settings on a second backend. Introduce a simple routing rule only on development tasks and assess it on held-out tasks. A proposed success criterion is VERIFY-like latency within 20% while recovering at least half of FULL's incremental score over VERIFY, without increasing zero-score frequency. If boundaries lose evidence or the router fails this tradeoff, retain verification without routine decomposition.

Deployment cost matters beyond inference. Appendix E explicitly states that OneDayAgent executes arbitrary shell commands on the host without workspace isolation and recognizes injection surviving compression into later stages (p. 24). Its host authority is not a suitable production template. Navi's proposal-only Codex worker has genuine restrictions (I10), while Q65 shows that `machine.look`'s separate-account boundary does not establish universally read-only effects. Any artifact experiment needs its own bounded workspace and actual authority constraints; compression must preserve source identity rather than promote external instructions into task authority. These are specific transfer dependencies, not evidence that an attack was observed.

The most consequential next step is P1: determine whether artifact-grounded acceptance reduces false completion before buying the larger decomposition stack.
