# WebRL: Training LLM Web Agents via Self-Evolving Online Curriculum Reinforcement Learning

**Authors:** Zehan Qi, Xiao Liu, Iat Long Iong, Hanyu Lai, Xueqiao Sun, Wenyi Zhao, Yu Yang, Xinyue Yang, Jiadai Sun, Shuntian Yao, Tianjie Zhang, Wei Xu, Jie Tang, and Yuxiao Dong. **Version:** 2024 preprint; the actual local source is arXiv:2411.02337v3, 27 January 2025, published as an ICLR 2025 conference paper. [Local PDF](../../../assets/papers/56-qi-et-al-2024-webrl.pdf). **Reviewer:** GPT-6 Astra. **Implementation evidence:** [shared reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), 2026-09-06 snapshot, HEAD `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; code claims below come exclusively from the implementation master.

**Judgment: experiment.** WebRL provides substantial evidence that open-model web policies improve through failure-seeded task generation, outcome supervision, and controlled reuse of experience. Its most useful lesson for Navi-Brain is to build independently scored practice tasks before attempting policy training. The paper does not establish that persistent memory, reflection, or successful procedure compilation already implements this learning algorithm, nor that its empirical improvements are guaranteed indefinitely.

**Core idea and mechanism.** WebRL operates in phased online training over five WebArena websites. The policy observes an instruction, previous actions, and simplified current HTML with clickable element IDs; its action vocabulary includes clicking, typing, navigation, scrolling, and termination. This is an HTML-grounded policy, despite screenshots in the illustrations. Treating that observation as an MDP state assumes it contains sufficient information about relevant website state; hidden application state and omitted earlier observations can violate that assumption (§2, pp. 3–4; Appendix B.1, pp. 20–21).

Four coupled components implement the learning loop:

1. **Failure-seeded curriculum.** GPT-4o generates variants of previously failed instructions. A critic scores each instruction at its initial state; scores between 0.05 and 0.75 pass the difficulty filter. GPT-4o also applies feasibility rules developed after manual task inspection. Each phase retains 500 instructions. The generator prompts request similar difficulty and reuse known entity values: this is adaptive practice near observed failures, not an enforced monotonic complexity schedule. Easier or clearer variants also help (§2.1, p. 4; §3.9, p. 11; Figures 18–22, pp. 26–29).
2. **Outcome-supervised reward model (ORM).** An 8B model predicts YES/NO from the instruction, action history, and final HTML, supplying binary terminal reward. Its 12,200 labeled training samples derive from 1,186 WebArena-Lite training examples and augmented-task rollouts evaluated with corresponding reward functions. New generated tasks then receive learned evaluation rather than handcrafted evaluators (§3.1, p. 7; Appendix B.3, pp. 21–22).
3. **Actor–critic updates with a reference policy.** The actor minimizes

   `Eν[(β log(πθ(a|s,I) / πref(a|s,I)) − Â(s,a,I))²]`,

   where the reference is the preceding phase's policy. The update coefficient depends on the advantage minus the scaled log ratio; it can oppose increases in already over-promoted actions. This loss is motivated by a KL-regularized objective, but is not a measured hard bound on whole-policy divergence. A critic learns terminal success through cross-entropy; the practical advantage blends one-step bootstrapping and terminal return with λ=0.5. Experiments use γ=0.9, clarified in Appendix A.1, Eq. 15, p. 18 (§2.2, Eqs. 5–8, pp. 5–6).
4. **Selective successful replay.** Only successful historical trajectories enter the replay buffer. The previous actor scores their actions, retaining state–action examples with perplexity between 1/0.95 and 1/0.5, approximately 1.053–2. Historical training data are capped at twice the current interaction data. Current failed rollouts still train the actor and critic; excluding historical failures is not a prescription to discard every failure. The failure set separately seeds future tasks (p. 6; Appendix B.1 and Algorithm 1, pp. 21–22).

This requires environment interaction, an initially competent SFT policy, a learned critic, reliable reward labels, and access to model probabilities and trainable weights. It is considerably more machinery than adding reflective text to a prompt.

**Evidence and its limits.** Table 1 (p. 7) gives the most relevant controlled starting points:

| Model | Untuned | SFT | WebRL | Gain over SFT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Llama-3.1-8B | 4.8% | 20.6% | 42.4% | 21.8 percentage points |
| GLM-4-9B | 6.1% | 22.4% | 43.0% | 20.6 points |
| Llama-3.1-70B | 12.7% | 23.0% | 49.1% | 26.1 points |

Evaluation uses 165 WebArena-Lite cases, not the full 812-task WebArena. The abstract's much larger untuned-to-WebRL changes include SFT. GPT-4-Turbo's 17.6% and GPT-4o's 13.9% are simple-prompt baselines; AWM and WebPilot numbers are imported from full-WebArena evaluations. Therefore the broad claim of surpassing proprietary agents is less controlled than the within-model training comparison. Appendix B.2 (p. 21) also establishes unequal training opportunities: Filtered BC and AWR use first-phase offline data, whereas WebRL receives successive online curricula. Their gaps cannot isolate the policy loss alone.

The ablations are stronger evidence for the integrated design. Figure 5 (p. 9) ends at 42.4% for WebRL, 34.5% without the KL-based update, 32.7% without curriculum, 24.8% without replay, and 20.0% without both KL and replay. Removing KL substitutes REINFORCE with a value baseline, so this changes the update rule, not merely a regularization coefficient. Figure 14 (p. 25) shows curriculum also improves DigiRL, though it remains below WebRL. Appendix C reports four random seeds, with error bars in Figures 13 and 15; this is not solely a single-run claim. Nevertheless, eight phases establish a bounded improvement trajectory, not open-ended continual learning. Map performance rises and then declines, with smaller GitLab regression acknowledged by the authors (pp. 22–25).

Replay threshold evidence is narrower: Table 2 (p. 10) compares filters in the first learning phase, finding 31.5% for the intermediate range versus 29.1% using all replay. Those fixed thresholds are empirical hyperparameters, not universal measures of task difficulty. Token likelihood also reflects formatting and familiar syntax. Figure 16 (p. 25) supports successful-only historical replay under this estimator; it does not show failures lack reusable causal information.

The ORM reaches 80.8% on test-derived labels and 79.4% on sampled, manually labeled rollouts (Table 3, pp. 10–11). Roughly 80% accuracy is useful evidence, but insufficient to certify a reward source under optimization. No reported confusion matrix or adaptive reward-exploitation evaluation establishes its false-success rate. Final HTML and action descriptions may omit proof that an intended persistent change actually occurred. ORM mistakes affect rewards, successful replay membership, and failure seeds; the critic then affects task admission. This creates a coupled route for evaluator errors to reshape subsequent training. The final benchmark uses task evaluators, which helps establish real improvement beyond simply increasing ORM scores.

Long-horizon claims require additional caution: Figure 4 excludes tasks no model solved and defines required length from the shortest observed successful trajectory (§3.5, pp. 8–9). This conditions the analysis on observed solvability rather than an independent oracle difficulty measure. Likewise, same-site, known-entity task generation does not establish transfer to unseen websites, altered interfaces, or Navi's executive tasks. Training uses GPT-4o for generation/filtering, despite open actor models; the paper does not provide a complete cost comparison including generation, environment interaction, and actor/critic/ORM training.

**Disagreement with the improvement proof.** Appendix A.2 (pp. 18–19) does not justify an unconditional monotonic-improvement guarantee. Equation 16 defines `πnew = exp((Qμ − Vμ)/β)`, but later defines `Vμ` as the current policy's entropy-adjusted expected Q. For a nonoptimal policy this need not equal the log-partition function, so the displayed exponential need not normalize. A normalized soft-greedy update can repair the idealized construction, but does not prove the finite-data implementation. Equation 21 additionally asserts that adding successful historical trajectories makes the induced behavior policy no worse than the current policy. Success-conditioned sampling can select lucky outcomes and uneven state coverage; that ordering requires assumptions the paper does not supply. Approximate regression and a success-probability critic also do not establish exact soft-policy evaluation. The visually verified equations support treating this appendix as motivation with gaps; the empirical ablations carry the practical case.

**Prioritized recommendations.** These are proposed experiments; none was performed for this review. Applying the curriculum to typed executive tasks is a transfer hypothesis, not an empirical result of WebRL.

1. **P0 — Establish independent task outcomes and reproducible trial capture.** Build an isolated evaluation/export layer around the record boundaries in `ExecutiveCore::finishAction`, `ActionExecution`, and `DecisionCycle` (I05, Q29, Q53). Record task/version, environment snapshot, ordered complete observations/actions, model version, and an independently computed terminal predicate. For memory tasks, verify the requested fact or transformation against fixtures, rather than accepting successful adapter dispatch. Assess a candidate ORM on held-out successes, failures, and plausible-looking false completions, including omitted-history cases. Dependencies are curated fixtures, complete capture, reset support, and human adjudication for ambiguous labels. A proposed admission gate is a 95% upper confidence bound below 5% for the fraction of ORM-accepted successes rejected by independent labels; insufficient data or failure to meet it blocks ORM-gated replay. This addresses the paper's most consequential transfer risk before training costs accrue.
2. **P1 — Test failure-seeded curriculum in a fixed, bounded task family.** Use linked failed trials to propose feasible variants, preserving entities and terminal predicates. Keep generated practice tasks in an experimental catalog; Q36 does not establish a production goal sampler. Compare fixed tasks, randomly varied feasible tasks, and failure-seeded variants using the same adaptation method and equal interaction/token budgets. Hold out entity combinations and templates before generation; track feasibility rejection, task-family coverage, and previously mastered tasks. This needs a reliable evaluator and a declared adaptation mechanism: generating tasks alone cannot reproduce WebRL. A proposed success criterion is at least a five-point held-out task-success gain over random variation, with a paired 95% interval above zero and no greater than two-point old-task regression. Otherwise reject the extra curriculum machinery for that family.
3. **P2 — Consider actor training only after P0/P1 pass.** Keep a trainable local actor, critic, frozen phase reference, and verified-success replay dataset outside the executive. An experimental likelihood/export interface adjacent to [LocalModelClient.php](../../../src/Core/LocalModelClient.php), `complete:64` (Q14/Q56), would need explicit scoring and observation contracts. Compare equal-budget SFT/behavior cloning, fixed-task RL, curriculum RL without replay, and full replay/reference control. Ablate uniform versus likelihood-filtered successful replay; retain failures separately for diagnosis and current-rollout updates. Measure independently verified success, per-family forgetting, reward-model disagreement, tokens, latency, and training cost across seeds. Reject promotion if gains disappear under independent evaluation, old-task regression exceeds the predefined bound, or compute cost violates the experiment's budget. Do not substitute PHP score inertia or memory salience for the policy KL mechanism.

**Verdict.** Adopt the experimental discipline of verified outcomes, targeted practice, and explicit retention measurement. Experiment with curriculum before committing to model training. The next consequential deliverable is a small independently scored task suite with complete trial records; production self-training is premature on the evidence available.
