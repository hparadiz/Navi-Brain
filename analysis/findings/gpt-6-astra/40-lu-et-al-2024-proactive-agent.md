# Proactive Agent: Shifting LLM Agents from Reactive Responses to Active Assistance

**Authors:** Yaxi Lu, Shenzhi Yang, Cheng Qian, Guirong Chen, Qinyu Luo, Yesai Wu, Huadong Wang, Xin Cong, Zhong Zhang, Yankai Lin, Weiwen Liu, Yasheng Wang, Zhiyuan Liu, Fangming Liu, and Maosong Sun. **Year/version:** 2024, local arXiv:2410.12361v3, dated 3 December; running header “A Preprint — December 4, 2024.” No conference venue is claimed for this version. **Source:** [local PDF](../../../assets/papers/40-lu-et-al-2024-proactive-agent.pdf). **Reviewer:** GPT-6 Astra. **Implementation evidence:** [shared master reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, HEAD `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; static evidence supplied by the implementation master, not independent code inspection or runtime validation.

## Core contribution and mechanism

The useful contribution is a trainable **task-offer-or-silence decision**, evaluated against judgments of whether assistance is appropriate. It is not a demonstrated general autonomous executive. The policy predicts `P_t = fθ(E_t, A_t, S_t)` from environmental events, user activities, and state; `P_t` may be empty. Acceptance rewards either an accepted offer or appropriate silence, and penalizes rejected offers and silence when assistance is needed (§3.1, PDF p. 3, equations 1–3). This makes abstention an explicit behavior rather than an accidental failure to generate text.

The training pipeline combines an environment gym, proactive agent, and simulated user (§§3.2–3.5, pp. 3–6; Figures 2–3). ActivityWatch-derived computer traces are aggregated and translated into descriptions. GPT-4o generates scenarios, user activities, events, and state updates, including simulated tools. The agent incorporates event/conversation history, drafts an offer, optionally revises it using feedback, and executes accepted tasks inside the gym. The actual interaction contract is significant: **offer first, execute after acceptance** (§3.4, p. 5; Appendix B, pp. 14–15), despite broader introductory language about autonomous task initiation.

Three annotators judge predictions as accept, reject, or reject-all; the last category indicates that no assistance is needed. Majority labels train a LLaMA-3.1-8B reward model. Generated training trajectories then fine-tune LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen2-7B agents. This is supervised adaptation using synthetic interaction data and a learned evaluator; the paper does not establish an online reinforcement-learning controller, a learned world simulator with validated dynamics, or a durable goal-management architecture.

The mechanism assumes that observable behavior reveals useful opportunities, annotators can represent the user's preferences, the gym preserves relevant context, and the reward model generalizes to new proposals. None of these assumptions makes an inferred need equivalent to user authorization or independently verified benefit.

## Evidence and limits

**Measured result.** ProactiveBench contains 6,790 training events across 136 scenarios in coding, writing, and daily life, plus 233 test events across 12 scenarios (Table 1 and introduction, p. 2). Test provenance is described unevenly: p. 2 discusses gym generation reflecting real contexts, while §4.2 calls the 233 events collected in the real world. Treat this as a benchmark grounded in real activity, with an incompletely specified processing boundary, rather than a demonstrated live deployment.

The reward dataset has 1,640 training and 120 test entries. Its fine-tuned evaluator attains 91.80% acceptance-classification F1 and 91.67% accuracy against human majority labels (Table 2, p. 6). This supports improved imitation of those labels. The split is random by entry, without a reported event/person/scenario grouping guarantee; because several predictions share an event context, related-context overlap is a possible weakness, not established leakage. The reported annotator agreement lacks enough detail to infer population preference reliability. Selecting five predictions with “minimum total cosine distance” (§3.5, p. 5) also leaves diversity/selection bias insufficiently explained.

Table 3 (p. 7) reports:

| Agent | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LLaMA-3.1-8B | 38.16% | 98.86% | 55.06% |
| LLaMA-3.1-8B-Proactive | 49.76% | 99.06% | 66.25% |
| Qwen2-7B-Proactive | 49.78% | 100.00% | 66.47% |
| GPT-4o | 48.15% | 98.11% | 64.60% |

Fine-tuning improves LLaMA F1 by 11.19 percentage points and Qwen by 5.73 points from its 60.74% baseline. The best Table 3 model beats GPT-4o by only 1.87 points, without reported confidence intervals or repeated training runs. Its precision still means approximately half its offers are rejected by the evaluator. These are evaluator judgments, not observed task completions, time savings, or sustained user satisfaction. Training used eight A100s for approximately 1.5 hours for the reward model and two hours for an agent training run (§§4.1–4.2); runtime cost and end-to-end intervention latency are unreported.

**The central metric problem.** Appendix C.5 (p. 17) counts a rejected nonempty proposal as FP and reserves FN for silence when help is needed. Consequently, always proposing gives FN = 0 and hence 100% recall whenever TP > 0, even if many needed tasks receive unusable offers. The metric conflates detecting a need with proposing an acceptable task; its positive denominator is not a fixed independently labeled set of needs. For recall 1, `F1 = 2p/(1+p)`, where `p` is offer precision. Substituting Qwen-Proactive's `p = 0.4978` reproduces approximately 0.6647. Thus the headline F1 does not establish adequate abstention or complete need coverage.

Table 3's “False-Alarm” is numerically `1 − precision`, a rejected-offer fraction, rather than the standard false-positive rate `FP/(FP+TN)`. Appendix C.5 does not provide its own false-alarm formula. Also, §4.2's verbal recall definition includes appropriate silence, whereas the appendix equation excludes TN. These distinctions matter when designing an always-on assistant where interruptions accumulate over time.

**Ablations temper the recommendation.** Allowing up to three offers counts an event successful if any offer is accepted (§4.3, Table 4, p. 8). GPT-4o's F1 rises from 64.60% to 77.72%, but the scoring rule charges nothing for two bad offers alongside one good one. It establishes set coverage, not reduced cognitive burden. Reward feedback alone lowers GPT-4o recall from 98.11% to 56.76% and F1 to 56.00%. Contrary to the adjacent prose's claimed LLaMA improvement, its Table 4 F1 declines slightly, 55.06% to 54.81%. Feedback can overproduce silence; candidate expansion can conceal unnecessary suggestions.

The local version has additional numerical inconsistencies: §4.2 prose says LLaMA improves 44.78%→61.74% and gives best Qwen F1 as 66.07%, conflicting with Table 3 and the abstract. This review uses the table values, visually verified in the PDF. More fundamentally, the same reward model helps generate/refine behavior and judges it, so improved scores may partly reflect evaluator-specific preferences. There is no held-out human evaluation of final agent policies establishing the claimed user benefit. The authors acknowledge the need for user-centered evaluation and lower false alarms (limitations, pp. 9–10).

A substantive disagreement concerns Figure 4 (p. 9): searching for an OAuth tutorial is treated as a missed need if the agent remains silent. That behavior can also represent deliberate independent work. Appropriate intervention is a user preference to measure, not an objective consequence of recognizing a search topic. Likewise, importing the full keyboard/clipboard/browser collection regime would add substantial exposure without demonstrated marginal benefit over narrower authorized context.

## Mapping to Navi-Brain

**Already implemented:** Navi has persistent event-sensitive cognition and real speech inhibition. `SensoryCortex::evaluate` publishes percepts and wakes existing threads ([source](../../../src/Perception/SensoryCortex.php), lines 311, 412–445; master **Q32/Q04**). `ExecutiveCore::selfPresenceSpeechGate` checks the parent intention, applies `ActionSelector::choose`, and samples affect-derived speech likelihood; answering bypasses these unsolicited-speech gates ([ExecutiveCore](../../../src/Core/ExecutiveCore.php), line 11420; [ActionSelector](../../../src/Core/ActionSelector.php), line 43; **I04/Q08**). Calling Navi purely reactive would erase working mechanisms.

**Partial transfer:** `SocialFeedback::registerUtterance` records utterance text, thread step, time, presence, and response window ([source](../../../src/Perception/SocialFeedback.php), line 45; **Q13S**). `OtherModel` learns observational social forecasts and contributes attention-based speech inhibition, but its projected reply/reaction/no-reply outcomes and context keys do not condition predictions on the content of alternative offers ([OtherModel](../../../src/Core/OtherModel.php), lines 72, 938, 1370, 1563; **I09/Q13S**). Predicting conversational reachability is different from predicting whether a particular assistance proposal is useful now.

**Absent within verified scope:** **Q32** found only the explicit CLI caller of `ExecutiveCore::createIntention`, not an internal event-to-new-intention bridge. **Q23/Q24** found fixed selection scores, without an action-specific posterior over human approval/utility. **Q14** found no parameter-training implementation in the searched production trees. The paper therefore suggests an additional proposal/acceptability mechanism, not simply turning on an existing learned task-offer policy. Existing typed adapters and execution verifiers remain useful downstream constraints (**I05**); their operational success is not a human acceptance label.

**Verified metric mismatch:** The master's **Q40** clarification establishes that `ExecutiveCore::computeMetricVector:7994` counts accepted/rejected **internal curator verdicts** on thread steps: `proposal_uptake = accepted/(accepted+rejected)` and `false_initiative = rejected/(accepted+rejected)` (lines 8087–8088). Neither measures human task acceptance. `SocialFeedback::closeWindows:77` scores observed engagement, while local Pet dispatch acceptance records delivery, not human approval. No explicit task-offer acceptance/rejection learner or candidate-conditioned acceptability gate was found in the inspected SocialFeedback/OtherModel/speech integration paths. Existing `UtteranceOutcome.id` and `thread_step_id` provide a concrete correlation boundary for additional offer-specific feedback. This is a bounded static absence finding; external feedback tools remain outside the master's scope.

## Prioritized recommendations

**P0 — Define useful opportunities independently of the generated offer.** Before changing production behavior, design an isolated replay evaluation around authorized event/workspace context and utterance provenance (**I03/Q13S/Q40**). Annotate each held-out context for whether help is wanted, acceptable task alternatives, and the time window in which an offer remains useful. Separately label each generated offer's relevance, timing, and acceptance; retain uncertain/missing judgments. Attach proposed feedback to the utterance/thread-step identity, recording silent opportunities separately. Add distinct reporting beside [OtherModelEvaluator](../../../src/Core/OtherModelEvaluator.php)::`report:16` and `ExecutiveCore::computeMetricVector`; preserve existing curation metrics under their actual semantics. Compare current gating, always-silent, always-offer, and candidate-conditioned policies. Report rejected offers per active user hour, independently defined missed opportunities, offer precision, and later verified task benefit. Split by session/day, cluster uncertainty estimates accordingly, and never equate no reply with rejection. **Cost/dependency:** representative context capture and human labeling dominate. **Failure criterion:** no adoption if improvement exists only under the paper's action-dependent F1, or if useful-opportunity coverage cannot exceed silence without exceeding the current rejected-offer budget.

**P1 — Experiment with a bounded offer-or-abstain proposal.** At the existing sensory-wake/worker boundary and `selfPresenceSpeechGate`, prototype a structured proposal containing task-or-null, source evidence IDs, observation time, expiry, and the intended benefit. Condition it on authorized observations and relevant history; distinguish a task suggestion from a committed intention. Check applicable existing authority and installed capabilities before any subsequent execution. Revalidate relevant evidence before presenting a delayed proposal: **Q02** establishes that ordinary decision integration does not generally compare saved workspace state with current state, though this does not prove every speech curator lacks freshness checks. **Cost/dependency:** P0 labels, bounded extra inference, and proposal lifecycle/expiry handling. **Proposed pass criterion:** at least 20% fewer human-rejected offers than current gating, with independently labeled useful-opportunity coverage falling by no more than five percentage points. Reject the design if stale/duplicate offers escape the specified expiry/repetition rules or unaccepted suggestions become new executable commitments without applicable standing authority.

**P2 — Test candidate selection and feedback before funding fine-tuning.** After P0, compare one draft, one draft plus judge feedback, and three internally generated candidates with at most one presented offer, using the same worker proposal boundary. The last variant is speculative synthesis: it tests whether candidate diversity helps under a presentation budget, which the paper's any-accepted scoring does not establish. Keep evaluating human labels independent of the judge used for revision; any later synthetic fine-tuning uses training-only scenarios. **Cost/dependency:** extra generation/judging calls and, only if justified, a separate offline training pipeline absent from the inspected executive. **Failure criterion:** reject a variant if its gain disappears under blind human scoring, breaches the interruption budget, or cannot outweigh additional latency/cost. Table 4 supplies evidence to test this tradeoff, not permission to assume reward feedback helps.

These are proposed evaluations, not experiments performed for this review. No production changes are recommended before the label and baseline work.

## Verdict and coverage

**Experiment with proposal-specific abstention and evaluation; defer model training and autonomous task execution.** The most consequential next step is to measure whether an offer helps Aku at a particular moment, independently of whether a judge likes its wording. ProactiveBench motivates that mechanism but its recall definition, evaluator coupling, and approximately 50% rejected-offer fraction make its headline score an inadequate deployment target. Transfer is at the interaction-policy level; the paper supplies no evidence about consciousness or continuous cognition.
