# A Framework for Inference Inspired by Human Memory Mechanisms

Xiangyu Zeng, Jie Lin, Piao Hu, Ruizheng Huang, and Zhicheng Zhang. **ICLR 2024; local version arXiv:2310.09297v2, 20 May 2024.** The filename's 2023 date identifies the preprint lineage, not the reviewed version. [Local PDF](../../../assets/papers/52-zeng-et-al-2023-pmi-memory-inference.pdf). Reviewer: **GPT-6 Astra**. Implementation evidence: [shared reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, git `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; section identifiers below refer to that reference.

PMI is a trained neural architecture that separates perception, working memory (WM), long-term memory (LTM), and their interactions. Its most useful proposal for Navi-Brain is **to evaluate how retrieved historical information changes the interpretation of current working evidence**. Its experiments support trying that interaction; they do not establish that persistent agent memory should be replaced with a tensor, or that a particular number of slots reproduces human memory.

The mechanism operates over computational steps, instantiated as Transformer layers. Perception supplies token or image-patch vectors. WM is an `N × Dm` matrix, while LTM is declared as `C × N × Dm`. Previous WM generates queries over current input keys and values; top-k selection restricts writes, followed by residual transformations, layer normalization, and input/forget gates. Algorithm 1 masks post-softmax weights without an explicit renormalization step. Its gate and Appendix D.1 include a tanh on the candidate update that main-text Equation 6 omits (PDF pp. 3–4, 14, 18). These are learned, numerical operations, distinct from selecting text records by relevance.

Updated WM is associated with previous LTM through an operation called an outer product, followed by residual addition **inside** layer normalization (Equation 7, visually checked on PDF p. 4). Input representations then query WM densely and LTM sparsely; the latter is first averaged over its fragment axis. LTM-derived representations supply queries over WM-derived representations, producing a cross-memory result, and the final output mixes that result with the original WM read using α (Equations 8–12, pp. 4–5). Thus historical information changes the addressing of current information: the mechanism is more specific than concatenating two memory sources. “Correction” here denotes a learned representation transform, not an explicit test of which proposition is true.

Trainable parameters receive backpropagation; memory states evolve in the forward computation (§2.1). Algorithm 1 initializes memories and shares them across layers. The paper calls this sharing “persistence” and its state evolution “consolidation.” Neither terminology establishes retention across unrelated examples, process restarts, or months of accumulated experience. The language-model experiments motivate longer dependencies, but the paper does not adequately specify segment carry/reset policy or causal memory-write masking. Those are unresolved replication details, not demonstrated leakage. Attention heatmaps stabilizing across layers likewise do not prove that LTM contents converge or that stored relations causally explain answers (§4.5.2; Appendices B.2/C).

Two mathematical qualifications matter for adoption. First, a literal outer product between the declared matrix and three-dimensional tensor does not return the declared LTM shape. Equation 7 and Algorithm 1 omit the contraction, projection, or index convention needed to close that update. Table 7 further lists each LTM segment as `5 × 256`, whereas the declared `N=8` suggests `8 × 256`. This is specification ambiguity, not evidence about the uninspected authors' code. Second, Equations 8 and 10 return one representation per input position. Consequently, ordinary MHC in Equation 11 constructs `T × T` attention scores. **Small memory slots do not by themselves make the complete architecture linear in sequence length.** A replication must measure the fusion cost, not just the input-to-memory bottleneck.

The empirical evidence is promising but uneven:

- **Text QA:** Table 1 reports bAbI mean error **2.55 ± 0.11%** versus **3.6 ± 0.46%** for TR+HSW, with best-run error 2.32%. However, Table 9 shows **47.89 ± 1.47% error on basic induction**, and exactly one failed task in every run. Strong performance on most synthetic tasks does not establish general relational induction. Table 1 describes 10k training examples, while Appendix F.2 specifies 9k training plus 1k validation per task; preserve that reporting distinction (pp. 7, 20, 22).
- **Communication ablations:** At the medium model's `N=8, M=5, k=5` setting, removing cross-memory correction while directly combining the two reads changes binary accuracy from **87.61% to 79.96%**, a **7.65 percentage-point** loss, but ternary accuracy from **62.45% to 62.40%**, only **0.05 points**. Removing sharing yields 80.29% binary and 60.06% ternary: losses of 7.32 and **2.39** points, respectively, rather than the prose's 6.88-point ternary claim. The no-sharing model also has 16.5M parameters versus 2.36M, so this changes recurrent structure and parameterization together. Table 5 does not isolate the outer-product operator against an equally sized alternative association update (pp. 8–9).
- **Capacity and sparsity:** Soft competition produces 79.64% binary accuracy versus 87.61% for top-5 at that setting. Yet increasing k helps some configurations and harms others, and the medium model's smaller `N=6, M=3` configuration scores 65.93% ternary, above 62.45% at the claimed optimal capacity. These results support task-dependent bottlenecks; they do not validate Miller's law or a universal eight-slot optimum (Table 5).
- **Broader tasks:** Reported results include 97.9% triangle detection, 0.96 Enwik8 bits/character, 16.5 WikiText-103 perplexity, and 31.04 PG-19 perplexity (§§4.3–4.4, Tables 2–4). The language comparisons vary model size/depth and provide no uncertainty estimates. On CIFAR-10, CNN+PMI gains only **0.08 points** over CNN+MLP, 78.69% versus 78.61%, while parameters increase from 0.11M to 1.75M; removing LTM guidance gives 78.63%. That experiment does not substantiate the abstract's blanket claim of significant improvement over original counterparts (Appendix B.3, p. 17).

The clearest evidence is therefore for a useful architectural inductive bias on selected tasks. There are no reported lifelong retention/interference experiments, human-memory fits, or consciousness measurements. Most ablations lack uncertainty estimates, and inspection of attention patterns supplies descriptive evidence rather than causal identification. Neither “higher-order” tensor shape nor biological motivation alone demonstrates systematically compositional relational knowledge.

Neither operation identifies which prior claim a correction invalidates or enforces support entailment. This preserves the real history-to-workspace influence while identifying a narrower missing contract. The following are proposed evaluations, not experiments performed in this review.

1. **Priority 1 — Experiment with explicit reconciliation at the reasoning boundary.** Use the saved workspace and retrieved source bundle at `DecisionStateMachine::start` to request a bounded intermediate result: each relevant current claim is supported, revised, or unresolved, with its original claim identity and the source IDs that justify that treatment. Q52 identifies this artifact contract as new. Feed the result into the existing proposal path without automatically rewriting canonical memory. This is a **functional adaptation** of Equations 11–12; the labels and provenance rules are additional engineering choices. Compare WM-only, history-only, combined evidence, and combined evidence with explicit reconciliation under equal total model-token/call budgets. Use independently labeled cases with changed facts, misleading older records, missing support, and two-/three-relation questions. Preregister success as at least a five-point gain in supported-answer accuracy over combined evidence, with the paired 95% confidence interval excluding zero, and no more than a one-point increase in false corrections of already-correct current evidence. Failure means no supported gain, increased false correction beyond that limit, or a gain that disappears under the budget control. The main costs are labeling and potentially an extra reasoning stage; historical familiarity must never itself authorize overriding fresh evidence.

2. **Priority 2 — Evaluate content-conditioned admission before enlarging memory.** PMI's old WM supplies the next write queries. At `CapsuleAssembler::gatherCandidates`, an isolated variant could append one bounded, unresolved incumbent claim to the stable concern query, retaining scope and source exclusions documented in Q46. Compare fixed versus content-conditioned queries, with several candidate/slot budgets and an unchanged total context budget. Measure retrieval of *all necessary supporting records* for a relation, downstream answer accuracy, irrelevant-record displacement, and repeated selection of a false incumbent's supporting material. Include paraphrases and deliberately wrong incumbents. Reject the variant if it fails to improve complete-support recall or if conditioning locks retrieval onto false assumptions. This requires no neural tensor and adds little inference cost, but can introduce self-confirming selection. Run on disposable frozen stores: QMEM establishes that `RANK_RECORDS` still learns query associations even when candidate-read counters are neutral.

3. **Priority 3 — Defer tensor adoption pending a separate replication.** A literal PMI implementation belongs in an isolated trainable-model experiment, not a rewrite of `tokmem.c`. Resolve Equation 7's dimensions, initialization and reset policy, sparse-weight normalization, and causal decoding first. Compare tensor versus matrix/additive association updates at matched parameter and compute budgets, preserving versus removing cross-memory correction. Validate that perturbing future tokens cannot change earlier predictions, then measure relation generalization, interference after intervening tasks, peak memory, and fusion latency. Stop if causal isolation fails, the tensor adds no repeatable benefit, or quadratic fusion defeats the intended resource budget. This has substantially higher training and instrumentation costs than the first two experiments; current evidence does not justify prioritizing it.

**Verdict: experiment with the interaction; defer production architecture changes.** Separate memory roles and constrained admission already have executable counterparts in Navi-Brain. The consequential next step is a controlled evaluation of whether historical evidence improves or corrupts interpretation of current evidence. The paper supports asking that question, while its reporting ambiguities, task-specific ablations, and lack of durable-retention measurements limit stronger adoption claims.
