## Core idea and mechanism

Friston proposes a common objective for perception, learning, attention and action: minimize variational free energy under an organism-specific generative model. The biological motivation is persistence within a restricted repertoire of viable states; the computational contribution is a tractable bound on sensory surprise, coupled to inference and environmental action (pp. 1–3, Box 1).

Writing observations as $s$, their hidden causes as $z$, model as $m$, and an internal recognition distribution as $q(z)$, Box 1 gives the equivalent identities:

$$
F[q,s]=\mathbb E_q[\log q(z)-\log p(s,z\mid m)]
=D_{KL}(q(z)\Vert p(z\mid s,m))-\log p(s\mid m)
$$

$$
F[q,s]=D_{KL}(q(z)\Vert p(z\mid m))-\mathbb E_q[\log p(s\mid z,m)].
$$

These require a specified probabilistic model and normalized recognition distribution, with appropriate support. Holding observations fixed, minimizing $F$ approximates posterior inference; equality with surprise requires reaching the posterior, which a restricted approximation family or optimizer may not achieve. Holding recognition fixed, actions can change observations to improve expected log likelihood. Lowering the bound through perception alone does not change the surprise of already observed data. Nor does the bound identity alone guarantee that every action reducing $F$ reduces actual surprise when the inference gap changes.

**Source correction:** visual inspection confirms that Box 1's diagram actually prints “μ = arg max Divergence.” This conflicts with its displayed identity, surrounding explanation and Figure 4's minimization. It is an apparent typographical error; the mathematically consistent operation is **arg min**.

The proposed neural implementation is hierarchical predictive coding: higher levels predict subordinate causes, lower levels return residuals, and recurrent updates adjust conditional expectations. Box 2 assumes continuous nonlinear dynamics, Gaussian fluctuations and a Laplace approximation; precision weights errors, ξ = Πε, with Π an inverse covariance. Learning changes generative parameters through a prediction-error-dependent delta rule, while precision learning changes effective gain (pp. 4, 6). This is more specific than generic Hebbian co-occurrence. A Gaussian likelihood contributes a quadratic residual and a log-determinant normalization term; free energy is not simply any scalar prediction-error score.

The proposed unification is conditional. Infomax appears as a special case ignoring representational uncertainty and action (p. 5). Attention is interpreted through precision-dependent gain (p. 6). Rewards become prior expectations about states and motion, potentially installed through evolution or learning (pp. 7–9). The control argument explicitly assumes an optimal policy following a value gradient (p. 8); it does not establish equivalence to every reward formulation or control algorithm. This 2010 account uses priors over dynamics to induce exploration, rather than supplying a general future-policy objective with an explicit epistemic-information-gain term.

## Evidence, limitations and disagreements

This is a theoretical review with illustrative simulations and synthesis of previously published findings, not a comparative experimental demonstration of a unified brain mechanism.

- **Perception:** Figure 1 (p. 5), reproduced from reference 130, shows inference of two control parameters generating synthetic Lorenz-attractor birdsong. The caption reports identification after approximately 600 ms and shows 90% conditional regions containing the generating values. This demonstrates inference within the constructed model, not biological recognition latency, broad calibration, or performance on unfamiliar real birdsong.
- **Reaching:** Figure 2 (p. 8) simulates a two-jointed arm. An imposed prior acts like an invisible elastic band between finger and target; action suppresses resulting sensory errors. The associated motor-control work is described as unpublished observations. Smooth illustrated reaching establishes feasibility in that plant, without comparative optimality, robustness or computational-cost results.
- **Exploration:** Figure 3 (p. 9) solves a mountain-car example using priors that destabilize states outside a target region. Actual friction is constant; expected friction changes with position, and action realizes the expected dynamics. Several starting trajectories are illustrated, but no trial count, success-rate distribution, baseline comparison or sensitivity analysis is supplied. Much of the useful policy structure is carried by the designed prior; the example does not establish that such priors can be learned efficiently in unfamiliar environments.

The cited receptive-field, mismatch-response, gain and plasticity literature supplies compatibility evidence for component accounts (pp. 4–6). Shared mathematical descriptions and compatible findings do not establish one uniquely identified biological algorithm. The conclusion itself calls the review “contrived to highlight commonalities” and leaves the neural realization as a challenge (pp. 9–10).

The deepest inferential gap is between biological persistence and a universal engineering objective. Restricted state occupancy does not by itself identify the correct model, viable-state distribution, approximation family or update dynamics. Relating time-averaged surprise to a state-distribution entropy additionally needs appropriate stationary/ergodic assumptions. The review's language about organisms “violating” the fluctuation theorem (p. 1) should not be transferred literally: information-theoretic free energy is distinguished from thermodynamic free energy on p. 2, and local order in an open system does not establish violation of thermodynamics.

Another unresolved burden is prior specification. Hierarchies can learn empirical priors (p. 3), but this relocates rather than eliminates assumptions about model structure and higher-level constraints. Encoding desired outcomes as expectations can produce useful control; it does not make frequent or predictable outcomes desirable for a software collaborator. A consistently mistaken model can be internally coherent.

**Coverage:** all 12 PDF pages were read, including main argument, Boxes 1–2, Figures 1–4, conclusion and bibliography. Equations/figures on pp. 2, 4, 5, 8 and 9 were visually checked. Supplementary boxes S1–S5 are linked externally and absent from the supplied PDF; their full derivations were not inspected. Consequently, the broader entropy, infomax and control equivalences are assessed at the level supported by the main text, not certified as independently checked proofs.

## Prioritized recommendations and falsifiable criteria

**P1 — Experiment with calibrated uncertainty before replacing selection.** Extend the existing `ForwardModel::observe/precision` boundary in an isolated evaluation design to distinguish forecast uncertainty, independently established source accuracy and downstream usefulness. For one declared numeric feature, store a predictive distribution before observing its target and score it with negative log likelihood and interval coverage. Compare current significance against calibrated residual weighting at `SensoryCortex::evaluate`; do not rename the existing score “free energy.” This follows from Box 2 and p. 6: precision must describe a statistical uncertainty to justify its weighting role.

Dependencies are timestamped forecast/observation pairs, feature-specific likelihoods and independently labeled relevant events. Include stationary noise, genuine regime changes, dropout and predictable-but-biased/replayed sources. Fit residual distributions for persistence and current extrapolation on calibration data so their predictive log scores are comparable; freeze all models before evaluation. Proposed acceptance criteria are a positive held-out log-score improvement with a 95% confidence interval excluding zero, 85–95% empirical coverage for nominal 90% intervals, and fewer false escalations at fixed relevant-event recall. Failure includes suppressing genuine changes, treating predictable false input as validated truth, or gaining apparent precision only by dropping observations. Keep an independently sampled audit stream because workspace entry can itself count as usefulness and tune future sensing (**Q04**). This is the highest-value, lowest-scope experiment.

**P2 — Only after P1, compare an explicit latent model with simpler forecasting.** At the same forecast boundary, prototype a small state-space model for one source family, separating a persistent hidden condition from measurement noise. Declare transition/observation distributions, recognition approximation and inference budget; compare against an exact finite-state filter where tractable. This operationalizes the distinction between hidden causes and sensory fluctuations in Box 2 without requiring neural changes to model workers. It costs model specification, parameter estimation and bounded inference time.

Evaluate hidden-state recovery on controlled synthetic sequences and next-observation prediction on held-out sequences, with abrupt changes and misspecification. Compare persistence, existing extrapolation, the latent model, and an ablation removing its latent dynamics. Reject added complexity if it improves its own optimized objective without improving held-out prediction or useful-event detection, or if a simple filter matches it at lower cost. Such a filter would validate a narrow inference mechanism, not the universal biological principle.

**P3 — Defer executive-wide active inference; isolate any control proposal.** A later experiment around `DecisionStateMachine::integrate` and `ProceduralMemory::simulateCandidate` could compare authorized `machine.look` alternatives using explicit distributions of observations under competing hypotheses and candidate actions. Keep user success conditions and action costs separately declared; do not derive preferences from observed frequencies or permit a desired outcome to count as evidence that it occurred. Action-conditioned diagnostic information gain would be **speculative synthesis beyond the algorithm supplied here**, motivated by the paper's action/perception distinction and exploration examples (pp. 3, 8–9).

This requires P1-quality uncertainty plus action-conditioned models unavailable in the inspected candidate simulation. Compare with fixed source choice and the current heuristic under equal observation/compute budgets. Include cases where useful evidence initially increases prediction error and where an uninformative predictable source is available. Require better independently verified diagnosis or task success per cost; fail the proposal if it repeatedly chooses comforting observations, merely lowers self-scored surprise, or requires more resources without practical gain. No production adoption follows from this paper alone.

## Verdict

**Experiment with calibrated predictive processing; defer a unified free-energy executive.** The paper supplies a precise language for distinguishing inference, learning and control, plus instructive simulations. Its broad biological unification and equivalences do not justify treating Navi's current scores, needs or memory associations as one free-energy mechanism. The consequential next step is the bounded calibration and salience ablation in P1, preserving independent evidence of task relevance and source accuracy. No experiments, tests, services or production changes were performed for this review.
