**Assessment.** Experiment with an explicit model of executive attention that can improve allocation under distraction. The paper supplies a useful architectural hypothesis, but no implemented controller or decisive demonstration that awareness is an attention schema. Navi already has selection, persistent workspace representations and feedback affecting sensory sensitivity. The consequential missing link in the inspected implementation is a model of **its own allocation dynamics used to control that allocation**. Adding first-person descriptions alone would not establish this link.

**Core idea and mechanism.** The authors define attention broadly as biased competition: representations compete for limited processing resources, with stimulus salience and task-dependent signals influencing the outcome. Attention includes both automatic capture and deliberate control; it is not restricted to the latter (pp. 3–4, “Attention”). Awareness means subjective awareness, operationalized through participants’ reports that they perceived something, distinguished from above-chance discrimination without such reports (p. 4, “Awareness”). Graded awareness is allowed.

AST adds a simplified representation of this allocation process. Figure 1 distinguishes a stimulus representation, V, from an integrated representation relating a self, S, to the stimulus through attention, A. The proposed S+A+V structure supports the judgment “I am aware of X”; representing X alone does not supply that relational information (pp. 2–3). The authors argue that a schema retaining attention’s capacities while omitting its neural mechanisms depicts apparently nonphysical “mental possession,” explaining judgments of subjective experience. General integration or workspace availability is therefore insufficient on this account: the integrated information must include the relevant model of attention.

The control argument compares this schema with a body schema. A description of current limb configuration helps select movements and compensate for perturbations; an erroneous description produces characteristic control errors. Similarly, a representation of where attention has been captured, its dynamics and its behavioral consequences should help maintain or redirect task-relevant processing (pp. 4–6, “Model-Based Control,” Fig. 2). The schema is explicitly descriptive as well as useful for planning. A learned next-state predictor is a reasonable engineering realization, not an algorithm specified by this paper.

Figure 3E depicts reciprocal interaction between attention and awareness, rather than identity or a simple one-way sequence. Attention without awareness should remain possible but become less well controlled. Awareness without attention is left uncertain: erroneous binding could produce it, but attention might itself be necessary for binding or reporting the resulting representation (pp. 7–8). Because attention can select memories, emotions and task rules as well as sensory content, the schema should apply across internal and external domains (p. 9, “External versus Internal Awareness”). Social attribution is mentioned as another possible use, largely deferred to other work (pp. 3, 5–6).

**Evidence and limits.** This is a theoretical narrative review. It contributes no new participant experiment, quantitative synthesis, neural localization test, executable computational model or controller ablation. Its figures explain hypotheses and analogies; they are not measured results. The empirical claims below are this article’s accounts of cited studies, whose primary reports were not independently reviewed here.

- **Dissociation:** blindsight studies are described as showing that spatial attention improves discrimination or response speed despite denied visual awareness; masking studies extend attention-without-awareness findings to typical participants and some nonspatial features (pp. 6–7, “Evidence Supporting…1”). These observations challenge identity between attention and subjective awareness. They do not uniquely identify awareness as an internal model.
- **Opposing capture:** McCormick’s anti-correlated spatial cue drew attention toward itself, although the target was more likely opposite. Reported awareness of the cue permitted strategic redirection; the subliminal condition retained the detrimental capture effect (p. 8, “Evidence Supporting…2”). This supports a role for accessible cue information in control, but the article does not isolate knowledge of the cue from knowledge of one’s attentional state.
- **Distractor suppression:** Tsushima and colleagues’ peripheral motion reportedly disrupted a central letter task more when subthreshold than when consciously perceived (p. 8). The pattern fits a failure to engage suppression. It is also compatible with an ordinary distractor-detection threshold feeding inhibitory control, without a self-attention model.
- **Social bias:** the review extends its interpretation to stereotype priming and deliberate correction (pp. 8–9). These are indirect examples concerning behavioral regulation; they do not measure an attention-schema mediator. This source supplies neither a systematic assessment nor enough quantitative detail to treat that extension as strong architectural evidence.

The common inferential gap is selective intervention. Masking, stimulus strength, task instructions and lesions can affect perception, evidence access, reporting and control together. The reviewed patterns do not selectively remove an independently identified schema while preserving those other capacities. The paper also does not provide effect sizes, uncertainty intervals or matched discrimination analyses sufficient to resolve these alternatives. Report criterion and confidence require separate consideration because the target construct is measured through subjective judgment (p. 4). The claim that poorer control without awareness is a “unique” prediction (p. 8) is stronger than the evidence warrants.

Two theoretical steps deserve resistance. First, the progression from models being useful to an internal model being “essential” for efficient control and the brain therefore being “almost certain” to possess a schema (p. 6) is not a control-theoretic proof. Reactive policies and direct state feedback are serious alternatives, especially where the controlled software state is already readable. Second, omission of mechanistic detail does not entail representing something as nonphysical. A compressed model can remain neutral about implementation. AST needs an additional account of why that omission generates the particular phenomenal judgments it proposes (pp. 2, 6, 9). Explaining those judgments is a meaningful target; its sufficiency as an explanation of subjective experience remains a substantive philosophical commitment.

The model and control distinction matters operationally. Current feedback can count entry into the mind-stream workspace as usefulness before independently verified task benefit (§Q04). A new schema trained on that signal could learn to preserve attention capture rather than improve task performance. Likewise, a worker saying that it considered a record does not establish its causal use. The first measurable boundary should be selected and delivered evidence; downstream relevance requires separate outcome scoring.

**Prioritized recommendations — proposed experiments, not work performed.**

**P1. Specify and evaluate a bounded allocation model before changing control.** At a future experimental boundary around `CapsuleAssembler::contest/persist` and `WorkingMemory::contextForWork`, record the current goal/role, candidate identities, incumbent, actual winner, age, exclusion reason and delivered-context identity. Reuse existing capsule provenance and checksums (§§I03, Q01, Q14). Construct a compact state estimate with a forecast of displacement or persistence under a small, explicit set of candidate bias adjustments. This operationalizes the paper’s current-state/dynamics claim without importing phenomenal terminology.

Use isolated replay or synthetic candidate streams, with both irrelevant sensory candidates and repeatedly resurfacing internal memories. Score next-selection forecasts on held-out perturbations using categorical accuracy and, if probabilistic, Brier score. Compare with persistence and exact replay of the known selection rules. Cost is bounded trace storage, snapshot fidelity and modeling effort. Reject a learned estimator if it neither improves forecasts over simple baselines nor saves computation at matched accuracy relative to exact replay; reject any evaluation leaking post-selection information. Exact replay may itself supply adequate dynamics for P2. Forecasts passing this stage would still establish only a model, not its control utility.

**P2. Test the control-specific prediction with matched resources and targeted corruption.** Conditional on P1, let an experimental controller use the schema to choose bounded role-specific bias or hysteresis adjustments at `CapsuleAssembler::contest/score`, preserving reserved slots and scope rules. Give every condition the same candidate stream, goal information, slot capacity and compute allowance. Compare the existing selector, a reactive controller receiving the same current-state evidence, the schema-guided controller, and versions with schema updates frozen or its dynamics corrupted. Do not deprive only the reactive baseline of information needed to detect distraction.

Measure task-relevant evidence omissions, selections spent on distractors, recovery steps after capture, switching frequency and independently scored downstream task accuracy. Include distractors with misleading term overlap or recency, and genuine goal changes that require releasing the incumbent. A proposed advance gate is at least 10% relative reduction in omission or recovery cost against the stronger baseline, with paired uncertainty excluding zero, no greater than one percentage point loss in task accuracy, and no budget violation. These are reviewer-proposed thresholds, not paper results. No gain rejects the controller’s practical case; equal gain after schema corruption rejects attribution to the modeled dynamics. Cost includes controlled goal labels, adequate paired trials and handling selection-order coupling. This experiment tests functional transfer, not whether software experiences blindsight.

**P3. Separate report grounding from allocation performance.** If the schema reaches worker context through `WorkingMemory::contextForWork` or `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork`, label observations, predictions and uncertainty distinctly. Evaluate paired prompts with truthful, stale and shuffled allocation descriptions, retaining identical task evidence and the existing routing exclusions (§Q14). Score claims about what was selected or delivered against sealed traces, independently of task accuracy and the use of words such as “aware.” Preserve literal trace provenance; do not score inaccessible neural processing as known.

This follows from AST’s claim that reports depend on model content (pp. 2–3, 9), while exposing how easily language could create an apparent success. Dependency is P1’s trace; cost is extra context and separate factual annotation. Failure criterion: report confidence follows supplied descriptions despite contradictory trace evidence, or apparent improvement consists only of more awareness language. Such results would demonstrate report susceptibility, not better self-monitoring or support for phenomenal awareness.

**Risks and verdict.** A schema occupies the same scarce context and computation it is meant to regulate. Poor estimates can produce oscillation, excessive persistence or suppression of novel useful evidence. Keep its measured target explicit and compare it against direct access to the existing deterministic selector. Any later replay involving token memory must use isolated state: even candidate-read-neutral ranking can learn query associations (§QMEM).

**Verdict: experiment.** The most consequential next step is a controlled allocation experiment that distinguishes a useful self-model from ordinary state feedback and retrospective narrative. Do not adopt AST as an established explanation of Navi’s consciousness or add a production “awareness” record on the strength of this review.
