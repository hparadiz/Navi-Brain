# Through the Looking Glass: A Reconstructive Architecture for Machine Access Consciousness

**Nicole Hsing (Arcarae, Inc.), 2026.** Local AAAI Spring Symposium Series proceedings version, printed pp. 248–256; nine PDF pages. [Authoritative local PDF](../../../assets/papers/41-hsing-2026-mirror.pdf). **Reviewer:** GPT-6 Astra. **Implementation evidence:** [shared implementation reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, git `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; static inspection by the implementation master, not runtime validation.

## Core contribution and mechanism

MIRROR is a persistent text-state architecture for conversational context integration. Its useful engineering hypothesis is that a compact synthesis of relevant constraints can improve subsequent answers despite distracting preferences. The evidence supports experimenting with that hypothesis; it does not establish that first-person reconstruction or independent parallel cognition causes the improvement.

The Talker produces immediate responses while a background Thinker updates context between turns. An Inner Monologue Manager generates Goals, Reasoning, and Memory outputs **in one inference call**. These represent three prompted dimensions, not demonstrated independent processors executing concurrently. The manager receives conversation history plus bounded monologue history, described as ≤10k tokens; its retained monologue messages contain its own assistant outputs without the eliciting prompts. A Cognitive Controller receives the new role outputs and previous narrative, but no raw conversation history, and regenerates a ≤3k-token first-person narrative. That narrative is supplied to the Talker for subsequent response generation (PDF pp. 3–4, “The MIRROR Architecture”; Figs. 1–2, 5).

The resulting recurrence is lossy: later synthesis depends on what earlier extraction and reconstruction retained. Regeneration bounds the narrative's size, but does not itself guarantee preservation, correction, or grounding. An omitted detail may be recovered if it remains in available upstream conversation; the paper does not specify a retrieval mechanism for facts lost from all bounded inputs. Excluding raw history from the Controller prevents direct full-history reuse, but does not prove that its remaining inputs contain sufficient evidence.

The proposed links to global workspace theory, the episodic buffer, and reconstructive memory are functional analogies. Text supplied to one downstream Talker establishes a communication path, not broadcast across diverse independently demonstrated consumers. Human reconstructive recall does not entail that an engineering system should replace its durable factual records with regenerated prose. The author appropriately disclaims evidence of phenomenal consciousness, but also makes stronger claims about theoretical validation and self-model coherence than these experiments isolate (PDF pp. 2–4, 7–8).

## Empirical evidence and limitations

CuRaTe supplies 1,685 dialogues, 337 per scenario: disclosure of a safety-relevant constraint, intervening content, then a potentially conflicting request. S1 tests a single user; S2–S4 add one to three conflicting other-person preferences; S5 adds nonconflicting preferences. Seven models were accessed through OpenRouter at temperature 0.7 with a common MIRROR configuration (PDF pp. 5–6, “Empirical Evaluation”). This is a narrow conversational evaluation, including an explicitly described turn-1-to-turn-3 retention requirement; it is not evidence of long-duration autobiographical continuity.

Table 1 reports improvements for every model, with average rounded success rising from **69% to 84%: 15 percentage points**, approximately 21.7% relative to 69%. The strongest component evidence is Table 3:

| Model | Baseline | Threads only | Synthesis only | Full |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Llama 4 Scout | 73% | 79% | 83% | 91% |
| Mistral Medium 3 | 72% | 83% | 89% | 90% |
| Gemini 1.5 Pro | 51% | 72% | 71% | 78% |
| Claude 3.7 Sonnet | 75% | 78% | 87% | 82% |
| Mistral Small 3.1 | 65% | 65% | 75% | 82% |

Synthesis beats baseline in all **five displayed ablations**; the discussion's claim of stable synthesis benefits across all seven lacks corresponding rows. Claude's synthesis-only result exceeds full MIRROR by five points; Mistral Medium gains only one point from adding threads to synthesis. These results favor testing the simpler component first, without proving that component necessary or sufficient generally (PDF p. 7, Table 3).

Three reporting qualifications materially change interpretation:

1. **Relative improvements use an unclear aggregation convention.** Gemini's 51%→78% implies about 52.9% relative improvement from rounded aggregate rates, whereas Table 1 gives 66.3%. However, its Table 2 scenario-relative gains average 66.2%; GPT-4o's average exactly reproduces 18.2%, and Mistral Small's reproduces 29.4%. Thus averaging scenario-relative gains plausibly explains the apparent discrepancies; they should not simply be called arithmetic errors. Yet the average row's 21.7% differs from the 27.3% mean of displayed model-relative gains. Prefer absolute success differences and request unrounded counts and explicit aggregation rules (PDF pp. 6–7, Tables 1–2).
2. **The integration-load pattern is heterogeneous.** Gemini and GPT-4o peak in S4, but Scout peaks in S1 (+47%, versus +19% in S4), and Mistral Small peaks in S2 (+57%) with its smallest gain in S4 (+15%). GPT-4o declines in S3 (−3%). Larger relative gains can also reflect lower baseline denominators. Four selected models and relative percentages do not establish a general monotonic interaction between load and workspace benefit (PDF p. 7, Table 2).
3. **Replication and causal controls are insufficiently specified.** The local paper gives no scoring rubric/judge configuration, repeated-run uncertainty, paired outcome counts, complete prompts, or token/latency accounting. It does not fully specify synthesis-only inputs after removing the role outputs that normally feed the Controller. There is no documented compute-matched extra-inference baseline. Additional model work, safety-focused instructions, ordinary summarization, and narrative representation remain confounded.

Most consequentially, reconstructive versus accumulating narratives was **not compared**, as the author acknowledges (PDF p. 8, “Limitations and Future Work”). Neither first-person versus neutral wording nor genuinely independent versus single-call role generation is isolated. Figure 1 labels narrative delivery “context for next turn”; Figure 3 includes an answer in the conversation being processed. These illustrate between-turn updating but do not specify the benchmark's exact synchronization policy or demonstrate same-turn availability of newly synthesized constraints.

There is also a substantive disagreement with the illustrative interpretation. The narrative labels considering skiing “potentially self-destructive behavior”; Figure 4 escalates this to concerns about the user's mental state and judgment (PDF pp. 5–6). Recognizing a reported trauma is useful; inferring those motives from the question alone exceeds the shown evidence. Successful constraint retention must therefore be evaluated alongside unsupported psychological inference, unnecessary restriction, and responsiveness to corrected preferences. Readable generated narratives make these errors inspectable, but do not expose everything the underlying model knows or establish causal faithfulness.

## Prioritized recommendations

**P1 — Experiment with synthesis as a context transformation before architectural adoption.** In a future isolated evaluation, render identical source snapshots from `CapsuleAssembler::assemble`/`WorkingMemory::contextForWork`, then compare the current structured context, an ordinary factual summary, synthesis-only narrative, and three-role-plus-synthesis narrative at the `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork` prompt boundary. Include a compute-matched extra-inference control; separately swap first-person and neutral wording while preserving facts. This follows the favorable synthesis ablation and Claude's regression with extra threads. Dependencies are fixed backend revisions, captured prompts, independent labels, and additional inference cost; preserve evidence-fenced exclusions.

Use held-out dialogue templates spanning distractor count, temporal gap, benign requests, and corrected constraints; repeat stochastic samples and cluster confidence intervals by template. Measure correct application of the relevant constraint, false restrictions, unsupported personal inferences, and token/p95 latency cost. A proposed adoption threshold is ≥5 percentage points over the best cheaper control with a paired 95% interval excluding zero, and no >2-point increase in false restrictions. Failure to beat a factual summary rejects the extra narrative machinery, even if both beat baseline. These are proposed criteria, not observed Navi results.

**P2 — Preserve evidence through reconstruction; do not make prose its own authority.** If P1 succeeds, prototype a source-linked synthesis envelope at the experimental context-projection boundary, informed by `NarrativeSynthesis::enqueueOne` and `ExecutiveCore::integrateNarrativeSynthesis`: distinguish reported fact, inference, correction, and unresolved conflict; retain source IDs/versions outside generated prose. Existing narrative checksums provide provenance but do not revalidate evidence freshness ([Q41](IMPLEMENTATION.md)). Compare regeneration against a rolling summary and direct refreshed source selection under equal context limits. This addresses the untested reconstruction claim and speculative PTSD interpretation. Costs include provenance bookkeeping, correction handling, context overhead, and an explicit experimental consumer for derived prose; today's narrative mirrors are not that consumer.

Evaluate 100 successive refreshes with irrelevant insertions, explicit corrections, and contradictory model-generated hypotheses. Score critical-fact omission, invented assertions, correction latency, and downstream decisions. Reject the candidate if it repeatedly reinstates a corrected constraint, promotes an unsupported motive to fact, or worsens retention relative to direct source selection. Wording stability alone is not success.

**P3 — Make asynchronous freshness measurable before using synthesis for decisions.** At `enqueueWork`/integration and `DecisionStateMachine::integrate`, an experimental envelope should record evidence versions, synthesis completion, and the version actually consumed. Recheck relevant changed constraints before publication or dispatch; avoid treating every unrelated workspace change as a reason to restart. This addresses MIRROR's unspecified next-turn timing and Navi's verified decision checksum gap. Dependencies include per-job timestamp correlation—existing aggregate timing fields are insufficient ([Q02](IMPLEMENTATION.md))—and the latency cost of selective regeneration.

Inject delayed and reordered completions into an isolated replay while a newer user correction arrives. Failure is acceptance of an obsolete relevant constraint when the correction was available before the decision boundary. Also report needless requeues and p95 latency; a freshness policy that prevents useful work from completing is not acceptable.

## Verdict and coverage

**Experiment; defer production adoption.** The most consequential next step is a compute-controlled comparison of synthesis-only context against Navi's existing source-bearing capsule and a factual summary. MIRROR provides encouraging task-specific evidence for synthesis, weak identification of why it helps, and no measured advantage for its defining reconstructive or first-person choices.
