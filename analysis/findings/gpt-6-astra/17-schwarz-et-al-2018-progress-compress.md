## Mechanism and assumptions

P&C maintains a fixed-size **knowledge base** and **active column**, each a neural network. During progress, knowledge-base weights remain fixed; the active column learns the current task using its own transformations plus trainable layerwise adaptors from knowledge-base activations. This preserves old parameters while allowing new-task learning without their regularization burden. Equation 1 specifies gated multilayer adaptors, with 1×1 convolutions for convolutional networks. Resetting the active column and adaptors between sufficiently different tasks is an experimentally consequential choice, not cosmetic initialization (§2.1, p. 2).

During compression, the learned active policy supplies teacher targets and the knowledge base becomes the student. Equation 2 combines teacher-to-student KL divergence on current-task inputs with a quadratic penalty around the preceding knowledge-base parameters:

\[
\mathbb E_{x\sim D_k}\!\left[\mathrm{KL}\left(\pi_k(\cdot\mid x)\Vert\pi^{KB}_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x)\right)\right]
+\tfrac12\sum_j\gamma F^*_{k-1,j}(\theta_j-\theta^{KB}_{k-1,j})^2.
\]

Here the input expectation is over current supervised examples or states encountered under the active policy; it does not require replaying previous-task data. The experiments additionally tune regularization strength λ and policy temperature. Distillation removes direct dependence on task reward scale, but does not establish equal downstream competence on all tasks (§2.2, pp. 2–3).

Online EWC retains one current parameter anchor and one accumulated diagonal Fisher approximation, updating `F*_k = γ F*_(k−1) + F_k` after learning. Individual task Fishers are normalized before aggregation. This avoids retaining an ever-growing collection of separate task penalties; storage scales with parameter count rather than task count (§4, pp. 4–5). It does **not** give unbounded representational capacity. The paper explicitly introduces forgetting to free capacity. By expanding the recurrence, an old contribution decays as γ to the number of subsequent updates: global age discounting, not a demonstrated estimate of that particular task's dispensability.

Several conditions matter. Task-distribution changes are supplied to the learner; eliminating task identities does not eliminate the need for boundaries (§6, p. 8). The diagonal Fisher is a local approximation that omits parameter interactions, and old Fishers are not recomputed after the anchor moves (§4, p. 4). Exact Bayesian order invariance therefore does not transfer to this approximate training procedure. Finally, Eq. 2 explicitly requires a fixed teacher policy. Because the active column consumes knowledge-base features, freezing active-column weights alone would not freeze its outputs while those features change. An implementation must preserve teacher-side features, targets, or a snapshot; the local main paper does not settle that engineering detail.

## Evidence and limits of inference

The experiments use 50 Omniglot alphabets, six Atari games, and eight related 3D navigation mazes. Omniglot is ordinary supervised alphabet classification, explicitly not the dataset's familiar few-shot regime. Reinforcement-learning experiments use a distributed actor–critic with shared convolutional policy/value encoding and four random seeds. Reported confidence intervals are generally 68% bootstrap intervals; Table 2 instead reports standard deviations across five task permutations (§5, pp. 5–8).

| Evidence | Result and warranted interpretation |
|---|---|
| Omniglot retention, Fig. 2a, p. 6 | Tracking an initial alphabet through 49 others shows substantial protection relative to finetuning. P&C still loses performance; this is mitigation, not immunity. Tracking selected initial tasks is narrower than a full task-by-time retention matrix. |
| Atari forward transfer, Table 1, p. 6 | With five previous games, the reset active column reaches 128% of single-task performance, versus 101% without resetting, 98% for finetuning, and 57% for online EWC. The result supports separating acquisition from regularization and testing resets under task diversity. It measures the active column, not successful preservation in the compressed knowledge base. |
| Maze transfer, Fig. 3, p. 6 | P&C's active column approximately matches finetuning and exceeds online EWC on a held-out maze after seven related mazes. This supports transfer where reusable structure is strong; it does not show that P&C improves every acquisition baseline. |
| Omniglot aggregate, Table 2, p. 8 | After one pass, P&C obtains 70.32 ± 3.3% versus online EWC's 69.99 ± 3.2%. After five passes, these become 82.84 ± 1.4% and 79.15 ± 1.9%; dedicated models reach 88.34%. The late advantage coexists with a negligible first-pass difference and a residual gap to separate models. |

Table 2 reports 659K parameters for P&C, 446K for online EWC, 11,100K for EWC, and 108,000K for Progressive Nets. These support the claimed task-count scaling advantage; they are not measurements of total RAM, optimizer state, energy, or latency. Two learning phases still consume compute.

The strongest aggregate results involve revisiting tasks. Omniglot trains to convergence on each visit and tunes hyperparameters for performance after five visits. Atari visits each game five times for 50 million environment frames per visit; Figure 4 plots knowledge-base performance during compression (§5.3, pp. 7–8). Gains are heterogeneous: Beamrider and Stargunner favor P&C, while Space Invaders favors EWC near the end, and Hero exhibits pronounced forgetting between revisits. Krull has an explicitly marked data-loss interval. These curves support a practical tradeoff, not uniformly preserved capability.

Revisiting an old task and relearning it is not evidence of immediate positive backward transfer from a different task. Nor do these finite, scheduled sequences establish performance under gradual drift, unannounced boundaries, or indefinitely increasing task diversity. Omniglot shows no final-generalization improvement from transfer over dedicated models; the additional faster-learning evidence is assigned to the missing appendix (§5.2, p. 7). The local source also does not establish a complete equal-compute comparison that accounts for compression and Fisher estimation. Its most defensible conclusion is improved acquisition–retention balance under these protocols.

## Mapping to Navi-Brain

The relevant distinction is between **parameter interference**, **availability of stored evidence**, and **competition for retrieval/context**. Only the first is directly addressed by EWC. Navi can preserve every source record yet answer an old question worse because new records alter ranking or displace context; conversely, deleting a source is not necessary for useful abstraction.

## Prioritized recommendations

**P0 — Measure acquisition, compression fidelity, and old-task retention separately.** For a future offline evaluation around `enqueueConsolidation`/`integrateConsolidation`, assemble chronological episode batches and independently scored probes covering stable facts, procedural contracts, and explicitly dated corrections. Evaluate separate store copies with consolidation disabled, current consolidation enabled, and older comparison context ablated. After every batch record `R[i,j]`, performance on task family j after batch i, plus retrieval evidence recall, unsupported claims, and tokens needed on a related new task. Inspect both native ACTIVATE and ordinary search: their ranking differs (I02/Q11).

This follows the paper's distinction between active-column progress and knowledge-base competence. It costs curated labels, isolated stores, and repeated inference with fixed model versions and matched budgets. Isolation is essential for measurement: ACTIVATE learns from queries, and even RANK_RECORDS is not wholly mutation-free (QMEM). A proposed advancement criterion relative to no consolidation is at least a five-percentage-point new-task benefit, with the upper 95% confidence bound on stable old-task degradation below two points. Any increased false assertion rate on dated corrections fails acceptance. These are proposed engineering thresholds, not paper results; failure means synthesis has not yet justified stronger compression or more automation.

**P1 — Make promotion conditional on preserved behavior if P0 shows useful gains.** Add a candidate-publication boundary after `integrateConsolidation` evidence validation, extending the path verified in Q17. Compare candidate visibility against the current memory view before acceptance, preserving source records and existing no-automatic-supersession checks. Record source hashes, proposed semantic identity, and the retention decision. Isolate candidate indexing/query learning as well as records. Reversion must cover the relevant token-engine and executive state; a persistence checkpoint alone is insufficient.

This is an explicitly **speculative transfer** of P&C's protected-incorporation principle, not EWC. Compare guarded and current publication at identical consolidation-call budgets, counting evaluator overhead separately. Reject the extension if old-task degradation fails P0's margin, new-task gains disappear, or evaluation overhead exceeds the declared budget without reducing harmful promotions. Include conflicting and near-duplicate evidence: freezing earlier answers would preserve stale errors, whereas correct retention preserves the ability to revise when evidence changes. A gate adds latency and snapshot/replay complexity and is justified only by measured regressions.

**P2 — Reserve literal P&C for a small, trainable subsystem experiment.** If P0 identifies retrieval ranking as the bottleneck, a future candidate is an offline neural reranker beside `query_memories`, keeping the existing ranker as baseline. Define explicit sequential query domains; use frozen knowledge-base features, a resettable active reranker, teacher-to-student ranking-distribution distillation, and online diagonal Fisher protection. This requires labeled retrieval outcomes, gradient access, fixed teacher targets, and versioned trainable artifacts, none supplied by current inference workers.

Compare finetuning, online EWC alone, and full P&C with/without active reset at matched total compute, counting compression and Fisher passes. Evaluate unseen queries, worst-domain forgetting, forward sample efficiency, and performance after compression across both one-pass and revisited sequences. Failure means no favorable retention–acquisition tradeoff over the simpler baseline, or task-dependent state growth beyond the declared fixed budget. Do not expand to the executive or language-model backbone merely because the two-phase diagram resembles sleep.

**Verdict.** Adopt the paper's separation of acquisition quality from incorporation quality as an evaluation discipline. Experiment first on Navi's actual memory-mediated behavior. Monitor literal neural P&C until a trainable component and measured interference problem exist; fixed-size columns and discounted Fisher penalties do not warrant promises of unlimited autobiographical retention. No experiments, runtime operations, or tests were performed for this review.
