# Hypothetical Minds: Scaffolding Theory of Mind for Multi-Agent Tasks with Large Language Models

Logan Cross, Violet Xiang, Agam Bhatia, Daniel L.K. Yamins, and Nick Haber. Local version: arXiv:2407.07086v2, 12 December 2024; the PDF identifies itself as a conference paper at ICLR 2025. [Source PDF](../../../assets/papers/79-cross-et-al-2024-hypothetical-minds.pdf). Reviewer: GPT-6 Astra. Implementation evidence: [shared implementation reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, git HEAD `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; section identifiers below refer to that reference.

**Judgment.** Experiment with content-conditioned, prospectively scored social hypotheses. The useful contribution is a loop in which alternative explanations produce distinguishable predictions, receive delayed feedback, and influence a strategy. Navi already implements substantial hypothesis storage and forecast evaluation. Its consequential gap is that incompatible proposition contents can produce identical forecasts. Copying HM's module names or validation threshold would leave that gap intact.

**Mechanism and assumptions.** HM combines a hand-engineered textual perception interface, episodic observation/interaction buffers, a language-based Theory of Mind module, a subgoal generator, and deterministic action execution. High-level strategies become short sequences of function calls; A* and other handwritten routines implement movement and interaction. This is an inference-time architecture, without updating the underlying LLM weights (§3.2, pp. 4–6; Appendix D, pp. 25–28).

After a duel or soup delivery, HM evaluates predictions made before the event. Without a validated hypothesis, it generates a new natural-language strategy hypothesis, showing the generator previously successful hypotheses for refinement. It predicts the next behavior separately under the new hypothesis and up to five retained hypotheses. The latest hypothesis normally conditions planning until an older or newer hypothesis reaches the validation threshold; subsequently the highest-valued qualifying hypothesis supplies the strategy. Separate streams track opponents in the eight-player arena (§3.2, p. 5; Appendix D.3, pp. 26–27).

The implemented scoring description is simpler than the paper's Bayesian framing:

\[
r_h=\begin{cases}+1&\text{predicted behavior matches the target label},\\-1&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\qquad V_h\leftarrow(1-\alpha)V_h+\alpha r_h.
\]

Defaults are \(\alpha=0.3\), validation threshold \(0.7\), and \(k=5\). Validation suspends generation and evaluation of alternatives while the selected hypothesis continues making scored predictions. Failure below threshold reopens search. The two-player matrix-game ToM cycle uses approximately \(3+k\) LLM calls before validation and two afterward; this excludes subgoal calls and is not a universal cost formula for every substrate (p. 5; Appendix D.3).

Equation 2's MAP language is motivational: HM does not calculate an explicit prior and normalized posterior over trajectory-generating models. Values are discounted binary prediction scores. As an analytical consequence of the stated update, four consecutive correct predictions from zero give \(V=0.7599\), while one error from any \(V\leq1\) gives \(V'\leq0.4\), immediately revoking validation. For stationary correctness probability \(p\), the mean fixed point is \(2p-1\); the threshold is neither 70% confidence nor a significance test. This mechanism favors responsiveness but can reopen expensive search after a single mislabeled observation.

Its strongest assumptions are a useful language hypothesis space, identifiable behavior features, reliable event boundaries, and good low-level adapters. The prompts encode game mechanics and strong behavioral priors, including that opponents almost always collect one resource type. They do not reveal the specific sampled opponent policy (Appendix G.1, p. 30). Thus online policy inference is real, but autonomous discovery of perception, rules, and scaffolding is not established.

**Evidence and limits.** The main comparison covers 30 scenarios in four Melting Pot substrates, with five episodes per LLM architecture/scenario and SEM error bars. Table 1's numerical means support the following descriptive findings; these counts are not significance tests (pp. 6–10, 14).

| Environment | Result and qualification |
|---|---|
| RWS Repeated | HM has the highest mean in 9/9 scenarios. Against the adaptive best-response bot it scores 23.2 ± 4.7 versus PlanReAct's 21.1 ± 5.0: the small gap does not establish reliable superiority on that scenario. |
| Collaborative Cooking | Highest mean in 3/3 scenarios. With a skilled partner, HM scores 628.3 ± 35.1 versus PlanReAct's 402.9 ± 70.1; with an unhelpful partner, HM's 426.8 ± 42.5 is much closer to Reflexion's 398.1 ± 33.6. |
| RWS Arena | Highest mean in 6/8 scenarios, demonstrating operation against seven other agents. Two scenarios have negative HM means; this is limited population scaling evidence. |
| Prisoner's Dilemma | HM leads the LLM baselines in 5/10 scenarios but all methods in only 2/10 by numerical means. OPRE performs better overall. The paper's broad introductory superiority claim must be qualified accordingly. |

The PlanReAct comparison controls for hierarchical planning and shares the subgoal backbone, making it more informative than comparison with a monolithic agent. RWS ablations show benefits from modular prompting, retaining hypotheses, evaluation, and refinement; improvements diminish beyond roughly three retained alternatives (§4.5, p. 10; Figs. 8, 11–12). However, the HE+HR variant described as removing mind prompting also replaces behavior-prediction rewards with actual or LLM-estimated counterfactual task rewards and changes reward/threshold parameters (Appendix F, pp. 28–29; Table 3, p. 20). It does not cleanly isolate the causal contribution of mental-state vocabulary.

A more serious qualification concerns the scoring target. In RWS and PD, the opponent's inventory is hidden. HM asks an LLM to infer it from its own inventory and received reward, then compares predictions against this inferred inventory's dominant category (Appendix D.3, p. 26; prompt, p. 32). Actual environment reward anchors the inference, but the label is not directly observed ground truth. In RWS, a scalar payoff and known own inventory do not generally identify the opponent's full mixed inventory; the near-pure-policy assumption does substantial work. Cooking uses coded action recognition, but an LLM judges whether a natural-language prediction matches the observed actions (pp. 26–27, 40). No independent target-label accuracy study is reported. Predictive success therefore supports useful behavioral modeling more strongly than truth of the verbal explanation.

Figure 3's improvement around hypothesis validation is observational and aligned on an endogenous success event; it does not independently prove validation causes higher reward. Likewise, cheaper episodes having higher reward does not show reducing compute improves reasoning: successful validation itself reduces calls. Table 5 reports approximately $10 and 45 minutes for HM-GPT4 versus $4 and 21 minutes for PlanReAct per 1,000-step RWS episode. These are historical costs, and comparisons are not inference-budget matched. PPO was trained for \(10^8\) steps in Appendix E.4, despite the introduction's blanket billion-step wording; OPRE scores are reused from Melting Pot rather than generated by the same experimental pipeline. LLM pretraining also prevents interpreting these comparisons as equal-information sample-efficiency measurements.

**Prioritized recommendations.** These are proposed experiments; none was performed for this review.

1. **P0 — Make hypothesis content predict an independently labeled event.** Extend the seam around `OtherModel::hypothesisDistribution` and `sealPredictions` with an explicitly experimental forecast producer consuming hypothesis text, evidence available before sealing, target definition, and observation deadline. Start with a controlled cooperative task where competing same-kind hypotheses predict which subtask a scripted partner takes next. Preserve existing provenance, timing checks, and proper scoring in [OtherModelEvaluator.php](../../../src/Core/OtherModelEvaluator.php), `report:16` and `onlineBaselines:221` (Q21). This requires a labeled event vocabulary and additional inference; activity recency alone cannot discriminate task content. Compare against the current kind-based model and an equal-budget history-only predictor on held-out policies. Predeclare log-loss as primary, bootstrap by episode, and require the confidence interval for improvement over both baselines to exclude zero. Reject the extension if contradictory hypotheses remain indistinguishable on diagnostic events, or gains disappear when future information and inferred labels are excluded.

2. **P1 — Evaluate bounded retention and evidence-driven refinement.** After P0, reuse the existing three-record limit and selection machinery, adding a separately typed strategy-refinement work item alongside `OtherModel::queueParser:262` and its integration boundary (Q79). Preserve direct-statement extraction's distinct evidence contract. Compare a latest-hypothesis condition, retained alternatives without refinement, and retained alternatives with refinement under equal total token budgets. Give refinement hypothesis IDs, predictions, observed contradictions, and scores; preserve parent/evidence links when generating replacements. Charge all inference calls and evaluate stationary, noisy, and abruptly switching partner policies. Measure post-switch adaptation lag, cumulative loss, and calls per resolved event. Reject refinement if it fails to improve held-out loss over retention alone or increases median adaptation delay by more than one resolved event. Separately ablate HM's generation-suspension rule against Navi's continuing generation; a cost benefit must survive noisy labels without worsening task return.

3. **P2 — Demonstrate useful planning uptake before deployment.** Once forecasts improve, pass selected hypothesis IDs, predictions, and explicit uncertainty into an offline version of [DecisionStateMachine.php](../../../src/Core/DecisionStateMachine.php), `start:34`, at its proposal boundary; retain installed adapter and authority constraints (I04–I05). Compare correct hypotheses, hidden hypotheses, and shuffled hypothesis-to-partner assignments in an interactive controlled task with identical low-level planners and compute budgets. Remove duplicate social-context routes when ablating access (Q14). Prediction replay alone cannot establish the counterfactual return of a changed policy. Measure completed joint tasks and coordination failures separately from forecast scores and mere action changes. Reject production uptake if improved predictions yield no task benefit or shuffled explanations perform equally well. This stage requires an interactive evaluation environment and entails the highest engineering cost; importing HM's competitive exploitation objective into collaboration with Aku is not justified by its game results.

**Verdict and coverage.** Experiment, with P0 as the decisive next step. Preserve Navi's delayed proper scoring and correction machinery; add falsifiable semantic predictions before adding more social reasoning calls. This paper supports structured behavioral adaptation in engineered games, not human-level mental-state understanding or claims about consciousness. The proposed forecast/refinement/planning extensions are engineering synthesis, not demonstrated Navi results.
