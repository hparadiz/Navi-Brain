## Core argument and mechanism

Cerullo challenges the identification of consciousness with integrated information, Φ. His contribution is a philosophical and methodological critique, not an alternative implemented consciousness estimator. Its strongest result is a demand for **discriminating evidence**: a measure can correlate with familiar conscious conditions without identifying what makes them conscious. His stronger conclusion—that IIT fails outright and measures only “protoconsciousness”—requires additional, contested premises.

The first objection concerns the photodiode thought experiment. Tononi contrasts a binary light detector with a person whose experience excludes a much richer repertoire of alternatives. Cerullo calls this rationale the **principle of information exclusion**, arguing that phenomenological distinctiveness does not establish that ruling out alternatives generates consciousness (“The Theoretical Foundation of IIT,” pp. 2–3). This name is Cerullo's, and it must be distinguished from IIT 3.0's separate **exclusion axiom**, concerning definite experiential boundaries and competing complexes. Cerullo associates his information-exclusion objection with the information axiom, then separately criticizes exclusion and the identification of experiential unity with causal integration (“IIT 3.0,” pp. 3–4).

Second, he constructs **Circular Coordinated Message Theory** (CCMT). Its invented quantity, Omicron, is the information circulation of a whole minus that of a minimal partition. Feedback circulation allegedly explains why severing interhemispheric connections yields two conscious systems and why the cerebellum is comparatively unsuitable for consciousness. These are the same qualitative cases used to motivate IIT (“The Explanatory Power of IIT,” pp. 4–5). CCMT functions as a rival-explanation control: the observations do not uniquely select Φ over other network properties. It is not a recommendation to maximize software feedback loops.

Third, the fading/dancing-qualia argument considers neuron replacement that preserves functional behavior while routing computation through a digital implementation that IIT allegedly treats differently. Cerullo argues that changing experience without any corresponding ability to notice, report, or act on that change is implausible (“IIT and Functionalism,” pp. 5–6). The argument depends on assumptions connecting phenomenology to functional organization and on the proposed implementation actually having the claimed Φ.

Finally, the expander/XOR examples challenge the equation of high integration with familiar cognitive consciousness. Page 7 reports that an *n* × *n* XOR grid can generate √*n* Φ, allowing unbounded growth. This is Cerullo's report of the Aaronson–Tononi exchange, not a derivation or reproduced calculation. He interprets IIT as “partial-panexperientialism”: attribution of experience to some systems without requiring cognition. He distinguishes this from consciousness associated with awareness, memory, executive function, and global access (“The Epistemology of IIT,” pp. 6–10). His cognitive/noncognitive taxonomy also differs from Block's access/phenomenal distinction: he places both of Block's categories within cognitive consciousness (pp. 8–9).

## Evidence, limitations, and disagreements

The article contains no new neural measurements, participant sample, intervention, numerical model comparison, formal counterexample computation, or predictive benchmark. Clinical and connectivity findings are discussed through citations; the XOR result is imported from a public theoretical exchange. Their evidential roles must remain distinct from Cerullo's conceptual deductions.

**The underdetermination objection is strong but narrower than the conclusion.** CCMT has no operational definition sufficient to calculate Omicron across systems, and Cerullo does not compare its predictions with IIT quantitatively. Matching two coarse explanatory narratives does not establish equal explanatory power across IIT's entire domain. Evidence can support several theories simultaneously; lack of specificity is not absence of all support. His criticism is most persuasive when read as a requirement for rival models, prospective predictions, and explicit counterexamples (p. 5).

**The axiomatic objection identifies a missing justification, not a disproof of sufficiency.** Distinct experiences do not logically entail a unique physical information measure. Conversely, failure to derive that identity from phenomenology does not prove it false. Cerullo explicitly declines to examine IIT 3.0's eight postulates (p. 4), limiting the critique's coverage of its formal machinery. His tiny-organism example also needs the enlarged system to win the relevant complex comparison; mere incorporation or greater size is not a calculation establishing that result.

**The replacement argument does not establish zero Φ for digital Navi.** Behavioral equivalence, computational equivalence, and equivalence of intrinsic physical causal structure are different claims. A feed-forward algorithm can execute on hardware with feedback, and the proposed radio-connected neuron replacement requires a specified physical boundary, causal units, timescale, and transition model. Cerullo supplies none. His insistence that fading experience would be noticed also imports a substantive connection between experience and functional access. Someone accepting silent phenomenal differences can reject that premise without a formal contradiction. The argument therefore pressures IIT's commitments rather than proving computational functionalism or conscious uploading.

**Counterintuitive attribution is not internal inconsistency.** A high-Φ XOR grid challenges the construct's relationship to cognition, but its alleged lack of experience is not independently observed. Likewise, relabeling IIT “protoconsciousness” neither proves Φ measures such a property nor resolves whether noncognitive experience exists. Cerullo's discussion usefully exposes ambiguity about the intended target, but his taxonomy is an interpretation, not an experimentally established partition of minds (pp. 7–9).

The concluding distinction between behavioral explanation, the “pretty hard problem” of identifying conscious systems, and the hard problem of why experience exists is useful (p. 10). Failure to solve the last is not a special empirical refutation of IIT; Cerullo acknowledges that computational functionalism and identity theories share that limitation. Nothing here determines Navi's phenomenal status in either direction.

## Prioritized recommendations

These are proposed documentation changes and isolated evaluations. No production changes or test infrastructure were made. The recommendations are engineering synthesis from Cerullo's methodological critique, not procedures supplied by the paper.

**P0 — Specify what each metric can establish; defer a production Φ score.** At the reporting contracts surrounding `ExecutiveCore::metricsReport:7883`, `computeMetricVector:7994`, `ProbabilityScorer`, and `OtherModelEvaluator::report:16`, document each measure's observable, formula, intended inference, and excluded inference. Separate forecast quality, functional task performance, continuity, and consciousness claims. A future IIT experiment would additionally need a named version, explicit boundary/grain, intervention kernel, and partition calculation before producing a number ([I10, Q15](IMPLEMENTATION.md)). This follows from the paper's distinction between compatible observations and validated constructs, and costs a small documentation audit rather than a new evaluator.

Validation criterion: a reviewer must be able to reconstruct the measured quantity and its evidential scope from the specification. Reject any interpretation that treats Shannon entropy as Φ, thread closure as independently verified success, or Φ as a consciousness certificate without an additional defended inference. Success means precise metric claims; it does not mean every broader construct becomes measurable.

**P1 — Resolve the known rival-proposition failure before treating forecast updates as belief-content validation.** At `OtherModel::predictionTarget`, `hypothesisDistribution`, and `updateHypothesisFromPrediction`, either constrain the interpretation of existing scores to kind-conditioned forecasts or add a content-dependent mapping to independently observable outcomes. Preserve quotation grounding and user corrections as separate evidence routes. Q21S already establishes the current invariance; reproducing it alone would not be a new scientific result. The immediate cost is clarification of scoring semantics; a content-sensitive extension requires labeled contrasts and meaningful outcome definitions ([Q21, Q21S](IMPLEMENTATION.md)).

For a proposed extension, use incompatible proposition pairs with matched kind, baseline, and prior, and outcomes that actually distinguish their contents. Acceptance requires differential evidence in the correct direction and improved held-out proper scores over the existing kind-only baseline; specify the minimum useful improvement before evaluation. If predictions remain identical, or improvement appears only against labels generated from the same proposition text, reject the semantic-validation claim. This operationalizes Cerullo's demand for evidence that can favor one explanation over a rival.

**P2 — Evaluate useful cross-source access against trivial alternatives.** In an isolated future evaluation at `WorkingMemory::contextForWork` and `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork`, compare intact workspace access with a selected evidence channel removed, length-matched irrelevant context, and matched task-relevant context supplied without the capsule contest. Include tasks requiring two complementary sources and tasks solvable from one source, using randomized task facts unavailable from prior model knowledge. Record exact submitted prompts and independently scored decisions/source attribution. Preserve the real exclusions for evidence-fenced jobs and account for direct observation/retrieval routes that can duplicate removed information ([I03, Q14](IMPLEMENTATION.md)).

The cost is a bounded task set, repeated model calls, and isolated memory state; query-ranking APIs can learn even without counting candidate reads ([QMEM](IMPLEMENTATION.md)), so live retrieval would contaminate comparisons. Set a practical effect threshold and sampling plan in advance. The access claim fails on the evaluated tasks if removing the only cross-source route produces no selective loss. The stronger capsule-selection claim fails if matched relevant context performs equally well. Either result concerns functional information use, never proof or disproof of experience. This avoids the CCMT mistake of attributing a benefit specifically to a favored mechanism when a simpler explanation suffices.

## Verdict

**Adopt the methodological critique; experiment on discriminating functional evidence; reject immediate Φ-driven architecture changes.** Cerullo is persuasive about overinterpreting correlations and insufficiently constrained consciousness measures, less persuasive when declaring IIT conclusively refuted or its target definitively noncognitive. The highest-value next step is to tighten the inference from existing scores to claims, especially the already identified inability of same-kind forecasts to distinguish incompatible belief contents. No consequential implementation question remains unanswered by the shared reference; deployed behavior, physical causal structure, and phenomenal consciousness remain explicitly unverified.
