# HiAgent: Hierarchical Working Memory Management for Solving Long-Horizon Agent Tasks with Large Language Model

Mengkang Hu, Tianxing Chen, Qiguang Chen, Yao Mu, Wenqi Shao, and Ping Luo (2024). Local version: arXiv:2408.09559v1, 18 August 2024; arXiv preprint, with no publication venue established by this version. [Local PDF](../../../assets/papers/53-hu-et-al-2024-hiagent.pdf). Reviewer: GPT-6 Astra. Implementation evidence: [shared implementation reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, git HEAD `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`, including the paper-specific Q53 clarification.

**Judgment: experiment.** HiAgent offers a useful policy for deciding which parts of an ongoing task deserve detailed context: preserve the current subgoal's trajectory, summarize earlier segments, and permit explicit restoration. Its reported gains justify a controlled trial of that policy. They do not establish that Navi needs another memory store, that summaries preserve correct state, or that the approach improves an already selective workspace.

## Contribution and mechanism

The paper distinguishes cross-trial experience from the history accumulated within one attempt. Its target is the common policy of appending every action and observation to each subsequent model prompt. HiAgent changes both task organization and context presentation (§§2–3, PDF pp. 2–3):

1. The model proposes a subgoal and grounded actions toward it.
2. While that subgoal is current, its detailed action–observation sequence remains visible.
3. At a subgoal transition, a summarizer receives the subgoal and trajectory and produces a concise observation plus an achievement judgment. Earlier detail is hidden from the prompt.
4. The model can issue `retrieve(subgoal_id)` to replace an earlier summary with its archived detail (§3.4; Appendix B.2, p. 16).

Using cleaned-up notation, the prompt contains `(g₀, s₀, …, gₖ₋₁, sₖ₋₁, gₖ, τₖ)`, where `τₖ` is the current trajectory and `sᵢ` summarizes an earlier one. Retrieval exposes `τᵢ` again. This is reversible context compression only because the original trajectory remains available; it is not deletion or reconstruction from the summary alone. The hierarchy has two practical levels—subgoal outcomes and primitive interactions—not an arbitrary recursive planner, learned option policy, or trained memory architecture.

There is a small specification tension: §§3.1–3.2 describe transitions after accomplishment, but Appendix B.2 advances from an explicitly failed subgoal to a repair subgoal and retrieves the failed trajectory (p. 16). A faithful implementation therefore needs failed/abandoned segment boundaries as well as successful ones. A segment being archived must not imply that its goal was achieved.

The principal assumption is **decision-relevant sufficiency**: an earlier segment's summary must retain facts needed by future subgoals, or leave enough cues for the model to recognize that retrieval is necessary. The paper neither proves sufficiency nor measures summary fidelity. An omitted prerequisite can also eliminate the cue that would have prompted retrieval. Human chunking motivates the design; no human experiment, neural equivalence, or consciousness claim follows from these results.

My scaling inference is also narrower than “bounded working memory.” With `K` prior subgoals, average summary length `S`, and current raw segment length `L`, visible history still grows approximately as `K·S + L`, plus restored segments. There is no demonstrated constant-space guarantee, cap on a stalled subgoal, or policy for indefinitely accumulating summaries. Thirty-step evaluations cannot settle those issues.

## Evidence and limitations

The experiments use AgentBoard implementations of Blocksworld, Gripper, Tyreworld, Barman, and Jericho; GPT-4 Turbo supplies both policy and summarization, temperature is zero, top-p is one, each task has one in-context example, and the maximum is 30 steps (§4.1, p. 4). Table 1 (p. 5) reports equal-weight averages across the five domains:

| Metric | Standard | HiAgent | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Success rate | 21.00% | 42.00% | +21 percentage points; exactly twice the baseline |
| Progress rate | 38.61% | 62.55% | +23.94 percentage points |
| Average steps | 26.41 | 22.61 | 3.80 fewer steps |
| Mean in-trial context | 100% | 64.98% | 35.02% relative reduction |
| Runtime | 100% | 80.58% | 19.42% relative reduction |

These are promising task outcomes, not merely improvements on a language-model proxy. However, effects vary sharply: Tyreworld success rises from 10% to 60%, Blocksworld from 30% to 60%, and Jericho from 5% to 10%. Gripper success remains 50% and progress falls from 87.75% to 86.25%, despite substantially shorter context. The conclusion's claim of outperformance “across all tasks” needs this qualification; “more than double” is also stronger than the aggregate table supports.

The most informative control is task decomposition without hiding prior trajectories, evaluated only on Tyreworld (Table 3, p. 7). Success is 10% for Standard, 40% with decomposition, and 60% with HiAgent; decomposition increases context to 112.8% and runtime to 105.7% of Standard, while HiAgent reduces both. This supports an incremental benefit beyond merely naming subgoals. It does not isolate semantic boundary selection from generic summarization, a sliding window, structured state tracking, or an equally budgeted retrieval baseline. Appendix B also supplies a HiAgent-specific failure/retrieval demonstration, so demonstration content and control instructions belong in any replication's controlled variables.

Table 2's module ablations are likewise confined to Tyreworld (p. 6). Replacing summarization with the last observation reduces success from 60% to 30%; disabling trajectory retrieval gives 50%; removing both gives 30%. Yet removing retrieval slightly **increases** progress, 75.8% to 76.9%. Retrieval therefore helps complete success in this setting without dominating every metric. The joint ablation ties the summarization ablation on success; it does not establish a universal synergistic interaction. There are reporting discrepancies: the prose says removing retrieval adds 1.2 steps, but the table gives 19.0→21.2, or 2.2; the prose gives a 20-point success loss for removing both, whereas the table gives 30 points. This review uses the visually checked table.

Section 5.5 reports Wilcoxon signed-rank results for progress (`W=144.0`, `p=2.38×10⁻⁵`) and steps (`W=112.5`, `p=0.0016`). The local paper does not clearly specify the paired sample count/unit, per-domain instance counts, repeated-run counts, or confidence intervals, despite affirmative reproducibility-checklist answers (p. 11). These reported tests support differences under the authors' sampling assumptions; they are not a significance analysis of every domain or success-rate ablation. No newer-model comparison is supplied.

Efficiency reporting is incomplete for deployment decisions. “Context” means mean in-trial context across steps, not total billed input/output tokens across policy, summarization, and retrieval calls. Runtime is normalized without absolute seconds or enough decomposition to distinguish fewer environment steps from model-call overhead. The handling of failed/truncated episodes in average steps is insufficiently explicit. Figures 3–4 (p. 6) show progress and executability across steps, but changing trajectories and episode termination can affect those comparisons; they do not independently establish that context length causes invalid actions.

A particularly useful caution appears in the supplied example itself. Appendix B.2's summary says, “You have jack and hub the-hub1 is elevated” (p. 17). Appendix A.3 says lifting the hub means “the agent will no longer have the jack” (p. 12). Both passages were visually verified. The summary appears to conflate an intermediate possession event with the final state. This is an inconsistency in the published example, not a measured model error rate, but it illustrates exactly why successful-looking summaries need temporal and state checks.

## Mapping to Navi-Brain

**Already implemented:** persistent, scoped working-memory slots and provenance-bearing prompt selection. [WorkingMemory.php](../../../src/Core/WorkingMemory.php), `refresh:62`, `publish:122`, and `contextForWork:223`, plus [CapsuleAssembler.php](../../../src/Core/CapsuleAssembler.php), `assemble:101` and `contest:446`, implement actual bounded components and incumbent replacement (reference I03, Q11). These are role/TTL/selection policies rather than a record of current and completed subgoal segments. Navi's baseline is therefore substantially more selective than the paper's full-history Standard.

**Partially reusable:** durable intentions, action outcomes, and worker evidence. [ExecutiveCore.php](../../../src/Core/ExecutiveCore.php), `createIntention:1385` and `finishAction:2281`, supplies task linkage and episodic outcome records; [ActionTrace.php](../../../src/Model/ActionTrace.php), [ActionExecution.php](../../../src/Model/ActionExecution.php), and [ProcedureRun.php](../../../src/Model/ProcedureRun.php) retain identities, outcomes, and generation-aware execution links (I05, Q29). Run results are primarily references/statuses, not complete environmental snapshots. Existing episodic-to-semantic consolidation groups related episodes and retains sources (I08, Q16); it is not equivalent to replacing a completed subgoal's prompt segment with a task-local summary.

**Absent in the inspected decision route:** model-controlled trajectory expansion and re-proposal. [DecisionStateMachine.php](../../../src/Core/DecisionStateMachine.php), `start:134–214`, performs initial retrieval using intention title, next action, and trigger; `integrate:254` accepts terminal candidates, while `impasse:815` ends the cycle. The master's complete-route inspection found no within-cycle model-generated query/retrieval loop (Q03). Q27/Q28 also found no general resolving-subgoal/resume path or hierarchical intention stack in their stated search scopes. A `parent_id` or reusable procedure is not that mechanism.

**Absent after the targeted clarification:** Q53 found no complete semantic-subgoal segmentation → boundary summary → prompt-segment replacement → addressable reconstruction path. Its bounded scope covers the previously audited procedure, decision, thread, context, and retrieval routes, targeted segment/subgoal references, and observation-capture producers. This is a scoped negative finding, not an unrestricted repository-wide absence claim.

**Capture completeness remains unverified beyond those paths.** Q53 gives a concrete limitation: [ProceduralMemory.php](../../../src/Core/ProceduralMemory.php), `completePendingLook:1110`, projects status, digest, and byte count into the execution outcome rather than stdout/stderr; `ExecutiveCore::rememberLook:4410` separately retains at most 600 characters of successful output or 200 of error output. Other stores may retain additional bytes, but joining action/run records alone cannot promise exact trajectory restoration. The master answered the bounded implementation question; this analyst did not inspect production source or runtime.

The appropriate transfer is a task-context compiler above existing evidence storage, with a separate task-local archive contract. Native token-memory association learning (I02) is neither the target nor an implementation of HiAgent. Closure also needs care: Q05 shows that current intention completion relies partly on model judgment and that automatic “completed” events can accompany canonical `released` status. A summary's achievement string must not become an additional unverified closure authority.

## Prioritized recommendations and falsifiable gates

**1. Prototype a subgoal-segment context compiler in an isolated evaluation.** Target the decision prompt assembled by `DecisionStateMachine::start` and the final context routing in `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork:6318` (I04, Q14). Give each segment an intention ID, stable segment ID, exact evidence references, temporal boundaries, provisional outcome, and summary. Keep current detail and original evidence; archive successful, failed, or abandoned segments explicitly. Separate facts true at the boundary from earlier events and unresolved constraints. Do not publish these summaries automatically as general semantic knowledge or close intentions from their prose.

Dependencies are an observation-capture audit, explicit boundary semantics, and a summarizer call per closed segment. Compare against current Navi context, decomposition with full history, and equal-budget generic summaries. Include tasks where a later subgoal needs an earlier side effect. Proposed acceptance gate: at least 20% lower total model-token use, with a paired 95% confidence interval excluding a success regression worse than five percentage points. Reject promotion if that gate fails or known-state cases produce additional false achievement claims. These are proposed engineering thresholds, not paper results.

**2. Evaluate bounded, addressable detail recovery as a separate factor.** At the decision proposal/integration boundary, experiment with a typed planning request for a known segment ID, followed by one bounded re-proposal. Validate intention scope, evidence identity, and segment version; expose whether capture is incomplete. This needs a new planning transition because installed `memory.search` is not currently a model-selectable terminal action (I05, Q03). Existing cycle/work IDs and execution guards remain relevant, but do not supply the new transition automatically. Evaluate the complete compression-plus-retrieval candidate even if compression alone fails.

Measure recovery on delayed prerequisite, incorrect-summary, and failed-subgoal cases, comparing compression with and without retrieval under equal total budgets. Record useful restorations, irrelevant restorations, success, and added latency. Require a paired 95% confidence interval above zero for recovery improvement before promotion; reject any task-scope violation. Deterministic retrieval when a summary is explicitly unresolved is a useful control for whether model discretion adds value; restoring an irrelevant segment of equal length controls for extra context alone.

**3. Measure and enforce the complete prompt budget.** Measurement is a prerequisite for the comparisons above. Instrument final prompt assembly at `enqueueWork`, counting the actual backend's tokens after task, workspace, and background context are combined. Charge summary/retrieval inputs and outputs to the same episode; reserve room for one restoration and cap a stalled current segment. Q11 found component limits but no aggregate ordinary-worker input-token enforcement. Q02 warns that existing decision timing fields do not isolate model latency or fully account for asynchronous waits, so correlate queue, inference, integration, and environment completion separately.

Evaluate 30-step tasks first, then substantially longer and interrupted tasks with growing numbers of subgoals. Fail the scalability claim if prompts exceed the declared budget, silent truncation loses required evidence, or total cost/latency eliminates the apparent context saving. Any later second-level summary policy would be a speculative extension beyond this paper and needs its own ablation.

## Verdict and coverage

**Experiment with subgoal-indexed context compression; do not adopt it as a proven general memory upgrade.** The consequential next step is a budget-matched comparison against Navi's existing selective context, with source-preserving segment summaries and independently checked task outcomes. The published example's state inconsistency makes fidelity a first-order acceptance criterion.
