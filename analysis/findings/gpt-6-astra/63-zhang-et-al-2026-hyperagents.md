**Central judgment.** HyperAgents provides credible evidence that an evolved software procedure for constructing agents can remain useful on a new task domain. Its transferable contribution is making that procedure an explicitly versioned, evaluated object. It does not establish unbounded improvement, acceleration per unit compute, or that adding persistent memory produces recursive self-improvement. For Navi-Brain, the appropriate next step is an isolated experiment with competent baselines and independent outcomes; production self-modification is not justified by this evidence.

**Mechanism and assumptions.** A hyperagent combines a task agent, which answers benchmark inputs, and a meta agent, which edits the task agent and its own modification machinery. Foundation-model weights remain frozen; learning occurs in editable Python code, prompts, control flow, tools, and stored artifacts. This removes a specific restriction in the paper's DGM comparison: its fixed, coding-oriented instruction generator. It does not remove dependence on a capable coding model, informative feedback, executable interfaces, and adequate search resources (§§3–4, pp. 4–6; Appendix D.1, p. 35).

DGM-H keeps an archive of executable variants. A selected parent runs its own modification procedure; the resulting child is evaluated and retained if valid, including children that perform worse. This permits later branching from intermediate designs. Main experiments retain a fixed evaluator and parent selector. The selector samples with weight proportional to `sigmoid(10 × (score − top-three mean score)) / (1 + compiled-child count)` (Appendix A.2–A.3, pp. 22–23). The child-count term encourages less-explored parents; it is not a direct estimate of descendant quality or semantic novelty. Task scores only indirectly select good meta procedures, leaving a credit-assignment problem when helpful task edits accompany ineffective meta edits.

The claim that Python can represent any computable machine is about expressivity. It supplies neither a guarantee of discovering that machine nor a bound on time, sample complexity, or future improvement. The paper acknowledges fixed task distributions and outer-loop components (§7, p. 14). Editable parent selection is explored separately, with median combined test scores of 0.491 versus 0.428 for random selection and 0.532 for the handcrafted selector; differences are not significant (Appendix E.5, pp. 57–59). Increasing editability did not establish a better search algorithm.

**What the experiments establish.** The study uses five runs per method, reporting medians and 95% bootstrap intervals from 1,000 resamples (§5, p. 7). Non-coding modification uses Claude 4.5 Sonnet; task evaluation uses GPT-4o for paper decisions, Claude 4.5 Sonnet for reward generation, and o4-mini for math grading. Thus transfer crosses both task and task-model boundaries, while retaining the modification model (Appendix D.1).

The strongest-baseline extension is valuable: starting from ProofAutoGrader yields test accuracy 0.700 versus 0.670, and reported full-benchmark accuracy 0.601 versus 0.561. Figure 10 shows why: correctly identified Almost cases rise from 13 to 43 and Partial cases from 47 to 72, while Correct cases fall from 275 to 257. This is a specific classification tradeoff, not uniform reasoning improvement. The full-benchmark evaluation is not identified as an additional disjoint held-out set (§5.3; Appendix E.4, p. 56).

Several qualifications materially constrain adoption:

- **Initialization and ablation strength.** Initial paper-review and math agents score zero because they fail output-format requirements, not because the models cannot judge papers or proofs. The frozen-meta baseline starts with little more than “Modify any part of the codebase.” The no-archive baseline automatically replaces its parent with the next valid child, including regressions. These comparisons demonstrate benefits over weak starting procedures, but do not show that self-modification and archives are universally necessary. A schema-correct initializer, competent fixed engineering agent, and elitist hill-climber are needed (§4.1; §5.1–5.2; Appendices A.1 and B).
- **Transfer does not isolate the meta component.** Both task and meta implementations are transferred. Transfer agents are selected retrospectively by average discounted descendant improvement, with discount 0.6 and at least three descendants—not by the maximum gain described in the main-text shorthand. Consequently, equal initial zero scores do not imply equivalent starting programs. A task/meta transplant factorial would distinguish meta-procedure benefit from inherited parsing, scaffolding, and shared artifacts (§5.2, pp. 10–11; Appendix D.4, pp. 36–37).
- **Evaluation scope is narrow.** Paper decisions and math grades use random 100/100/100 train/validation/test splits. Conference acceptance prediction measures conformity to recorded decisions, not scientific correctness. Robotics uses six repetitions of walking during optimization and six of torso-height maximization at test, on the same simulated robot—not six distinct training and test tasks or physical deployment. Staged screening can assign zeros to unevaluated cases; joint-domain failure prevents full evaluation in both domains (§4.2; §5.1; Appendix C).
- **Statistical specification is incomplete.** The paper reports Wilcoxon signed-rank significance at `p < 0.05`, without specifying sidedness, approximation, or pairing. For five nonzero paired run differences, the smallest exact two-sided signed-rank p-value is 0.0625. A one-sided or approximate calculation could explain the claims, but the described protocol does not resolve this. Bootstrap intervals from five search runs also leave substantial uncertainty (§5, p. 7).
- **Budget and metric ambiguities matter.** Main text selects the best agent on validation and measures its test gain; Appendix D.3 instead writes a maximum over the same evaluation set used to score the gain. I interpret reported results using the main-text protocol, while flagging the mismatch. Fifty generations need not mean equal compute when procedures can add retries or nested search. Appendix D.2 estimates 33M modification tokens per 100 iterations; its stated `33M + 0.506M × 100 = 88.6M` contains an arithmetic discrepancy—the terms total 83.6M—and does not quantify simulator/RL compute. No cost-normalized acceleration follows.

The generated mechanisms are nevertheless instructive: evaluation summarization, remaining-budget guidance, label-distribution diagnostics, persistent performance records, reusable prompts, and timestamped memory (Appendix E.3, pp. 50–56). Their presence and accompanying trajectories support plausible explanations. No component ablation establishes that memory, templates, or tracking individually caused the transfer gains. Stored “root cause” narratives remain hypotheses about changes, not controlled causal results.

Procedure learning already compiles repeated successful installed-adapter shapes (`ProceduralMemory::observe:399`, [I08](IMPLEMENTATION.md)); it does not rewrite its compiler. Native memory access counts measure retrieval, while no semantic-principle-to-task-outcome utility updater was found ([Q60](IMPLEMENTATION.md)). Neither should be labeled improvement-procedure fitness. External research harnesses and live operational performance remain unverified; no consequential additional implementation question was needed.

**P1 — Establish independent evaluation before evolving the improver.** Propose a new isolated research runner, for example under a future `analysis/experiments/hyperagents/`, using source-revision/hash provenance analogous to `PublicReflection::runOnce`. Define task-agent and meta-agent artifacts separately, immutable evaluator identities, train/validation/test partitions, full change lineage, task outcomes, parser failures, model identity, and actual token/latency cost. Begin with offline evidence-classification or procedure-proposal cases whose expected results can be independently checked. A schema-correct task agent and a competent fixed proposer must be baselines. This requires curated cases and a separate artifact executor; current reviewed proposals cannot execute themselves. Acceptance requires detecting seeded wrong outputs and evaluator tampering in a predeclared integrity set, plus reproducible scoring of saved outputs. If invalid formatting remains the main source of error, repair that baseline before attributing gains to meta-learning.

**P2 — Test transferable meta improvement with controlled transplants.** After P1, allow candidate meta procedures to edit bounded experimental task code and themselves, retaining a fixed evaluator and parent-selection controller. Compare frozen versus evolved meta procedures on identical task-agent starts, then repeat with fresh versus inherited task code on a held-out domain. Include an archive and an elitist single-lineage control; cap both generations and total inference budget. Pre-register a pilot of 20 paired search seeds and validation-selected test gain, separately reporting validity and semantic accuracy. A practical continuation threshold is at least five percentage points of additional held-out accuracy over the competent frozen proposer, with a paired 95% interval excluding zero under matched budgets. Reject the transfer claim if benefit vanishes with matched starting task code or compute. This is a new research subsystem, not a setting change to `CodexSparkWorker::invokeModel` ([I10, Q59](IMPLEMENTATION.md)).

**P3 — Evaluate improvement memory as an intervention.** In that runner, store change ID, parent version, affected task family, observed outcomes, failed hypotheses, and next proposed checks; use `PublicReflection`'s evidence-identity pattern rather than canonical memory writes. Compare no history, raw evaluation history, and synthesized improvement notes with matched context budgets. Appendix E.3 motivates this experiment but does not settle it. Require fewer repeated known failure classes and higher held-out gain per million tokens across independent runs; reject added synthesis if it merely lengthens prompts or increases unsupported causal diagnoses. Keeping source results distinct from inferred lessons also avoids mistaking Navi's existing read reinforcement for measured utility ([I02, Q60](IMPLEMENTATION.md)).

**Risks and verdict.** The paper's own safety model keeps objectives fixed, sandboxes execution, limits resources, and retains human oversight; Appendix F explicitly identifies evaluation gaming and amplification of benchmark biases. Optimizing Navi's closure or proposal-uptake proxies would create a particularly direct route to better-looking records without better task outcomes. Existing interrupt records are also not a universal dispatch stop ([Q23](IMPLEMENTATION.md)); an experiment needs containment independent of those records. These are specific dependencies of executable self-modification, not reasons to suspend ordinary documentation or memory use.

**Verdict: experiment.** Adopt the distinction between task capability and measured agent-generation capability. Prioritize P1, then demand controlled, compute-matched transfer before expanding edit authority. This paper supports a testable engineering hypothesis about reusable improvement procedures; it supplies no evidence of consciousness, unlimited capability growth, or autonomous production readiness.
