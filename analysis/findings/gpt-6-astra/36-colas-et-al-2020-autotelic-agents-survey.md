## Core contribution and mechanism

The useful contribution is an architectural decomposition of autonomous skill acquisition. A system must distinguish **what it can try, what counts as achieving it, how it selects practice, and what improves through practice**. Merely producing novel thoughts satisfies none of these requirements by itself.

The authors distinguish knowledge-based intrinsic motivation, concerning prediction, surprise or information, from competence-based motivation, concerning control of self-selected outcomes (§1, pp. 3–5). Both can support exploration. Learning progress can belong to either family: progress in predicting observations differs from progress in achieving goals.

A goal is the pair \(g=(z_g,R_g)\): a compact representation and a goal-achievement function (§2.2, p. 7). Its meaning depends both on how achievement is evaluated and on the policy that acts to achieve it. A skill associates a goal with a policy; a repertoire associates a goal space with a goal-conditioned policy. The underlying problem is a reward-free MDP \(\{S,A,T,\rho_0\}\), with the agent constructing its own learning signals (§3.1, p. 9). “Reward-free” therefore describes the environmental task specification, not an absence of engineered objectives or internal rewards.

The RL-IMGEP framework separates four learning problems: goal embeddings, support of the goal distribution, sampling priorities, and goal-conditioned reward functions (§3.3, pp. 11–13; Figure 2). Algorithm 1 resets the environment, observes initial context, samples a goal, executes a goal-conditioned policy, stores transitions, relabels experience in hindsight, recomputes rewards, and updates policy and goal-related modules. Hindsight allows a trajectory unsuccessful for its intended goal to teach achievement of another goal; it requires enough recorded evidence to evaluate that alternative reward. Episodic resets and adequate transition observations are substantive transfer assumptions for a persistent executive operating through delayed tools.

The survey's operational definition is deliberately broad: selecting one's own goals suffices for its minimal “autotelic” category, even with predefined embeddings, support and rewards (§3.3, pp. 11–12). Learning all components is a stronger achievement, and few reviewed systems do so. This distinction prevents treating complete autonomous representation learning as a prerequisite for every useful experiment—or treating an internal sampler as proof of it.

Goal types include choices among objectives, target state features, abstract binary predicates, and weighted objective mixtures (§4, pp. 13–16). Language-based predicates fit Navi's tasks better than Euclidean distance between arbitrary text embeddings. The support question remains separate: a well-formed description need not denote an attainable outcome.

For practice allocation, §6.1 (pp. 20–22) contrasts intermediate difficulty, novelty/diversity and medium-term competence progress. A stochastic goal may succeed half the time while offering nothing learnable. Absolute learning progress instead favors regions whose success changes, including deteriorating skills worth relearning. The survey reports regional bandit selection and, in one cited system, the priority \((1-\mathrm{success\ rate})\times\mathrm{absolute\ learning\ progress}\). These are proxies for long-term improvement, not proven optimal objectives.

## Evidence, limits and disagreements

This is a conceptual and narrative survey, not a new benchmark or controlled comparison. Table 1 (p. 27) classifies architectures by goal type, learned representation/reward and sampling strategy; it contains no common performance measurements. Figure 3 (p. 17) illustrates heterogeneous environments, from mazes and manipulation to language interaction and procedural worlds. Statements about hindsight's sample efficiency, IMAGINE's generalization, or uniform difficulty outperforming GoalGAN summarize cited work (§§2.5, 5.4, 6.1); the local paper supplies no pooled effect sizes, uncertainty estimates or matched-budget replication. Its taxonomy is stronger evidence than any implied universal ranking of methods.

Its evaluation discussion is particularly valuable (§3.2, pp. 9–11): measure exploration, held-out goals, downstream transfer, internal representations and robustness, while acknowledging that human judgments influence which outcomes seem interesting. Downstream performance is an evaluation proxy, not necessarily the discovery objective. An evolving self-generated goal set must not become its own sole success criterion: changing what is attempted can improve reported success without improving capability.

Two claims deserve qualification. Section 5.4 (p. 19) says the described goal generators remain within the convex hull of experienced goals. That is not a general mathematical restriction on generative models or predefined spaces. The defensible concern is insufficient exploration beyond observed effects, not a universal convex-hull theorem. Likewise, §7.1 (pp. 22–23) discusses Markov requirements as a difficulty for time-extended goals, but explicitly permits augmented state or reward functions with memory. Navi's trajectory constraints do not require pretending every goal is an instantaneous state target.

Absolute progress also has unresolved estimator risks: finite-sample noise, changing goal difficulty and evaluator drift can produce apparent progress. Repeated forgetting and relearning can consume budget without expanding retained capability. Increasing goal entropy is not sufficient either. Under the identity \(I(Z;S)=H(Z)-H(Z\mid S)\) (§6.1, p. 21), high mutual information also depends on reducing uncertainty about goals given achieved states; varied text labels or token associations establish neither controllability nor empowerment.

The “curious scientist” and socio-cultural development discussion (§§7–8, pp. 23–26) is a research vision. The survey does not establish general scientific reasoning, human-like motivation, indefinite open-ended development, or consciousness.

Existing execution, memory and provenance are useful foundations. Its scope combines PHP/C term searches in `src` and `memories/src` with focused motivation, intention, procedure and thread inspection. Procedure counters are real histories, but do not supply this loop. The paper-specific question is resolved; external training, deployed behavior and performance remain unverified.

## Prioritized recommendations

**P0 — Establish a bounded, versioned goal-achievement contract before changing motivation.** Following §§2.2 and 4.3, prototype a goal record with typed parameters, evaluator identity/version, admissible context, required observations and an achieved/not-achieved/unknown result. Join evaluations to existing action/procedure evidence; likely integration points are `ExecutiveCore::finishAction:2281`, completion handling, and the intention model (I05, Q05, Q29). Freeze evaluator versions within a measurement period. Keep descriptive success text for communication, but do not use model confidence, artifact counts or `released` as reward.

Start in an isolated retrieval task with known relevant record IDs: “retrieve both supporting records” succeeds only if both are returned, even though the existing adapter contract accepts zero results. Retrieval is a planning adapter, so this experiment does not assume the current terminal-action selector dispatches it (I05). Include missing observations, stale evidence, and successful calls with wrong results. **Validation:** require correct deterministic verdicts on all specified cases, with unknown never credited as success. Reject the contract if irrelevant successful calls or closure metadata increase competence. Cost is evaluator design and outcome/context capture; most open-ended intentions should remain unevaluable until a valid measurement exists.

**P1 — Compare competence-progress practice allocation against simple samplers.** After P0, estimate changes in success on comparable instances within a few fixed goal families. Use separate early/recent windows and uncertainty-aware shrinkage; keep a uniform exploration fraction so temporarily impossible goals can be revisited after prerequisite learning. A bandit over families follows §6.1; adding cost normalization and a noise correction is proposed engineering synthesis. Implement an experimental sampler alongside `MotivationCompiler`, consuming achievement records rather than modifying need pressure. A future dispatch connection must be explicit because prompt ranking alone is not action selection (Q31).

Use identical initial resources and learner/update rules across uniform, novelty, intermediate-difficulty and absolute-progress conditions. Include learnable goals, mastered goals, impossible goals, stochastic uncontrollable outcomes and a controlled forgetting event. Retain a fixed held-out evaluation distribution distinct from sampled practice. **Validation:** predeclare a minimum five-percentage-point held-out success gain at equal total budget, with a paired 95% confidence interval excluding zero, as the pilot advancement criterion. These are proposed thresholds, not paper results. Reject if improvements disappear under fixed-difficulty evaluation, stochastic distractors retain priority, or mastery loss grows. Count evaluator and model costs; also freeze learning in an ablation to distinguish selecting easier tasks from acquiring skills.

**P2 — Pilot evidence-preserving hindsight and bounded goal composition.** Algorithm 1 and §§5.4/7.3 motivate extracting achieved predicates from recorded outcomes and proposing new combinations. For the retrieval example, failing to retrieve both records might supply evidence for the narrower goal of retrieving one specified record. Preserve the original failure and add a separately evaluated alternative-goal annotation. This is retrospective learning evidence, not successful execution of the original request.

Prototype beside `ProceduralMemory::observe` and the action ledgers (Q06/Q27/Q29). Do not route a failed action into the existing success-streak compiler by changing its label. Require fresh verified re-execution before admitting an alternative reusable procedure. Begin with a typed finite grammar and evaluator-composable conjunctions; an LLM can suggest candidates, but wording does not establish reachability. General conjunctions of reachable atomic goals need not be jointly reachable. Cost includes richer outcome capture, deduplication and prospective replay; this is a transfer experiment, not a drop-in HER implementation.

**Validation:** compare no hindsight, verified hindsight, and verified hindsight plus composition at equal budget, evaluating repeatable achievement on held-out combinations. Reject if only the number of goal descriptions grows, if stochastic accidents become claimed skills, or if original failure records/commitments change. Keep this subordinate to P0/P1: more generated goals are useful only when their achievement and learning value can be measured.

## Verdict

**Adopt the decomposition; experiment with the curriculum; defer a full autotelic-RL rewrite.** Navi-Brain already has substantial goal, outcome, memory and procedural machinery. The next useful step is a small independently scored goal family that separates operational success from actual achievement. Only then can competence-progress selection demonstrate added capability rather than reward its own bookkeeping.
