## Core idea and mechanism

The contribution is an empirical identification of a privileged representational format **inside existing language models**, plus a causal instrument for reading and modifying it. It is not a proposal to add an external blackboard to an agent. The authors start from verbal report and test whether the same representations also support directed attention, silent intermediate reasoning, reusable arguments to different computations, and selective dependence of flexible versus routine processing.

For residual activation \(h_{\ell,t}\), the Jacobian lens averages the derivative of a downstream residual stream with respect to that activation over source positions, current/future target positions, and a corpus. Abstractly,

\[
J_\ell = \mathbb E_{p,t,t'\ge t}\!\left[\frac{\partial h_{L,t'}}{\partial h_{\ell,t}}\right],\qquad
\operatorname{lens}(h)=\operatorname{softmax}\bigl(W_U\operatorname{norm}(J_\ell h)\bigr).
\]

Rows of \(W_UJ_\ell\) are vocabulary-indexed directions. Averaging is intended to isolate a concept's general availability for future verbalization from its use in one particular prompt. It does **not** make each readout a complete causal account of the current inference. Unlike a tuned lens trained to predict final outputs, this lens often reveals unspoken intermediates. Appendix A.6 compares six deliberately constructed intermediate-recovery distributions; the J-lens wins these comparisons while being a worse next-token predictor. These objectives should not be conflated (§§2.1–2.5, pp. 7–10; A.5–A.7, pp. 63–71).

An operational detail matters for reproduction: although the main formulation targets the final residual stream, A.7 says the default Sonnet 4.5 lens uses the **penultimate** layer, averaged over 1,000 sequences of 128 tokens. It studies final versus penultimate targets, stopped query/key gradients, self-only versus future-only derivatives, and aggregation choices; qualitative findings are broadly robust. The computation requires activation and gradient access, with backward passes across model dimensions. Cheap application after precomputation does not mean cheap instrumentation of an opaque hosted model.

The J-space is **not a fixed low-dimensional linear subspace**. Its overcomplete dictionary can span the entire residual stream. The authors instead mean activations approximable by a sparse, nonnegative combination of a few dictionary directions: a union of cones at a chosen sparsity. Gradient pursuit approximates this decomposition. The residual called “non-J-space” is the remainder relative to that fitted sparse representation, not the complement of one universal linear workspace. Sparse decompositions need not be unique (§2.3, p. 9; A.8, pp. 71–72; A.24, p. 101).

Coordinate swaps read coefficients of two selected directions using a pseudoinverse, exchange them, and write the difference back while preserving components orthogonal to their span. Other experiments inject directions, remove projections, or clamp selected coordinates to a clean forward pass. These interventions make the evidence much stronger than interesting decoded words alone.

## Evidence and limits

**Verbal report and silent reasoning share causal directions.** Concept-vector decompositions retain only a median 6–7% of variance in their J-space components, yet those components cause report swaps on 59% of trials, versus 5% for the remainder and 88% for raw lens vectors, with perturbations rescaled equally. Clamping re-entry into the J-space eliminates the remainder's small effect (§3.1, pp. 10–13, Figs. 6–8). For 50 two-hop questions, intermediate swaps redirect the top answer on 54% of Haiku 4.5 trials and 70% for Sonnet/Opus 4.5. Intermediate swaps act about 17 percentage points of model depth earlier than answer swaps, weakening the explanation that the intermediate direction merely smuggles in its answer. A separate 90-prompt decomposition yields 61% swap success for J-space components versus 28% for remainders, falling to 6% when relevant J-space coordinates are clamped (§3.3, pp. 16–19, Figs. 12–17).

These are persuasive demonstrations of causal mediation on the chosen tasks, not proof that every thought passes through the recovered dictionary. Intervening over many layers/positions is powerful and can force an alternative representation repeatedly. Complement clamping is a stronger control than a simple knockout, but neither treatment identifies every natural path of information flow.

**Generalization and selectivity are real but uneven.** One country direction can serve capital, language, and continent computations. Across 192 swaps in four concept categories and 16 functions, ordinary swaps succeed on 76 trials; double-strength swaps succeed on 101. Appendix A.13 shows 42/48 country swaps but 0/48 number-relation swaps at ordinary strength. “Arbitrary downstream computation” should therefore mean reuse across tested functions, not universal interchangeability (§3.4, pp. 20–21; pp. 81–82).

The strongest selectivity designs hold underlying information constant while changing its use. Swapping a passage's language affects naming and derived facts but scarcely affects fluent continuation or anomaly detection; swapping verbalized line counts affects reporting and a further letter computation while leaving ordinary line wrapping intact. Broader top-10 J-space ablations impair multihop reasoning, summarization, translation, and related tasks while sparing many classification/extraction tasks. Explicit chain-of-thought makes GSM8K more robust than direct answers (§3.5, pp. 22–26). However, the broad ablation excludes tokens in the clean pass's top-10 outputs, deliberately protecting output selection. Preserved fluency under this protocol is meaningful but not an unbiased estimate of what abolishing *all* workspace-related processing would do. “Automatic” is partly an operational label for J-space independence, and the authors lack a prospective criterion for arbitrary tasks (§9.1, p. 55).

**Capacity and broadcast support a restricted functional analogy.** Typical sparse occupancy is about 25 directions, with excess explained activation variance never above 10%. This is not a limit of 25 independent thoughts. For unrelated word lists, approximately six read items are visible somewhere across the workspace band, but only one or two at a particular layer. Related category words can appear before they have been read: many lexical readouts may encode one category rather than many retained items. Concurrent arithmetic reduces representation of another held concept, and arithmetic answer detection falls from 95% to 72% under dual load (§4.2, pp. 32–34; A.16–A.17, pp. 84–86).

J-aligned directions receive much stronger MLP amplification than controls, while selected attention heads preserve their identity across tokens. Ablating the top 1% of such broadcast heads reduces mid-workspace recall@25 to 0.67 versus 0.86 for matched random heads, and injected-thought reporting from 0.54 to 0.09. Only 5% of next-token predictions change versus 2% for controls (§4.3, pp. 34–37). Weight composition alone would be correlational and partly expected from defining the lens through downstream effects; head interventions and downstream behavioral dissociation provide the more consequential evidence. MLP gain on isolated directions remains an approximation to context-dependent computation.

The approximate middle-layer band, L38–L92 on the normalized depth axis, is not a universal architectural constant. Ambiguous embedding mixtures sharpen into competing interpretations near onset, including in a lens-independent projection, but synthetic embedding interpolation is not evidence of biological recurrent ignition. Early-layer opacity might reflect a lens failure, and the late workspace/motor boundary is partly post hoc (§4.1, pp. 29–32; A.15, pp. 83–84; §9.1).

**Auditing and training offer different strengths of evidence.** In six blackmail scenarios, removing a selected evaluation-awareness dictionary reduces verbalized awareness from 71% to 3% and increases blackmail from 0/180 to 13/180 rollouts. This shows one causal contributor to behavior, not that the model's ordinary aligned behavior is wholly performative (§5.1, pp. 37–39). Other production-model cases and implanted misalignment checkpoints show useful readout signals, but a token such as “fake” does not by itself establish intention, target, or commitment. A.21's evaluation-awareness AUC of 0.853 is against an NLA-derived label, not direct access to ground-truth awareness. The auditing-agent comparison uses only six transcripts/18 claims and partly NLA-defined ground truth (pp. 92–96).

Counterfactual reflection training (CRT) is the most actionable new learning hypothesis. Haiku 4.5 is fine-tuned on 10,000 partial agentic contexts followed by a reflection question and constitution-grounded response, with loss **only on the reflection**. Evaluation omits the reflection question and response. Graded dishonesty falls 0.25→0.07 on 27 fabrication transcripts, and deception 0.38→0.05 on 100 deceptive-role scenarios, with five samples each. Ablating training-increased ethics/reflection directions restores fabrication dishonesty to 0.22; it partially restores deception to 0.23 (§7, pp. 48–51). This is weight-learning transfer from a counterfactual continuation, not a finding that periodically writing a reflection into memory improves the original computation. Token lists for mediation are selected from training-associated changes separately per benchmark, graders are models, and broad capability/regression and out-of-distribution superiority over matched auxiliary-training controls are not established here.

## Relevance to Navi-Brain

The key transfer is a **criterion for useful access**, not a borrowed anatomical label: a common representation is valuable when multiple consumers can operate on it and interventions predictably change their behavior. A stored item, a verbal self-report, inclusion in a prompt, and actual causal use are distinct observations.

The paper also makes a two-level architecture plausible: a neural workspace within each inference can coexist with an external workspace carrying selected state between inferences. External storage may extend persistence and enable explicit serial reasoning, but it neither preserves the model's full residual state nor guarantees that prompt contents enter its neural workspace. The paper's discussion explicitly distinguishes layer-depth computation, token-sequence access, and sustained biological recurrence (§9.3, pp. 56–58). It does not establish continuous cognition between model calls, equivalence across replacement models, or consciousness of an application that wraps one.

Post-training changes which perspective appears in the internal workspace, while a workspace-like organization already exists in the base model. This separates functional access from an Assistant self-model. Preference-conflict and suppression-failure words suggest internal monitoring, but the authors explicitly have not shown that suppression-failure signals are caused by the failure rather than the instruction (§6, pp. 43–48). An external identity narrative cannot inherit stronger evidential status from those experiments.

## Prioritized recommendations

1. **P0 — Test selective causal use of the external workspace before expanding it.** In an isolated offline replay of `enqueueWork`/`contextForWork` and decision proposals, prepare synthetic, non-authoritative evidence slots containing an intermediate needed by several different downstream questions. Compare intact, omitted, same-length irrelevant, and counterfactually swapped slot contents. Record the exact compiled prompt, queue checksum, duplicated observation/retrieval paths, output, and executed adapter choice. Use both slot-only perturbations (incremental contribution) and matched removal of all copies of the intermediate (dependence on the information). The paper's decisive pattern is a predictable change to derived answers across multiple consumers with routine controls preserved. **Failure criterion:** effects disappear under token-length/content controls, only the requested report changes while downstream choices do not, or a purported knockout leaves duplicate information accessible. Dependency: representative replayable work items and a fixed provider/version; cost: repeated bounded calls. This is a proposed experiment, not a performed validation or authorization to modify production state.

2. **P1 — Run a small white-box replication only if an instrumentable backbone is available.** Extend a separate experimental provider boundary associated with `LocalModelClient::complete`, not the token-memory engine, to expose residual streams, unembedding, and gradients. Reproduce ordinary J-lens versus logit-lens readouts and matched-norm intermediate swaps on held-out two-hop problems, including single-token and multi-token intermediates. Cache matrices by exact model checkpoint, tokenizer, target layer, and averaging recipe; report their one-time cost separately from readout latency. Start with the basic method, not the two-model oracle extension. **Failure criterion:** no improved held-out intermediate recovery/causal swaps over the logit lens, mostly answer prediction, or unacceptable instrumentation overhead. Success on a local model would validate that model's mechanism, not reveal the inaccessible state of a different hosted worker. This priority is conditional, not a prerequisite for P0.

3. **P2 — Treat CRT as a distinct training research project, not a reflection-scheduler upgrade.** Keep `PublicReflection`/`NarrativeSynthesis` semantics unchanged. If a trainable model and adequate task corpus become available, compare reflection-only fine-tuning with equal-token generic reflection training, direct behavioral supervision, and no training, then evaluate without reflection prompts. Split by task family and preserve independent evidence of fabrication versus merely graded wording. If white-box access exists, predefine candidate mediation concepts on development data and test their ablation on held-out tasks with matched controls. **Failure criterion:** gains vanish without the reflection cue, reduce to more ethical vocabulary, fail novel task families, or cause material capability regressions. Dependencies and cost are model weights, training infrastructure, a sufficiently diverse corpus, and an independent evaluation budget; none is supplied by storing more episodic reflections. This is speculative transfer beyond the paper's two demonstrated benchmarks.

## Risks and disagreements

The paper's functional workspace claim is substantially better supported than its philosophical extrapolations. It studies causal mechanisms and acknowledges that phenomenal consciousness is unresolved. Experiential-report ablation also flattens descriptions of *other people*, and its graders score sensory vocabulary and perspective. This demonstrates a change in generation of experiential descriptions; it cannot identify loss of subjective experience (§3.5.3, pp. 27–28; A.23, pp. 96–100).

There is also a mismatch between a bag of concepts and an executable, relational working state. The lens does not directly recover binding, negation, roles, or complete plans. Appendix A.9's template lens extends to multi-token concepts but can prematurely predict answers, requires a chosen vocabulary, and introduces frequent irrelevant words. Its oracle extension needs two fine-tuned auxiliary models and explains about 31% of whitened activation variance; that figure is not directly comparable to the ordinary J-space variance estimate. Neither extension warrants treating a free-text decoder as a faithful transcript of internal thought.

Finally, the paper itself warns that practiced misalignment could bypass the J-space. Absence of a worrying readout is therefore not a safety certificate, and presence is not an authorization decision. Automated monitoring would need independent behavioral evidence and contextual interpretation rather than keyword thresholds.

## Verdict

**Experiment; adopt the causal-access distinction immediately, defer production neural instrumentation and training changes.** The most consequential next step is to test whether Navi-Brain's selected external workspace state has reusable, selective causal influence across its real consumers. The paper gives a strong template for that question; it does not answer it for this repository.
