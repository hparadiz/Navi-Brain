**Central finding.** Adopt the paper's insistence on inspectable cognitive functions and independently checked epistemic artifacts. Treat its unified architecture as a research agenda. Its strongest transfer is to Navi's existing executive and workspace; its formal objectives are insufficient foundations for a memory compressor or curiosity controller.

**Core idea and mechanism.** The report reorganizes world-model research around six functions attributed to cognitive architecture theory: memory, perception, language, reasoning, imagination, and motivation. It adds metacognition as monitoring and control over those functions. Classification concerns what a work *innovates*, rather than everything it implements; a second researcher confirms assignments (§2.2, pp. 6–7).

The proposed framework combines bounded multimodal representation, persistent state, language interfaces, action-conditioned transitions, hypothetical rollouts, reward-based selection, and metacognitive routing (§3, pp. 8–17; Fig. 3). Equations (5)–(6) express familiar model-based planning: predict future latent states under candidate actions and select a trajectory by expected cumulative reward. This assumes the representation retains decision-relevant information, transitions remain accurate over the planning horizon, and rewards capture the intended outcome. The paper does not establish these assumptions for a unified implementation.

Its distinctive extension is **Epistemic World Models**: literature, datasets, hypotheses, and experimental outputs form an explicit knowledge state; retrieval, analysis, and tool operations change that state. A shared workspace makes intermediate artifacts available to specialized generation and evaluation processes (§6, pp. 35–41; Fig. 6). This is a useful systems perspective for research assistance. The transferable mechanism is a traceable propose–evaluate–revise loop, not an obligation to instantiate every specialist as another model process.

**Evidence and limits.** This is a narrative survey and conceptual synthesis, not a new trained model, controlled cognition experiment, or proof of architectural sufficiency. Table 1 compares survey coverage; Tables 2–4 classify contributions. The report repeats individual systems' measurements—for example, over 65% lower one-second LiDAR prediction Chamfer distance for Copilot4D (§5.1, p. 29) and a 7.4% FVD improvement for Epona (p. 31). These are heterogeneous, secondary reports of geometry and video quality; they do not measure the benefit of combining all seven functions or transfer to Navi.

The classification procedure is partly specified, but there is no reported reproducible search/inclusion protocol, agreement statistic, or uncertainty analysis for the category assignments. Moreover, a cross means “not a contribution,” not “capability absent.” Inferring a field-wide capability deficit from sparse innovation columns therefore exceeds the table's semantics. Table 4 also mixes implemented systems, benchmarks, position papers, surveys, and a product announcement—reference [126] is explicitly an announcement (p. 49)—without separating their evidential weights.

The strongest absence claims are internally unstable. The introduction and §2.2 assert no demonstrated metacognitive capability (pp. 2, 6), while Table 2 checks metacognition for RLVR-World (p. 19), and §6 attributes functional metacognition to several epistemic systems. “Intrinsic motivation is underdeveloped” is defensible as a judgment; its broad exclusion is harder to reconcile with the report's own Plan2Explore and InDRiVE entries (pp. 27, 29, 33). Designer-selected objectives can still operationalize intrinsic information seeking. The relevant questions are what drives behavior and whether that behavior improves learning.

**Two consequential mathematical problems.** These are visible in the PDF, not extraction artifacts.

First, Eqs. (3)–(4) minimize conditional mutual information between history and representation given future reward or reward trajectory (§3.1, p. 10). Even interpreting `min` charitably as choosing a minimizing representation, a constant representation achieves zero conditional mutual information. No prediction-loss constraint or reward-sufficiency requirement rules it out. Conditioning on a longer reward trajectory does not fix this degeneracy or establish “traversability.” Likewise, the upper information bound in Eqs. (1)–(2) constrains retained information without guaranteeing useful retention. A practical objective needs an explicit predictive/task distortion constraint or competing relevance term; these equations cannot justify deleting Navi's evidence to achieve compression.

Second, Eqs. (9)–(10) present the recognizable variational-free-energy bound, but Eq. (11) labels a positive KL between a policy-conditioned future-state distribution and a state prior “information gain,” then minimizes it (§3.3, p. 15). That divergence is not the expected posterior change after a future observation. Minimizing a positive information-gain term would also oppose information acquisition. A risk interpretation may be possible, but would require additional definitions and terms to recover the claimed epistemic behavior. For example, equally preferred observation channels can have different diagnostic value while leaving the displayed state-marginal KL unchanged. The paper supplies no derivation resolving this issue. Do not implement Eq. (11) as written.

**Architectural disagreement.** A changing workspace is a stateful process; it does not by itself establish a world model that predicts consequences of alternative interventions. Section 3.3's assertion that a changing chat history meets the traditional definition (p. 17) conflates represented state, state update, and predictive model. For Navi, reserve the stronger claim for action-conditioned predictions that can fail against independent observations. Similarly, inspectable text is not necessarily faithful introspection. The report explicitly distinguishes functional from biological emulation (p. 6), yet elsewhere equates metacognition with consciousness (p. 5). Its survey supports no such equivalence. More modalities, critics, or routed experts also consume bandwidth and inference time; the claim that the proposals need not cost one another (§7, p. 42) overlooks those shared resource constraints.

**Prioritized recommendations.** These are proposed isolated evaluations and potential extensions, not changes performed in this review. Thresholds below are suggested preregistered acceptance rules, not paper results.

1. **P0 — Evaluate a correction-aware epistemic state contract.** Build an offline prototype alongside `DecisionStateMachine::finishVerified:743` and `WorkingMemory::publish`, with source/artifact version, observation ID, preceding action ID, proposed state delta, and assertion status distinguishing observation, hypothesis, and retraction. Consult Q54 for the missing recurrent contract and Q35 for missing dependent-claim reconsideration. This makes §6's evolving knowledge workspace operational. Start with bounded document tasks containing source corrections, conflicting versions, and unsupported model assertions; compare current carryforward with the typed update gate at equal context budget. Require at least a 50% relative reduction in stale-claim reuse and no more than a two-percentage-point loss on unchanged-source tasks. Fail if corrections merely append contradictory text, or hypotheses enter the observation ledger. Cost: annotated version histories and a dependency index; no latent-model training required.

2. **P1 — Measure the marginal value of metacognitive checking.** Prototype a bounded accept/reject/retrieve-again decision before candidate integration, using the existing queue and source-validation boundaries (`ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork`, `DecisionStateMachine::integrate`, `integrateConsolidation`; I04, I08, Q14). Compare no critique, same-model critique, and independently evidence-grounded checking on claims with known support and injected plausible errors. Hold total token/tool budgets equal and score blinded claim correctness, false acceptance, false rejection, and latency. Accept only if the 95% confidence interval for correctness improvement excludes zero while false rejection rises by at most two percentage points. Ablate access to decisive evidence across *all* duplicated prompt routes. This evaluates the propose–judge separation advocated on pp. 39–41; fluent criticism and increased workspace traffic are insufficient. Cost is additional checking and adjudication, with correlated model errors the main risk.

3. **P2 — Experiment with action-conditioned information seeking.** Following the intrinsic-motivation agenda, specify competing hypotheses and predicted observation distributions for a small set of authorized evidence queries. Estimate expected posterior information gain and query cost explicitly; do not reuse Eq. (11), access counts, or raw surprise. An isolated selector could later connect to `DecisionStateMachine::integrate` and need satisfaction, where Q03/Q20 establish the missing objective. Compare fixed allocation, random queries, surprise selection, and gain-per-cost selection in learnable, already-known, and irreducibly noisy regions. Require improved held-out predictive log loss per query and no sustained preference for noise after the initial sampling budget. Failure includes uncertainty reports improving without prediction improvement. This is speculative synthesis requiring likelihood calibration and outcome labels, not evidence for human-like motivation.

4. **P2 — Validate compression by delayed evidence recovery.** Before applying §3.1 to memory, vary capsule budgets and retained fields around `CapsuleAssembler::assemble:101` and `WorkingMemory::contextForWork:223` (I03, Q14). Compare ordinary selection with versions preserving decisive source identifiers and unresolved hypotheses, using tasks whose answer becomes clear only after later evidence. Require lower token use at noninferior delayed-answer accuracy within a predefined two-percentage-point margin, with source-trace recovery measured separately. Failure is a compact, coherent answer that cannot recover its decisive evidence. Cost is an annotated delayed-query set. Keep the resident token architecture: this survey supplies no result favoring its replacement by neural latent compression.

**Verdict: adopt the vocabulary cautiously; experiment with the mechanisms; reject direct adoption of the formal objectives.** The consequential next step is an isolated correction-aware workspace evaluation, followed by evidence that selective checking improves decisions per unit cost. Navi already has much of the external scaffolding described here. The missing evidence concerns accurate revision, causal predictive use, and measured benefit—not whether its components can be assigned cognitive names.
