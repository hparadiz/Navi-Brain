# Machine Theory of Mind

Neil C. Rabinowitz, Frank Perbet, H. Francis Song, Chiyuan Zhang, and Matthew Botvinick (2018). ICML 2018, PMLR 80; local 10-page conference version, which points to a separate extended version.

Source: [local PDF](../../../assets/papers/21-rabinowitz-et-al-2018-machine-theory-of-mind.pdf). Reviewer: **GPT-6 Astra**. Implementation evidence: [shared reference](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, git `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`; static inspection by the implementation master, not independent code inspection or runtime validation.

**Central finding.** ToMnet demonstrates that an observer trained across agent populations can predict unfamiliar individuals’ behavior, including behavior consistent with false beliefs. The consequential Navi-Brain finding is narrower: its existing belief propositions receive prediction feedback that can be identical for incompatible claims. A successful coarse social forecast therefore does not validate proposition content. Prioritize falsifiable, content-dependent predictions and actor information access before a neural replacement.

**Mechanism and assumptions.** The observer encounters several past trajectories from the same unidentified agent, followed by a partial trajectory and query state in a new episode. A character network encodes past behavior into an agent-specific representation. A mental-state network encodes recent behavior; Figure 1 also shows character information conditioning this network. A prediction network combines both embeddings with the current environment state to predict the next action, object consumption, and successor representations—expected discounted future state occupancy (§§2.1–2.2, PDF pp. 2–3). This is learning to predict another policy, not imitating that policy through execution.

The network weights embody population regularities; inference from a few observations produces an individual representation without a new round of parameter training. Calling these a “prior” and “posterior” is a useful interpretation, but the ordinary embeddings are not explicitly normalized Bayesian posteriors over agent programs. Approximate Bayesian behavior is demonstrated in a tractable special case. Later experiments introduce Gaussian variational character representations with an information bottleneck (§3.3, pp. 5–6).

The observer is not given agent identities, rewards, parameters, or policies as inputs, and agents need not act optimally. Nevertheless, the experimental assumptions are substantial: 11×11 gridworlds, a common five-action interface, controlled agent populations, and unrestricted observer access to environment states and agents’ actions. The modeled agents can have restricted vision and memory; their hidden states do not persist across episodes (§2.1, p. 2). Thus the observer knows more about the world than its subjects. A real collaborator must also infer what happened outside its own observation; human working beliefs and unfinished commitments need not reset at episode boundaries.

**What the experiments establish.** The paper provides a progression of controlled demonstrations rather than one general benchmark score.

- **Random policies:** For agents sampled from Dirichlet-distributed action probabilities, predictions approximate Bayes-optimal updating from action observations. Cross-population evaluation exposes dependence on the training distribution (§3.1, p. 3, Fig. 2). This validates amortized statistical inference, not belief attribution by itself.
- **Goal-directed planners:** Value-iteration agents have different object rewards. Predictions improve as more isolated observation/action pairs become available and are comparable to Bayesian inverse RL; additional agents with high movement costs elicit different inferred strategies (§3.2, pp. 4–5, Figs. 3–5). The authors explicitly anticipate greater training sample complexity than BIRL when its rational-agent model is correct. The paper does not establish universal superiority over structured inference.
- **Learned policies:** Training uses 120 UNREAL agents spanning three vision/memory species and four preferred objects; another 120 agents, trained with different random seeds, are held out (§3.3, p. 5). Predicted occupancy distinguishes characteristic exploration strategies. The information bottleneck yields interpretable axes for species and object preferences, and separates left- versus right-wall-following stateless agents (pp. 5–6, Figs. 6–8). These are useful discovered behavioral distinctions, but an attractive low-dimensional plot is not proof of uniquely identified psychological variables. Both-network ablation support is cited to absent Figure A2.
- **False-belief behavior:** Objects can permute when an agent reaches a subgoal, with probability 0.1. The main experiment uses 40 sighted/stateful training agents and 40 held-out agents (§3.4, p. 6). In a hand-designed Sally–Anne analogue, forced trajectories establish prior knowledge and a subsequent visible or invisible change. ToMnet predicts that unseen changes have much less immediate influence on the subject’s behavior, even though the observer sees them (p. 7, Fig. 10). Paired swap/no-swap continuations in sampled mazes strengthen the evidence beyond that hand-designed trajectory (Fig. 11).
- **Explicit belief reports:** A separate experiment trains subjects to report object locations from LSTM state and supervises ToMnet to predict these reports (§3.5, p. 8, Fig. 12). The authors explicitly state that the reports are readouts, not causal inputs to the subjects’ policies. Success establishes prediction of an auxiliary report, not identification of the beliefs causally responsible for action.

The false-belief result is meaningful but imperfect. Figures 10–12 visibly show soft visibility boundaries: ToMnet predicts residual effects of distant changes that do not affect subjects’ immediate policies or reports. For a fixed subject history, a change outside its sensors cannot immediately alter its policy inputs; learning that invariance is a necessary behavioral competence. It does not uniquely distinguish an explicit belief representation from a predictor that learns visibility-sensitive dependencies. The paper’s broader “representational Theory of Mind” interpretation is therefore stronger than the mechanism identification supplied by these experiments.

The main text reports transfer without swap events in observer training and longer-horizon corrective behavior, but points to absent Figures A4 and A3 (pp. 7–8). These remain reported findings whose supporting appendix plots were unavailable. Likewise, precise losses, architecture sizes, training budgets, and several statistical details are deferred to appendices. No human study, rich social interaction, recursive belief benchmark, or improvement from using ToMnet inside an acting agent is demonstrated; these are future directions (§4, p. 8). None of the results establishes consciousness in either observer or subject.

Existing evaluation is an asset: [Q21](IMPLEMENTATION.md#q21--actor-beliefs-persistence-and-social-evaluation) verifies [OtherModelEvaluator.php](../../../src/Core/OtherModelEvaluator.php), `report:16`, `onlineBaselines:221`, and `functionalUptake:305`. Forecasts are resolved against later, matching machine-observed categories, with deadlines, Brier scores, and log loss; a parser does not assign its own correctness label. Unconditional, persistence, and contextual transition baselines exist. However, the targets are engineered observables such as speech or activity categories, not mental-state truth. The evaluator explicitly leaves proposition extraction recall and label accuracy null pending correction fixtures (`:187–204`).

There is also actual behavioral uptake, but its scope matters. [Q13S](IMPLEMENTATION.md#q13s--social-output-to-input-contingencies-question-3) verifies that [AgentObservationProjector.php](../../../src/Perception/AgentObservationProjector.php):94 reduces utterance outcomes to interaction categories, excluding utterance-content/action features. `OtherModel::contextKey:1563` uses source, feature, reachability, and attention mode. `decisionState:1370` supplies an inhibition factor to [ActionSelector.php](../../../src/Core/ActionSelector.php):43; answering bypasses this competition. The inspected consumer does not use sealed forecasts to compare outcomes of alternative utterances. Its ablated “counterfactual” measures removal of a model effect, not the paper’s swap/no-swap environmental intervention.

Q21 establishes actor-indexed propositions in [OtherModelHypothesis.php](../../../src/Model/OtherModelHypothesis.php):38 and [OtherAgentFrameFact.php](../../../src/Model/OtherAgentFrameFact.php):35, including provenance/access labels and corrections. Hypothesis TTLs range from 300 to 3,600 seconds; corrected facts can persist. This is partial separation of transient hypotheses and durable facts, not learned character versus current-state inference. The inspected producer and consumer paths use `primary_user`; a multi-actor generalization mechanism and a belief-state updater tracking witnessed/unwitnessed world changes were not found in OtherModel, projection, model-record, and evaluator inspection. This bounded finding excludes external model internals.

More seriously, [Q21S](IMPLEMENTATION.md#q21s--identical-forecasts-for-incompatible-propositions) verifies that `predictionTarget:1113` and `hypothesisDistribution:1145` use hypothesis kind rather than proposition content: beliefs target hearing/speech state, with fixed boosts to direct speech and quiet. Two incompatible belief propositions under identical baseline/context receive identical distributions and likelihood evidence. `updateHypothesisFromPrediction:908` nevertheless multiplies their posterior odds by forecast likelihood divided by baseline likelihood, clipped to [0.1,10]. Different starting posteriors may remain different, but these observations cannot discriminate the claims. Grounding and explicit corrections are separate evidence; this forecast channel validates a kind-conditioned prediction, not each belief’s semantics.

**Prioritized recommendations—all proposed, none performed.**

1. **P1: make belief evidence discriminative, then benchmark information access.** First isolate the Q21S collision: incompatible same-kind propositions must not acquire semantic credibility merely because their identical speech forecast succeeds. At `OtherModel::updateHypothesisFromPrediction`, distinguish forecast reliability from proposition support unless an outcome discriminates the proposition; preserve direct grounding/corrections. This recommendation follows from ToMnet’s behavioral prediction objective rather than reproducing its algorithm.

   Use synthetic collaboration episodes: a subject sees artifact version A; it changes to B; notification is delivered, withheld, or uncertain. Hold final observer world state fixed while changing subject exposure. Compare a new content/exposure-aware predictor with current-world-only and last-observed-state baselines; report existing coarse OtherModel outputs separately because they do not predict artifact-directed actions. Extend `AgentObservationProjector` and `OtherModel::sealPredictions/resolvePredictions` only experimentally with a declared action target, actor, proposition, exposure evidence, timestamps, and distinct observation/belief uncertainty (Q21). This is a symbolic engineering synthesis inspired by §3.4.

   Cost: an isolated episode generator, prediction adapter, and explicit event semantics. Pre-register paired action-distribution error and proper scores by visible/invisible/uncertain changes. Require improvement over the strongest simple baseline with a paired uncertainty interval excluding zero. Fail if irrelevant speech outcomes still increase semantic confidence, invisible changes leak into immediate predictions, visible corrections are ignored, or gains vanish under renamed artifacts and reordered layouts. A replay adapter’s result is not production capability.

2. **P2: test persistent characteristics separately from current state.** If P1 identifies a useful prediction task, prototype two bounded evidence summaries at the OtherModel inference boundary: slower estimates of repeatable behavior across episodes, and current availability/exposure information with explicit expiry. Feed both into predictions while retaining provenance and uncertainty. Compare population-only, persistent-only, current-only, and combined predictors, including shuffled-history ablations. This transfers the character/mental distinction of §2.2 without assuming a neural embedding or inventing a missing production schema.

   Cost: episode segmentation and enough independent histories to identify stable effects. With one primary user, blocked chronological holdouts can measure future-session adaptation; they cannot establish generalization to unseen people. Use synthetic held-out agent identities and policy families for that separate claim. Reject added complexity unless the combined predictor improves held-out log loss over the strongest single component, remains calibrated after behavior changes, and loses its purported personalization advantage when relevant history is shuffled. Temporary distraction becoming an enduring “trait” is a concrete failure mode.

3. **P3: evaluate useful control separately from forecast accuracy.** Preserve the existing distinction between predictive scoring and functional uptake (I09). At `OtherModelEvaluator` and `ActionSelector::choose`, first evaluate in an isolated setting whether better forecasts improve a declared interruption/defer objective while preserving responses to direct requests. Measure both forecast quality and intervention outcomes; movement in normalized action scores alone is insufficient. Candidate-utterance-conditioned prediction would require new action features and controlled data (Q13S), and is a later experiment rather than an existing capability or a result established by this paper.

   Cost: independent outcome labels and enough comparable decisions to distinguish benefits from simply speaking less. Reject deployment if proper-score gains do not improve the declared interaction objective, or apparent success comes from suppressing all initiative. Observational no-response categories must not be relabeled as annoyance or a latent preference without supporting evidence.

**Verdict: experiment.** Adopt the separation of individual history, current information access, and externally scored predictions as design criteria. Prioritize the paired exposure benchmark; retain the existing bounded social inference and evaluation infrastructure. Defer neural meta-learning, broad personality inference, and expanded action authority until simpler models fail on a demonstrated task. Predicting what another agent will do also supplies neither that agent’s endorsement nor permission to act.
