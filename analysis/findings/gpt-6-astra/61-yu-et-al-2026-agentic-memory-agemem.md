# Agentic Memory: Learning Unified Long-Term and Short-Term Memory Management for Large Language Model Agents

**Authors:** Yi Yu, Liuyi Yao, Yuexiang Xie, Qingquan Tan, Jiaqi Feng, Yaliang Li, and Libing Wu. **Version:** arXiv:2601.01885v3, 23 July 2026; local arXiv preprint, with no publication venue established here. **Source:** [local PDF](../../../assets/papers/61-yu-et-al-2026-agentic-memory-agemem.pdf). **Reviewer:** GPT-6 Astra. **Implementation reference:** [shared verified implementation master](IMPLEMENTATION.md), snapshot 2026-09-06, git HEAD `ee1590009d6fd4c48035c63e15b3896a585757d7`, reread before finalization. **Central judgment.** Experiment with joint control of retrieval and context reduction, but do not transplant AgeMem's reward function or assume that exposing memory tools reproduces its gains. The paper supplies promising controlled evidence for a trained memory policy. Its actual credit assignment remains trajectory-wide, its maintenance incentives reward operations without establishing their usefulness, and its strongest supplemented baselines substantially narrow the advantage. For Navi-Brain, the immediate opportunity is a bounded model-proposed memory-control loop over existing storage, accompanied by an independent delayed-task evaluation.

**Mechanism and assumptions.** AgeMem treats memory management as part of the same LLM policy that reasons and answers. The state comprises context, persistent memory, and task information; actions include language generation and six tools: ADD, UPDATE, DELETE, RETRIEVE, SUMMARY, and FILTER (§§3.1–3.2, pp. 4–5). “STM” means the model's message context. RETRIEVE copies top-k dense-embedding cosine matches from LTM into that context; SUMMARY replaces selected conversation spans with an LLM summary; FILTER removes messages matching a specified removal criterion. ADD/UPDATE maintain content and embeddings; DELETE removes a record (Appendix A.1, pp. 12–16). The contribution concerns control over these operations, not a novel associative retrieval algorithm.

Each training rollout has three consecutive phases: expose information and construct LTM; clear STM while preserving LTM and introduce distractions; then reveal the task and solve it using retained memory. LTM starts empty for each rollout, rather than accumulating indefinitely across training episodes (Algorithm 1, p. 19). These are phases inside a joint generate-and-optimize loop, not three separately demonstrated training runs. Resetting context is intended to make downstream performance depend on earlier external storage; parametric knowledge could still supply some answers. It also imposes an artificial information bottleneck whose generality needs testing.

Eight rollouts per task provide group-normalized terminal advantages. Crucially, every action in a trajectory receives the **same** advantage, including early storage and later reasoning (§3.4, pp. 5–6; Algorithms 1–2, p. 19). This trains actions across context discontinuities, but does not identify which particular update, deletion, or summary caused success. Equation 6 specifies an importance-ratio objective with KL regularization; it does not display a clipped surrogate. “Step-wise” describes the experience representation and application of the shared signal, not demonstrated fine-grained causal credit assignment.

The controller also retains substantial scaffolding: Stage 1 automatically retrieves at every step, FILTER uses a fixed similarity threshold, SUMMARY uses a separate summarization prompt, and training distractors come from an external LLM (pp. 13, 18, 20, 27). Eliminating a separate memory manager does not eliminate auxiliary model calls or deterministic retrieval rules.

**What the experiments establish.** Training uses HotpotQA only; evaluation spans ALFWorld, SciWorld, PDDL, BabyAI, and HotpotQA with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-4B-Instruct (§4.1, pp. 6–7). This reported transfer beyond the training domain is the strongest positive evidence. The primary metrics mix success rate, planning progress rate, and a Qwen-Max answer score, so their unweighted average is a descriptive benchmark aggregate, not one calibrated probability of task success.

Memory Quality is Qwen-Max's assessment of stored supporting facts against the current HotpotQA question, answer, and expected facts (Appendix C.2, pp. 24–25). It measures relevance and coverage for that question, not retention across unrelated future tasks. Figure 2 gives MQ 0.533/0.605 for AgeMem, versus best baseline 0.527/0.587; these absolute margins are modest and have no reported confidence intervals.

Efficiency evidence is similarly specific. Figure 3 reports prompt-token reductions of 3.1% and 5.1% against trained RAG variants, reaching 2,117 and 2,191 tokens (p. 7). Total tool calls nevertheless increase after training, 4.33→4.92 and 7.50→8.67 per episode (Table 3). Average prompt size is insufficient to establish lower total billed tokens, latency, GPU cost, or lifetime storage. Retrieval frequency falls alongside improved scores; that association does not by itself establish the authors' explanation that better storage caused more selective retrieval.

**Material limitations and disagreements.** The reward definitions are weaker than the prose's claims of “meaningful” maintenance and dominant task performance. Appendix A.2 gives:

- `Rmaintenance = 1[update or delete performed]`, regardless of demonstrated benefit.
- `Rpreventive = 1[context-reduction tool invoked before overflow]`, without requiring that reduction was necessary.
- A preservation indicator based on tokens/phrases from the query, which need not preserve the evidence required to answer it.

With uniform top-level and within-component weights, each maintenance/prevention indicator contributes up to 1/9 of total reward; task correctness contributes only 1/3 (pp. 17, 25). That creates an incentive for unnecessary maintenance or compression. Other reward terms can counteract it, so exploitation is a risk implied by the objective, not a measured failure. The increase in operation counts is therefore not independent evidence of useful learned maintenance. Likewise, convergence curves for differently shaped rewards cannot alone establish faster improvement on a common task objective.

Generalization to persistent dialogue remains unmeasured, as the authors acknowledge (Limitations, p. 10). Distractor sensitivity varies only three, five, and seven short messages; the generator explicitly excludes shared entities and key concepts with the target (Table 8, p. 27). This does not test plausible contradictions, changing facts, mixed relevant/irrelevant messages, or delayed reuse of material previously judged distracting. Context capacity is 8,192 tokens, while reported average prompts are roughly 2,100–2,300 tokens. Neither memory-corpus scaling nor repeated destructive maintenance is evaluated.

Reproduction also requires resolving specification ambiguity. Page 18 says Stage 1 never reveals the query, while Algorithms 3–4 put `q` in the policy state (p. 20); this is an observation-interface ambiguity, not proof of leakage. Equation 15 retains messages with similarity **below** the criterion threshold, consistent with the removal schema on p. 15, but p. 13 also describes the criterion as “what to keep.” Literal whole-message removal could erase relevant facts packaged alongside a distractor. The illustrative cases use `confirmed` instead of the schema's `confirmation`, and summary span `full` instead of documented `all` (pp. 16, 21–22). They should be treated as illustrations rather than independently reproducible execution traces.

The local report does not give exact evaluated sample counts/splits per benchmark, repeated-seed uncertainty, or enough augmented-baseline training detail to establish strict compute matching. The ablations support the combined package, but do not isolate curriculum ordering or uniform advantage broadcasting against equally trained alternatives. None of these gaps negates the reported gains; they limit architectural conclusions drawn from them.

Two existing properties matter to evaluation. First, native reads can reinforce query associations and increase access priority; even `RANK_RECORDS` learns query state (QMEM, Q57D). A “fixed controller” comparison can therefore still change its retriever. Second, consolidation already validates source partitions/hashes and rejects automatic supersession (`ExecutiveCore::integrateConsolidation:5020`, I08). AgeMem's unrestricted-looking update/delete semantics should not bypass those established provenance boundaries.

**Prioritized recommendations — proposed, not performed.**

1. **P0: Establish a delayed-memory evaluation before changing control.** Build an isolated research evaluator around recorded decision/context inputs, reusing cycle retrieval IDs and workspace snapshots; Q58 finds no existing chronological persistent-memory task evaluator. Expose facts before revealing held-out questions, insert context resets and both topical and contradictory distractions, then query current and older facts. Compare current control, frozen-model tool control, and joint control at matched model-call/token budgets. Reset isolated native stores from identical fixtures or explicitly separate retrieval-learning effects; a checkpoint is not a restore image (I02). This costs curated tasks, complete input capture, and repeated runs. A proposed adoption gate is at least a three-point success gain whose paired 95% confidence interval excludes zero, with no greater than one-point loss on delayed old-fact queries. Failure to meet either gate blocks production adoption; the thresholds are engineering choices, not paper results.

2. **P1: Prototype bounded retrieval and reversible context reduction.** Extend the proposal/integration boundary in `DecisionStateMachine` with at most two memory-control rounds before terminal selection. Initially allow a targeted search or summary/filter proposal referencing exact context-message IDs; retain original text in the isolated evaluator. Add backend-tokenizer accounting at `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork`/client assembly, since native-memory tokens are not established as model tokens (Q11). Preserve required intention/constraint evidence and existing source fences. Ablate adaptive retrieval and context reduction separately, then together. Require successful budget enforcement and improvement in task success per total token; reject if additional calls erase savings, critical evidence becomes unrecoverable, or rare delayed-query performance deteriorates. This directly tests coordination without first buying a training stack.

3. **P2: Make maintenance outcome-sensitive before considering RL.** In a later isolated prototype, route corrections through existing explicit replacement, retaining historical provenance rather than permanently deleting evidence. Log proposed operation, before/after context and memory IDs, subsequent use, and independently scored task outcomes. Evaluate corrections under both current-fact and historical-fact questions. Compare answer-only reward, the paper's full reward, and a cost-aware reward with no payment merely for issuing a tool call. Permit a no-op alternative. Reject the shaping rule if it increases pointless update/delete frequency or suppresses historical recall without task gains. Only after P0/P1 succeed should a separate trainer supply old-policy likelihoods, reference-policy KL, reproducible rollout grouping, and matched training budgets (Q14, Q56). This outcome-sensitive variant is a proposed synthesis, not an AgeMem result.

**Verdict: experiment.** AgeMem supports treating memory control as an explicit, task-evaluated decision problem. It does not establish that its particular reward proxies, destructive maintenance, or training curriculum are necessary for Navi-Brain. The most consequential next step is an isolated delayed-task comparison that measures joint retrieval/context decisions against the existing controller while separating retrieval side effects and total inference cost.
