**Central assessment.** Adopt the paper’s challenge to identifying consciousness from wiring or behavior alone. Its useful result is an identifiability problem: different implementations can preserve specified functional observations while receiving different structural consciousness assignments. Its stronger claim that all causal-structure theories are necessarily false or unscientific exceeds what the computational construction establishes without additional assumptions about admissible evidence and consciousness measurement. For Navi-Brain, this supports functional replacement controls and explicit system boundaries; it does not justify removing persistent feedback or declaring the system conscious.

**Argument and mechanism.** The authors distinguish theories identifying consciousness with causal structure, especially their formulations of IIT and recurrent processing theory, from theories identifying relevant functions such as broadcasting or higher-order representation (§§1.2, 2.6; pp. 5, 9). Their four premises state that consciousness experiments depend on reports or report-calibrated measurements; recurrent and feedforward systems can implement the same input-output function; such systems cannot be distinguished through that interface; and internal neural measurements cannot simply be assumed to measure consciousness (§2.1, p. 7).

Given those premises, a recurrent system and its functionally equivalent feedforward replacement produce the same evidence but receive different consciousness assignments from the targeted theories. Accepting identical consciousness abandons recurrence as necessary; insisting on different consciousness leaves the distinction unsupported by that evidence. The auditory-implant example applies this reasoning to a subsystem, acknowledging that it needs an isolatable interface (§2.2, pp. 7–8). The authors distinguish this construction from merely imagining philosophical zombies (§2.5, p. 8).

A precise bounded version is straightforward. For a transition system

\[
s_{t+1}=T(s_t,x_t),\qquad y_t=O(s_t,x_t),
\]

fix the initial state and horizon \(H\), and replace repeated application of \(T\) with \(H\) successive copies. The resulting acyclic computational graph preserves outputs for every admissible length-\(H\) input sequence, provided all relevant state and stochastic inputs are represented. This reconstruction makes the temporal assumption explicit; it is not a theorem newly proved or experimentally demonstrated in the paper. Fig. 4 illustrates a corresponding workspace: successive layers copy information forward and downstream networks access it, preserving a broadcasting function (p. 9).

Appendix A adds a different construction. Decompose \(F=f_\beta\circ f_\alpha\), insert an alleged high-\(\phi\) device implementing \(g\), then cancel its functional effect:

\[
f_\beta\circ g^{-1}\circ g\circ f_\alpha=F.
\]

Under the paper’s IIT assumptions, the inserted recurrent complex determines the consciousness assignment while feedforward cancellation preserves the external function (pp. 11–12, Fig. A1a). Appendix B proposes high-\(\phi\) reservoirs as another route. Appendix C substitutes a device assigned particular qualia, producing the provocative possibility of reporting a visible cat while supposedly experiencing the smell of ham (pp. 11–13, Fig. A1b). These are theoretical constructions, not measured dissociations of experience.

**Evidence, limitations, and disagreements.** The article introduces no new participants, trained model comparison, robot experiment, numerical IIT calculation, or measured consciousness outcome. Fig. 1 draws on masking, rivalry, recurrence, and perturbational-complexity research (p. 4). These motivate the argument rather than test the proposed replacements. The Atari examples (§2.1, p. 7) show that recurrent and feedforward agents can achieve strong performance on related tasks. Comparable game performance does not establish identical policies, observation histories, timing, or intervention responses.

The main inference needs four qualifications.

1. **Approximation, exact equality, and indefinite operation differ.** Universal approximation is subject to domain and regularity conditions; it does not by itself establish exact equality for arbitrary functions or unbounded input streams. Finite unrolling can preserve transitions exactly, but the required graph grows with the horizon. The existence of a separate finite equivalent for each experiment is weaker than one fixed finite feedforward device matching a persistent recurrent system indefinitely. This limits the paper’s unrestricted wording, while leaving its challenge for bounded behavioral observations substantial (§2, pp. 5–6).

2. **Interface equivalence does not eliminate internal causal evidence.** P3 explicitly excludes measurements of internal workings (p. 7). Such measurements and interventions can distinguish mechanisms even when ordinary behavior matches. The authors correctly note that TMS can be represented as an input (§2, p. 5), but this requires specifying an enlarged intervention interface and demonstrating equivalence over it. Matching ordinary sensory responses supplies no such guarantee. Conversely, distinguishing mechanisms still does not independently establish which mechanism is conscious. The defensible conclusion is conditional underdetermination of consciousness assignments, not a general invalidation of mechanistic neuroscience.

3. **The measurement premise carries philosophical weight.** Rejecting an uncalibrated neural consciousness indicator is sensible; excluding every possible evidential role for internal structure is stronger. The argument does not establish that an independently justified bridge between internal measurements and experience is impossible. Nor does an alternative implementation erase predictive correlations within human brains. The authors themselves retain the possibility of useful human consciousness markers in §2.4 (p. 8). Calling the construction a “double dissociation” must not imply that conscious experience was independently measured in either artificial system.

4. **The appendices add assumptions beyond unrolling.** Fig. A1 explicitly requires injectivity for the inverse on the image. Appendix A supplies an invertible-device example, but Appendix C does not establish that every desired quale admits a suitable injective realization. An approximate inverse also needs an error bound to support an indistinguishability claim. Appendix B requires appropriate reservoir dynamics; high \(\phi\) alone does not prove universal computational suitability. The claims about maximal complexes and arbitrary \(\phi\) remain dependent on the selected IIT formulation, system boundary, state, and grain. The paper’s simplified recurrent/feedforward classification should not be treated as a version-independent IIT calculator.

Finally, §2.6 exempts functional formulations of GWT, higher-order theory, and predictive processing from this particular objection. It does not establish their sufficiency or truth. Footnote 3 explicitly restores the objection if GWT instead makes recurrence itself essential (p. 9).

**Mapping to Navi-Brain.** The relevant level is the executive’s computational organization and measurable information use. A software loop, a learned association graph, a transformer invocation, and a physical causal complex are distinct objects. Mapping one to another requires an explicit abstraction and intervention scheme.

**Prioritized recommendations — proposals, not completed work.**

1. **P0 — Specify what each cognition claim predicts; make no production topology change for this paper alone.** In future evaluation documentation associated with `computeMetricVector`, capsule provenance, and decision traces (I03–I04, I10), record the system boundary, observation/intervention interface, horizon, resource limits, and independent outcome for each proposed indicator. Separate architectural facts, operational proxies, functional effects, and phenomenal hypotheses. This follows directly from P1–P4 and costs documentation effort rather than inference compute. **Falsifiable criterion:** every claimed functional benefit must specify an outcome that could fail despite its indicator increasing. A measure supported only by more generated self-reports or more graph edges fails this adoption gate; it supplies no validated functional benefit or consciousness conclusion.

2. **P1 — Compare workspace substitution with workspace removal in an isolated benchmark.** At `WorkingMemory::contextForWork`, `ExecutiveCore::enqueueWork`, and `DecisionStateMachine::start/integrate` (I03–I04, Q14), propose three conditions: normal persistence; identical serialized state supplied through an explicit, step-indexed replay interface; and removal of the task-relevant carried evidence. Use synthetic delayed-cue and conflicting-update tasks with independently specified correct outcomes and stubbed terminal effects. Evaluate held-out histories, recomputing state from past inputs without future information or recorded answers. Freeze or explicitly carry other executive/token-memory state. Match permitted inputs, evidence, ordering, scope, and model settings except for the designated intervention. Account for duplicated direct observation/retrieval routes; suppressing one prompt block is insufficient. This tests information availability against a particular storage implementation. Cost: an offline harness, controlled snapshots, and repeated model calls. **Acceptance/failure:** proposed engineering margins are ±2 percentage points of accuracy for substitution, with its 95% confidence interval contained inside that margin, and a removal-induced drop whose interval exceeds 5 points. Failure leaves equivalence or the specified material benefit unestablished; passing supports only this task suite. This is software interface substitution, not a physical feedforward-brain or consciousness experiment.

3. **P2 — Measure the resource limits of that equivalence.** Extend P1 over geometrically increasing interaction horizons, comparing durable compact state with reconstruction from an explicit history. Measure accuracy, actual input tokens, storage, and directly instrumented end-to-end latency. Fix both an information contract and a deployment budget before comparing; do not silently remove history from one condition. This follows from §2.4’s efficiency concession and addresses Navi’s indefinite-operation goal. Dependencies include input accounting, which is not globally enforced in inspected worker assembly (Q11), and additional correlated timing: existing `reason_model_ms` includes queue time, while stage totals can omit asynchronous waits (Q02). **Failure criterion:** if a replacement exceeds the preset budget or loses the P1 equivalence margin at longer horizons, reject that replacement for that operating envelope. If durable state offers no advantage, its claimed efficiency benefit is unsupported there. Neither outcome identifies phenomenal consciousness.

**Verdict: adopt the methodological constraint; experiment on functional substitution; do not accept the categorical rejection of causal-structure theories as established.** The most consequential next step is P1’s matched-information substitution plus removal control. It can establish which persisted information helps Navi perform a defined task while exposing hidden state and duplicated access routes. The paper gives no reason to add recurrence, maximize an integration score, or dismantle working memory merely to obtain a preferred consciousness classification.
