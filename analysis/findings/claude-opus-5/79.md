# Record #79 — Hypothetical Minds: Scaffolding Theory of Mind for Multi-Agent Tasks with Large Language Models (Cross, Xiang, Bhatia, Yamins & Haber, ICLR 2025)

Filename retains the requested 2024 preprint year; the current revision is
published at ICLR 2025 (`assets/papers/MANIFEST.md` note for #79).

The first paper in this block that does not require a symbolic world model. Its
hypotheses are English sentences, and its scoring loop is structurally identical
to something Navi already runs.

## 1. Mechanism

An LLM agent (perception, memory, hierarchical planning over two levels) plus a
**Theory of Mind module** that generates, scores, and refines natural-language
hypotheses about other agents' strategies.

**Hypotheses are sentences, not formulas.** No PDDL, no epistemic logic, no
planner. The module writes candidate explanations of the other agent's strategy
in plain language.

**Selection is MAP over hypotheses:**

```
h* = argmax p(h_i | τ) = argmax p(τ | h_i) p(h_i) / p(τ)
```

with two substitutions that make it computable:
- **Likelihood** `p(τ|h_i)` is approximated by *predictive accuracy*. The LLM
  predicts the other agent's next behaviour conditioned on each hypothesis
  separately; hypotheses whose predictions come true score higher.
- **Prior** `p(h_i)` is "the background knowledge embedded in the weights of an
  LLM from pretraining," plus a refinement mechanism that shows the current
  top-valued hypotheses to the LLM when it generates new ones.

**Scoring is Rescorla-Wagner.** Let `φ(τ)` be an observed behavioural feature
(e.g. "the inventory played by an agent") and `φ̂(τ)` the LLM's prediction:

```
r_i = +c  if φ̂(τ) = φ(τ)
r_i = −c  if φ̂(τ) ≠ φ(τ)

δ = r_i − V_h
V_h ← V_h + α·δ
```

Defaults: c=1, α=0.3, validation threshold **V_thr = 0.7**, set a priori from
the Rescorla-Wagner dynamics. A hypothesis crossing the threshold is **validated**
and conditions high-level planning "until it no longer makes good predictions and
its value subsequently falls below the threshold." Separate hypothesis streams
are maintained per other agent.

**The cost structure is inverted from the usual story.** Each ToM call costs
`(3 + top_k)` LLM calls while nothing is validated, and **only 2 calls after
validation**. So confidence is cheap and confusion is expensive. The ablation
confirms it empirically: "higher costs (> $10 per episode) actually correlate
with decreased performance... primarily because hypothesis validation reduces
subsequent LLM calls, leading to both lower costs and better performance."

**Results.** Melting Pot benchmark — competitive (Running With Scissors repeated
and arena), collaborative (Collaborative Cooking), mixed-motive (Prisoner's
Dilemma), 5 seeds per model. HM beats LLM-agent and RL baselines on every
scenario in RWS Repeated and Collaborative Cooking, is best on 5/10 Collaborative
Cooking scenarios and 6/8 RWS Arena scenarios. **OPRE (an RL method) beats it on
Prisoner's Dilemma**, where "fixed heuristic policies such as always defecting,
playing tit-for-tat, etc. can achieve high rewards without needing to" model
anyone.

**Ablations** (RWS Repeated), five ToM variants from Vanilla Mind Prompting up to
the full model:
- Modularity helps (MMP > VMP); adding hypothesis evaluation helps more; adding
  refinement helps most on the hardest scenario.
- "While GPT-4 could sometimes identify correct hypotheses through reasoning
  alone, it did so inconsistently, highlighting the value of systematic hypothesis
  evaluation."
- Removing ToM-specific prompting (HE + HR) "excelled with adaptive opponents but
  struggled against fixed strategies" and produced "verbose, less focused
  hypotheses."
- `top_k`: the categorical jump is from 0 to any value > 0 — "a categorical
  difference when the best hypothesis is kept around, giving the agent some memory
  of past guesses, while poor hypotheses naturally fall out of the top k."
  Diminishing past 3, "may become detrimental past 5."
- **Base model matters a lot**: "GPT-4 significantly outperformed the other
  models across all tasks." Llama-3-70B-Instruct lands between GPT-4 and GPT-3.5
  on two environments and below GPT-3.5 on Prisoner's Dilemma, and could not be
  evaluated on RWS Arena at all "due to context length limitations."

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) This dissolves the hypothesis-space problem the rest of the block defers

#75 and #76 both state it outright — they infer over "a fixed set of
context-relevant goals," "leaving aside how people come up with these contextual
hypotheses." #78 needs a hypothesis space to sum posterior mass over. That gap is
the single largest blocker to building an other-model for Henry, because nobody
can enumerate what he might be doing today.

Natural-language hypotheses generated and then *scored empirically* is the answer
that fits Navi. A hypothesis is a sentence — "he is trying to get the heartbeat
service to survive a restart" — that makes checkable predictions. The
predictions must resolve against channels that actually exist, which constrains
them more than the paper's inventory-matching does: reachability from
`desktop_presence`, reply/no-reply and latency after an utterance, coarse
activity continuing or stopping, and topics recurring in accepted
`HearingStream` speech. Predictions naming a file, project, or command are not
resolvable today (71.md §2e, 78.md §2c) and must be rejected as unscoreable
rather than accepted and quietly never resolved.

Combined with #77 §2c — Henry states his goals out loud and that channel is
currently discarded — the pipeline closes: **generate hypotheses from what he
says, score them by what he does.** No PDDL, no enumeration, no planner.

### (b) The scoring loop already exists in `ForwardModel`

`src/Perception/ForwardModel.php` makes a one-step forecast per source, resolves
it against the next reading as matched or violated, stores normalized error in a
bounded 64-prediction history, and recalibrates precision from recent errors
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:236-243`).

Line up the two:

| Hypothetical Minds | `ForwardModel` |
|---|---|
| `φ̂(τ)` — predicted behaviour | persisted one-step forecast |
| `φ(τ)` — observed behaviour | the next reading |
| `r = ±c` on match/mismatch | matched / violated resolution |
| `V_h ← V_h + α·δ` | precision recalibrated from recent errors |
| bounded history for recency | bounded 64-prediction history |

The machinery is built and running. What is missing is that predictions are made
about *sources* rather than about *hypotheses concerning an agent*. Adding a
`hypothesis_id` alongside `source_key` in `ForwardPrediction`, and a value
updated by prediction error, is a small extension of an existing, tested loop —
not a new subsystem. This is the second time the corpus has landed on
`ForwardModel` as the integration point (#75 §2b was the first, for the
replanning trigger), which is a strong signal about where this organ attaches.

### (c) V_thr = 0.7 is a threshold Navi already chose, for the same reason

`docs/architecture-gaps.md:154`: "Completing requires confidence at or above 0.7,
because a finished intention is a claim about the world." Hypothetical Minds sets
V_thr = 0.7 for a hypothesis to be trusted enough to condition planning.

Two independent designs arriving at the same number for the same kind of reason —
a threshold above which an inference is allowed to drive behaviour — is worth
adopting deliberately rather than coincidentally. The useful part is the
*structure*: below threshold the hypothesis still exists, still updates, and still
does not steer anything. Navi's other-model needs exactly that two-tier state, and
#72 §2e's `no_rational_interpretation` is what happens when nothing reaches it.

### (d) Confident is cheap, confused is expensive — this fits the actual budget

The cost result is the most useful practical finding here. `(3 + top_k)` calls
while searching, 2 calls once validated, and empirically higher spend correlates
with *worse* performance.

`docs/architecture-gaps.md:269-272` names model contention as the largest source
of failed work: one available model, two degraded, five unavailable, one local
inference slot shared by heartbeat, reflection and workers. A design where a
settled model of Henry costs almost nothing and only an unsettled one spends is
exactly the right shape for that constraint. It also gives a natural circuit
breaker: if no hypothesis validates for a long stretch, cost rises, and that is
itself the signal to stop and record an impasse rather than keep spending.

### (e) `top_k > 0` is the whole effect — keep a small hypothesis set

The ablation's cleanest finding is that the categorical improvement comes from
keeping *any* previous hypotheses around rather than only the latest, with
diminishing returns past 3 and possible harm past 5.

For Navi that argues for a small, bounded table of live hypotheses about Henry —
3 to 5 — that persists across cycles, with poor ones falling out by value. That
is `WorkingMemorySlot` shaped: "one active occupant per symbolic role in shared or
thread scope, updated in place and expired rather than accumulated as a
transcript" (`docs/architecture-gaps.md:222-226`). A bounded hypothesis set with
values is a natural resident of the existing working-memory hub, and it explicitly
should not accumulate.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: the plan is waiting on a formalism that this paper shows is
optional.** Everything in `docs/architecture-gaps.md:205-216` about theory of mind
reads as a large modelling project, and #71-#78 reinforce that by requiring a
symbolic domain. This paper gets competitive results on a real benchmark with
hypotheses that are sentences and a value update that is five lines of
arithmetic. The parts Navi cannot afford — the planner, the PDDL, the particle
filter — turn out not to be load-bearing for the *practical* capability. What is
load-bearing is a prediction loop, and Navi has one.

**Second: the prior is an LLM's generic notions, and for a model of one specific
person that is the wrong prior.** `p(h_i)` here is "the background knowledge
embedded in the weights of an LLM from pretraining." In Melting Pot that is
reasonable — the LLM has read about game strategies. For Henry it means the prior
over what he is doing comes from a model's generic impression of programmers,
which is both weakly informative and a source of stereotype. Navi has something
much better available: a real semantic-memory frame about him (#74 §2a) —
his projects, his machine, his init system, his hours. The prior should come from
there, and hypothesis generation should be *conditioned* on stored frame facts
rather than left to pretraining. This is a genuine improvement over the paper
available specifically because Navi has one subject and a durable store.

**Third: the scoring rule has no defence against vague hypotheses, and #78 says
that matters.** `r = +c` whenever the predicted feature matches. A hypothesis
that generates weak, near-unfalsifiable predictions ("he will keep working") gets
rewarded as reliably as a sharp one. In Melting Pot this is contained because
`φ(τ)` is a discrete, crisply defined game feature. In Navi's setting predictions
are much softer, and the failure mode is a bland hypothesis about Henry that
validates forever and explains nothing. #78's normalized-likelihood finding is the
fix — people credit a claim by *evidence for it*, not by how easy it is to be
true — and it has to be imported alongside this scoring rule, not instead of it.
Concretely: score the prediction by how much it *narrowed* the outcome space, not
just by whether it matched.

**Fourth: the base-model gap is a direct threat to running this locally.** GPT-4
"significantly outperformed" GPT-3.5 and Llama-3-70B across all tasks, and
Llama-3-70B could not even be run on one environment for context-length reasons.
Navi's local models are far smaller than 70B. `docs/architecture-gaps.md:269-270`
already reports one available model against two degraded and five unavailable.
The honest reading is that the *architecture* transfers and the *performance* may
not, and a first implementation should be evaluated on whether hypotheses validate
at all before anything is built on top of them.

**Fifth, on framing:** this is a benchmark paper about maximizing reward against
opponents, including reasoning that "agents should only interact with opponents
that it knows it can beat." Navi and Henry are not opponents, there is no reward
to maximize, and the authors' own suggested extension — "personalizing chat bot
responses to users with hidden preferences" — is exactly the framing #74's MNM
exists to prevent. Take the hypothesis machinery; leave the objective entirely.

**Agreement:** the modular ablation result — separate LLM calls per reasoning
step beat one call doing everything — matches Navi's existing bounded-worker
discipline and is a reason to keep it.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** ICLR 2025, published conference paper. Peer reviewed, competitive
venue. Code released.

**Strengths:**
- **A real benchmark with baselines that are not straw men** — Melting Pot,
  against both LLM agents (Reflexion, PlanReAct) and RL methods (OPRE), across
  competitive, collaborative, and mixed-motive settings.
- **The ablation ladder is genuinely informative**: five variants isolating
  modularity, evaluation, refinement, and ToM-specific prompting, with the
  interesting cross-result that removing ToM prompting helps against adaptive
  opponents and hurts against fixed ones.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis** on `top_k`, α, and V_thr, with the
  useful negative finding that `top_k` past 5 may hurt.
- **Cost is measured and reported**, and the cost/performance correlation is
  reported even though it is counterintuitive.
- **A reported loss**: OPRE beats HM on Prisoner's Dilemma, with an honest
  explanation (fixed heuristics suffice there).

**Where it is thin:**
- **5 seeds per model** on a stochastic LLM pipeline.
- **Prompts and scaffolding are hand-built**, and the authors say so: the
  implementation "requires human intervention to establish scaffolding and
  prompting," and "knowledge of game rules and mechanics must be explicitly
  encoded in the prompts rather than learned from the environment." So a
  meaningful fraction of the task knowledge is supplied by the authors, and the
  comparison to RL baselines that learn from the environment is not like for like.
- **The intrinsic reward is only as good as `φ(τ)` is crisp.** It works because
  Melting Pot exposes discrete checkable features like inventory. No result here
  speaks to soft-featured domains.
- **No human subjects anywhere.** Despite "Theory of Mind" in the title, this is
  an agent-performance paper; it makes no claim to match human mentalizing and
  offers no evidence about it. Different evidence type from #71-#78 entirely.
- **Base-model dependence is large and under-discussed** — the ablation shows it
  and the conclusion does not revisit it.
- **The "natural language approximation of Bayesian inference" framing is
  loose.** `p(h)` is LLM pretraining, `p(τ|h)` is an accuracy counter, and the
  update is Rescorla-Wagner rather than Bayes. It is a well-motivated heuristic
  value system, not an approximation with error bounds, and calling it Bayesian
  oversells it.

**Is the headline supported?** That hypothesis evaluation and refinement improve
LLM-agent performance in multi-agent tasks — yes, well supported by the ablations
across scenarios. That this constitutes theory of mind — unsupported and not
tested; no human comparison exists. Read it as an engineering result about
maintaining and scoring beliefs about other agents, which is what Navi needs
anyway.

## 6. Do not take

- **The reward-maximizing objective and any competitive framing.** No opponents
  here.
- **The suggested application.** "Personalizing chat bot responses to users with
  hidden preferences" is inference-for-solicitation, the thing #71 §4 and #74 §2c
  both rule out. Inferring Henry's state to decide when to *stay quiet* is the
  authorized direction.
- **`p(h)` from pretraining.** Use the stored frame about Henry instead (§4,
  second point).
- **The raw `±c` reward without a specificity term.** Import #78's
  evidence-weighting alongside it or the hypothesis set will fill with bland
  survivors.
- **`top_k` above 3-5.** The paper's own sensitivity analysis says diminishing
  then possibly harmful, and every extra hypothesis is an extra LLM call while
  unvalidated.
- **Multiple hypothesis streams.** Built for many other agents; Navi has one
  person to model, and #74 §2d already fixed depth at 1.
- **Reading validation as truth.** A validated hypothesis is one that has
  predicted well for a while at α=0.3 — roughly a recency-weighted accuracy
  streak. It is a working assumption that steers behaviour, not a fact about
  Henry, and it must not be consolidated into the semantic tier on the strength of
  its value alone.

## 7. Key citations

- The core loop: the ToM module "scaffolds the high-level planning process by
  generating hypotheses about other agents' strategies in natural language. It
  then evaluates and iteratively refines these hypotheses by reinforcing
  hypotheses that make correct predictions about the other agents' behavior"
  (Abstract).
- Selection: `h* = argmax p(h_i|τ) = argmax p(τ|h_i)p(h_i)/p(τ)` (Eq. 2), with the
  likelihood "approximated by a hypothesis evaluation mechanism" and the prior
  corresponding "both to the background knowledge embedded in the weights of an
  LLM from pretraining and to the refinement mechanism."
- The update: `r_i = +c` if `φ̂(τ) = φ(τ)` else `−c`; `δ = r_i − V_h`;
  `V_h ← V_h + α·δ`, with c=1, α=0.3, `V_thr = 0.7` "set apriori based on analysis
  of the Rescorla-Wagner dynamics."
- Validation gates behaviour: a validated hypothesis "will then continue to be
  used for planning until it no longer makes good predictions and its value
  subsequently falls below the threshold."
- The cost inversion: "Each ToM module call uses (3 + top_k) LLM calls if no
  hypothesis is validated yet and only 2 calls after validation," and "higher
  costs (> $10 per episode) actually correlate with decreased performance...
  because hypothesis validation reduces subsequent LLM calls, leading to both
  lower costs and better performance."
- Why explicit evaluation is needed: "While GPT-4 could sometimes identify correct
  hypotheses through reasoning alone, it did so inconsistently, highlighting the
  value of systematic hypothesis evaluation."
- The `top_k` finding: "Performance improves most dramatically when maintaining
  any hypotheses beyond just the last generated one... This reflects a categorical
  difference when the best hypothesis is kept around," with returns diminishing
  past 3 and possibly detrimental past 5.
- Base-model dependence: "GPT-4 significantly outperformed the other models across
  all tasks," and Llama-3 "could not be evaluated on RWS Arena" due to context
  length.
- A reported loss: "OPRE achieves the highest rewards overall on Prisoner's
  Dilemma. Fixed heuristic policies such as always defecting, playing tit-for-tat,
  etc. can achieve high rewards without needing to" model the other agent.
- The authors' limitations: the system "requires human intervention to establish
  scaffolding and prompting for the agent. Additionally, knowledge of game rules
  and mechanics must be explicitly encoded in the prompts rather than learned from
  the environment" (Conclusion).
- The proposed application, which Navi should not take: extending the ToM module
  "to more complex social interactions such as adaptive tutoring or personalizing
  chat bot responses to users with hidden preferences" (Conclusion).
