# Record #81 — GPT-4o Lacks Core Features of Theory of Mind (Muchovej, Royka, Lee & Jara-Ettinger, arXiv:2602.12150)

The first of three guardrails against reading #77-#80's benchmark numbers as
capability. Its contribution is not the negative result — it is the **evaluation
design**, and one of its three tests is free for Navi to run and is the missing
validation criterion #79 and #80 both need.

## 1. Mechanism

**The argument.** Benchmarks test whether a model *produces* human-like social
judgements. Theory of mind is a claim about *representations* — specifically that
there is a causal model of how mental states generate behaviour. Borrowing
Gopnik & Meltzoff's definition of a theory, the authors extract three desiderata
and build a test for each:

- **Coherence** — predictions follow systematically from a set of core
  representations.
- **Abstractness** — the same causal model applies across superficially different
  domains.
- **Consistency** — the forward model (mental states → behaviour) and the backward
  model (behaviour → mental states) are the same model, used in two directions.

Crucially, **none of these requires the model's theory to be human-like.** The
authors are explicit that this makes their test *more* charitable than
benchmarking: "there is not one ToM," it differs between children and adults,
across primates, across cultures, and "LLMs might have their own emergent ToM —
one that differs from human ToM and therefore might be missed by benchmark
evaluations which typically use adult human responses as ground-truth."

**Paradigms.** Two fully enumerable worlds with a 1:1 mapping between them.

*ContainerWorld*: a character, a closed box adjacent, a covered basket "about 50
steps away." Beliefs `B` and states `S` over {apples, oranges, both}, desires `D`
over {like, dislike} per fruit, action `A` ∈ {box, basket}. 9 × 3 × 9 tuples,
fully enumerated.

*MovieWorld*: a film festival, screenings in 5 minutes and 90 minutes, movies 120
minutes long, genres {action, romance, action-romance}. Same cardinalities, same
logical structure — **but cost is now time rather than physical effort.**

**Measurement.** They do not ask the model for a number. They extract the
**probability distribution over next tokens** ("box" vs "basket") and use that as
the action distribution. Comparison is against `HumanToM` (integrates beliefs,
desires and costs by rational-action principles) and five lesioned variants
(`Cost`, `Desire`, `DesireCost`, `BeliefDesire`, …).

Model: `gpt-4o-2024-05-13`, evaluated November 2024, temperature 1, top_p 1,
JSON response format.

**Study 1 — coherent? Probably yes.** GPT-4o's highest agreement is with
`HumanToM` (87.24%), above every lesion. The authors check their own paradigm for
degeneracy: 85% of `HumanToM` predictions are "open the box," but the trivial
`Cost` model (always box) still underperforms, "suggesting that the simplicity of
our paradigm did not prevent us from sufficiently differentiating between the
candidate models."

**Study 2 — abstract? No.** In MovieWorld, agreement with `HumanToM` is still
highest but "notably attenuated" (≈60% vs 87%), and the second-place model shifts
from `Cost` to `DesireCost`. The decisive test is whether GPT-4o's own
ContainerWorld distribution predicts its own MovieWorld distribution on logically
identical items:

| Transfer across the 1:1 domains | r | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Action prediction | 0.48 | [0.41, 0.56] |
| **Belief inference** | **0.78** | [0.68, 0.85] |
| Joint belief-desire inference | 0.39 | [0.20, 0.57] |
| **Desire inference** | **0.18** | [0.09, 0.27] |

All of these should be at ceiling. The authors attribute the action-prediction
drop to the cost representation changing from effort to time — the only
substantive difference between the two worlds.

**Study 3 — consistent? No.** Two tests of whether the forward and backward
models are the same model:

- *Bayesian evaluation* (strict): Bayesian-invert the extracted forward model `F`
  to get the expected posterior, correlate with the LLM's own direct inference.
  Correlations sit near zero, and Figure 4's axis runs **−0.3 to 0.1**.
- *Validity evaluation* (generous): does the inferred mental state, fed back into
  `F`, produce the action it was supposed to explain? Many mental states could;
  only one needs to. Results land roughly 55-89% across the two worlds — nowhere
  near the ceiling this test demands.

Conclusion: "GPT-4o's action predictions (from mental-states) are unrelated to its
mental-state inferences (from actions)."

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) The consistency test is free, and it is the validation criterion the block was missing

This is the most actionable item in the last three records. Navi's architecture
already contains both directions:

- **Forward** — `src/Perception/ForwardModel.php` makes a persisted one-step
  prediction and resolves it against the next reading.
- **Backward** — the other-model infers Henry's goal/belief from what he did.

The consistency check is a comparison of two quantities she would already be
computing. Given a hypothesis "he is debugging the heartbeat service," the
forward model predicts he opens the OpenRC scripts. Separately, given that he
opened the OpenRC scripts, the backward inference produces some goal. **If those
disagree, the model is incoherent and neither half is trustworthy**, no matter how
plausible either looked in isolation.

That closes a real gap across #79 and #80:

| Record | Stopping / trust criterion | What it misses |
|---|---|---|
| #79 | predictive accuracy (Rescorla-Wagner value ≥ 0.7) | a bland hypothesis that predicts vaguely and always "matches" |
| #80 | low entropy (`R = −H`) | confidently wrong under-modelling — the authors admit it |
| **#81** | **forward/backward agreement** | catches both: a vague or wrong model fails to round-trip |

None of the three subsumes the others, and #81's is the only one that costs
nothing extra. It should be the gate on whether the other-model is allowed to
influence `ActionSelector` at all.

### (b) The abstractness failure predicts exactly how Navi's model will break

GPT-4o transferred at r=0.48 between two worlds that were **logically identical**
and differed only in whether cost was measured in steps or minutes.

Navi's contexts differ far more than that, and the thing that varies between them
is precisely cost:

- At the desk, switching windows is nearly free.
- In a meeting (which `HearingStream` already detects —
  `docs/architecture-gaps.md:20-23`), interrupting is expensive.
- Away from the machine, every action costs differently or is unavailable.

An other-model calibrated on heads-down desk work and then applied during a call
is running exactly the effort-to-time cost shift that halved GPT-4o's transfer.
The mitigation is not more training data; it is to **make cost an explicit,
context-conditioned variable** rather than something implicit in the model —
which is also what #73's `NoCost` lesion (belief inference r=0.10) demands
independently. Two records now converge on the same missing term.

### (c) The desire/belief ordering inverts for LLMs, and that changes an earlier recommendation

#72 §2c argued for a structural confidence differential: belief attributions about
Henry should carry a lower confidence ceiling than goal attributions, because
Baker's human data show desires are inferred more reliably than beliefs (0.90 vs
0.76), with a mechanistic story about actions pointing at goals more directly than
at beliefs.

**For an LLM-driven implementation, that ordering reverses.** Here belief
inference is the *only* thing that transfers across domains (r=0.78), while desire
inference is the *worst* (r=0.18) — below even the joint inference.

So the human failure profile and the LLM failure profile are different, and a
confidence policy derived from #71-#73 would be miscalibrated for a system built
on #77-#80. The correct statement of the constraint is narrower than what #72 §2c
proposed: **cap confidence according to which component actually does the
inferring**, and measure it rather than assuming it. If Navi's goal inference is
LLM-mediated, its outputs are the ones that need distrusting; if it is the
deterministic dwell-time/lexical path (#75 §2c, #77 §2b), the human-derived
ordering is the better prior. This is worth resolving empirically before any
ceiling is written into a schema.

### (d) Read logprobs, do not ask for confidence

Extracting the token distribution over "box" vs "basket" rather than asking the
model to state a probability is a methodological choice worth copying. A model
asked how confident it is produces a narrative; a distribution over a constrained
next token is a measurement.

For Navi this is a concrete note on `LocalModelClient` / `LocalModelWorker`: where
the other-model needs a likelihood, constrain the answer to a small token set and
read the distribution. It composes directly with #80's design, where the LLM's
only job is estimating local conditionals — those are exactly the quantities that
should come from logprobs rather than from prose.

### (e) Enumerate a small world and test it offline

The whole evaluation works because the paradigm is small enough to enumerate —
243 tuples. Navi could construct the same thing: a handful of Henry-contexts
(heads-down / meeting / idle) × a small goal set × a small observation set, fully
enumerated, with a hand-specified `HumanToM`-style reference model.

That gives an offline, cheap, repeatable coherence and consistency test for the
other-model that does not require waiting for real behaviour, and it is exactly
the shape of the ablation experiments already in `docs/experiments/`. It is also
the only way to run these checks *before* a model that might be wrong starts
influencing what Navi says.

## 3. Seam

Primary: **`metrics-benchmark`** — this record is an evaluation methodology, and
its home is `docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md` and `docs/experiments/`.

Also touches:
- `other-model` — the consistency gate on whether it may influence behaviour.
- `forward-model` — supplies one half of the round-trip for free (§2a).
- `self-model` — component-specific confidence ceilings, measured not assumed
  (§2c).
- `worker-schema` — constrained-token likelihood extraction (§2d).
- `speech-gate` — the gate's practical consequence: an inconsistent model does
  not get to time an interruption.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: every metric in `docs/architecture-gaps.md` measures whether a
mechanism *ran*, and this paper is about whether a mechanism is *coherent*.** The
"What to measure" table (lines 249-260) counts intentions completed, `next_action`
values changed, procedural memories compiled, model calls per decision, forecasts
resolved, candidates arbitrated. All good operational counters, and none of them
would detect a subsystem that produces plausible outputs from no consistent
underlying model. For most of Navi's organs that is fine — a heartbeat either
fires or does not. For the other-model it is not, because its outputs are
*interpretations*, and a wrong interpretation looks identical to a right one in
every counter listed. The other-model needs a coherence metric before it needs a
throughput metric.

**Second: the plan's own "cross-model comparison" is the right instinct pointed
at the wrong question.** `decision:compare` groups completion, impasse,
verification, procedure reuse, candidates and latency by model ID
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:260`) — comparing models by *outcome*. #81's method
compares a model against *itself*, across domains and across inference direction.
That is a strictly better diagnostic for this organ and reuses the same
infrastructure: run the same enumerated probe set through each configured model
and report round-trip agreement. It would slot into `decision:compare` as another
grouping rather than as new machinery.

**Third: this paper's conclusion collides with the plan's most likely
implementation.** #79 and #80 are the cheap, buildable designs, and both put an
LLM at the centre of mental-state inference. #81 says the LLM at the centre has no
consistent causal model of mental states and behaviour — coherent on one task,
non-transferring across a logically identical one, and with its forward and
backward inferences "unrelated." That does not make #79/#80 unusable; both wrap
the LLM in scoring or marginalization machinery that #81 does not test. It does
mean the wrapper is load-bearing for correctness, not just for accuracy, and that
any design which lets the model answer a mental-state question directly inherits
these failures. The corpus's own architecture recommendation (#77 §2a: LLM as
parser, never as reasoner) is the mitigation, and this record is the evidence for
why it is not merely a stylistic preference.

**Agreement:** the choice to evaluate representations rather than outputs matches
what Navi's design already does elsewhere — procedural memory compiles only after
three consecutive verified matches, not after confident prose
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:176-181`). Extending "do not believe it because it
sounds right" from actions to interpretations is the same principle.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** arXiv preprint (2602.12150) in CogSci proceedings format, 8 pages.
Not a peer-reviewed journal article. Prompts and materials posted on OSF, which
is better disclosure than most of this corpus.

**Strengths:**
- **The design is the contribution and it is well made.** Fully enumerable
  paradigms, a 1:1 logical mapping between them, lesioned comparison models, and
  three tests targeting three named properties.
- **The evaluation is deliberately non-anthropocentric.** By not requiring the
  LLM's theory to match humans', it removes the most common objection to negative
  ToM results. The model fails a test it could have passed with an alien theory.
- **Token-distribution extraction** rather than sampled self-report.
- **They check their own paradigm for degeneracy** (the 85%-box concern) and
  address it with the `Cost` baseline rather than ignoring it.
- **Two metrics for consistency**, one strict and one deliberately generous, with
  both reported.
- Confidence intervals throughout.

**Where it is thin:**
- **One model, one snapshot.** `gpt-4o-2024-05-13`, tested November 2024. The
  authors state the limitation plainly — "One limitation of the current
  investigation is that we only evaluate one LLM" — and then generalize anyway:
  the discussion says the work "should cast serious doubt on the prospect of LLM
  ToM." The title is correctly scoped; the conclusion is not.
- **No reasoning models tested.** o1-class models existed by November 2024, and
  #80's Table 1 shows large reasoning models behaving differently from GPT-4o on
  ToM benchmarks. The absence is unexplained.
- **No scaffolded systems tested.** #77-#80 all argue that a bare LLM is the wrong
  unit of analysis. This paper tests exactly the configuration those papers say is
  inadequate, and reports that it is inadequate. That is consistent, but it is not
  evidence about AutoToM or Hypothetical Minds.
- **The Bayesian consistency metric presupposes the LLM should be a Bayesian
  inverter.** The authors acknowledge this and add the validity metric, which is
  the defensible one — and under that metric GPT-4o scores 55-89%, not zero. The
  headline word "unrelated" is earned under the strict metric and overstated under
  the generous one.
- **ContainerWorld's skew** is real even if defended: 85% of correct answers are
  one option, so agreement percentages are compressed against a high baseline.
- **The domains are still toy worlds**, which is the same criticism #83 will make
  of everything else.

**Is the headline supported?** For GPT-4o specifically: yes, convincingly. The
cross-domain transfer at r=0.48 on logically identical items, and desire inference
at r=0.18, are hard to explain away, and the forward/backward disagreement is a
clean internal-inconsistency result that needs no human comparison. As a claim
about LLMs generally, it is one model on two toy paradigms.

## 6. Do not take

- **"LLMs lack theory of mind" as a settled fact.** One model, one snapshot, no
  reasoning models, no scaffolded systems. The authors' own limitation section
  says so more carefully than their discussion does.
- **The conclusion that LLM-based components are therefore unusable for Navi.**
  The finding is about a bare model answering mental-state questions directly.
  #77-#80 all wrap that. What transfers is the requirement that the wrapper be
  tested, not the conclusion that the approach is dead.
- **The Bayesian consistency metric as the primary gate.** It demands the
  component be a Bayesian inverter. The validity metric — does the inferred mental
  state, fed forward, reproduce the observed behaviour — is the one to implement,
  because it is the weaker and therefore more meaningful test to fail.
- **ContainerWorld/MovieWorld as Navi's probe set.** The *design pattern* (two
  enumerated 1:1 domains differing in cost structure) transfers; fruit and film
  festivals do not. Navi's pair should be two real contexts with genuinely
  different cost structures — desk versus meeting.
- **Reading Study 1's 87% as reassurance.** The paper's own arc is that coherence
  on a single task is exactly what benchmarks measure and exactly what does not
  generalize. Any single-context evaluation of Navi's other-model has the same
  status.

## 7. Key citations

- The construct-validity argument: benchmark evaluations "do not test for the
  actual representations posited by ToM: namely, a causal model of mental states
  and behavior" (Abstract).
- The three desiderata: "since ToM is a theory, it possesses three defining
  characteristics: coherence, abstractness, and consistency. Here, we propose that
  these three characteristics can serve as desiderata when evaluating whether or
  not LLMs have a ToM" (Introduction).
- Why this is more charitable than benchmarking: "there is not one ToM... LLMs
  might have their own emergent ToM — one that differs from human ToM and
  therefore might be missed by benchmark evaluations which typically use adult
  human responses as ground-truth" (General Discussion).
- Measurement method: "We then extracted the LLM's probability distribution over
  next tokens (e.g., 'box' or 'basket') and used this as the LLM's distribution
  over predicted next actions" (Study 1, Procedure).
- Coherence holds: GPT-4o's "responses have the highest coherence with HumanToM,
  providing congruent evidence with past claims that LLMs appear to have a
  human-like ToM" (Study 1, Results).
- Abstractness fails: "ContainerWorld does not reliably predict MovieWorld
  (r = .48, CI95% [0.41, 0.56])" (Study 2), with belief inference the only
  reliable transfer at r = .78 [.68, .85], joint inference r = .39 [.2, .57], and
  desire inference r = .18 [.09, .27].
- The cost representation is the suspected cause: agreement was "notably attenuated
  in MovieWorld relative to ContainerWorld. This likely stems from the change in
  cost representation — in ContainerWorld cost is an effort-based measure while in
  MovieWorld cost is a time-based measure."
- The consistency definition: "in either direction of ToM, we expect the
  ⟨B, D, S, A⟩ to be the same, even if predictions might be 'wrong'" (Study 3).
- The generous metric: "a mental-state inference, when used as input to F,
  produces the target action to be explained, a requirement of any causal model."
- The verdict: "these failures illustrate that GPT-4o's action predictions (from
  mental-states) are unrelated to its mental-state inferences (from actions)"
  (Study 3, Results).
- Why it matters practically: "Claiming that an LLM possesses a ToM implies that
  it can make reasonable, coherent mental state judgments even when asked to
  generalize far outside of its training data" (General Discussion).
- The limitation, in the authors' words: "One limitation of the current
  investigation is that we only evaluate one LLM with our proposal... there remains
  an open question: is this restricted to GPT-4o or generalizable across LLMs?"
- Model and date: `gpt-4o-2024-05-13`, "Evaluations reported in this work are from
  November 2024," temperature 1, top_p 1.
