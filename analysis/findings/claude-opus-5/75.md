# Record #75 — Online Bayesian Goal Inference for Boundedly-Rational Planning Agents (Zhi-Xuan, Mann, Silver, Tenenbaum & Mansinghka, NeurIPS 2020)

The most directly implementable paper in the theory-of-mind block. It replaces
the noise model that #71-#73 rest on, and it supplies the tractable inference
#74 said was missing.

## 1. Mechanism

**The reframing.** Every model so far treats sub-optimality as *action* noise:
the agent knows the optimal policy and occasionally picks a worse action
(Boltzmann/softmax β). This paper says the real limitation is that **planning is
expensive**, and models agents as *boundedly rational with respect to how much
they plan* — interleaving resource-limited search with execution.

The authors also name the cognitive-plausibility problem with the older approach
directly: "If inferring an agent's goals requires knowing the optimal solution to
reach each goal, then an observer would need to compute the optimal plan or
policy for all of those goals in advance... Outside of the simplest problems and
domains, this is deeply intractable." That is an indictment of #71-#73 as
implementable systems, from inside the same lab.

**The agent model.** An agent searches for a plan up to a budget η, executes a
partial plan toward a promising intermediate state, then replans.

```
η ~ NEGATIVE-BINOMIAL(r, q)
```

η is the maximum number of nodes expanded. `r` (max failure count) and `q`
(continuation probability) "characterize the persistence of a planner who may
choose to give up after expanding each node." With r > 1 the distribution peaks
at medium η, "modeling agents that are unlikely to form extremely long plans,
which are costly, or extremely short plans, which are unhelpful."

Search itself is stochastic A*, expanding successor s with probability

```
P_expand(s) ∝ exp(−f(s,g)/γ),    f(s,g) = c(s) + h(s,g)
```

**The replanning trigger is the detail that matters most here.** The agent calls
the planner when it reaches the end of its last plan **or encounters a state
`s_t` not anticipated by the plan**. Otherwise it continues executing and
performs *no additional computation*. The authors note the side effect: "by
replanning when the unexpected occurs, the agent automatically handles some
amount of stochasticity, as well as errors in its environment model."

**Inference: SIPS.** The exact posterior (Eq. 8) marginalizes over states,
actions and plans and is intractable, so they run a sequential Monte Carlo
particle filter. Each particle is a (goal, partial plan) hypothesis. Particles
extend as observations arrive and are reweighted by `w_i ← w_i · P(o_τ | s_τ^i)`.
Resampling fires when effective sample size drops below a threshold, followed by
Metropolis-Hastings rejuvenation with a **heuristic-driven goal proposal**:

```
Q(g) := SOFTMAX([h(o_τ, g) for g ∈ G])
```

— propose goals by estimated distance from the current observation, then correct
with the proposal ratio. Cheap heuristic proposes; principled machinery accepts
or rejects.

**Results.**

*Human-likeness* (the headline): a pilot study, **N=8**, ten trajectories, six
sub-optimal or failed, inferences collected every six timesteps. On the Figure 1a
backtracking trajectory, SIPS correlates with the human average at **r=0.99**
versus oracle BIRL at r=0.80. Across all trajectories, SIPS r=0.89 versus BIRL
r=0.51.

The diagnosis of *why* BIRL fails is the important part: "Boltzmann action noise
assigns significant likelihood to the undoing of past actions." An action-noise
model cannot treat backtracking as informative, because backtracking is exactly
what noise looks like. So it keeps mass on the wrong goal after the agent has
visibly abandoned it.

*Accuracy and speed*: four domains — Taxi (|G|=3, |S|=125), Doors/Keys/Gems
(|G|=3, |S|~10⁵), Block Words (|G|=5, |S|~10⁵), Intrusion Detection (|G|=20,
|S|~10³⁰). SIPS beats unbiased BIRL on 3 of 4 domains, "often several orders of
magnitude" faster — largely because unbiased value iteration "fails to converge
except for the highly restricted Taxi domain," even after 10⁶ iterations. Against
*oracle* BIRL (which is given the trajectories in advance and is "inapplicable in
practice"), SIPS is "comparable."

**10 particles per goal sufficed** with matched parameters, without rejuvenation
moves.

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) Henry is a replanning agent, not a noisy one — and this is why the current model would fail

This is the paper that describes the actual subject. Henry does not execute a
slightly-corrupted optimal policy. He forms a partial plan, works it, hits
something he did not anticipate, and replans. He backtracks. He abandons things
mid-way. `CLAUDE.md` lists roughly thirty active codebases; the observable
behaviour is constant partial-plan switching.

Under #71's β, all of that reads as *noise*, and the fitted response is to lower
β — i.e. to conclude Henry is erratic and to integrate evidence more slowly. That
is the M1 failure mode from #71 exactly (M1 "could only explain these actions as
noise, and low β values allowed M1 to filter out this noise"). Under the
replanning model, the same behaviour is *structure*: evidence about his planning
budget and about where his plan ran out.

The practical difference is sharp. A backtrack — Henry returning to a project he
left an hour ago — is nearly uninformative under Boltzmann noise and strongly
informative under this model. Given that switching between projects is his most
frequent observable action, choosing the wrong noise model throws away most of
the available signal.

### (b) The replanning trigger is already computed: `ForwardModel` prediction error

"Encounters a state not anticipated by the plan" is a prediction error, and
`src/Perception/ForwardModel.php` already computes exactly that per source: a
one-step forecast, resolution against the next reading, normalized error,
precision recalibrated from a bounded 64-prediction history, and edge
significance precision-weighted by the error on the fields that actually changed
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:236-243`).

Two consequences, both cheap:

1. **Run goal inference on surprise, not on a schedule.** The paper's agent
   performs *no additional computation* while its plan holds. Navi should do the
   same with her other-model: re-infer Henry's goal when the prediction error on
   his observable channels spikes, and otherwise carry the previous posterior
   forward. This is a value-of-computation policy that falls out of the model
   rather than being bolted on, and it matters given one contended inference slot
   (`docs/architecture-gaps.md:271-272`).
2. **A surprise on Henry's channels is the moment his goal is most likely to have
   changed.** That is #71's γ, but triggered by evidence rather than assumed as a
   constant rate. Better than a fixed decay and no more expensive.

### (c) η is partly measurable from activity timing, today

`r` and `q` parameterize *persistence* — how long the agent searches before
committing or giving up. For Henry there is a partial observable proxy already
flowing into `SenseReading`: the gaps between writes on the coarse activity
channels (`shell_activity`, `terminal_activity`, `process_activity`,
`conversation_activity`), which give the length of uninterrupted runs. A
run-length histogram is a negative-binomial-shaped object, and fitting `r` and
`q` to it is arithmetic, not inference. No model call.

**Scope correction:** this yields *whole-machine* persistence, not per-project
dwell. Window identity is unavailable (see 71.md §2e), so "how long he stays on
one thing" is only recoverable as "how long activity continues without a gap."
That is weaker than the paper's η but still a real, cheap estimate of how deep a
run is relative to his own baseline.

That yields a genuinely useful quantity Navi does not have: **how deep is he
right now, relative to his own baseline.** Which is, verbatim, the thing
`docs/architecture-gaps.md:207-208` says is missing — "There is no model of what
he is working on, whether he is heads down, or what he cares about right now."
Half of that sentence is answerable with a histogram.

### (d) A particle filter is within budget; this is #74's missing piece

#74 ended by naming particle filtering as the promising approximation and
in-progress work. Here it is, working, at 10 particles per goal. For a Navi-scale
goal set (5-8 enumerated hypotheses: *working on project X*, *in a meeting*,
*reading*, *debugging something failing*, *idle*, *away*), that is 50-80
particles carrying a (goal, partial-plan) pair each, extended on observation
arrival and reweighted by a likelihood.

In PHP, on the existing `SenseReading` stream, this is an ordinary background
job. It fits the `decide_1m` rhythm shape already established
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:194-199`). The expensive part is not the filter; it
is §6's problem — the environment model.

### (e) The proposal/correction split is a pattern Navi should copy

`Q(g) = softmax([h(o_τ, g)])` proposes goals by cheap heuristic distance, and the
MH acceptance ratio corrects for the bias that introduces. Navi's analogue is
immediate: match recent accepted `HearingStream` speech against goal
descriptions using the existing ranked lexical `memory:search`
— which is explicitly "ranked lexical search, NOT embeddings" and is a known
boundary — to *propose* a goal, then let the weighting over the observation
history decide whether to accept it.

This is the right way to use a known-weak retrieval mechanism: as a proposal
distribution, where being merely-better-than-uniform is enough, rather than as a
verdict. It converts a documented limitation into an appropriate role.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: this paper says the sensible build order for the missing organ is the
reverse of the obvious one.** The instinct — and the shape of #71-#73 — is to
start from a rationality assumption and add noise. This paper starts from the
observation that planning is expensive and derives the sub-optimality from that.
For Navi the practical version is: **model Henry's persistence and his replanning
events first, and his goals second.** Persistence is measurable from timestamps
already in the database. Goals require a hypothesis space that has to be designed
and defended. `docs/architecture-gaps.md:207` frames the missing model as "what
he is working on... whether he is heads down... what he cares about" — three
things of very different difficulty, and the middle one is nearly free.

**Second: the plan's forward model is currently one-sided in a way this paper
exposes.** `ForwardModel` predicts sensor readings and calibrates precision from
error. That is the right machinery pointed at the wrong class of source. For a
person, the interesting quantity is not "was the reading as predicted" but "did
*he* encounter something *he* did not predict" — and those are different
questions with different answers. Navi can see him hit an unexpected state
(compile fails, service down, a message arrives) through her own channels, and
that observation is worth more as evidence about his replanning than as evidence
about the sensor. Nothing in the current design distinguishes the two, because
sources have no agency.

**Third: the accuracy numbers rest on a matched model, and the human numbers rest
on eight people.** The main accuracy table uses non-optimal trajectories
*generated by the same replanning agent model* SIPS assumes, with parameters
r=2, q=0.95, γ=0.1. Robustness to mismatch is tested on two of four domains. The
human-likeness claim — the one that justifies calling this a theory-of-mind model
rather than a plan-recognition algorithm — comes from "a pilot study with N=8
subjects," and the r=0.99 is against a cross-subject average on a *single*
trajectory. That is a suggestive result presented with the confidence of a
demonstrated one. The mechanism (backtracking is informative) is convincing on
its own terms; the correlation is decoration.

**Fourth, and this is the real cost: the whole architecture assumes a symbolic
environment model.** PDDL, logical predicates, numeric fluents, a domain-general
planner (A*) with an informative heuristic. Navi has no PDDL model of Henry's
work and no plausible route to one — "which gem is behind which door" has no
analogue in "which of thirty repositories is he in and what would count as
progress." Every cheap piece above (§2b, §2c, §2e) survives without the symbolic
model; the actual SIPS algorithm does not. The honest scoping is: **adopt the
agent model and the triggers, not the planner.**

**Agreement:** the online/incremental commitment is exactly right for Navi's
situation and fixes #72's worst limitation. #72 dropped its "Middle" judgment
points — the online condition — because subjects found them confusing, leaving
the retrospective case as the evidence base. Navi has to decide *now*, mid-path,
whether to speak. This paper is built for that case.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** NeurIPS 2020, main conference. Peer reviewed, competitive.

**Strengths:**
- The core mechanistic claim is supported by a clean qualitative story with a
  named failure mode for the baseline: Boltzmann noise cannot learn from
  backtracking because backtracking is what its noise model predicts. That
  argument does not depend on the correlation numbers.
- Domains span five orders of magnitude of state space, including one at |S|~10³⁰
  where value iteration is simply not an option.
- The oracle BIRL baseline is constructed to be *unfairly strong* (given
  trajectories in advance, explicitly "inapplicable in practice") and is included
  anyway as an upper bound. That is honest baselining.
- Robustness experiments on mismatched data-generating models are run at all,
  which is more than most papers in this corpus do.

**Where it is thin:**
- **N=8 pilot study** for the entire human-likeness claim, on 10 trajectories,
  with inferences sampled every six timesteps. The word "pilot" is the authors'.
- **r=0.99 is one trajectory** against a cross-subject average. The across-dataset
  number is 0.89, and even that is against means.
- **Matched parameters** in the headline accuracy experiments; mismatch tested on
  half the domains.
- **"Comparable accuracy and speed to the oracle BIRL baseline"** is the actual
  ceiling result. SIPS decisively beats the *unbiased* baseline mostly because
  that baseline fails to converge — which is a statement about value iteration in
  large state spaces more than about goal inference.
- **Trajectories are synthetic.** Optimal ones from A*, non-optimal ones from the
  authors' own agent model. No human-generated trajectories in the accuracy
  evaluation; humans appear only as judges.
- **The goal space is small and enumerated** (3-20 goals) and given in advance.
  The paper never addresses where the hypothesis space comes from, which is
  Navi's hardest unsolved piece.
- **Gen + PDDL dependency.** The performance claims are entangled with a
  probabilistic programming system and fast general-purpose planners. Nothing
  here transfers to an environment lacking a symbolic domain model.

**Is the headline supported?** The modelling claim — bounded rationality belongs
at the planning level, and this lets you infer goals from failed and backtracking
behaviour — is well supported by mechanism and by the qualitative figures. The
efficiency claim is supported. The human-likeness claim is a pilot.

## 6. Do not take

- **PDDL, Gen, and A\* over a symbolic domain.** The infrastructure assumption is
  the paper's load-bearing dependency and Navi cannot satisfy it. Take the agent
  model; leave the planner.
- **The oracle BIRL comparison as a win.** It is a tie against a baseline the
  authors themselves call inapplicable.
- **r=0.99.** One trajectory, eight subjects, cross-subject average.
- **η as a literal node-expansion count.** For Henry it is a latent persistence
  parameter estimated from dwell times, not a count of anything. Do not write
  "nodes expanded" into a schema describing a person; it will invite a
  false-precision reading of a quantity that is a curve fit over window
  timestamps.
- **Rejuvenation with full trajectory resampling** (`s', p', a' ~ P(·|g)` over
  `1:τ`). The paper found 10 particles per goal sufficed *without* rejuvenation
  under matched parameters. Start there; the MH machinery is the part that
  reintroduces unbounded cost.
- **Treating a goal posterior as a reason to intervene.** The paper's motivation
  is assistance — "so they might assist us when our plans fail," citing infants
  helping adults after observing failed plans. That is a genuinely appealing
  framing and it is the exact path to unsolicited help, which is the behaviour
  `docs/architecture-gaps.md:47` records as the thing this work replaced. A
  detected failed plan is a reason for Navi to *know* something, and separately
  and later a reason to consider saying something. #74's MNM applies: inferring
  that his plan failed does not license acting on his mental state.

  respect to how much they plan, interleaving resource-limited plan search with
  plan execution" (Intro).
- The indictment of optimal-planning ToM: "If inferring an agent's goals requires
  knowing the optimal solution to reach each goal, then an observer would need to

  the simplest problems and domains, this is deeply intractable" (Intro).
- The budget: `η ~ NEGATIVE-BINOMIAL(r, q)`, where r and q "characterize the

  peaking at medium η to model agents "unlikely to form extremely long plans,
  which are costly, or extremely short plans, which are unhelpful" (§3.2).
- Stochastic search: `P_expand(s) ∝ exp(−f(s,g)/γ)` with `f(s,g) = c(s) + h(s,g)`
  (Eq. 7).
- The replanning trigger: the agent replans when "it will reach the end of its
  last made plan `p_{t−1}` or encounter a state `s_t` not anticipated by the

- The free side effect: "by replanning when the unexpected occurs, the agent
