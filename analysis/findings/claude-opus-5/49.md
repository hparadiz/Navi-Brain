# Record #49 — Attention Schema in Neural Agents (Liu, Bolotta, Zhu, Bengio, Dumas, 2023)

## 1. Mechanism

The paper's actual contribution is **a taxonomy plus an ablation grid**, not an
algorithm. It takes the five diagrams from Webb & Graziano 2015 (record #18) of
how attention and its "internal control" can relate, implements each as a
concrete PyTorch architecture, and races them in cooperative MARL.

**The two primitives.**

- *Attention* is a standard multi-head key-value block,
  `Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QK^T / sqrt(d_k)) V` (p. 3). The visual
  observation is cut into patches `o^pa_{j,t}` of shape (n_patches x
  pixels_per_patch); `K = W^k o^pa`, `V = W^v o^pa`, and the query `q_j` is
  `o^pa_j` averaged across the patch dimension — "an agent will decide where to
  focus based on the whole view it observes" (p. 3, §4.2). Output vector is
  `h1_{j,t}`.
- *Internal control* (IC) is a GRU, full gate equations given on p. 3. Output
  vector is `h2_{j,t}`. It is **recurrent** — it carries hidden state `h_{t-1}`
  across timesteps. This recurrence is load-bearing for the paper's thesis.

**The five hypotheses** (Figure 1, p. 3; architectures in Figure 2, p. 4):

| H | Relation | Wiring | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IC == attention | No IC module at all. `h1` -> policy. | Baseline (p. 3, §4.2) |
| 2 | IC precedes attention | Patches -> IC -> `o*`; q/k/v computed from `o*`; `h1` -> policy | p. 5, §4.3 |
| 3 | Attention precedes IC | `h1` -> IC -> `h2` -> policy | p. 5, §4.4 |
| 4 | Independent/parallel | `h1` -> policy AND `h2` -> policy, both from patches | p. 5, §4.5 |
| 5 | IC *is* the attention schema | `h1` -> IC -> `h2`; `h2` never touches the policy | p. 5, §4.6 |

**Hypothesis 5 is the only one where the schema does two distinct jobs, and the
paper is explicit that `h2` is kept off the policy path** (p. 5, §4.6: "the
output is not used as an input to the policy"). Instead:

1. **Prediction.** `h2` is pushed through an "h predictor" head to produce
   `h1'_m`, trained against the actual masked attention output `h1_m` with
   `ContrastiveLoss = (h1'_m - h1_m)^2` (p. 5). Despite the name this is plain
   squared error, not a contrastive objective — no negatives, no temperature.
   That is a sloppiness worth noting.
2. **Control.** `h2` also feeds a *pair* of MLPs described as a "neural
   activator and suppressor," whose paired outputs go through a Gumbel-softmax
   (Jang et al. 2017) to binarize into a mask `M_att in [0,1]^{d_att}` applied
   elementwise to the attention scores themselves:
   `AttScore' = AttScore ∘ M_att`, where 1 = attention allowed, 0 = suppressed
   (p. 5, §4.6).

So the direct answer to "predictor or controller": **in the winning variant it
is both, and the paper's own ablations exist precisely to separate the two.**

**Ablation grid over H5** (p. 5, §5) — this is the more informative experiment:

- **5.1** — no binary mask. Schema predicts attention but does not control it.
  ("In other words, there is no control.")
- **5.2** — mask applied to the *action distribution* from the policy net.
  Control on action, not on attention.
- **5.3** — mask applied to the attention *output* `h1_m`. Control on the
  output of attention, not the mechanism.
- **5.4** — mask applied to the attention *scores* (the canonical H5). "The only
  one in which control is exerted on attention."
- **5.5** — the predicted `h1'_m` is fed to the policy *instead of* the real
  attention output.

**Training and tasks.** All architectures trained with PPO; shared component
architectures but no shared parameters "to make a fair comparison" (p. 3, §4.1).
Two gridworlds with RGB partial observation and 4-way discrete movement (p. 5-6):
*GhostRun* (reward = negative count of ghosts visible across all agents, minus 1
per step) and *MazeCleaners* (13x13, 2 agents, collective reward per tile
cleaned; OOD = randomized spawn). Evaluated on rewards over the final 100 of
1000 episodes, IID and OOD, 10 random seeds (p. 6, §6.1).

**Result.** H5 wins, and within H5 the winner is 5.4 (with 5.5 close). Section 5
says "Hypothesis 5 shows **marginal** advantages over other hypotheses" and
"hypotheses 5.4 **and 5.5** show the best results" (p. 5); section 6.1 escalates
the same data to "achieves the best performance" (p. 6). See §5 below — the
paper never reports a single number, so "by how much" has no answer.

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

The paper needs PPO, gradients, and a GRU. None of that transfers. What
transfers is the **taxonomy used as a design decision table**, and one specific
architectural commitment that survives translation.

**The translation.** In Navi-Brain, "attention" is not softmax weights — it is
*which records got selected into the bounded context capsule for one operation*.
Under that mapping:

- H1 = today's system. The capsule builder is the only attention story; there is
  no separable record of the selection.
- H3 = write a post-hoc digest of the selection into the step. **Navi-Brain
  already does a degenerate version of this**: `ExecutiveCore::…self_presence…`
  stuffs `need`, `gate`, and `recent_moments` into `ThreadStep.pre_state` before
  queueing the worker (`src/Core/ExecutiveCore.php`, around lines 2840-2850).
  That is an attention-schema-shaped record that nothing ever reads back.
- H5.4 = the schema's accumulated state gates the *next* capsule's candidate
  set.

**The one thing to steal, at implementation altitude.** A per-step
`attention_schema` JSON field on `thread_steps` (or a namespaced sub-key of the
existing `pre_state`), with four slots:

```
candidates      [record_id, ...]        everything eligible for this capsule
selected        [{record_id, score_components}]   what went in
suppressed      [{record_id, reason}]   what was masked out
predicted_cited [record_id, ...]        what the schema predicts the accepted
                                        proposal will actually rely on
```

Then two update rules, both deterministic PHP, no model:

1. **Prediction error (the `(h1'_m - h1_m)^2` analogue, zero training).** After
   the curator accepts or repairs a proposal, a deterministic comparator diffs
   `predicted_cited` against the record IDs the accepted output actually cited.
   Store the symmetric difference as `attention_schema_error`. This is a free,
   falsifiable, *observed* metric — it satisfies the "verification must observe a
   predicate, not self-report" rule, because it never asks the worker how well it
   attended. It also gives the agenda a real uncertainty term instead of a
   guessed one.
2. **The mask (H5.4).** Maintain per-record running counters
   `selected_count` and `useful_when_selected` (incremented only when the record
   appears in an accepted proposal's citations). Capsule assembly multiplies each
   candidate's retrieval score by a 0/1 include flag derived from a threshold on
   that ratio. This is the activator/suppressor pair degraded to a counter and a
   comparison.

**Cheap-version caveat the paper hands you implicitly.** Gumbel-softmax gives
H5.4 stochastic exploration for free; a deterministic PHP threshold does not. A
record suppressed once can never earn evidence that it was useful — the mask
becomes self-fulfilling and the capsule collapses onto whatever was retrieved in
the first ten episodes. **A deterministic port must add an epsilon floor**: a
fixed small quota of suppressed records forced back into every Nth capsule, plus
a decay on `selected_count` so old suppressions expire. This is not in the paper
and is required for the port to be sound.

**Also transferable, and free:** the structural rule that **the schema's output
must never enter the decision path.** H5 deliberately keeps `h2` off the policy
network; the schema only shapes *what the policy gets to see*. That is exactly
the invariant Navi-Brain wants — an attention schema may influence capsule
composition, never curator verdicts. This is an architecture-level safety
property, not a heuristic, and it comes free from copying H5.4's wiring.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

Four real ones. The first is the sharpest.

**(a) The winning variant is recurrent, and the doc's capsule spec has no slot
for carried attention state.** §2.3 (p. 2) says H3's specific defect is that
"internal control is not modeling attention in a recurrent way. This makes the
system less flexible, and therefore less efficient in keeping up with
ever-changing environments." The whole H3-vs-H5 gap the paper claims is the
recurrence. `docs/ongoing-cognition.md` lines 262-269 enumerate the capsule
contents — authority snapshot, thread reason/goal/ceiling, accepted evidence,
last observed result, one operation, output schema — and there is **no slot for
"what I attended to last time and whether that turned out to be the right
selection."** This does not contradict "fresh capsule, never a growing
transcript" — the recurrence can live entirely in SQLite counters rather than in
prompt text, which is arguably the cleaner realization. But it does mean the
capsule spec as written is H3-shaped, and the paper's claim is that H3 loses.
Adding one line to the capsule spec closes this.

**(b) The plan gates in the place the paper says is wrong.** Navi-Brain's design
puts control at the curator and the deterministic checks — i.e. on the
*proposal*, after the worker has run. In the paper's grid that is 5.3 (mask on
attention output) and arguably 5.2 (mask on the action distribution). Both are
reported as underperforming 5.4, which gates the *selection*. If that
generalizes, gating exclusively at the curator is structurally the weaker
placement, and Navi should gate at capsule assembly *as well as* at the curator.
I would not act on this as an instruction — it is a 2-agent 13x13 gridworld with
no reported effect size — but it is a testable hypothesis the doc currently does
not entertain at all, because capsule composition is implicit and ungoverned.

**(c) A missing gap in the doc's own gap list.** The six known gaps
(`docs/ongoing-cognition.md`) cover prompt genericity, unresolved `input_refs`,
undifferentiated proposals, missing curator, missing actuator, missing outcome
observer. None of them is "there is no policy for *what goes into the capsule*."
The agenda's value-of-computation layer chooses *which operation* to run; this
paper's mask operates one level below that, choosing *which items the chosen
operation sees*. That layer does not exist in Navi-Brain and is not on the build
order. Unresolved `input_refs` (gap 1) is a plumbing bug; capsule composition
policy is a separate, unnamed design decision.

**(d) It agrees with the plan on one thing worth banking.** H5 keeping `h2` off
the policy path independently arrives at the same invariant as "no worker writes
canonical memory." That is a mild confirmation, not a challenge.

**Where it does *not* challenge the plan:** nothing here touches authority,
corrigibility, or the continuity-as-invariant stance. The attention schema is a
filter over inputs and carries no effect-level implications.

**On the assigned question — is "a model of what the system is currently
attending to" worth materializing as a durable record?** This paper's answer is
narrower and more useful than a yes: **only if something consumes it as a control
signal.** H5.1 *is* the ablation for "write the schema down but don't wire it
back," and it is reported as losing to 5.4. A descriptive attention record with
no consumer is the condition the paper tested and rejected. So: build it as a
per-step field with a named next-step consumer and a prediction-error metric, or
do not build it. Do not ship the record and defer the consumer.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Preprint. Not peer reviewed.** arXiv:2305.17375v3 [cs.AI], 14 Jul 2023 (p. 1
sidebar). ICML template with no proceedings footer — a workshop-style submission
at best. The word "preliminary" appears in the abstract, in the title of §5, and
in the caption of every single results figure (Figures 6, 7, 8).

**What was measured.** Reward over episodes 900-1000, in GhostRun and
MazeCleaners, IID and OOD, 10 random seeds (p. 6, §6.1). Plus a continual-learning
variant where a ghost is added every 50 episodes (p. 6, §6.2; Figure 7).

**The headline is not supported by the experiment shown, on two counts.**

1. **There are no numbers anywhere in the paper.** No table, no means, no
   confidence intervals, no significance test, no effect size. Figures 6, 7, and 8
   are violin plots; the only quantitative information recoverable is the axis
   ranges (GhostRun IID roughly 0 to -6000, OOD 0 to -3000). "Which variant won
   and by how much" therefore has no answerable second half. The paper's own
   §5 word for the margin is "**marginal**" (p. 5) — which §6.1 then restates as
   "the best performance" (p. 6) without new data. Take the §5 wording as the
   honest one.
2. **The abstract's social claim is untested.** The abstract closes with
   "equipping artificial agents with a model of attention can enhance their
   **social intelligence**" (p. 1). §7 then concedes: "future work will test
   whether the control module is indeed capable of representing and predicting
   the attention of others" (p. 8). Nothing in the paper measures attention
   inference, opponent modeling, or theory of mind. Cooperative-task reward is
   not a social-intelligence measure — a purely single-agent improvement in
   attention control produces the identical reward curve.

**Baselines.** Entirely internal — the four rival hypotheses and the five H5
sub-variants. No external MARL baseline (no MAPPO, QMIX, or comparable), no
no-attention control, and critically **no single-agent condition**, which is the
one ablation that would separate "better resource control" from "better modeling
of others." That omission is not acknowledged.

**Limitations the authors admit** (p. 8): 2D-only environments; the GRU internal
control "is still far from being a rich, coherent, descriptive, and predictive
model of attention"; the linguistic / higher-cognition and theory-of-mind
directions are unexplored.

**Limitations they do not admit:**

- **Parameter count is not controlled.** §4.1 says shared components use "the
  same architectures … but without sharing parameters" (p. 3) — that equalizes
  architecture across hypotheses that *have* an IC module, but H1 has no IC at
  all, and H5 adds an activator MLP, a suppressor MLP, and a predictor head on
  top of the GRU. H5 beating H1 is partly confounded with H5 simply being bigger.
- Compute, hyperparameter search, and PPO settings are unreported; no code link.
- Agent count in GhostRun is never specified ("multiple agents").
- OOD is defined differently in the two environments (unspecified shift vs.
  random spawn), so §6.3's "replication" is not a clean replication.
- The mask is never inspected. Sparsity, entropy, stability, whether it correlates
  with anything task-meaningful — all unreported. For a paper whose thesis is
  "control on attention is what matters," never showing what the control does is
  a serious omission.
- `ContrastiveLoss` is squared error; the name is wrong.

**Net.** Treat this as a well-motivated **design taxonomy with a suggestive but
unmeasured preference ordering**. It is legitimate to cite as "here is the space
of ways a schema can relate to attention, and here is which corner the authors
found promising." It is not citable as evidence that an attention schema improves
performance by any amount.

## 6. Do not take

- **Any of the learning machinery.** PPO, GRU, Gumbel-softmax, MLP
  activator/suppressor, the squared-error predictor head. Navi-Brain does no
  gradient updates and never will. The mask must be a stored counter and a
  threshold.
- **H5.5 — reject on safety grounds regardless of its reward.** 5.5 feeds the
  schema's *prediction* `h1'_m` to the policy in place of the real attention
  output, and the paper reports it among the best performers (p. 5). Ported to
  Navi-Brain that means letting the worker act on the capsule the system
  *predicted* it would retrieve rather than the records actually retrieved. That
  is structurally identical to acting on hallucinated context, and it breaks the
  provenance chain that makes `memory:search` results auditable. 5.4 is the only
  acceptable variant even if 5.5 scored higher.
- **Do not put attention state in `self_model_facts`.** This is concrete and
  code-grounded: `src/Model/SelfModelFact.php` declares a **unique index on
  `fact_key`** and a required `evidence_event_id`. It is a one-durable-row-per-key
  store for stable, evidence-backed properties. A moment-to-moment "currently
  attending to X" fact would rewrite the same row on every heartbeat, make
  `confidence` meaningless, and detach the evidence pointer from anything
  falsifiable. Attention state belongs in `thread_steps`; `self_model_facts` is
  the right home only for a *stable* derived fact such as "capsule prediction
  error over the last 200 steps is 0.31," written occasionally with a real
  evidence event.
- **The MARL / social-coordination framing.** Navi is single-agent on one
  workstation. The "use your own schema to infer others' attention" prediction is
  the paper's stated motivation and is not tested even by the paper.
- **The consciousness/awareness register.** AST is a theory of subjective
  awareness; this paper wisely does not make that claim, and Navi-Brain's
  self-presence worker explicitly forbids claiming consciousness, sentience, or
  personhood. Keep the mechanism, drop the philosophy.
- **Any quantitative claim sourced to this paper.** There is nothing to quote.

## 7. Key citations

- The five-way taxonomy of attention/internal-control relations, adapted from
  Webb & Graziano 2015 — identity, IC-first, attention-first, parallel, and
  recurrent schema (Figure 1, p. 3; described §2.1-2.5, p. 2).
- H5's schema does prediction *and* control simultaneously: `h2` predicts the
  attention output via `ContrastiveLoss = (h1'_m - h1_m)^2` **and** generates a
  binary mask `AttScore' = AttScore ∘ M_att` over attention scores (p. 5, §4.6).
- H5's schema output is deliberately excluded from the policy path: "the output
  is not used as an input to the policy" (p. 5, §4.6) — an input-selection
  filter, not a decision contributor.
- The ablation that matters most for a system considering a purely descriptive
  record: "Hypothesis 5.1: There is no binary mask. In other words, there is no
  control" — reported as inferior to 5.4 (p. 5, §5).
- 5.4 is singled out as "the only one in which control is exerted on attention,"
  with control-on-action (5.2) and control-on-attention-output (5.3) both losing
  to it (p. 5, §5).
- The margin, in the authors' own words: "Our preliminary results suggest that
  Hypothesis 5 shows **marginal** advantages over other hypotheses" (p. 5, §5) —
  contrast with the stronger "achieves the best performance" at p. 6, §6.1, on the
  same data.
- Recurrence is the claimed differentiator: H3 fails because "internal control is
  not modeling attention in a recurrent way. This makes the system less flexible"
  (p. 2, §2.3).
- The social-intelligence headline is admitted untested: "future work will test
  whether the control module is indeed capable of representing and predicting the
  attention of others" (p. 8, §7), against the abstract's claim of enhanced
  social intelligence (p. 1).
- Experimental scale: two 2D gridworlds, 10 random seeds, rewards from the final
  100 of 1000 episodes, IID and OOD (p. 6, §6.1); no numeric results are reported
  anywhere in the paper.
