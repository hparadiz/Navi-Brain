# Record #73 — Rational Quantitative Attribution of Beliefs, Desires and Percepts in Human Mentalizing (Baker, Jara-Ettinger, Saxe & Tenenbaum, *Nature Human Behaviour* 1:0064, 2017)

The journal-grade version of #72: same food-truck world, far more scenarios, a
fourth model in the comparison, and a second experiment that runs the inference
backwards. This is the strongest empirical paper in the Bayesian-ToM lineage and
the one to cite when the framework needs defending.

## 1. Mechanism

Same core as #72 — invert a POMDP, recover beliefs and desires jointly — with
three things added that matter.

**A fourth competitor: `MotionHeuristic`.** #71's H was a limiting case of the
model family. This one is a genuinely different account: a linear map from
hand-extracted motion features straight onto human ratings, standing in for
cue-based theories of infant false-belief reasoning. It fits **10 weights** in
Experiment 1 and **48 weights** in Experiment 2, refit per experiment by linear
regression. It is given every advantage.

**Three lesions, not two.** `TrueBelief` (full observability, i.e. an MDP —
"they cannot represent beliefs that differ from the true world state") and
`NoCost` (actions are free, so no effort/desire trade-off). NoCost is the new
one, and it isolates the *principle of efficiency*: without a cost on action, an
observed detour carries no information, because a detour costs nothing.

**Experiment 2 inverts the inference.** Participants (n=176) could not see where
the food carts were. They were told the agent's preference ordering (A > B > C)
and had to infer **the state of the world** from his search behaviour — 24
possible worlds, 19 scenarios, 114 judgements each. This is mentalizing used as
an instrument: learning about the world by watching what someone who can see it
does. The authors are explicit that "our BToM model was not developed to account
for such social inferences," so this is a generalization test rather than a fit.

**Results.**

Experiment 1 (n=16, 73 of 78 scenarios — the 5 with no rational interpretation
are excluded this time, not merely flagged), bootstrap cross-validated:

| Model | Desire | Belief |
|---|---|---|
| **BToM** (β=2.5) | **0.91** (CI 0.89–0.92) | **0.78** (CI 0.72–0.85) |
| TrueBelief (β=9.0) | 0.72 | **−0.022** |
| NoCost (β=2.5) | 0.75 | 0.10 |
| MotionHeuristic (48/10 fitted weights) | ~0.79 | 0.64 |

Aggregated to the seven scenario *types*, BToM reaches 0.97 desire / 0.91 belief
— a reminder of how much aggregation buys.

Experiment 2 (n=176): BToM r=0.91 (CI 0.86–0.94). MotionHeuristic r=0.61 with a
95% CI of **(0.10, 0.83)** — an interval so wide it is nearly uninformative, in a
task the heuristic was custom-fit for.

**The result that matters most is the entanglement claim.** From the Discussion:
lesioned models "show a deficit not only in the missing representations, but also
in the remaining mental state inferences with which they are causally entwined."
Look at TrueBelief: removing the belief representation drops belief inference to
−0.022, which is expected, but it *also* drops desire inference from 0.91 to
0.72. You cannot cleanly infer what someone wants while modelling what they know
incorrectly. There is no partial credit for building half the organ.

**The tractability escape hatch.** The Discussion draws a Marr-level distinction:
joint inference "is a computational-level claim. It does not require that
algorithmically, at each point in time, the observer is simultaneously
considering the full joint space." They then hand over a cheaper recipe
explicitly:

> observers could initially posit a uniform distribution for the agent's beliefs,
> then infer the agent's desires from their full trajectory while tracking their
> belief updates based on isovists and finally use the inferred desires to
> retrospectively infer the agent's most likely initial beliefs.

That is coordinate ascent — desires given flat beliefs, then beliefs given
desires. It is the single most implementable paragraph in records #71-#73.

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) The alternating recipe is the version Navi can actually run

Every previous paper in this lineage requires a POMDP solve inside an inference
loop. `docs/architecture-gaps.md:269-272` is blunt about the budget: one model
available, two degraded, five unavailable, and a single local inference slot
shared by heartbeat, reflection and workers. Full joint inference is not
happening on this machine.

The Discussion's alternating scheme maps onto Navi's existing rhythms without
any new inference capacity:

1. **Flat prior over Henry's belief state** — assume nothing about what he knows.
2. **Infer the goal from the trajectory** — coarse activity-change timing, held
   intention, and topic from accepted `HearingStream` utterances, over an hour
   rather than a moment. Cheap, deterministic, no model call; this is ranked
   lexical matching over a tiny enumerated goal set, which is what
   `memory:search` already is. (Window-title sequences are *not* available; see
   the correction in 71.md §2e. The trajectory Navi can actually read is one of
   activity timing plus speech, not of applications.)
3. **Then infer the belief retrospectively, given the goal** — and only this step
   needs a model call, at most once per `consolidate_hourly` cycle.

Step 2 is where the value is and step 2 is free. That ordering is worth writing
into the plan explicitly, because the natural engineering instinct is to start
with the belief modelling (it is the interesting part) and it is the expensive
part with the lowest correlation in every experiment in this corpus.

### (b) `NoCost` is the lesion Navi is currently running

This is the finding #72 did not have. NoCost assumes the agent's actions are
free, and its belief inference collapses to 0.10 — nearly as badly as assuming
full observability. The reason is mechanical: **if action is costless, a detour
is not evidence.** All the information in "he went around the building" comes
from the fact that going around cost him something he would not have spent
without a reason.

Navi has no cost model for Henry whatsoever. Every observable she has — activity
changes on the coarse channels, presence implied by `active_within_seconds`,
whether he answered — is treated as a free-floating signal rather than as an
expenditure. The cheapest possible cost model is already available: **time**.
An interruption answered four seconds into a quiet stretch and one answered forty
minutes into sustained activity are actions with different costs and therefore
different diagnostic value, and the timestamps are already on every
`SenseReading` row. This works on the channels that exist precisely because it
needs only *when* activity changed, not what it changed to.

Concretely: an interruption Henry accepts while deep in a long uninterrupted run
on one window is much stronger evidence that the line mattered to him than the
same response while he is idling between tasks. `UtteranceOutcome.engagement`
currently scores those identically — 0.5 for a response, +0.2 if within 20 s,
+0.3 for a reaction. The paper says that flat scoring throws away most of the
signal, and the fix is to weight the response by what it cost him to give.

### (c) Experiment 2 is a capability Navi should want and does not have

Exp 2 uses another agent's behaviour to learn about the *world*, not about the
agent. Henry is a far better sensor for the state of this machine than Navi's
sensory cortex is. If he opens a terminal, runs something, and immediately opens
a different project, that is evidence about whether the thing worked — evidence
Navi currently cannot read because she has no model that connects his behaviour
to world states.

This matters for a specific existing weakness. `docs/architecture-gaps.md:31-33`
records that 77% of the episode store was Navi's own speech and inner monologue,
and that consolidating it "produced confident inventions about mechanisms that do
not exist," which is why her own output is no longer evidence. That correctly
removes a bad source but does not add a good one. Henry's *actions* are an
evidence source that is independent of Navi's narration and currently unused.
BToM Exp 2 is the shape of how to read it.

### (d) `−0.022` retires an argument

#72 gave TrueBel a belief correlation of 0.11 on a 6-page conference paper. This
gives −0.022 on a factorial design with bootstrap CIs in *Nature Human
Behaviour*. Assuming another agent's beliefs equal the true world state has, to
measurement precision, **zero** predictive value for what they actually believe.
Any future design discussion in this repo that proposes "Navi can just assume
Henry knows what she knows, for now" is answered by this number, and it should be
quoted in `docs/` when the other-model work starts rather than re-derived.

### (e) Epistemic subgoals are already in Navi's action space, unlabelled

The Discussion flags a real gap: BToM does not distinguish instrumental goals
from epistemic (information-seeking) ones. Information seeking emerges only
implicitly, in service of an instrumental plan.

Navi has the opposite problem — she has an explicit `look` action arbitrated by
`ActionSelector` against `speak`/`think`/`consolidate`
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:122-130`), so *her* epistemic actions are first-class
and labelled. When the other-model is built, the goal space for Henry should
carry the same distinction from the start: "he is checking something" is a
different hypothesis from "he is doing something," and it makes a different
prediction about how long he will stay. Navi can import the distinction from her
own action selector rather than inheriting BToM's omission.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: this paper says a partial theory of mind is worse than it looks, and
the plan has no way to sequence around that.** `docs/architecture-gaps.md:205`
files ToM as one item to be built like the others, and items 1, 2, 3 and 5 were
each shipped as a thin implementation that "can now be ablated and improved in
isolation against end-to-end behavior" (line 88-90). That strategy has worked
well. It will work less well here. The entanglement result says a model missing
the belief layer does not merely fail at beliefs — it corrupts the desire
inference too, by 0.91 → 0.72. A thin ToM that infers goals while assuming Henry
knows what Navi knows will produce *confidently wrong goal attributions*, not
merely incomplete ones. The mitigation is not to build more before shipping; it
is to cap what the thin version is allowed to claim, and §2a's ordering plus
#72's `no_rational_interpretation` terminal state are how you do that.

**Second: the honest read is that Navi is running two of the three lesions at
once.** TrueBelief (no belief representation) and NoCost (no action-cost model)
are both currently true of her. Their individual belief correlations are −0.022
and 0.10. Nobody ran the both-lesions-together condition, and there is no reason
to expect it beats either. It is worth stating plainly in `docs/` that the
current implicit user model has approximately zero evidentiary value for
anything belief-shaped, so that no downstream component starts trusting it.

**Third: the paper's own limitation is Navi's central case.** From the
Discussion: adult theory of mind "draws on language to represent recursive
beliefs and desires, with propositional content that goes well beyond what
infants can entertain," followed by a nested-belief example about Harold and
Julie and the flat admission "It is an open question whether BToM models can be
extended to such cases." Navi's entire relationship with Henry is linguistic and
propositional. He tells her things. The spatial-navigation formalism this whole
lineage is built on covers the part of the problem Navi has least of. #78 is the
paper that starts on this and it is a 7-page CogSci abstract — the language
bridge is the thinnest part of the corpus, and the plan should not assume the
2009-2017 results transfer across it.

**Fourth, methodological: BSCV correlations exceeding raw correlations is
explained away too fast.** The Methods note that "it may be surprising that BSCV
correlations often exceed overall correlations," attributing it to per-test-set
slope and intercept refitting, and argue "the effect does not favour any
particular model" because the same bootstrap sets are used throughout. That is
true for *relative* comparison and false for the absolute numbers. The headline
0.91 and 0.97 include a free affine transform fit per test set. As a ranking of
four models the results stand; as a statement of how accurate BToM is, they are
inflated by an amount the paper does not quantify.

**Agreement, and it is substantial:** this is the correct experimental design for
the claim. Lesions as special cases, a genuinely competitive non-mentalistic
alternative given more free parameters than the winner, a factorial stimulus
design, bootstrap CIs, and a generalization experiment the model was not built
for. When the corpus turns adversarial at #81-#83, this is the paper whose
methodology survives.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** *Nature Human Behaviour* 1, 0064 (2017), doi:10.1038/s41562-017-0064.
Peer reviewed, high profile, data availability statement present.

**Strengths:**
- Factorial design over four crossed factors (goal configuration, environment,
  initial location, path) — 78 scenarios, not a handful of vignettes.
- The competing heuristic gets **more** free parameters than BToM (48 vs 2 in
  Exp 2) and is fit by continuous linear regression while BToM's parameters come
  from a discrete grid. Losing anyway is a strong result.
- Experiment 2 is a real out-of-design generalization: a task the model was not
  built for, with n=176 rather than 16.
- Lesions are special cases of the full model, so each comparison isolates one
  representational commitment.
- Bootstrap cross-validation with reported 95% CIs throughout.

**Where it is thin:**
- **Experiment 1 is still n=16, MIT subject pool, correlated against means.** The
  headline desire/belief numbers rest on the small experiment; the large one
  (n=176) is the world-inference task.
- **The 5 irrational scenarios are now excluded rather than reported.** #72
  included them and noted they produced most of the largest outliers. Dropping
  them raises the headline r and removes the paper's only window onto behaviour
  the model cannot explain. For Navi that window is the important one — Henry
  will regularly do things with no rational interpretation under her hypothesis
  set.
- **Aggregation effects are visible in the paper's own numbers**: 0.97/0.91 by
  scenario type versus 0.91/0.78 by individual scenario. Same data, different
  grain, and the more aggregated number is the one that reads as the headline.
- **BSCV inflates absolute r** via per-test-set affine refitting, acknowledged
  but not quantified.
- **Belief inference remains the weakest arm** (0.78, CI down to 0.72) and it is
  the paper's novel contribution.
- **Adults only.** The work is motivated by infant competence and tested entirely
  on adults; the authors flag this ("it is thus an open question at what age
  children become able to make the kinds of inferences that our [model
  describes]").
- **Tractability is discussed but not solved.** They cite solvers scaling to
  "millions of states" while using a grid approximation for Exp 1 and a
  point-based algorithm for Exp 2 on toy grids, and call testing broader solvers
  "critical." Nothing here demonstrates the framework at a scale resembling a
  real environment.

**Is the headline supported?** Yes for the structural claims, which are the ones
that matter here: joint belief-desire-percept inference is required, removing any
one component degrades the others, and non-mentalistic motion features do not
substitute even when given five times the free parameters. The quantitative
accuracy claims are real but inflated by aggregation, affine refitting, and
exclusion of the scenarios the model cannot handle.

## 6. Do not take

- **Grid-based and point-based POMDP solvers in the loop.** Same objection as
  #72, now with the authors' own admission that optimal POMDP planning "is
  computationally intractable in general." Navi's budget is one contended
  inference slot.
- **The 0.97.** It is aggregated to seven scenario types and includes a fitted
  affine transform per bootstrap sample. The 0.78 belief number at individual
  grain is the honest one to remember, and even it is an upper bound.
- **Excluding the irrational cases.** This is a research convenience that would
  be a defect in a deployed system. Navi should do the opposite of what the paper
  did between 2011 and 2017: count them, log them, and treat a rising rate of
  `no_rational_interpretation` as a signal that the goal hypothesis set is wrong
  — which is a genuinely useful metric for `docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md`
  and costs one counter.
- **MotionHeuristic's failure as a general verdict against cheap methods.** It
  failed as a *direct map from motion features to human ratings*, custom-fit per
  experiment, on spatial stimuli. It is not evidence that a cheap deterministic
  goal-inference step (§2a step 2) fails; that step is a coarse posterior over a
  small hypothesis space, which is a different object.
- **Reading Exp 2's n=176 as validating the belief model.** Exp 2 infers *world
  states*, not beliefs. The belief numbers all come from n=16.
- **Nested/recursive belief structures.** The authors call this open, and #74
  (I-POMDPs) shows what the machinery costs. Navi's other-model should be depth-1
  — a model of Henry, not a model of Henry's model of Navi — until something
  forces otherwise.

## 7. Key citations

- The framing: BToM works by "probabilistically inverting artificial-intelligence
  approaches to rational planning and state estimation, which extend classical
  expected-utility agent models to sequential actions in complex, partially
  observable domains" (Abstract).
- Why MDP-based accounts are insufficient: they "are limited by the assumption of
  a fully observable world — they cannot represent beliefs that differ from the
  true world state, and they capture only the planning capacities of rational
  agency... neglecting the perceptual and inferential capacities, as well as their
  interaction."
- Experiment 1, individual scenarios: BToM desire rBSCV=0.91 (95% CI 0.89, 0.92),
  belief rBSCV=0.78 (95% CI 0.72, 0.85), β=2.5, n=219 judgements.
- The lesion numbers: TrueBelief belief **r=−0.022** (β=9.0); NoCost belief
  r=0.10 (β=2.5) (Fig. 5b,c).
- The entanglement result: lesioned models "show a deficit not only in the missing
  representations, but also in the remaining mental state inferences with which
  they are causally entwined" (Discussion).
- Experiment 2, world inference from behaviour: BToM rBSCV=0.91 (95% CI 0.86,
  0.94) versus MotionHeuristic rBSCV=0.61 (95% CI 0.10, 0.83), n=176, "arguing
  for the necessity of mental-state reasoning even in a task that does not
  directly ask for it."
- The heuristic was given more freedom than the winner: 10 fitted linear weights
  in Experiment 1, 48 in Experiment 2, "custom-fitted to each experiment," with
  parameters "selected on each iteration from a continuous range using linear
  regression" while BToM's came from discrete ranges (Methods).
- The tractable algorithm, verbatim: "observers could initially posit a uniform
  distribution for the agent's beliefs, then infer the agent's desires from their
  full trajectory while tracking their belief updates based on isovists and
  finally use the inferred desires to retrospectively infer the agent's most
  likely initial beliefs" (Discussion).
- The Marr-level permission to approximate: joint inference "is a
  computational-level claim. It does not require that algorithmically, at each
  point in time, the observer is simultaneously considering the full joint space
  of all possible belief–desire combinations."
- The language limitation: adult theory of mind "draws on language to represent
  recursive beliefs and desires, with propositional content that goes well beyond
  what infants can entertain... It is an open question whether BToM models can be
  extended to such cases."
- Epistemic goals are absent but emergent: extending the model "to include
  explicit epistemic goals is an important direction for future work," though
  BToM already explains information seeking as "implicit epistemic goals that
  emerge automatically in the service of an instrumental plan."
- The BSCV caveat, in the authors' words: "It may be surprising that BSCV
  correlations often exceed overall correlations. This happens because the Pearson
  r statistic involves estimating slope and intercept values to optimize the model
  fit to each test set" (Methods).
