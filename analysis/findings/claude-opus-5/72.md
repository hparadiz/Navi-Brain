# Record #72 — Bayesian Theory of Mind: Modeling Joint Belief-Desire Attribution (Baker, Saxe & Tenenbaum, CogSci 2011)

The sequel to #71 that adds the piece #71 explicitly deferred: beliefs. Six
pages, one experiment, and the cleanest lesion study in this half of the corpus.

## 1. Mechanism

#71 assumed Belief = Environment. BToM removes that assumption by upgrading the
forward model from an MDP to a **POMDP** and inverting it, recovering the
agent's belief state and reward function jointly.

Two principles, both named in Fig. 1(a):

- **Principle of rational belief** — how beliefs arise from observations.
  Bayesian updating: `b_t(y) ∝ P(o_t | x_t, y) · b_{t-1}(y)`.
- **Principle of rational action** — how beliefs and desires produce actions.
  Softmax over the lookahead Q-function: `P(a|b,x,y) ∝ exp(β·Q_LA(b,x,y,a))`.
  Same β as #71: "the degree of determinism with which the agent executes its
  policy."

**The isovist is the load-bearing mechanism.** The agent's observation at each
step is the polygonal 360° line-of-sight region from its location (Davis &
Benedikt, 1979). `P(o|x,y)` encodes which world states are consistent with what
is visible from `x`. Observation noise is a single parameter ν — "as if the agent
failed to notice something that should otherwise be visible." This is the formal
answer to *what could this agent have known, given where it was standing*, and
it is the single most portable idea in the paper.

**Inference.** The observer maintains a DBN (Fig. 1(b)) over the agent's
desires `R`, states `X`, observations `O`, beliefs `B`, and actions `A` through
time, and computes the joint posterior marginal `P(b_t, r | x_{1:T}, y)` —
explicitly "analogous to the forward-backward algorithm in hidden Markov
models." Note that inference runs over `x_{1:T}` with `T ≥ t`: **beliefs at time
t are inferred using evidence from after t.** The experiment then asks subjects
to rate what the agent believed *before it set off*, judged from the whole path.

**Domain.** A hungry grad student crossing campus for a food truck. Three trucks
(Korean, Lebanese, Mexican), two parking spots, buildings occluding one spot.
Actions: N/S/E/W/Stay/Eat. Each action costs 1; reward comes from `Eat` at a
truck; everything stops once eaten. Belief and reward spaces are discretized on
a grid of resolution 7, rewards ranging −20 to 100.

The worked example (Fig. 2) is the paper's best argument. The student passes the
visible Korean truck — so he wants L or M, and believes his truck is likely
behind the building. He then rounds the corner, sees Lebanese, and turns back to
Korean. Conclusion: **he wanted Mexican most**, a truck that is not in the scene
at all. Preference for an absent option, recovered from a change of direction.

**Two lesions, each removing one representational commitment:**

- **TrueBel** — the agent knows everything; state fully observable. This is
  exactly #71's Fig. 1(b) assumption, now run as a control.
- **NoObs** — the agent has an initial belief but never updates it. Belief
  exists; the observation process does not.

**Results** (n=17, 1 excluded → 16; correlations against per-condition means):

| | Desire | Belief |
|---|---|---|
| **BToM** | **0.90** | **0.76** |
| TrueBel | 0.67 | 0.11 |
| NoObs | 0.61 | 0.39 |

TrueBel's belief correlation of **0.11** is the number worth carrying: it is
what you get when you predict another agent's beliefs by reporting the true
state of the world. People do not do that. NoObs at 0.39 shows that *having* a
belief representation without a model of how it updates from observation buys
you about half of nothing.

**The asymmetry.** Desires are inferred more accurately than beliefs (0.90 vs
0.76), and the authors connect this to a classic developmental finding: goal
inference is present around five months, robust false-belief reasoning only
around age four. Their mechanistic gloss: "Actions often point with salient
perceptual cues directly toward an agent's goal... In contrast, no motion so
directly indicates what she believes to be inside the glass."

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) `couldHaveLanded` is a one-bit isovist, and this paper says build the rest

`SocialFeedback::closeWindows()` already computes an observation-possibility
test before scoring evidence:

```php
$couldHaveLanded = $outcome->present_at_utterance === true
    || $responses !== [] || $reactions > 0;
```

That is `P(o|x,y)` collapsed to a single boolean. And `present_at_utterance`
itself comes from `DesktopAwareness::presence()`, which returns
`using_computer` as `true`/`false`/`null` derived from KIdleTime `active` /
`away` / `unknown` plus an `active_within_seconds` window.

The paper's structure says what the richer version looks like, and every
component is already observable on this machine:

- **Was the utterance audible?** Pet TTS actually played, no other audio
  dominating.
- **Was he in a conversation with someone else?** `HearingStream` already does
  meeting detection — `docs/architecture-gaps.md:20-23` describes density, run
  length and gap over a real 26-minute call, 66 chunks, 9 reaching attention.
  A detected meeting is an occluding building.
- **Was he at the machine, and how recently?** `active_within_seconds`, already
  a continuous quantity being thresholded into a bit.
- **Was he doing something else?** The coarse activity channels
  (`shell_activity`, `terminal_activity`, `process_activity`,
  `conversation_activity`) each carry the age of the newest write, so recent
  activity elsewhere is observable even though *what* he is doing is not.

Each of those is a term in an isovist for Henry. The upgrade is not new
plumbing; it is refusing to collapse four continuous signals into one boolean
before the evidence is weighed.

### (b) TrueBel r=0.11 is the argument against Navi's default stance

Navi's implicit model of Henry is TrueBel: he is treated as sharing her world
state. She speaks about `AX200 capture path` or `the photo identity index`
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:47`) with no representation of whether he currently
knows what she is referring to, whether he heard the previous line, or whether
he has the context that makes the line land. When a line does not land, the
descriptor takes the blame.

0.11 is what that assumption is worth for belief attribution in a domain where
the ground truth was fully available to the experimenters. It is the strongest
single number in the corpus for "you cannot skip the belief layer," and it costs
nothing to state as a design constraint: **the other-model must carry a
representation of what Henry has and has not been told, separate from what Navi
knows.** Navi already has the raw material — every `UtteranceOutcome` row is a
record of something said to him, and `response_text` records what came back.

### (c) The desire/belief confidence asymmetry should be a hard prior

0.90 vs 0.76 is not a rounding difference; it reflects that actions are
diagnostic of goals and only indirectly diagnostic of beliefs. Navi persists
claims with confidence (`SelfModelFact` carries evidence and confidence, and
`docs/architecture-gaps.md:154` sets a 0.7 completion bar for intentions because
"a finished intention is a claim about the world").

An other-model should inherit that discipline with a **ceiling differential**:
belief attributions about Henry get a lower confidence cap than goal
attributions, structurally, not by tuning. A stored claim of the form "he
believes X" should never outrank a claim of the form "he is working on Y" from
the same evidence.

### (d) Retrospective belief inference maps onto the existing window close

Subjects rated what the agent believed *before setting off*, using the whole
subsequent path — and the model's inference is explicitly forward-backward over
`x_{1:T}`, `T ≥ t`. That is the same temporal shape as `closeWindows()`, which
runs after a 120 s window has elapsed and assigns a value to a past moment.

So the smoothing structure Navi needs is already where her social measurement
lives. What is missing is that the current close revises only a *score*; a
BToM-shaped close would revise a *belief about his state at the moment of
speaking* — including "he had not registered that I was talking to him yet."

### (e) The irrational trials are an impasse, and the impasse machinery exists

Five of 78 trials "had no rational interpretation under the BToM model," and
they "account for most of the largest outliers" in Fig. 3. The model has no way
to say *I cannot explain this*, so it emits a bad posterior instead.

`DecisionStateMachine` already refuses to do this in Navi's own reasoning:
`impasse()` (src/Core/DecisionStateMachine.php:663) records
`decision_cycle.impasse` when "No proposed terminal action satisfied an
installed adapter contract" (line 423), and `docs/architecture-gaps.md:198`
frames it as recording an impasse "rather than broadening authority."

An other-model must have the same terminal state. When Henry's behavior has no
rational interpretation under the current goal hypotheses — and it regularly
will, because he is a person with reasons Navi cannot see — the correct output is
`no_rational_interpretation`, not a confidently wrong posterior over a goal.
This is cheap to build and it is what keeps a user model from becoming a
confident fiction. It is also the mechanism that makes §2c's low belief ceiling
enforceable rather than aspirational.

## 3. Seam

Also touches:
- `social-feedback` — `couldHaveLanded` is the isovist stub (§2a).
- `perception` / `HearingStream` — meeting detection is occlusion detection.
- `self-model` — confidence ceilings for attributed beliefs (§2c).
- `decision-cycle` — `impasse()` is the existing analogue of the irrational
  trials (§2e).
- `speech-gate` — a belief state for Henry is what "is this a good moment"

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: `docs/architecture-gaps.md` treats presence as a data-quality
problem, and this paper says it is a modelling problem.** Line 274 reads
"Engagement evidence is unreliable while the microphone is intermittent and
presence detection is coarse. Do not tune anything against it yet." The implied
fix is better sensors. BToM's answer is different and cheaper: **the
observation process is part of the model, not a defect in it.** ν exists
precisely to represent an agent failing to notice something visible. You do not
need a reliable microphone to model Henry; you need to represent the microphone's
unreliability as part of what determines whether he could have heard. Waiting for
clean presence data before building the user model has the dependency backwards.

**Second: the paper's own worked example is an argument against the descriptor
table, and it is stronger than the one #71 makes.** The Fig. 2 inference
recovers a preference for a truck *that never appears in the scene*. Applied to
Navi: the informative case is not "which of Navi's descriptors correlates with
engagement," it is "what was he hoping I would say instead." `descriptorStats()`
can only rank options that were taken. A latent-preference model can rank an
option that was never exercised. That is a categorically different capability and
it is the one that would actually change what Navi says.

**Third: β and ν were not really fit.** "Parameter fits are not meant to be
precise; we report the best values found among several drawn from a coarse
grid." β=1.0, ν=0.1. For a paper whose headline is a correlation comparison
against two lesions, hand-picking the free parameters of the winner from a
coarse grid — while the lesions have only β — is a genuine asymmetry in the
comparison's favor. It does not overturn the 0.11, which is a structural result
rather than a fitted one, but it means the 0.90/0.76 numbers are ceilings.

**Fourth: they dropped 31% of trials after the fact.** Only "Ending" judgment
points were analyzed — 54 of 78 — because "debriefing of subjects suggested that
many were confused by the 'Middle' judgment point trials." The Middle trials are
the *online* condition: judgment made partway through, before the agent's path
resolves. That is precisely the condition Navi operates in. She has to decide
whether to speak now, mid-path, not after the fact. The paper's evidence is
weakest exactly where Navi's use case sits, and nothing downstream in this
corpus fixes that — #75 is the paper that takes the online case seriously.

**Agreement:** the DBN in Fig. 1(b) is the right target shape, and Navi's
existing separation of observables from interpretation in `UtteranceOutcome`
(the schema comment splits them explicitly) is the correct instinct at the
storage layer.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** CogSci 2011 proceedings — a refereed conference paper, 6 pages, not a
journal article. Lighter review than #71 and #73, and it shows in the reporting.

**Strengths:** the lesion design is excellent. TrueBel and NoObs are constructed
as *special cases of BToM* rather than as separate models, so the comparison
isolates one representational commitment at a time. Randomization is thorough —
scrambled truck labels, vertical/horizontal reflection, random agent colors and
names per trial, explicitly so subjects would not generalize one student's
mental states to the next.

**Where it is thin:**
- **n = 16** after exclusion, MIT subject pool again. Correlations against
  condition means, so the same aggregation inflation as #71.
- **54 of 78 trials analyzed**, with the excluded condition chosen after seeing
  that subjects found it confusing. Post-hoc trial exclusion on a 6-page paper
  with no preregistration.
- **The 5 "irrational" trials account for most of the largest outliers** and are
  reported separately. They are not excluded, to the authors' credit, but the
  headline r values include a domain where the model is known to be inapplicable.
- **Parameters chosen from a coarse grid**, admittedly imprecise, and the
  winning model has two while the lesions have one.
- **Belief inference is the weak arm** at 0.76 — described in the text as "less
  well but still reasonably." That is the novel contribution of the paper, and
  it is the number that carries the least.
- **No subgoal or goal-sequence representation.** The authors flag it directly:
  the model produces the B1 behavior without "positing these explicit subgoals or
  the corresponding parse of the agent's motion into subsequences." For Navi,
  whose subject works on many projects at once and switches between them, an
  unstructured single-reward model is a poor fit — and #71's M3 result says
  subgoals matter.
- **Tractability is not discussed.** Point-based value iteration over a
  discretized belief space, for each hypothesized reward function, over a small
  grid world. The cost of this is real and the paper never states it.

**Is the headline supported?** The structural claims — you need beliefs, and you
need an observation model that updates them — are well supported by the lesion
contrasts, and 0.11 vs 0.76 is far too large a gap to be an artifact of the
parameter asymmetry. The quantitative claim that human ToM "comes surprisingly
close to an ideal rational model" is supported for desires and only partially for
beliefs, on a small aggregated sample, with a third of trials removed.

## 6. Do not take

- **Point-based value iteration over a discretized belief space, per reward
  hypothesis.** Grid resolution 7 across beliefs and rewards, with a POMDP solve
  inside the loop. `docs/architecture-gaps.md:269-272` reports one inference slot
  already contended by heartbeat, reflection and workers. This does not fit and
  should not be attempted; #75 and #80 exist because this does not scale.
- **The isovist literally.** Navi is not inferring what Henry can see on his
  screen, and should not be. `LocalVisualObserver` runs a privacy-filtered,
  no-archive vision gate for a reason. The transferable idea is the *observation
  possibility function*, computed over audio reachability, presence recency, and
  meeting state — not over pixels.
- **`Eat` ends the episode.** The food-truck MDP terminates on goal achievement
  and all rewards and costs cease. Henry's day has no terminal state; he finishes
  a task and starts another. A model with an absorbing goal state will keep
  inferring that he is about to be done.
- **Single static reward function per agent.** `R` is inferred once and held
  fixed across the trial. #71's own Experiment 2 says goals change and that
  representing the change is worth 0.57 → 0.95. Do not import 2011's static `R`
  after reading 2009's γ result.
- **Belief attributions as durable memory without expiry.** A belief inferred
  about Henry at 14:00 is not evidence about him at 18:00. If these are written
  anywhere near the semantic tier they need a decay or an explicit validity
  window, or consolidation will promote a snapshot into a standing claim about a
  person — the same failure mode `docs/architecture-gaps.md:31-33` already caught
  once, when 77% of the episode store was Navi's own speech and consolidating it
  "produced confident inventions about mechanisms that do not exist."
- **The 0.90/0.76 numbers as a target.** Coarse-grid parameters, aggregated
  ratings, 54 trials, one lab.

## 7. Key citations

- The upgrade over #71: BToM "expresses the predictive model of belief- and
  desire-dependent action at the heart of ToM as a partially observable Markov
  decision process, and reconstructs an agent's joint belief state and reward
  function using Bayesian inference" (Abstract).
- Two principles: the principle of rational belief formalizes "how the agent's
  belief is affected by observations in terms of Bayesian belief updating"; the
  principle of rational action is rational POMDP planning (Computational
  Framework).
- Belief update: `b_t(y) ∝ P(o_t|x_t,y) b_{t-1}(y)`; policy:
  `P(a|b,x,y) ∝ exp(β Q_LA(b,x,y,a))`.
- The observation model is line-of-sight: observations "are represented by the
  isovist from the agent's location: a polygonal region containing all points of
  the environment within a 360-degree field of view," with noise ν modelling the
  agent having "failed to notice something that should otherwise be visible."
- Inference is smoothing, not filtering: the posterior is `P(b_t, r | x_{1:T}, y)`
  for `T ≥ t`, "analogous to the forward-backward algorithm in hidden Markov
  models."
- The lesion result: BToM desire 0.90 / belief 0.76; TrueBel 0.67 / **0.11**;
  NoObs 0.61 / 0.39 (Fig. 3 table). "TrueBel's belief predictions are based on
  the actual state of the world in each trial; the poor correlation with people's
  judgments demonstrates that people did not simply refer to the true world state
  in their belief attributions."
- Why NoObs fails: "without a theory of how beliefs are updated from
  observations it must posit highly implausible initial beliefs."
- Preference for an absent option: after the agent checks behind the building,
  finds Lebanese and returns to Korean, "it also seems likely that the agent
  wants M more than either K or L, despite M being absent from the scene!"
- The desire/belief asymmetry: "inferences from actions back to beliefs will
  frequently be more difficult and indirect than inferences about desires...
  no motion so directly indicates what she believes to be inside the glass."
- Parameters were not carefully fit: "Parameter fits are not meant to be precise;
  we report the best values found among several drawn from a coarse grid."
- Post-hoc trial exclusion: "many were confused by the 'Middle' judgment point
  trials... our analyses only include trials from the 'Ending' judgment point
  condition, which accounted for 54 out of the 78 total trials."
- No goal decomposition: the model interprets the check-then-return behavior
  "without positing these explicit subgoals or the corresponding parse of the
  agent's motion into subsequences," named as future work.
