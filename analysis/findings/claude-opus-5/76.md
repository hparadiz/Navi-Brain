# Record #76 — Modeling the Mistakes of Boundedly Rational Agents Within a Bayesian Theory of Mind (Alanqary, Lin, Le, Zhi-Xuan, Mansinghka & Tenenbaum, CogSci 2021)

The first author is **Arwa** Alanqary per the PDF; eScholarship's metadata
renders the given name as "Alwa" (`assets/papers/MANIFEST.md` note for #76).

Direct extension of #75. Its contribution is a **taxonomy of error levels**, and
that taxonomy is the most useful thing in the ToM block for deciding when Navi
should open her mouth.

## 1. Mechanism

The generative model separates sub-optimality into three independent levels:

```
g_0 ~ P(g_0)                        goal prior
g_t ~ P(g_t | g_{t-1}, g_0)         GOAL   mistake  — temporary confusion, ε_g
p_t ~ P(p_t | s_{t-1}, p_{t-1}, g)  PLAN   mistake  — bounded search, η ~ NegBin(r,q)
a_t ~ P(a_t | s_t, p_t)             ACTION mistake  — execution error, ε_a
s_t ~ P(s_t | s_{t-1}, a_t)
o_t ~ P(o_t | s_t)
```

**Goal mistakes.** With probability ε_g the original goal `g_0` is *corrupted*
into a semantically similar one, or a corrupted goal is corrected back. The
canonical example is spelling: intending "pear" and building "paer". Note the
structure carefully — `g_0` **persists** throughout; `g_t` is a temporary
corruption of it. This is not #71's M2, where the goal genuinely changes. It is
"still wants the same thing, currently pursuing a corrupted version of it," and
the model can recover the intended goal from a mis-executed one.

**Plan mistakes.** Inherited wholesale from #75: bounded search budget
η ~ NegativeBinomial(r,q), stochastic A* with `P_expand(s) ∝ exp(−f(s,g)/γ)`,
replan only when the plan runs out or the state falls off it. The failure mode is
myopia — using your only key on the wrong door.

**Action mistakes.** With probability ε_a, execute a uniformly random action
other than the intended one. Dropping a block, taking one step too many.

**How to tell them apart — the magnitude heuristic.** The authors state the
disambiguation intuition plainly: "if someone walked a step out of the house
before turning to get their keys, an observer might take that as a mistaken
action, but if they walked all the way to the bus stop before turning around, a
mistaken plan would seem more likely." **Scale of deviation maps to level of
error.**

**Inference** is SIPS from #75, over PDDL-specified domains, with observation
noise on Boolean predicates (ε_o) and Gaussian noise on numeric ones (σ²_o).

**Lesions:** G-Lesioned, P-Lesioned, A-Lesioned (each removing one error level),
plus a Boltzmann/BIRL baseline that precomputes V(s) over the whole state space
and follows `π(a|s) ∝ exp(α[R(s,a,s′) + V(s′)])` — i.e. the #71-#73 noise model.

**Results.**

*Experiment 1, Doors/Keys/Gems* (n=18 after exclusions, 16 stimuli): full model
**r = 0.87 ± 0.04**. The illustrative case is irreversible failure — the agent
burns its key on the wrong door and can no longer reach the red gem. Humans lock
onto the red gem at t=8 "despite knowing that the agent won't be able to reach
it." The Boltzmann model does the opposite: red *decreases* at t=8 and mass moves
to the yellow gem, "since it becomes the only gem reachable without any keys."

That contrast is the paper in one figure. An action-noise model conflates *what
the agent wants* with *what the agent can still get*. People do not.

*Experiment 2, Block Words* (n=27 after exclusions, 16 stimuli): full model
**r = 0.86 ± 0.02**. Humans identify "pear" as soon as all four letters are
stacked, wrong order and all (t=7), and stay confident when the agent corrects
itself at t=15. Boltzmann instead favours "reap," because from the misspelled
state "paer" it takes fewer steps to reach.

**The authors report their own negative results**, which is worth crediting: on
action-mistake stimuli the full model "performed slightly worse than models that
account only for action noise," and in Block Words "surprisingly, the Boltzmann
model correlated highly even when trajectories exhibited short-sighted planning
mistakes" — hypothesized to be because their plan mistakes "require backtracking
only 1–2 actions" and so read as action noise. A-Lesioned "performed worst across
the board" in Block Words.

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) The error taxonomy answers the speech-timing question the plan says is open

`docs/architecture-gaps.md:206-212` states the problem: "Navi cannot tell a good
moment to speak from a bad one." The three-level taxonomy gives a principled
answer that does not require solving goal inference in general:

| Observed deviation | Level | Should Navi speak? |
|---|---|---|
| Quick correction, small deviation | **action** slip | No. He has already noticed. |
| Sustained divergence, still recoverable | **plan** mistake | Probably not. He may be right and Navi's goal hypothesis wrong. |
| Divergence heading to an **irreversible** state | plan mistake, terminal | This is the case with real value. |
| Behaviour matching a corrupted version of a known goal | **goal** confusion | Maybe — depends on cost of being wrong. |

The Doors/Keys/Gems domain is built entirely around irreversibility: keys are
single-use, so a myopic plan permanently locks out the goal. A destructive
command against the wrong target is a single-use key.

**Availability correction:** Navi cannot currently observe this. `CommandSense`
and the unprivileged `navi-senses` daemon return output from commands **Navi
chose** to run — her own epistemic action, not Henry's command trajectory. The
coarse `shell_activity` and `terminal_activity` channels carry only the age of
the newest write, never the command, its target, or its result. So the
irreversibility predicate has no observable to bind to today, and building the
trigger before the evidence exists would mean inferring destructive intent from
timing. Per `docs/architecture-gaps.md`, observing user commands is a separate
and more sensitive perception grant than coarse desktop context.

Held as a *design target* rather than a next step, this is still the strongest
positive case in the corpus for unprompted speech, and it is narrow by
construction: not "he might like to know," but "the state is about to become
unrecoverable and the model says that is inconsistent with the goal he has been
pursuing for the last hour." It stays unavailable until that grant exists.

Two cautions that belong with it. First, the same mechanism is one loosened
threshold away from the interrupt-on-everything behaviour that
`docs/architecture-gaps.md:47` records as the thing this whole line of work
replaced — irreversibility has to be a hard predicate, not a score. Second, #74's
MNM applies: this is Navi changing the environment in a way he can observe, which
is permitted, and it must not become a habit of steering.

### (b) Goal *confusion* is a concept Navi's own intention machinery lacks

`releaseFocus()` accepts exactly three Cohen-Levesque reasons — `achieved`,
`impossible`, `reason_lapsed` — and rejects everything else
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:143-148`). That is a good, defensible commitment
model, and it has no representation for *still wants the same thing, currently
doing a corrupted version of it*.

For an other-model this matters concretely. When Henry does something that does
not match the inferred goal, the current vocabulary forces a choice between "the
goal changed" and "the inference was wrong." The εg mechanism supplies the third
reading, and it is often the right one: same target, wrong route, will
self-correct. Under that reading the correct response is to *hold the goal
posterior steady* rather than revise it — which is precisely what humans did in
Figure 4(a), staying confident in "pear" through the misspelling and through the
correction.

There is a symmetric observation about Navi herself. `docs/architecture-gaps.md`
records 13 intentions with 0 ever completed, and that low-confidence
"looks basically done" judgements advance `next_action` rather than closing the
goal (line 154). A temporary-corruption model is a better description of some of
those cases than either release reason, and the concept arriving via the
other-model is a reason to reconsider the self-model's vocabulary too.

### (c) The magnitude heuristic is computable from data already stored

"One step out of the house" versus "all the way to the bus stop" is a threshold on
deviation size, and Navi has a coarse version of the raw quantity: run lengths
and gaps in the activity-channel `SenseReading` stream, the same series #75 §2c
uses to fit persistence. A forty-second detour and a forty-minute detour are different
categories of event, and nothing in the current pipeline distinguishes them —
`SocialFeedback` has a single fixed 120-second window
(`DEFAULT_WINDOW_SECONDS = 120`) applied uniformly.

This is the cheapest concrete step from this record: bucket deviations by
magnitude before interpreting them at all.

### (d) A-Lesioned worst across the board: model the boring errors first

The most counterintuitive result is that removing *action* noise — the crudest,
least interesting error type — hurt more than anything else in Block Words. The
sophisticated levels do not substitute for the simple one.

Applied to Navi: before any goal or plan modelling, the other-model needs a
plain "he did something that meant nothing" channel. Mistyped commands, a stray
click, a window focused by accident, a half-sentence into a microphone. Without
that, every meaningless observable gets absorbed into a goal hypothesis, and the
posterior degrades. This is the same lesson as the existing habituation work
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:34-37`, where unconsumed sense edges made "the same
sentence twenty times") arriving from the other direction.

### (e) Lesion-per-error-level is the ablation design for the eventual metric

Each error level is separately removable and separately evaluable, and the paper
shows the honest result of that: models specialized for one error type beat the
full model *on that type* and lose badly elsewhere. `docs/experiments/` already
holds `05-cognitive-component-ablation.md`, and `docs/architecture-gaps.md:88-90`
states the design intent that thin implementations "can now be ablated and
improved in isolation against end-to-end behavior."

So the other-model should be built with the same seams from the start: goal
confusion, plan bounding, and action noise as independently disableable terms,
scored per stimulus category rather than in aggregate. Aggregate correlation
would have hidden both of this paper's negative results.

## 3. Seam

Primary: **`other-model`**.

Also touches:
- `speech-gate` — irreversibility as the trigger predicate (§2a). This is the
  most actionable item in the ToM block for `ActionSelector`.
- `intention` / commitment — goal confusion as a missing release/hold reason
  (§2b), applying to both the other-model and Navi's own.
- `metrics-benchmark` — per-error-category scoring, not aggregate r (§2e).
- `forward-model` — deviation magnitude as the classifier input (§2c).
- `perception` — where an irreversibility signal would have to come from, and
  currently does not exist; a separately authorized grant, not a refactor.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: the plan has no concept of irreversibility, and this paper says that
is where the entire value of modelling another agent's mistakes concentrates.**
Navi's action space and safety invariants are built around *her* not doing
irreversible things — no autonomous shell execution, an unprivileged senses
account holding "no write permission anywhere" (`docs/architecture-gaps.md:44`),
procedural writes requiring explicit override. All correct, and all about her own
reversibility. Nothing anywhere models the reversibility of *his* actions. Yet
that is the single dimension along which "should I say something" has a
defensible answer. Adding an `irreversible` predicate to observed command events
is small, and it is the precondition for the one unprompted-speech behaviour this
corpus actually justifies.

**Second: the full model was beaten by simpler models on simple cases, and the
plan's incremental strategy will feel that.** On action-mistake stimuli, the full
model lost to P-Lesioned and Boltzmann; in Block Words the Boltzmann baseline
matched it on plan mistakes. Richer models pay for their range. Since
`docs/architecture-gaps.md` measures progress by whether each item moves a
baseline number, there is a real risk that a first other-model *lowers* an
aggregate score while being strictly better on the cases that matter. The
mitigation is §2e — commit to per-category evaluation before building, so the
result is interpretable when it arrives.

**Third: parameters were fit by grid search maximizing correlation against the
same human data being predicted.** No held-out set, no cross-validation. #73 used
bootstrap cross-validation for exactly this reason and this paper does not. The
authors partially cover the gap with a sensitivity analysis showing the full model
dominates across most parameter settings, and that is real evidence — but "our
model wins across most of the grid" and "our model would win on new data" are
different claims, and only the first is supported. Both headline r values (0.87,
0.86) are fitted, not predicted.

**Fourth: the plan-mistake stimuli were mild, by the authors' own diagnosis.**
Block Words plan mistakes "require backtracking only 1–2 actions," which is why
the Boltzmann baseline kept up. So the evidence that plan-level modelling is
necessary rests almost entirely on Experiment 1's irreversible-failure cases —
which is a narrower base than the abstract implies, and, conveniently, exactly
the case Navi should care about.

**Agreement:** the decomposition itself is right, and it maps cleanly onto
distinctions Navi's own architecture already draws elsewhere (action traces vs.
intentions vs. next-actions). Adopting the vocabulary costs nothing and improves
how deviations get logged even before any inference exists.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** CogSci 2021 proceedings. Refereed conference, 8 pages. Same tier as
#72; more careful in method than #72 was.

**Strengths:**
- **Two domains**, with the model generalizing across them — the goal-confusion
  term only activates in Block Words, where goals are compositional.
- **Four-way lesion design** with each error level separately removable.
- **Sensitivity analysis across the full parameter grid**, explicitly to check
  for overfitting. Rare and worth crediting.
- **Bootstrap CIs** (500 resamples) on the correlations.
- **Real participant hygiene**: comprehension checks, incentive-compatible
  scoring (points proportional to probability assigned to the true goal),
  pre-stated exclusion criteria, exclusions reported (2 of 20; 5 of 32).
- **Negative results reported rather than buried.**

**Where it is thin:**
- **n=18 and n=27**, AMT, correlations against participant averages. Better than
  #75's N=8 pilot, still small.
- **Parameters fit to maximize correlation with the test data.** The sensitivity
  analysis mitigates but does not replace held-out validation.
- **16 stimuli per domain**, hand-designed to make particular mistake types
  identifiable. The authors are explicit: "we designed trajectories to make some
  mistakes more likely than others." That is necessary for the science and means
  the disambiguation problem is easier in the stimuli than in life.
- **Plan mistakes in Block Words were too shallow to discriminate models**, by
  the authors' own account.
- **Compute grew sharply from #75**: 300 particles/run in Exp 1 and 500 in Exp 2
  (100 per goal), each averaged over 10 runs — versus #75's "10 particles per
  goal." A 10× increase in the particle budget, unremarked, once the model has
  three noise levels instead of one.
- **PDDL environment model assumed throughout**, shared between agent and
  observer. Same blocking dependency as #75.
- **Goal space fixed and given.** Stated as out of scope: "leaving aside how
  people come up with these contextual hypotheses." That remains the unsolved
  piece for Navi across this entire block.

**Is the headline supported?** The decomposition claim is supported — the lesions
each fail on their own error type, and the Boltzmann baseline fails in a specific,
mechanistically explained way (conflating reachability with desire). The
quantitative claim is weakened by in-sample parameter fitting. The generality
claim rests on two gridworld-style domains with hand-designed stimuli.

## 6. Do not take

- **The r values as predictive accuracy.** Fitted to the data being predicted.
- **CORRUPT as a domain-general operator.** In Block Words it is a random
  permutation of a word. There is no natural corruption operator over "what Henry
  is working on," and inventing one would manufacture a hypothesis space rather
  than infer over it.
- **PDDL and SIPS wholesale.** Same as #75: the taxonomy and triggers transfer,
  the symbolic planner does not.
- **100 particles per goal as a default.** #75 got by on 10. The increase tracks
  model complexity; a simpler Navi model should start at the low end and only
  grow if the posterior is visibly unstable.
- **Assistance as the motivating frame.** The paper opens on teaching, offering
  assistance, and "blame or forgiveness," and cites infants helping adults after
  observing failed plans. Detecting a mistake is not authorization to correct it.
  Everything in §2a is gated behind irreversibility for that reason, and the
  blame/forgiveness framing in particular has no place in a system that models one
  specific person's errors and stores the results.
- **Modelling Henry's capability limits or fatigue.** The Discussion floats
  extensions to "capability limitations" and "time inconsistency." Combined with a
  durable store, that becomes a persistent record of a person's diminished
  capacity, which is both a privacy problem and the shortest path back to wellness
  check-ins. #74 §6 flags the same trap from the "complete type" direction.

## 7. Key citations

- The gap being filled: prior BToM models "only account for mistakes through
  Boltzmann-distributed action noise... This fails to capture higher-level
  mistakes" (Intro).
- Three levels, verbatim: "goals may be confused with semantically similar
  states, plans may be misguided due to resource-bounded planning, and actions may
  be unintended due to execution errors" (Abstract).
- Goal confusion preserves the original goal: with probability ε_g "the original
  goal `g_0` may be corrupted to produce a similar temporary goal `g_t`, or the
  corrupted goal corrected to the original one" (Computational Model).
- The magnitude heuristic for disambiguation: "if someone walked a step out of
  the house before turning to get their keys, an observer might take that as a
  mistaken action, but if they walked all the way to the bus stop before turning
  around, a mistaken plan would seem more likely" (Experiments).
- The irreversibility result: humans "recognize the true goal as soon as the first
  door is unlocked (t=8) despite knowing that the agent won't be able to reach
  it," while for the Boltzmann model "the probability of the red gem decreases at
  t=8, and more weight goes to the yellow gem, since it becomes the only gem
  reachable without any keys" (Exp 1 Results).
- Desire is not reachability: Boltzmann "fails to account for 'paer' as a
  misspelling, assigning higher probability to a goal like 'reap' because it takes
  less steps to reach from 'paer' compared to 'pear'" (Exp 2 Results).
- Headline fits: full model r=0.87 ± 0.04 (Doors, Keys & Gems, n=18);
  r=0.86 ± 0.02 (Block Words, n=27).
- The authors' own negative results: on action mistakes "our model performed
  slightly worse than models that account only for action noise"; and
  "surprisingly, the Boltzmann model correlated highly even when trajectories
  exhibited short-sighted planning mistakes... because the plan mistakes in our
  dataset are sufficiently short-lived (i.e. require backtracking only 1–2
  actions)."
- Simple errors matter most: "the model without action mistakes (A-lesioned)
  performed worst across the board, demonstrating the importance of modeling
  execution errors in this domain."
- Fitting procedure: "We fit the parameters of each model via grid search,
  maximizing the overall correlation (Pearson's r) between average human
  judgements and model inferences" (Analysis), with a sensitivity analysis
  "to validate whether any the models performed better just because of
  overfitting."
- Compute: "10 runs of the SIPS particle filter with 300 particles per run"
  (Exp 1); "500 particles per run (100 particles per potential goal)" (Exp 2).
- Scope limit on the hypothesis space: "we restrict our scope to inference over a
  fixed set of context-relevant goals with associated prior probabilities, leaving
  aside how people come up with these contextual hypotheses."
