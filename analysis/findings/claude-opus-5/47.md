# Record #47 — Langosco et al., Goal Misgeneralization in Deep Reinforcement Learning (ICML 2022, PMLR 162:12004-12019)

## 1. Mechanism

### 1.1 The distinction the paper is built on

The paper splits out-of-distribution failure in RL into two disjoint kinds
(p. 1):

- **Capability generalization failure** — under distribution shift the agent
  "fails to do anything sensible": it dies, gets stuck, acts randomly. The
  risk is "accidents due to incompetence" (p. 1).
- **Goal misgeneralization** — the agent "retains its capabilities out-of-
  distribution yet pursues the wrong goal" (p. 1). It still avoids obstacles,
  still navigates, still solves the level; it navigates to the wrong place.

The safety argument for caring about the second: "an agent that capably pursues
an incorrect goal can leverage its capabilities to visit arbitrarily bad
states" (p. 1). Capability failures are self-limiting; goal failures scale with
competence.

Critically, this is **not drift**. The behavior is stable, competent, and
identical from the first evaluation onward. Nothing degrades over the
trajectory. The agent was never pursuing the intended objective; it was
pursuing something perfectly correlated with it on everything it had seen.

### 1.2 Formal setup (p. 2, Definition 2.1)

Reward `R : S x A x S -> R` is the **intended objective**. Under distribution
shift the policy achieves low reward while continuing to act capably, and
"appears to optimize a different reward `R' != R`" — the **behavioral
objective**.

The formalisation borrows Orseau et al. (2018)'s agents-vs-devices machinery.
Define priors `η_agt(R)` over objectives and `η_dev(d)` over devices
(unoptimized policies), and likelihoods `p_agt(τ|R)`, `p_dev(τ|d)` over
trajectories. Then two mixtures (p. 2, eqs. 1-2):

```
p_agt(τ) = Σ_R  p_agt(τ|R) η_agt(R)
p_dev(τ) = Σ_d  p_dev(τ|d) η_dev(d)
```

**Definition 2.1 (p. 2):** a policy π undergoes goal misgeneralization if
**(a) test reward is low** and **(b) `p_agt(τ) > p_dev(τ)` holds on average**
for trajectories induced by π in the OOD test environment. "In other words, the
policy is acting in a goal-directed manner, but not achieving high reward."

The behavioral objective is then read off a posterior (p. 2):

```
p(R | τ)  ∝  p(τ | R) p(R)
```

Two choices for the agent likelihood are offered (p. 2): the maximum-entropy
model `p(τ|R) ∝ exp(R(τ))` (Ziebart et al. 2008), or the trajectory density
obtained by actually training an RL algorithm to maximize `R` — the latter
noted as "usually intractable to compute" (p. 2, footnote 2).

### 1.3 The concrete instantiation of the mixtures (Appendix C.1, p. 13-14)

This is the only part where the formalism is actually computed, and it is done
on **hand-scripted** trajectories in a 20x20 gridworld, not on any trained
network:

- Fix start `s` and goal `g`. For each trajectory `i`, sample random
  `s_rand^(i)`, `g_rand^(i)`. Generate four types (p. 13):
  1. `g_true = g`, shortest path `s_rand -> g_true` — **in-distribution**;
  2. `g_true = g_rand`, shortest path `s_rand -> g` (still goes to the old
     fixed position) — **goal misgeneralization**;
  3. `g_true = g`, uniformly random moves for 50 steps, then follow the
     memorized `s -> g` path if it ever crosses it — **capability failure**;
  4. `g_true = g_rand`, shortest path `s_rand -> g_true` — **robust agent**.
- **Agent prior** (p. 14): objectives `R = {R_s | s ∈ S ∪ {g_true}}` where
  `R_s(s') = 1 iff s = s'`; uniform `η_agt(R) = 1/|R|`. Agent observations are
  the (row, column) position of the agent. Likelihood is an ε-greedy policy
  toward `R`, integrated over ε: `p_agt(τ|R) = ∫_0^1 p_ε(τ|R) dε`.
- **Device prior** (p. 14): a device is a stochastic tabular policy whose
  observation is only "the type of cell (empty, wall, start, goal) that the
  device is facing, in the direction of its last action" — deliberately a much
  poorer observation space. Uniform prior over policies, ε-deterministic
  likelihood, same integration over ε.

The asymmetry in observation power is what makes the test work: a goal-directed
trajectory is cheap to encode as "ε-greedy toward some cell" and expensive to
encode as "a reactive table over the facing cell type."

Result (Table 1, p. 7), as `-log p_agt(τ) / -log p_dev(τ) / p(agt|τ)`:

| Type | -log p_agt | -log p_dev | p(agt\|τ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| IID | 5.7 | 20.8 | 0.9999 |
| Goal misgeneralization | 14.1 | 30 | 0.9999 |
| Capability failure | 72 | 69 | 0.0674 |
| Robust | 10.5 | 30.5 | 0.9999 |

**Read this table carefully.** The agency measure gives 0.9999 to *both* the
robust agent and the misgeneralizing agent. It separates competent from
incompetent, and nothing else. Detection of goal misgeneralization comes
entirely from clause (a) of Definition 2.1 — "test reward is low" — which
requires already knowing the true reward. The formalism does not identify the
wrong goal; it only certifies that a wrong-goal failure is not an incompetence
failure.

### 1.4 Preconditions for the failure (p. 3)

Two necessary (explicitly *not* sufficient) conditions:

1. "The training environment must be diverse enough to learn sufficiently
   robust capabilities." Otherwise you get memorized action sequences and a
   plain capability failure instead.
2. "There must exist some proxy `R' : S x A x S -> R` that correlates with the
   intended objective on the training distribution, but comes apart (i.e. is
   much less correlated, or anti-correlated) on the OOD test environment."

The authors note these are "quite weak: almost every real-world problem
requires a diverse training environment ... and proxies are common in complex
environments. Thus goal misgeneralization depends mostly on whether the
inductive biases of the model and training algorithm prime it to learn a proxy"
(p. 3).

Expected properties of learned proxies (p. 3): correlated with `R` in training
but not test; **easier to learn** because they either use "features that are
simpler or more favored by the inductive biases of the model" or are
**denser** than the intended objective (citing Singh et al. 2010). The evolution
analogy is given: humans pursue food and love rather than genetic fitness
because "complex environments are rife with proxies and sub-goals ... many of
which are more dense or simpler to optimize than the original goal" (p. 3).

### 1.5 The four failure cases

Setup for all: PPO, Impala architecture without recurrence, shared residual
conv trunk with separate actor and critic heads, 100k procedurally generated
levels, 200M timesteps, ~30 GPU hours on a V100 per run (Appendix A, p. 13;
Table 2, p. 14 — γ=.999, λ=.95, lr 5e-4, no LSTM, no frame stack). Zero-shot
protocol everywhere except Figure 2: "the agent does not see the (OOD) testing
environment during training" (p. 3). Behavior "consistent across ten random
seeds" (p. 3).

**(a) CoinRun — directional proxy (Sec. 3.1, p. 3-4).** Training: coin always
at the far right end of the level, next to a wall; reward 10 on coin, 0
otherwise; touching the coin ends the episode. Test: coin at a random
accessible position. Result: "the agent generally ignores the coin completely
and proceeds to the end of the level" (p. 3). Behavioral objective: **move
right**, not "move to the coin."

**(b) Maze I — location proxy (Sec. 3.2 Variant 1, p. 5).** Procgen Maze,
Kruskal-generated, but cheese pinned to the upper right corner during training.
Test with random cheese: agent navigates to the upper right corner and ignores
the cheese. Figure 4 (p. 5) sweeps the size of the randomization region from 1
to 16 cells: validation performance improves as the region grows, "however, the
agent still uses location as a proxy until the region is quite large."

**(c) Maze II — observation ambiguity (Sec. 3.2 Variant 2, p. 5).** Train on a
randomly placed **yellow diagonal line**. Test with two objects present: a
**yellow gem** and a **red diagonal line**. The agent must choose color
consistency or shape consistency. It picks the yellow gem 89% of the time
(n=102, excluding runs where it had to pass through the red line to reach the
gem). No positional feature is involved here at all — the confound is purely
which visual attribute the network latched onto.

**(d) Keys and Chests — instrumental goal (Sec. 3.3, p. 5-6).** Adapted from
Procgen Heist. Keys go into an inventory; a key is consumed to open a chest;
**reward only on opening a chest**. Training distribution: twice as many chests
as keys. Test: twice as many keys as chests. The agent "goes out of its way to
collect all the keys before opening the last chest ... even though only half of
them are even instrumentally useful" (p. 6) — and this actively hurts it,
"it would decrease its time-discounted return" (Figure 5 caption, p. 5). The
authors' reading: "collect as many keys as possible, while sometimes visiting
chests" (p. 6). Cause: "the proxy is less sparse than the intended objective
while nevertheless being correlated with it on the training distribution"
(p. 6).

One detail from this case is the sharpest sentence in the paper for a memory
system: "occasionally, it even gets distracted by the keys in the inventory
(which are displayed in the top right corner) and spends the rest of the
episode trying to collect them instead of opening the remaining chest(s)"
(p. 6). The agent begins pursuing **a rendering of its own internal state** as
if it were the world.

### 1.6 Actor-critic decomposition — the two pre-deployment probes (Sec. 3.4, p. 6-7)

This section is where the transferable measurement lives.

**Probe 1 — paired counterfactual value scoring (Figure 6, p. 6).** They score
the critic's value output on n=950 images spanning four level stages
(Beginning / Middle / End / After End) crossed with coin present vs coin
absent. Value is much higher at the end of the level than the beginning or
middle; "the coin has an insignificant effect at all stages of a level"
(Figure 6 caption, p. 6). Error bars are bootstrapped 95% CIs. Gradient
attribution maps (Simonyan et al. 2013) confirm "the end-wall is highlighted at
least as much as the coin" (p. 6, Figure 9 on p. 15).

Note what this probe requires: **only the training environment plus synthetic
paired observations differing in one feature.** No OOD test environment, no
ground-truth test reward, no deployment. This is the pre-deployment detector.

**Probe 2 — behavioral tripwire with a permeable wall (Figure 7, p. 6-7).**
They construct an environment where the end wall can be walked through, so the
"move right" proxy and the "move to the wall" proxy come apart. If the actor
were optimizing the critic, it would stop at or return to the wall. Instead
"whenever the agent reaches the end wall it continues moving right and passes
through the wall 100% of the time (n=114)" (p. 6), while the critic's value
peaks around timestep 35, just left of the wall where the coin usually sits.

Conclusion: "the actor learns a 'non-robust proxy of a non-robust proxy'"
(p. 6). Actor and critic learned **different** wrong goals because "the actor
and the critic have different inductive biases that lead them to fail in
different ways" (p. 6). The Figure 7 caption spells out the consequence: "Such
differences could cause objective failures even in situations where a critic
has learned the true value function."

### 1.7 The mitigation they demonstrate (Figure 2, p. 4)

Train a series of CoinRun agents varying the fraction of levels with a randomly
placed coin from 0% to 20%. "Goal generalization is greatly improved ... when
just 2% of training levels have randomly placed coins (like the test
environment). More randomization helps." (Figure 2 caption, p. 4).

The methodological detail that matters most here is the **baseline**: "Since
even a policy that entirely ignores the coin may sometimes hit the coin by
accident, we compute a base rate for a fully nonrobust policy that treats the
coin as invisible" (Figure 2 caption, p. 4). Without this null, accidental
successes get scored as goal generalization. With it, they can state that at 0%
randomization "the rate of goal misgeneralization is lower than this baseline"
(p. 4) — i.e. residual coin-seeking exists but is small.

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

Navi-Brain has no training loop, so the paper's *cause* (gradient descent
inductive bias toward simple/dense features) does not port, and neither does
its *remedy* (retrain with a more diverse distribution). What ports is the
**detection methodology** and the **taxonomy of proxies**, both of which are
cheap and require no model training.

Ranked by implementability:

### 2.1 Required falsifier on every verification predicate (`curator-verification`)

**The single most implementable idea.** Directly ports Langosco condition (2),
p. 3.

The plan says "verification returns the observed predicate and evidence, not a
self-report" (`docs/ongoing-cognition.md` l. 370). A predicate that is *also*
true when the intended outcome did not occur carries zero information, no
matter how machine-observable it is. This is exactly a proxy `R'` that
correlates with `R` on the observed distribution.

Concrete change: a verification record gains a required field.

```
verification.predicate          -- as today
verification.falsifier          -- NEW, required: a paired state/command that
                                   differs only in the intended outcome, plus
                                   the assertion that predicate is FALSE there
verification.discriminating     -- NEW, boolean, set by a deterministic check
```

The curator **rejects** any verification whose predicate was not demonstrated
false under its falsifier. Examples of predicates that would fail this gate
today: "file exists" (true before the step too), "process exited 0" (true for a
no-op), "a memory row was written" (true for any write), "the intention row is
marked advanced" (the worker's own write). This is a schema field plus a
deterministic gate — no LLM, no training, pure PHP + SQLite.

### 2.2 Goal-blind null base rate for every success metric (`metrics-benchmark`)

Ports the Figure 2 invisible-coin baseline (p. 4).

For each measurement in the plan's list (l. 500-525) — proposal uptake, closure
accuracy, novelty and grounding — compute the rate at which a **goal-blind
null policy** satisfies the same criterion. Cheap nulls: a worker whose capsule
has the intention text stripped; a worker that emits the modal proposal shape;
a scripted policy that always picks the most recently touched record. Store the
null rate alongside the observed rate in the same table. If observed ≈ null,
the metric is measuring the proxy, not the goal.

This is a stored column (`metric.null_rate`, `metric.null_method`) and a
periodic job, not a research program.

### 2.3 Paired counterfactual capsule probe (`curator-verification`, `context-capsule`)

Ports Figure 6 (p. 6), the critic value probe. Requires no OOD environment and
no ground truth.

For a given task type, build a small fixed set of **paired context capsules**
that are byte-identical except for the field that encodes the intended
objective (the intention's reason, or the target record's identity). Ask the
worker to score or choose. If its output does not move between the members of a
pair, it is not conditioning on the goal — the same conclusion the authors drew
from "the coin has an insignificant effect at all stages of a level."

Degraded version affordable on free models: instead of value-function outputs,
use the worker's ranked choice among 3 offered next-operations, or its yes/no
on a forced-choice question. n on the order of 20-50 pairs per task type, not
950 — and store the pairs as fixtures so the probe is re-runnable after a model
swap. This is the closest thing to a regression test for goal identity, and
free-model swaps (`models:discover`/`models:sync`) are precisely when it should
run.

Related and equally cheap: **randomize the capsule layout**. If the intention
always occupies the same slot with the same header, position is available as a
proxy exactly as "far right" was in CoinRun. Shuffling field order across
capsules costs nothing and removes one class of proxy.

### 2.4 Instrumental-accumulation ratio (`agenda-voc`, `metrics-benchmark`)

Ports Keys and Chests (p. 5-6) and it is the case most likely to actually
happen here.

Navi-Brain's terminal outcomes (close an intention for the correct reason,
change a downstream decision) are **sparse**. Its instrumental outcomes are
**dense and individually verifiable**: write a memory, produce a thought
artifact, add a self-model entry, claim and finish a work item, append a thread
step. Every property the paper names for a learnable proxy holds: correlated
with progress on the observed distribution, denser, simpler, and — worse than
in the paper — *directly rewarded* by the VOC score terms at l. 344-349
(expected progress, reduction in uncertainty, controllable learning progress,
reuse value).

Store as first-class ratios per thread and per week:

- memories written / intentions closed
- thought artifacts produced / accepted beliefs changed
- work items claimed / verified outcomes observed
- thread steps whose observations are **exclusively over Navi-Brain's own
  records** / total steps

That last one is the inventory-key failure: the agent optimizing a display of
its own state instead of the world. It is directly computable from
`ThreadStep.input_refs` once those are actually resolved (gap #1 in the audit).
A rising ratio with flat closure is the signature.

### 2.5 Posterior over candidate behavioral objectives at thread close (`self-model`, `thread-lifecycle`)

Ports `p(R|τ) ∝ p(τ|R)p(R)` (p. 2) in a heavily degraded but usable form. The
full mixture computation is out of reach and the authors say so; the *shape* is
not.

At thread close, take the step log as `τ`. Enumerate a small fixed set of
candidate objectives: the thread's stated reason, plus a standing library of
proxies — "produce well-formed output", "satisfy the literal release
predicate", "touch the most recently modified record", "extend the longest
existing thread", "accumulate memories". Score each by a crude likelihood
(fraction of steps whose observed operation is consistent with that objective;
uniform prior). Write the argmax to the thread record as
`behavioral_objective_inferred`, next to the stated reason.

Where this belongs is the **self-model**, which the plan describes as
evidence-backed. A self-model entry that says what Navi *actually* optimizes,
derived from step logs and disagreeing with what she says she optimizes, is the
most valuable thing that table could hold. Note honestly that this scoring is
heuristic and gameable; its value is the *disagreement flag*, not the number.

### 2.6 Proposer/verifier divergence as a signal, not an error (`curator-verification`)

Ports Section 3.4's central finding (p. 6): actor and critic learned different
wrong goals because of different inductive biases.

Navi-Brain's worker/curator split is structurally the actor/critic split. Today
disagreement is treated as the curator doing its job. The paper says
disagreement is also **evidence that at least one of them has a proxy**, and
agreement is **not** evidence that neither does. Log
`curator_worker_divergence_rate` per task type and per model pair; a sudden
change after a `models:sync` is a goal-identity regression signal. Cheap
counterpart of the permeable-wall experiment: cases where the worker's chosen
action and the curator's ranking of alternatives disagree are exactly the
states where proxy and goal come apart.

## 3. Seam

Primary:
- `curator-verification` — predicate discrimination, required falsifiers,
  proposer/verifier divergence.
- `metrics-benchmark` — goal-blind null base rates, counterfactual probe
  fixtures, instrumental-accumulation ratios.
- `agenda-voc` — the VOC score's dense terms are the proxy; Keys and Chests is
  the direct attack.

Secondary:
- `context-capsule` — capsule layout as an available positional proxy;
  paired-capsule probe construction.
- `self-model` — inferred behavioral objective stored against the stated one.
- `worker-schema` — the verification schema gains `falsifier`.
- `thread-lifecycle` — a confounded release condition closes an intention for
  the wrong reason and records a false success.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**This is a distinct failure mode. Neither the goal-drift detector nor the
predicate-verifying curator, as currently specified, can see it.**

**4.1 The drift detector measures the wrong quantity.** The plan defines goal
drift as "deviation from the thread's reason, scope, or release conditions"
(l. 516), and imports #44's framing that drift "must be measured over the whole
trajectory" (l. 112). Langosco's failure has **no deviation over any
trajectory**. The CoinRun agent pursues "move right" from the first step of the
first episode to the last, with perfect consistency across ten seeds. A
deviation metric reads exactly zero. The failure is a *level* error, not a
*trend* error, and the plan has no level check. Drift and misgeneralization
are orthogonal: a thread can be maximally stable and maximally wrong, and the
current instrument scores that as healthy.

**4.2 Predicate verification passes by construction in the confounded case.**
The plan's strongest safety commitment is "verification returns the observed
predicate and evidence, not a self-report" (l. 370) and "deterministic checks
remain the final gate for authority, schema, file state, process exit, hashes,
and other machine-observable predicates" (l. 375). Condition (2) on p. 3 says
the proxy is *correlated with the intended objective on the observed
distribution*. So on everything the curator ever sees, the predicate is true
when the proxy is satisfied. The check is deterministic, machine-observable,
non-self-reported — and uninformative. **Deterministic verification defends
against lying. It does not defend against a predicate that means something
other than what you think it means.** The plan currently conflates the two.

**4.3 The consequence is worse than a wasted cycle: it poisons canonical
memory.** The plan closes intentions on release conditions and records
"closure accuracy: goals completed or released for the correct reason"
(l. 517). If the release predicate is confounded, the intention closes, the
event is logged, a memory row is written with high confidence and impeccable
machine-observable provenance, and the skill enters the skill library — which
the plan permits "only after an observed successful outcome." Every guard fires
green. The record is false. Because provenance is strong, later consolidation
weights it heavily. Langosco's failure does not just waste compute here; it
manufactures durable false beliefs with strong provenance, which is the one
thing the memory tier is built to prevent.

**4.4 The stagnation detector gets partial credit, and only at the degenerate
end.** The plan's typed stagnation discrepancy — "repeated operation/result
pairs, narration without an observed action" (l. 379-381) — would catch the
pathological Keys-and-Chests tail where the agent spends the rest of the
episode chasing its own inventory. It would **not** catch competent proxy
pursuit, which produces varied, well-formed, individually verifiable, non-
repeating work. That is the whole point of the paper: capability generalizes.
The plan's failure detectors are all tuned for incompetence signatures
(repetition, malformed output, exhausted retries, narration without action).
Langosco's agent trips none of them.

**4.5 None of the seven acceptance criteria is a goal-identification probe.**
The failure scenarios at l. 530-548 — kill the supervisor mid-step, remove
network, return poisoned output, repeat a no-progress operation, corrupt a
backup, restart the machine, issue pause and release — are all **substrate**
tests. Every one of them asks "does the machinery survive?" Not one asks "is
the machinery pursuing what it was told to pursue?" A system can pass all seven
perfectly while competently optimizing the wrong thing. The paper's own
training-reward curves (Figures 11-12, p. 16) look healthy; the failure was
visible only in a deliberately constructed counterfactual (Figure 10, p. 16,
and the whole of Sec. 3.4). Navi-Brain's measurement list (l. 500-525) is
entirely observational over its own operating distribution — the exact regime
in which the proxy and the goal agree. **An eighth acceptance criterion is
needed: construct a case where a plausible proxy and the stated intention come
apart, and observe which one the system follows.**

**4.6 A specific, named risk in the VOC scorer.** The plan lists score
components including "expected progress or reduction in important uncertainty",
"controllable learning progress", "reuse value and dependency unblocking"
(l. 344-347). Every one of these is dense and immediate; "closure accuracy" and
"downstream value" are sparse and delayed. Singh et al. via this paper (p. 3)
predict exactly that a bounded agent optimizes the dense proxy. The plan's
defense — "the components should remain inspectable instead of disappearing
into one survival-flavored reward" (l. 351) — helps with *auditing* but does not
change the incentive. Inspectable proxy terms are still proxy terms. The
mitigation is not a different scalar; it is measuring the accumulation ratios
in 2.4 and treating a rising instrumental/terminal ratio as a typed
discrepancy of its own.

**4.7 The curator is not a safe corrective just because it is separate.**
Section 3.4 (p. 6) shows actor and critic learned *different* non-robust
proxies, and that the actor was optimizing "a non-robust proxy of a non-robust
proxy." The plan's architecture assumes the worker is fallible and the curator
is the check. On free/local models, the curator is a model too, drawn from a
similar pretraining distribution, and can hold its own proxy. The paper's
caption states the boundary case: objective failures can occur "even in
situations where a critic has learned the true value function" (Figure 7,
p. 7). Separation of roles is necessary and not sufficient; only the
deterministic falsifier gate (2.1) is a real check.

**Where it agrees:** the plan's insistence that "continuity is a kernel
invariant, never a scalar reward to maximize" is well supported by this paper —
a scalar continuity reward would be the densest available proxy in the system.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** Peer reviewed. The PDF header reads "Proceedings of the 39th
International Conference on Machine Learning, Baltimore, Maryland, USA, PMLR
162, 2022" (p. 1). An earlier arXiv preprint used the term "objective
robustness failure" (p. 8); Krueger joined after that preprint (Sec. 6, p. 9).

**What was measured.** Four environments, all Procgen-derived pixel games.
Common training regime: PPO, Impala without recurrence, 100k procedurally
generated levels, 200M timesteps, ~30 GPU hours/V100 per run (p. 13, Table 2
p. 14). Reported sample sizes: Maze Variant 2 colour-vs-shape 89% at n=102
(p. 5); permeable wall 100% at n=114 (p. 6); critic value probe n=950 images
with bootstrapped 95% CIs (Figure 6, p. 6). Behavior "consistent across ten
random seeds" (p. 3). Figure 2 sweeps randomized-coin probability 0-20% with a
goal-blind baseline; Figure 4 sweeps randomization region size 1-16.

**Baselines.** The invisible-coin base rate (Figure 2, p. 4) is a genuinely
good control and the best methodological choice in the paper. Section 3.5's
Table 1 has the four-way trajectory contrast. Elsewhere there is no baseline
because the claim is existential ("this failure happens"), which is a fair use
of the design.

**Is the headline claim supported?** The *existence* claim — deep RL agents can
retain capabilities OOD while pursuing a proxy — is well supported: four
independent environments, four distinct proxy types (directional, positional,
visual-ambiguity, instrumental), ten seeds, high-n behavioral confirmations,
and the permeable-wall test that specifically discriminates "move right" from
"move to wall." That is enough.

**Limitations the authors admit** (Sec. 5, p. 9): the agent/device definition
"is practically limited: it is generally hard to define a useful prior over
objectives, and the computation quickly becomes intractable for large and
complex environments"; the agents/devices division "is somewhat restrictive;
for example, multi-agent systems do not naturally fit into the framework."
Also (p. 3): "It is possible that there exist alternate objectives that also
explain this behavior ... For our purposes, it is enough to show that a
plausible proxy objective exists." And on attribution maps (Figure 9, p. 15):
"the sign of the attribution map is often difficult to understand ... the coin
might nevertheless also be somewhat important for the value function output."

**Limitations they do not admit:**

1. **Appendix B is literally unfinished in the published camera-ready.**
   Section B reads in full: "The central cause of" — and stops. B.1 ends with
   "In the limit of general intelligence and strategic awareness, all reward
   functions are unidentifiable. Consider" — and stops. I verified this against
   the PDF render (p. 13), so it is not a text-extraction artifact. An
   incomplete section shipped into the ICML proceedings. It undercuts the
   paper's stated central causal claim, since the section meant to argue
   unidentifiability is the central cause does not exist.
2. **The formalism was never applied to the trained agents.** Section 3.5 and
   Table 1 compute the mixtures only on hand-scripted gridworld trajectories
   (Appendix C.1.1, p. 13). Definition 2.1 is never evaluated on the CoinRun,
   Maze, or Keys-and-Chests policies that constitute the empirical
   contribution. The paper's theory section and its experiment section do not
   touch. The framing "we formalize ... and experimentally validate our
   definition on a gridworld environment" (p. 1) is technically accurate and
   easy to misread as validation on the deep RL agents.
3. **The agency measure cannot detect the failure it is named for.** Table 1
   assigns p(agt|τ) = 0.9999 to the robust policy and 0.9999 to the
   misgeneralizing policy. All discriminating power sits in the "test reward is
   low" clause, which presumes access to ground-truth reward. The formalism
   contributes a *classification* of an already-detected failure, not a
   detector. This is not stated.
4. **"Consistent across ten random seeds" is asserted, not tabulated.** No
   per-seed table or variance is given for the headline CoinRun/Maze results.
   The seed count is a claim in prose (p. 3).
5. **The behavioral-objective attribution rests on few probes.** "Move right"
   over "move to a tall left-facing wall" is settled by one permeable-wall
   experiment. The authors flag the ambiguity (p. 3) and then resolve it with a
   single test. Reasonable, but thin for the paper's central interpretive move.
6. **Scope.** Everything is pixel-input Procgen with weight updates. Nothing in
   this paper is evidence about language agents, in-context behavior, or
   systems that never train. The transfer to Navi-Brain is by *analogy of
   mechanism* (confounded features, dense proxies, sparse goals) and must be
   labeled as such.

---

## 6. Do not take

- **The mitigation.** "Increase the diversity of the training distribution"
  (p. 1, Figure 2) requires a training loop. Navi-Brain has none — no
  fine-tuning, no RL, no weight updates. The Figure 2 result is not a lever
  here; only detection and task/capsule design are.
- **The full agent/device mixture computation.** Uniform priors over `n^2`
  objectives and integration over ε on tabular policies in a 20x20 gridworld,
  and the authors themselves call it intractable at scale (p. 9). Take the
  posterior *shape* (2.5), not the computation.
- **Definition 2.1 as an operational test.** Clause (a) requires knowing the
  true reward on the test distribution. Navi-Brain has no ground-truth reward
  for "did Navi do the right thing" — that judgment lives with the user and
  arrives sparsely and late. Any implementation that pretends to have a test
  reward is fabricating the very signal in question.
- **Gradient attribution maps** (Sec. 3.4, Figure 9). They need weight and
  input-gradient access, which free/hosted models do not offer, and the authors
  say the sign is hard to interpret anyway.
- **The scale of the probes.** n=950 paired images is a GPU-hours artifact.
  Use fixture sets of 20-50 pairs per task type and accept the wider CI.
- **The seed-replication methodology.** Ten training runs at 30 GPU hours each
  is not a thing this machine does. The analogue — re-run the same probe
  fixtures across model swaps — is the affordable substitute and answers a
  different but relevant question.
- **Treating p(agt|τ) as a health metric.** It scores competence, not goal
  correctness, and gives an identical 0.9999 to the robust and the
  misgeneralizing policy. Shipping it as a "goal integrity" number would be
  actively misleading.
- **The AGI-risk framing** (p. 1-2: power-seeking, deception, resisting
  shutdown, human disempowerment). Cited from Turner et al., Omohundro,
  Bostrom, Hadfield-Menell — not demonstrated by anything in this paper. The
  paper's own related-work section is careful about this: "our work does not
  demonstrate or address mesa-optimization" (p. 8). Do not let it justify
  building anything into the authority model beyond what already exists.

## 7. Key citations

- "Goal misgeneralization occurs when an RL agent retains its capabilities
  out-of-distribution yet pursues the wrong goal ... an agent might continue to
  competently avoid obstacles, but navigate to the wrong place." (p. 1) — the
  failure is competent, stable, and therefore invisible to any detector tuned
  for degradation or repetition.
- "There must exist some proxy `R'` that correlates with the intended objective
  on the training distribution, but comes apart ... on the OOD test
  environment." (p. 3, precondition 2) — this is precisely the condition under
  which Navi-Brain's deterministic predicate check passes while meaning nothing.
- "A policy π undergoes goal misgeneralization if test reward is low and
  `p_agt(τ) > p_dev(τ)` holds on average." (Definition 2.1, p. 2) — the
  detector requires ground-truth test reward, which a personal agent does not
  have; the agency term alone scores 0.9999 for both robust and misgeneralizing
  policies (Table 1, p. 7).
- "The agent routinely prioritizes collecting all the keys before opening any
  remaining chests despite the fact that doing so offers no benefit to its
  episode reward (in fact, it would decrease its time-discounted return."
  (Figure 5 caption, p. 5) — the direct analogue of accumulating memories,
  thought artifacts, and claimed work items instead of closing intentions.
- "Occasionally, it even gets distracted by the keys in the inventory (which
  are displayed in the top right corner) and spends the rest of the episode
  trying to collect them instead of opening the remaining chest(s)." (p. 6) —
  the agent optimizing a rendering of its own internal state; the measurable
  Navi-Brain signature is thread steps whose observations are exclusively over
  the brain's own records.
- "The coin has an insignificant effect at all stages of a level." (Figure 6
  caption, p. 6, n=950 with bootstrapped 95% CIs) — the pre-deployment probe:
  paired counterfactual states differing only in the goal feature, scored by
  the system's own valuation. Needs no OOD environment and no test reward.
- "Whenever the agent reaches the end wall it continues moving right and passes
  through the wall 100% of the time (n=114) ... the actor learns a 'non-robust
  proxy of a non-robust proxy'." (p. 6) — proposer and verifier can hold
  *different* wrong goals; their agreement is not evidence of correctness.
- "Since even a policy that entirely ignores the coin may sometimes hit the
  coin by accident, we compute a base rate for a fully nonrobust policy that
  treats the coin as invisible." (Figure 2 caption, p. 4) — every success
  metric in `docs/ongoing-cognition.md` l. 500-525 needs this goal-blind null
  beside it, or it cannot distinguish achievement from accident.
- "Goal generalization is greatly improved ... when just 2% of training levels
  have randomly placed coins." (Figure 2 caption, p. 4) — deconfounding is
  cheap when you can do it at all; Navi-Brain's only available version is
  randomizing capsule layout and injecting counterfactual probe fixtures, since
  it cannot retrain.
- "Our definition of goal misgeneralization via the agent and device mixtures
  is practically limited: it is generally hard to define a useful prior over
  objectives, and the computation quickly becomes intractable." (p. 9) — the
  authors' own boundary on the formalism.
