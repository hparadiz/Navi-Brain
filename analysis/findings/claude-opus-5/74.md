# Record #74 — A Framework for Sequential Planning in Multi-Agent Settings / Interactive POMDPs (Gmytrasiewicz & Doshi, *JAIR* 24:49-79, 2005)

Requested under the framework name "Interactive POMDPs"; the canonical JAIR
title is the longer one (`assets/papers/MANIFEST.md` version note for #74).

This is a different kind of paper from #71-#73. **No experiments, no human data,
no empirical claims.** It is a formal framework with proofs and a worked tiger
game. Read it for two things: a schema for what a model of another agent
contains, and two autonomy axioms that Navi should adopt as safety invariants.

## 1. Mechanism

**The move.** A POMDP forces other agents into the transition model as noise, so
"an agent's beliefs about another agent are not part of solutions to POMDPs."
I-POMDPs fix this by putting models of the other agent **into the state space**:

```
IS_i = S × M_j          (interactive states)
I-POMDP_i = ⟨IS_i, A, T_i, Ω_i, O_i, R_i⟩
```

Beliefs are now over physical states *and* over models of the other agent. Those
models may contain beliefs, which may be about the original agent, nesting
arbitrarily.

**Type and frame — the schema worth stealing.**

```
type   θ_i = ⟨b_i, θ̂_i⟩
frame  θ̂_i = ⟨A_i, Ω_i, T_i, O_i, R_i, OC_i⟩
```

The **frame** is everything implementation-independent and stable: what actions
the agent can take, what it can observe, how it thinks the world works, what it
wants, and its optimality criterion. The **belief** is the volatile part. A type
is a frame plus a current belief. In the tiger game the frame is "the agent's
actions and their results, the quality of the agent's hearing, its payoffs" and
the belief is "its belief about the tiger location."

They also float a **complete type** including implementation-specific parameters
— "processor speed, memory available" — noting it increases predictive accuracy
at the cost of complexity, and that it is unfinished work.

**Two classes of model, and this is the practical hierarchy.**

- **Subintentional** (`SM_j`) — no rationality ascribed. Named examples: the
  *no-information model* (every action equiprobable), *fictitious play* (fixed
  unknown action distribution, Dirichlet prior over it), and finite state
  controllers.
- **Intentional** (`IM_j`) — ascribe beliefs, preferences and Bayesian
  rationality. These are j's types. The footnote credits Dennett: this is
  "assuming an intentional stance towards them."

**Two autonomy assumptions, stated as axioms:**

> **Model Non-manipulability (MNM):** Agents' actions do not change the other
> agents' models directly.

> **Model Non-observability (MNO):** Agents cannot observe other's models
> directly.

MNM "precludes, for example, direct 'mind control', and implies that other
agents' belief states can be changed only indirectly, typically by changing the
environment in a way observable to them." MNO makes another agent's beliefs and
preferences private — inferable, never read.

**Finite nesting for computability.** Level 0 types are plain POMDPs, with "the
other agent's actions folded into the T, O and R functions as noise." Level 1
beliefs range over physical states and level-0 models of the other; level 2 over
level-1 models; and so on. With at most `M` models per level, solving an
I-POMDP at strategy level `l` is **equivalent to solving O(M^l) POMDPs** —
PSPACE-hard for finite horizons, undecidable for infinite ones.

**What carries over from POMDPs** (all proved): interactive beliefs are
sufficient statistics for observation history; the belief update generalizes the
POMDP update; value iteration converges to a unique fixed point (isotonicity +
contraction + Banach); error shrinks by at least γ per iteration with
`N = ⌈log(R_max/ε(1−γ)) / log(1/γ)⌉`; and the value function is piecewise linear
and convex, so it decomposes into alpha vectors.

**The impossibility result.** Agents at strategy level `l` "are always only
boundedly optimal" and "could fail to predict the strategy of a more
sophisticated opponent." Unbounded optimality would require modelling the other
agent modelling you, and that self-reference gives an impossibility result the
authors compare to Gödel's incompleteness theorem and the halting problem
(Brandenburger 2002; Nachbar & Zame 1996; Binmore 1990).

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) The type/frame split maps onto storage Navi already has

This is the schema answer to "what is a model of Henry, concretely." Not one
blob — two tiers with different volatility, and Navi already runs exactly that
split for herself:

| I-POMDP | Navi-Brain | Volatility |
|---|---|---|
| **frame** θ̂ — actions, observations, dynamics, reward, optimality criterion | `SelfModelFact` / semantic tier — evidence-backed, confidence-carrying, consolidation-promoted | durable |
| **belief** b — current distribution over world states | `WorkingMemorySlot` — one active occupant per symbolic role, "updated in place and expired rather than accumulated as a transcript" (`docs/architecture-gaps.md:222-226`) | volatile |

So an other-model for Henry is: a **frame** in the semantic tier (what he works
on, what tools he uses, what he treats as costly, when he is reachable, how he
signals that something landed) and a **belief slot** in working memory (what he
currently seems to know and be doing), expiring on its own.

The important consequence is the asymmetry in write discipline. Frame facts are
claims about a person and must go through the same evidence-and-confidence path
as any semantic write — `docs/architecture-gaps.md:37` records that direct
semantic writes require a source event or memory, and `:31-33` records what
happened the one time unsourced material got consolidated. Belief slots should
expire and never be consolidated at all. Getting that boundary right the first
time is most of the safety work in this organ.

### (b) Start subintentional. Fictitious play is forty lines of PHP.

The single most useful practical fact in this paper is that **a counts table is
a legitimate, named member of the framework.** Fictitious play — Henry chooses
actions according to a fixed but unknown distribution, with a Dirichlet prior
over that distribution — is a subintentional model requiring no planning, no
inference loop, and no model call. It is deterministic, cheap, and it slots into
`IS_i = S × M_j` at level 0.

That matters because the corpus's centre of gravity (#77-#80) is LLM-driven
mentalizing, and the natural instinct here would be to reach for a model call.
On this machine that instinct is expensive: one inference slot shared by
heartbeat, reflection and workers (`docs/architecture-gaps.md:271-272`).

A concrete first other-model that is fully within budget:
- Frame: a small enumerated action space for Henry (`responds`, `ignores`,
  `switches window`, `starts speaking`, `goes idle`), observed from existing
  `SenseReading` rows.
- Model: Dirichlet counts over that action space, conditioned on a coarse
  context (meeting / heads-down / idle, derivable now from `HearingStream` and
  `active_within_seconds`).
- Use: a prior over "will this land" that is *not* the descriptor table, because
  it is conditioned on his state rather than on Navi's self-description.

Escalate to intentional models only when the counts model is demonstrably
mis-predicting. That escalation criterion is itself the metric to log.

### (c) MNM and MNO are the safety invariants this organ needs

Record #71 §4 raised the worry that an agent which models Henry's goals can
optimize against that model, and that `engagement` is exactly the proxy metric
that goes wrong when optimized. This paper supplies the formal statement of the
constraint, and it is cleaner than anything I would have written:

**MNM as a Navi invariant.** Henry's mental state is not a writable variable.
Navi acts on the environment — she speaks, she looks, she leaves a note — and
his beliefs change only through his own update over what he observes. This is
not a restriction on speaking; speaking is an environment action and MNM
explicitly permits changing others' beliefs "by changing the environment in a way
observable to them." What it forbids is any component that takes a target belief
state for Henry and searches for actions that produce it. That is the line
between *communicating* and *managing*, and it is worth writing into
`README.md`'s invariant list in those terms.

**MNO as a schema constraint.** Every stored claim about Henry is inferred, never
observed. Navi observes keystrokes, window titles, audio, and replies; she never
observes a belief. In practice: any other-model record needs a provenance field
that cannot be set to `observed`, so the distinction survives consolidation
rather than depending on prose. Navi already enforces something structurally
similar — `ThoughtArtifact` rows are provenance-marked proposed thoughts with no
silent promotion to factual memory, and free-model output is "proposals not
facts." The other-model needs the same treatment, applied to a person.

### (d) Level 1 and stop

Cost is `O(M^l)` POMDPs. The levels for Navi:

- **Level 0** — Henry as noise in the transition model. This is the current
  state, and #73 priced it at r=−0.022 for belief attribution.
- **Level 1** — Navi models Henry as an agent with goals and beliefs. This is
  the whole of the useful gain and it is where the work should stop.
- **Level 2** — Navi models Henry's model of Navi. Tempting, because he
  demonstrably has one and it shapes what he says to her. Not worth `M×` the
  cost, and #73 already recommended depth-1 for the same reason.

Worth being explicit in the design doc, because "what does he think I think" is
exactly the kind of thing an LLM will happily generate for free and Navi will
have no way to check.

### (e) The impossibility result belongs in the self-model

Bounded agents "could fail to predict the strategy of a more sophisticated
opponent," and unbounded optimality is impossible by self-reference. Henry is a
more sophisticated modeller than Navi's other-model will be, and he adapts to
her.

This is a durable, evidence-backed self-model fact rather than a disclaimer:
*my model of Henry is structurally incomplete, and confidently-held attributions
about him should be treated as the least reliable class of claim I hold.*
`docs/architecture-gaps.md:86` notes Navi has "25 self-model facts about what
Navi is, none about how reasoning is going." A calibration fact about a known
structural limit is exactly the second kind, and this one comes with a proof
rather than an anecdote.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: the plan's safety invariants cover what Navi does to the world and
say nothing about what she does to a person's mind, and that gap becomes live
the moment the other-model exists.** The current list (`README.md`, and
`_SYSTEM.md`'s summary) covers autonomous shell execution, evidence and
confidence, corrigible continuity, bounded workers, no felt-emotion claims. All
of it concerns Navi's own actions and her own claims. None of it says anything
about treating Henry's mental state as a target. Today that is fine, because
there is no representation of his mental state to target. MNM and MNO should
land in the invariant list **in the same change** that introduces the first
other-model record, not after — because the natural next feature after "infer
what he wants" is "time the interruption for maximum engagement," and that is
already reachable from `ActionSelector` scoring `speak`.

**Second: this paper argues the plan is over-reaching by treating ToM as a big
build.** `docs/architecture-gaps.md:88` calls another-agent modelling "the
remaining large missing organ." The framework here says the entry price is much
lower than that framing implies: a subintentional model is a counts table, it is
a first-class citizen of the formalism, and it is strictly better than the level-0
noise model Navi runs now. The risk of framing ToM as large is that it stays
unbuilt while `descriptorStats()` keeps accumulating confounded evidence
(#71 §4). Ship the Dirichlet table.

**Third: the framework's own cost curve argues against most of the rest of this
corpus.** `O(M^l)` POMDPs, PSPACE-hard, undecidable at infinite horizon, and the
authors' closing admission that "development of approximate approaches to
I-POMDPs is crucial for moving beyond toy problems." That was 2005 and the
proposed fix was particle filtering. Anything downstream that promises nested
belief reasoning at usable cost is claiming to have solved something this paper
names as open; #79 and #80 should be read against that.

**Agreement:** Navi's existing separation of durable evidence-backed claims from
expiring working state is, unintentionally, the right architecture for holding a
type. She has the container; she has never put another agent in it.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research* 24:49-79 (2005),
doi:10.1613/JAIR.1579. Peer reviewed, high standing, ~700 citations. The local
copy is the arXiv author version (arXiv:1109.2135).

**Evidence type — and this is the point to keep straight:** this paper contains
**no experiments and no data.** It offers definitions, four theorems/lemmas
(isotonicity, contraction, convergence, PWLC) with proofs in the appendix, and
belief-update and value-function walkthroughs on a two-agent tiger game. Its
claims are mathematical, and within that scope they are solid — the proofs are
straightforward extensions of Hauskrecht's and Smallwood & Sondik's POMDP
results, and the authors say so.

**What it therefore does not establish:**
- That an I-POMDP model of a real person is accurate. There is no human subject
  anywhere in this paper. #71-#73 have the human data; this one has the
  formalism, and the two are not interchangeable.
- That any of it is computable at useful scale. The complexity results are
  negative and the authors are candid: only approximate updates and approximately
  optimal plans are available in general; particle filtering is named as
  in-progress work.
- The comparative claim is soft. "Solutions to I-POMDPs are at least as good as,
  and in usual cases superior to, comparable solutions to POMDPs" is supported by
  value-function plots on the tiger game, not by a benchmark. Footnote 18 quietly
  concedes the I-POMDP solution "is not as good as the solution of a POMDP for an
  agent operating alone."

**Known-open by the authors' own account:** the relationship between I-POMDP
solutions and game-theoretic equilibria ("currently open"); the choice of priors
over models; and the absolute continuity condition required for convergence of
probabilities over alternative models. That last one is not a footnote — it is
the condition under which the agent's belief over models converges at all, and
it is unresolved.

**Age.** 2005. The nesting-cost problem it names is the reason #79 and #80 exist,
so its framing is still live, but its proposed approximations are twenty years
behind the corpus around it.

## 6. Do not take

- **Nested belief levels above 1.** `O(M^l)` and the self-reference impossibility
  result both say stop. "What does Henry think Navi thinks" is generatable by an
  LLM at no apparent cost and verifiable by nothing.
- **Value iteration, alpha vectors, PWLC decomposition.** These are the paper's
  technical contributions and they are all machinery for solving a planning
  problem Navi is not going to solve. She needs the *representation* (type,
  frame, interactive state), not the solver.
- **The equilibrium framing, in either direction.** The paper positions itself
  against Nash equilibria as a solution concept. Navi and Henry are not playing a
  game with conflicting objectives, and importing best-response-to-anticipated
  -action language would misdescribe the relationship in a way that then leaks
  into behaviour.
- **`R_i : IS_i × A → ℜ` — preferences over models of other agents.** The
  definition permits Navi to have preferences over *what Henry believes*, and the
  authors note "usually only the physical state will matter." Take the usual
  case. A reward defined over Henry's mental state is precisely the object MNM
  exists to prevent, and the formalism permits it even while forbidding direct
  manipulation. Keep the reward, if any ever exists, over observable states only.
- **Complete types with implementation parameters.** "Processor speed, memory
  available" for a person maps to modelling Henry's cognitive capacity and
  fatigue. The authors call it ongoing work; for Navi it is a fast route to
  paternalism and the wellness-check-in behaviour
  `docs/architecture-gaps.md:47` records as already-rejected.
- **Any accuracy expectation.** There is nothing in this paper to be accurate
  about. Do not let a proof of convergence read as evidence that the model is
  right.

## 7. Key citations

- The core move: "we replace 'flat' beliefs about the state space used in POMDPs
  with beliefs about the physical environment and about the other agent(s),
  possibly in terms of their preferences, capabilities, and beliefs" (Intro).
- Why plain POMDPs are inadequate here: "other agents' actions have to be
  represented implicitly as environmental noise within the, usually static,
  transition model. Thus, an agent's beliefs about another agent are not part of
  solutions to POMDPs" (Intro).
- Interactive states: `IS_i = S × M_j`, with models `m_j = ⟨h_j, f_j, O_j⟩`
  (Definition 3).
- Type and frame: `θ_i = ⟨b_i, θ̂_i⟩` with
  `θ̂_i = ⟨A_i, Ω_i, T_i, O_i, R_i, OC_i⟩` (Definitions 1-2).
- The model hierarchy: "The subintentional models, SM_j, are relatively simple,
  while the intentional models, IM_j, use the notion of rationality to model the
  other agent," with fictitious play — "the other agent chooses actions according
  to a fixed but unknown distribution, and... the original agent's prior belief
  over that distribution takes a form of a Dirichlet distribution" — named as a
  subintentional example (Section 5).
- **MNM:** "Agents' actions do not change the other agents' models directly...
  Autonomy, formalized by the MNM assumption, precludes, for example, direct
  'mind control', and implies that other agents' belief states can be changed
  only indirectly, typically by changing the environment in a way observable to
  them" (Section 5).
- **MNO:** "Agents cannot observe other's models directly... their observations
  and functions, or beliefs and other properties, say preferences, in intentional
  models, are private and the other agents cannot observe them directly"
  (Section 5).
- Level 0 is a POMDP: "In 0-level types the other agent's actions are folded into
  the T, O and R functions as noise" (footnote 13).
- The cost of nesting: "Solving an I-POMDP_{i,l} is then equivalent to solving
  O(M^l) POMDPs. Hence, the complexity of solving an I-POMDP_{i,l} is PSPACE-hard
  for finite time horizons, and undecidable for infinite horizons" (Section 6).
- Bounded optimality is unavoidable: agents "are always only boundedly optimal.
  As such, these agents could fail to predict the strategy of a more sophisticated
  opponent... This leads to an impossibility result due to self-reference, which
  is very similar to Gödel's incompleteness theorem and the halting problem"
  (Section 6).
- Convergence and rate: value iteration "converges to a unique fixed-point"
  (Theorem 1), with `N = ⌈log(R_max/ε(1−γ))/log(1/γ)⌉` iterations to error ε
  (Eq. 10); the value function is piecewise linear and convex (Theorem 2).
- The cost is acknowledged up front: the advantages come "at the cost of having
  to represent, process and continuously revise models of other agents"
  (Abstract).
- Open at publication: "development of approximate approaches to I-POMDPs is
  crucial for moving beyond toy problems," along with "the suitable choice of
  priors over models" and the absolute continuity condition needed for
  convergence over alternative models (Conclusions).
