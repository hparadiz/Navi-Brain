# Record #71 — Action Understanding as Inverse Planning (Baker, Saxe & Tenenbaum, *Cognition* 113:329-349, 2009)

This is the root of records #72-#78, #80 and the target of #81-#83. Read it as
the specification of the organ `docs/architecture-gaps.md:85` marks **absent**.

## 1. Mechanism

The claim: understanding an action is inverting a model of planning, the way
vision is inverting a model of graphics (p. 330). Forward direction is an MDP —
goal plus environment produce actions. Inverse direction is Bayes:

```
P(Goal | Actions, Environment)
  ∝ P(Actions | Goal, Environment) · P(Goal | Environment)      (Eq. 1, p. 333)
```

The likelihood *is* probabilistic planning. Prediction of future behavior is the
posterior predictive, marginalizing the inferred goal out (Eq. 2, p. 333):

```
P(Actions' | Actions, Env) = Σ_Goal P(Actions' | Goal, Env) · P(Goal | Actions, Env)
```

**The maze MDP.** 17×9 grid, 9 actions (8 compass + Stay), costs proportional to
distance moved, `Stay` costing 1 "to capture the desire for continual progress"
(p. 332). Reaching the goal ends the cost. Formally a stochastic shortest-path
problem.

**The one free parameter that does all the work: β.** Agents choose actions by
softmax over expected value, and β is the determinism level (p. 332). High β
fits shortest paths well and noisy paths terribly; low β fits everything
mediocrely. β is the observer's estimate of *how rational the observed agent is*
— and the paper's fitted β values move around per experiment, which turns out to
be the most reusable fact in it (§2c).

**Three goal priors, deliberately impoverished.**

- **M1** — one fixed goal state, pursued until achieved. Its *only* way to
  explain a detour is "the agent is noisy" (low β).
- **M2** — goals can change mid-sequence, with prior change rate γ per action.
  γ=0 collapses to M1. γ→1 means only the last action counts.
- **M3** — subgoals en route to a final goal, prior rate κ. κ=0 collapses to M1.
- **H** — the heuristic alternative: the goal is whatever the agent most
  recently moved toward. Formulated deliberately as **M2 with γ=1** (p. 334), so
  the comparison is a limiting case of the same family rather than a straw man.

**Beliefs are assumed away.** Fig. 1c is the full theory — Perceptual Access →
Belief, General Preferences → Goal, both → Action. Fig. 1b is what they actually
build: the limiting case "in which agents are assumed to have complete
Perceptual Access to the Environment, constraining their Beliefs to be equal to
the Environment" (p. 331). Belief inference is named as future work requiring
POMDPs, with the admission that "optimal planning in POMDPs is computationally
intractable" (p. 347). Record #72 is the paper that closes this.

**Results.** Three experiments, correlations against average subject ratings,
bootstrap cross-validated to control model complexity:

| | M1 | M2 | M3 | H |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exp 1 — online inference (n=16) | 0.82 | **0.97** | 0.93 | 0.96 |
| Exp 2 — retrospective (n=16) | 0.57 | **0.95** | 0.58 | 0.91 |
| Exp 3 — goal-based prediction (n=23) | 0.03 | 0.54 | **0.96** | 0.54 |

The interesting structure is not that inverse planning wins. It is that **no
single goal prior wins across the three tasks**, and the authors know it
(p. 346). They patch it post hoc with a hierarchical Bayesian model that selects
the goal-representation class per context, and show the class with the highest
marginal likelihood is the one that matched people in each experiment (Table 3,
p. 347).

**Where H fails, precisely.** The targeted analysis (p. 339) is the load-bearing
evidence. In Gap-obstacle conditions at judgment point 10, recent actions are
ambiguous between goals A and B, but the *whole path* disambiguates — the agent
went around rather than through. Subjects rated A nearly twice as likely as B.
M1/M2/M3 reproduce this by integrating the full path; H rates them nearly equal
because it only sees the last move. Those trials "account for all of the outliers
of H and most of the outliers of M1 and M3." Then at point 11, when a move
strongly reverses the evidence, subjects flip to B — M2 and H track it, M1 does
not, still dragging its accumulated history. **The right model integrates
history but stays revisable.** That sentence is the entire transferable result.

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) `SocialFeedback` is not merely H — it is below H

`src/Perception/SocialFeedback.php` implements: speak → observe a 120 s window →
count responses and reactions → score `engagement` ∈ [0,1] → a reflective pass
attaches a free-form `descriptor` → `descriptorStats()` averages engagement per
descriptor. The class comment is explicit and honest about the design: "Nothing
here declares which descriptors are good... valence is discovered from
co-occurrence rather than asserted up front."

That is a stimulus-response contingency table. H at least posits a latent goal
and infers it from the most recent action. `SocialFeedback` posits no latent
variable for Henry at all — it correlates *Navi's own descriptor of her own
utterance* against whether interaction followed. Every reason Henry did not
respond that has nothing to do with the utterance (he was mid-compile, on a call,
holding a thought, deep in a different goal) lands as evidence against the
descriptor. This is not a small bias. The paper's whole point is that the same
observed action means opposite things under different goal hypotheses.

Concretely: the same line gets `engagement = 0.0` when Henry is heads-down on
Artemis-Base and `engagement = 0.7` when he is idling, and
`descriptorStats()` will average those into a verdict about the *descriptor*.

### (b) The `inconclusive` branch is hand-coded marginalization

`closeWindows()` already contains the paper's insight in embryo (lines 131-153):

```php
$couldHaveLanded = $outcome->present_at_utterance === true
    || $responses !== [] || $reactions > 0;
if (!$couldHaveLanded) { /* status = 'inconclusive' */ }
```

The comment — "Silence only means something if someone was there to break it" —
is correct and is exactly what a latent-variable model does *automatically*, by
marginalizing over the unobserved state instead of special-casing one binary.
`present_at_utterance` is a tri-state (`true`/`false`/`null` from KIdleTime), and
`docs/architecture-gaps.md:274` already flags that engagement evidence is
unreliable while presence detection is coarse and says do not tune against it.

The upgrade is not a bigger `if`. It is: replace the binary gate with a
posterior over a small latent attention/goal state, and weight the evidence by
that posterior rather than admitting or discarding it. An observation collected
while `P(attending) = 0.3` is worth 0.3 of an observation, not zero and not one.

### (c) β already exists in the codebase, pointed at the wrong thing

This is the strongest structural finding. `src/Perception/ForwardModel.php`
already implements, per `source_key`: a one-step prediction, resolution against
the next reading, normalized error stored in a bounded 64-prediction history, and
a `precision()` recalibrated from recent errors that then weights how significant
a sense edge is.

That is β. β is precisely "how much of this agent's deviation from the expected
action should I read as noise rather than as signal about a different goal."
Navi computes a calibrated per-source noise estimate from observed prediction
error and uses it to discount surprise. She just never applies it to a source
whose readings are generated by a policy over a latent goal.

Implication: modelling Henry does not require a new subsystem. It requires
registering him as a sensory source whose forward model is `P(action | goal,
env)` instead of delta-extrapolation, and letting the existing precision
machinery estimate his β. `ForwardModel::predict()` currently uses "conservative
one-step delta extrapolation" for numerics and "persistence as the explicit
baseline" for categoricals (`docs/architecture-gaps.md:236-243`). Persistence is
the correct null for a thermostat and the *wrong* null for a person — it is M1
with β→0, the worst model in two of three experiments.

### (d) γ is the parameter Navi needs and does not have

Exp 2 is the retrospective task, and it is the one that maps onto
`closeWindows()`, which also looks backward over an elapsed window and assigns a
score. In that task M2 (γ high) scores 0.95 and static-goal M1 scores 0.57 — the
largest gap in the paper. Retrospective judgment *requires* representing that
the other agent's goal may have changed during the window.

Navi has a rich commitment model for **herself**: `releaseFocus()` takes exactly
one of Cohen and Levesque's three reasons — `achieved`, `impossible`,
`reason_lapsed` — and rejects anything else (`docs/architecture-gaps.md:143`).
That is a considered stance on goal persistence. Henry gets no equivalent. The
asymmetry is the whole finding of this record: Navi models her own goal dynamics
with care and models his with a 120-second stopwatch.

Minimum viable version: a `γ`-like decay on how far back engagement evidence is
allowed to reach, and an explicit `goal_changed_during_window` hypothesis that
downweights the whole observation rather than scoring it.

### (e) Path history, not last event

The targeted analysis says: integrate the whole trajectory, stay revisable. Navi
has *some* of the raw material — `SenseReading` rows for `pet_hearing` and
`desktop_presence`, plus the coarse activity channels (`shell_activity`,
`terminal_activity`, `process_activity`, `conversation_activity`), each of which
records the **age of the newest write**. Read as a sequence those give a
trajectory of *when* something changed, which is enough to treat behaviour as a
path rather than as independent samples.

**Correction:** an earlier draft of this record claimed window titles were
available. They are not. `DesktopAwareness.php:117`'s `active_window` is a
Spectacle capture-scope flag (`--activewindow`) on the privacy-gated
`desktop_look` path, not a window-title reader, and `bin/hearing_context.php`
emits `active_window_caption/class/name = unavailable`. Window identity, dwell
by application, and project switching are **not observable** and would require a
separately authorized perception grant — see the channel table in
`docs/architecture-gaps.md`. What survives here is change *timing*, not change
*identity*.

## 3. Seam

Primary: **`other-model`** — the organ `docs/architecture-gaps.md` calls
"Theory of mind | **absent** | the user is a stimulus source, not an agent with
goals" (line 85) and "not started" (line 205). This record is its spec.

Also touches:
- `forward-model` — β and `precision()` are the same object (§2c).
- `social-feedback` — `UtteranceOutcome.engagement` is a confounded measurement
  until a latent state is marginalized out.
- `speech-gate` — `ActionSelector` scores `speak` against `look`/`think`/
  `consolidate`, but "is this a good moment for *him*" is not among the inputs.
  Note `docs/architecture-gaps.md:110` deliberately excludes *answering* from
  scoring; goal-inference input belongs on the unprompted-speech path only.
- `metrics-benchmark` — goal inference needs held-out prediction, not fit.
- `self-model` — an inferred goal for Henry is a claim about a person and needs
  the evidence+confidence discipline `SelfModelFact` already enforces.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: the plan treats theory of mind as one more organ to add, and this
paper says it is a correction to a measurement Navi is already taking and
already learning from.** `docs/architecture-gaps.md:205-216` files ToM under
"not started," listing two consequences (can't tell a good moment to speak; the
engagement evidence needed hand-discounting). But `descriptorStats()` is running
now, accumulating a table that maps Navi's self-descriptions to an engagement
mean that is confounded by Henry's unmodelled goal state. The order of
operations matters: **every day the social loop runs without a latent user state,
it is fitting a descriptor vocabulary to noise, and that table is durable.** ToM
is not the next feature; it is a prerequisite for the feedback loop already
installed being worth anything. If it cannot be built soon, the honest move is
to stop trusting `descriptorStats()`, which `docs/architecture-gaps.md:274`
already half-says.

**Second: "the user is a stimulus source" is doing real safety work, and the
paper's framing does not acknowledge what replaces it.** An agent that models
Henry's goals can optimize against that model. Baker et al. are describing
*observers*, who infer and predict; they never close the loop to an agent that
acts on the inference. Navi would. A goal posterior over Henry plus an action
selector that scores speaking is, structurally, a system that learns which
moments to interrupt to maximize a response — and `engagement` is exactly the
kind of proxy metric that goes wrong when optimized. The safe framing is
**inference for suppression, not for solicitation**: the first authorized use of
a goal posterior over Henry should be to *stay quiet*, and to mark evidence
inconclusive. Extending it to timing an interruption for better engagement is a
separate decision with its own authority, and should not ride in on the same
commit. (Record #23, Hadfield-Menell's off-switch game, is already in the corpus
and is the right companion here.)

**Third: the cost function is handed to the model, and Navi has no way to get
it.** The authors admit their models "were also given certain basic aspects of
the MDP setup, such as the agent's cost function and the probabilistic dynamics"
(p. 346). In a maze, cost is distance and everyone agrees. What is Henry's cost
function while working? There is no ground truth and no clean observable. This
is the real reason ToM is hard here, and it is not what the gaps doc lists.
Practical consequence: the first version must have a **tiny, enumerable goal
space** grounded in things actually observable — reachability from
`desktop_presence`, speech topic from accepted `HearingStream` utterances, held
intention — not an open-ended goal representation. Baker's hypothesis space was
three lettered squares. Note that the observable set is thinner than it first
appears (see the correction in §2e): without window identity, "which project" is
not derivable and must come from what Henry actually says.

**Fourth, mild: no single prior won.** M2 wins two experiments, M3 wins the
third, and M1 gets r=0.03 on Exp 3 — worse than chance-adjacent. Any Navi
implementation that picks one goal-prior shape and freezes it will be wrong in
some regime. Either keep the space small enough that the difference does not
arise, or accept the HBM structure (select the representation class, then the
goal), which is materially more machinery than a first pass should carry.

**Agreement:** the plan's instinct to treat perception as prediction-plus-error
(the forward model built per `docs/architecture-gaps.md:236-243`) is the exact
substrate this paper needs. Navi is one representational change away from the
forward half of inverse planning.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** peer reviewed, *Cognition* 113 (2009) 329-349,
doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2009.07.005. Received 2007, revised and accepted 2009 —
a long review. Roughly 3,700 citations since; this is the field's founding paper,
and #72-#80 are largely its descendants.

**Design strengths, which are real:**
- The heuristic is formulated as a *limiting case* of one of the models (γ=1),
  not as an outside straw man. That is unusually honest comparison design.
- Bootstrap cross-validation to control for M2/M3 having extra parameters.
- A targeted analysis on the trials that actually discriminate the models, with
  the outliers traced to those trials — not just an aggregate correlation.
- Parameter-sensitivity sweeps reported, showing a robust plateau rather than a
  knife-edge fit.

**Where it is thin:**
- **n = 16, 16, 23, all MIT subject pool.** Small and unrepresentative even by
  2009 standards.
- **Correlations are against subject *averages*.** Aggregating across
  participants suppresses individual variance and systematically inflates r. An
  r=0.97 against a group mean is not an r=0.97 against a person, and every
  headline number in the paper is the former. For Navi this matters directly:
  she has exactly one user, so the paper's best-supported quantity — the average
  observer's inference — is the one she can never occupy.
- **Differences between models are small where they are significant.** Exp 1:
  M2 0.97 vs H 0.96, p=0.032. That is one marginally significant hair, and the
  authors themselves raise the concern ("some of the differences between models,
  while statistically significant, were small," p. 339) before answering it with
  the targeted analysis. The targeted analysis is convincing; the aggregate
  correlation on its own would not be.
- **The HBM model-selection result is post hoc.** It was constructed after
  observing that different models won different experiments, and it is validated
  on the same three experiments it was built to explain. Table 3 is a
  consistency check, not a prediction.
- **Domain is 2-D maze navigation**, chosen partly because humans may be
  near-optimal there specifically (p. 348, citing Barrett et al.). The authors
  say plainly it "is not yet clear whether the mental representations uncovered
  by our inverse planning models will generalize to explain human action
  understanding outside of these laboratory tasks" (p. 348). Navi's domain —
  inferring what a person is doing at a computer — is not maze navigation, and
  the transfer is assumed by everyone downstream rather than demonstrated.

**Is the headline supported?** Yes, narrowly and well: in this domain, human goal
inference integrates the full action history under a rationality assumption
rather than reading the most recent motion cue, and richer goal representations
than "one fixed goal" are required. The framework's *generality* is asserted
(golf, chefs, POMDPs, multi-agent) and not tested anywhere in the paper.

## 6. Do not take

- **The full-observability shortcut.** Fig. 1b sets Belief = Environment. For
  Navi that would mean assuming Henry knows everything she knows — including
  that she is about to speak, and what is in her memory store. This is the single
  worst assumption to import, because the interesting cases are exactly the ones
  where his information state differs from hers. #72 exists for this reason; do
  not build 1b and call it done.
- **Exact Bayesian inversion over a planner.** They solve MDPs by dynamic
  programming over a 17×9 grid with 3 goals. There is no tractable analogue over
  "what Henry is doing today," and `docs/architecture-gaps.md:269-272` already
  reports one inference slot, contended. Any design that needs a planner in the
  loop per observation is dead on arrival on this machine.
- **The r values as a target.** 0.97 is fit-to-group-average on a toy domain.
  Quoting it anywhere in Navi's docs as an expected accuracy would be false
  advertising, and record #83 exists to make that mistake expensive.
- **`Stay` costs 1.** A background assumption that idling is always costly. For a
  user model this is actively wrong — Henry deliberately sits still, reads, and
  thinks, and a model that treats stillness as failed progress will conclude he
  is stuck when he is concentrating. It would reproduce, in a more sophisticated
  form, exactly the wellness-check-in behavior that
  `docs/architecture-gaps.md:47` records as the thing this work replaced.
- **The HBM representation-class selector**, for now. Two levels of inference to
  fix a problem a three-hypothesis goal space will not have yet.
- **Any framing that Navi is doing what people do.** The paper models *human*
  judgments; matching them is its success criterion. Navi has one subject, no
  training set of his ratings, and no way to validate against a group mean. The
  claim available to her is "a latent goal variable predicts observed responses
  better than a descriptor table does," which is testable on her own logs and is
  a much smaller claim.

## 7. Key citations

- The thesis: action understanding is "a kind of 'inverse planning' or 'inverse
  reinforcement learning'," inverting a planner the way vision inverts graphics
  (p. 330).
- The inference: `P(Goal|Actions,Env) ∝ P(Actions|Goal,Env) P(Goal|Env)`
  (Eq. 1, p. 333); prediction marginalizes the goal out (Eq. 2, p. 333).
- β is the observer's estimate of the agent's determinism: actions "distributed
  in proportion to the softmax function of the expected value... higher β values
  yield greater determinism (less noise)" (p. 332).
- The heuristic is a limiting case, not a straw man: H is "a special case of M2
  in which agents' goals can change arbitrarily after every action, i.e. γ is set
  to its extreme value of 1" (p. 334).
- Beliefs are assumed away: Fig. 1b is the case "in which agents are assumed to
  have complete Perceptual Access to the Environment, thereby constraining the
  contents of their beliefs to be equal to the environment" (p. 331).
- The discriminating evidence — history integration with revisability: at
  judgment point 10 subjects rated A "nearly twice as likely as B because the
  agent had gone around, not through the obstacle," which H missed; at point 11
  under reversing evidence subjects flipped to B, which M1 missed (p. 339).
- Retrospective judgment needs changing goals: Exp 2, M2 0.95 vs M1 0.57
  (Table 2, p. 342).
- No single prior wins: M2 best in Exps 1-2, M3 best in Exp 3 (r=0.96, with M1 at
  0.03), reconciled post hoc by a hierarchical model selecting the goal class
  (p. 346-347, Table 3).
- The admission about handed-over structure: the models "were also given certain
  basic aspects of the MDP setup, such as the agent's cost function and the
  probabilistic dynamics by which the agent moves" (p. 346).
- The scope caveat, in the authors' words: "It is not yet clear whether the
  mental representations uncovered by our inverse planning models will generalize
  to explain human action understanding outside of these laboratory tasks"
  (p. 348).
- Belief inference deferred to POMDPs, with the tractability warning: "Optimal
  planning in POMDPs is computationally intractable, so we would require some
  approximate algorithm" (p. 347).
