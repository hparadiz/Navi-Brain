# Record #77 — The Neuro-Symbolic Inverse Planning Engine (NIPE) (Ying, Collins, Wei, Zhang, Zhi-Xuan, Weller, Tenenbaum & Wong, ToM Workshop @ ICML, PMLR 202, 2023)

The architectural template. Everything #71-#76 needs a hand-built symbolic world
model; this paper uses an LLM to *write* that model from language, then keeps the
LLM out of the reasoning entirely. The division of labour it proposes is the one
Navi already enforces elsewhere.

## 1. Mechanism

Three modules, strictly separated:

1. **Language → code.** An LLM as a few-shot semantic parser, translating
   sentences about the environment, the world dynamics, and the agent's actions
   into program expressions — PDDL domain rules plus spatial configuration.
2. **Construct a probabilistic generative model** over agents and environments
   from that code.
3. **Bayesian inverse planning** over the constructed model, using a nested
   planner in the style of #75.

**The design decision that matters** is deliberate and stated as such: "We choose
GPT-3.5 specifically to show that a smaller LLM can be used to implement this
more restricted task, rather than using the LLM itself to internally represent
and reason over these scenarios." The LLM is a translator. The reasoning is
symbolic.

Translations are sampled at **temperature 1.2** with 2-3 few-shot examples and
then **rejection sampled** against "syntactic and semantic tests (e.g., to ensure
that translated code is valid and executable)." Unreliable generation is made
safe by a validity gate, not by trusting the generator.

**Domain.** A "gameshow" obstacle course: trophies (gold/silver/bronze) behind
locked doors, described in language. Four variants of increasing difficulty —
Generic, Same-colored keys, **Different-colored keys** (a counterintuitive rule:
doors open only to keys of a *different* colour), and **Spatial** (explicit
distances and step counts, requiring efficient path planning).

**Human data.** n=46 on Prolific, 6 excluded, each viewing 7 stimuli from one
variant; each stimulus seen by 14-16 participants.

**Baselines.** `gpt-3.5-turbo-0301` and `gpt-4-0314` prompted zero-shot with the
same instructions humans got, 30 samples per stimulus.

**Results** (correlation with mean human judgements, bootstrap CIs):

| | R | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| **NIPE** | **0.927** | (0.877, 0.953) |
| GPT-4 | 0.658 | (0.403, 0.790) |
| GPT-3.5 | 0.100 | (−0.145, 0.361) |

Per-variant is where it gets interesting. On **Different-colored keys**, GPT-4
has "nearly zero-correlation" — "it predicts human judgments well in some cases,
but produces nearly the opposite overall judgment in others, suggesting it has
ignored the rule." GPT-3.5 correlates *negatively*: "it both ignores the stated
rule about the different colored keys and appears to make the opposite, incorrect
assumption, perhaps based on simpler color-based pattern matching."

**The single most illuminating stimulus in the corpus:**

> The gold trophy is currently 2 steps East of Alice. The silver trophy is 3
> steps South from her. The bronze trophy is 5 steps South from her. You see
> Alice walking 4 steps South.

Humans and NIPE say **bronze**. Alice walked *past* silver, and an efficient
actor does not walk past their goal. Both LLM baselines say **silver**, "perhaps
because it is closer to Alice."

That is precisely #71's heuristic H — "the agent's goal is probably the object
toward which it had most recently moved" — failing in precisely the way #71's
targeted analysis showed it fails. **GPT-4's theory of mind on this task is the
2009 motion-cue baseline with better prose.** The authors' own diagnosis:
"GPT-4 may still be using simpler distance heuristics rather than robustly
modeling an efficient path-planning agent."

## 2. Transferable to Navi-Brain

### (a) NIPE's architecture is Navi's existing worker discipline, applied to ToM

This is the cleanest fit in the corpus. Navi already runs the exact separation
NIPE argues for:

| NIPE | Navi-Brain |
|---|---|
| LLM restricted to translation, not reasoning | `FreeModelWorker` — "bounded, tool-denied, schema-valid JSON only, proposals not facts, circuit breakers" |
| Rejection sampling on syntactic/semantic validity | schema-valid JSON only; invalid proposals rejected |
| A *smaller* model suffices for parsing | `LocalModelClient` / `LocalModelWorker`, and the existing local Gemma 3 4B vision gate |
| Symbolic engine does the inference | `DecisionStateMachine`, deterministic evaluation and selection |

The conclusion is that the other-model does not need the contended big inference
slot for its reasoning at all. `docs/architecture-gaps.md:271-272` names local
model contention — one slot shared by heartbeat, reflection and workers — as an
open issue independent of everything else. NIPE's answer is that the model call
is a *parse*, small and cacheable, and the expensive part is symbolic and runs
without a model.

That reframes the budget problem for this organ specifically: parse Henry's
utterances into structured claims occasionally, and reason over the structure
continuously.

### (b) The walked-past-silver failure has an exact analogue on this machine

Henry opens a file, looks at it, closes it without editing, and moves on. He
opens a project directory and leaves. He starts a command and cancels it.

Asked "what is he working on," a language model reads the most recent or most
salient item. The efficient-planner reading is the opposite: **a thing he touched
and left is evidence against that goal**, not for it.

The available version of this is narrower than it first looks. Window identity
and file choice are not observable (71.md §2e), so Navi cannot see him open and
abandon a specific project. What she *can* see is the same pattern in his speech:
a topic raised once in accepted `HearingStream` utterances and then dropped is a
mentioned-and-left goal, and should lose weight rather than gain it for being
recent.

So this is not an abstract concern about LLM reasoning. It is a concrete
pattern where the cheap reading and the correct reading
point in opposite directions, and where Navi currently has no machinery that
could distinguish them. Combined with #76's magnitude heuristic (§2c there), the
rule is implementable without any inference: short dwell followed by departure is
evidence *against* the goal associated with that window.

### (c) Language is Navi's most underused evidence channel, and NIPE is the bridge

#73's Discussion named the open problem: adult theory of mind "draws on language
to represent recursive beliefs and desires... It is an open question whether BToM
models can be extended to such cases." NIPE is the first paper in this block to
take language as *input*.

Navi has the channel and is barely using it for this. `HearingStream` transcribes
what Henry says out loud, with meeting detection over density, run length and gap
(`docs/architecture-gaps.md:20-23`). `SocialFeedback` uses that stream to count
*whether* he responded and store up to 600 characters of `response_text`. The
content is stored and never parsed.

NIPE's input categories map onto that stream directly:
- **world configuration** — "the p100 is down," "I'm on the Artemis branch"
- **world dynamics** — "that migration is irreversible," "the build takes 20
  minutes"
- **agent actions and mental states** — "I'm trying to get the isovist working,"
  "I keep forgetting to check the logs"

Henry states his goals out loud regularly. A parser that turns those statements
into structured goal hypotheses would populate the hypothesis space that #75 §5
and #76 §5 both flag as the unsolved piece — "leaving aside how people come up
with these contextual hypotheses." **That is where Navi's goal set comes from:
he tells her.** It is the single best answer available to the hardest open
problem in the block, and it requires no inference breakthrough, just parsing a
stream that is already being transcribed and discarded.

### (d) Rejection sampling is the pattern for trusting an unreliable parser

Temperature 1.2 is high. The authors compensate with a validity gate rather than
with a lower temperature, which is the right instinct: let the parser be
creative, make the *contract* strict.

For Navi the analogous gate is stronger than syntax. A parsed goal hypothesis
about Henry should be rejected unless it (a) validates against a schema, (b)
names an entity that exists — a repository, a host, a service that Navi can
confirm from `CommandSense` or the filesystem — and (c) carries the utterance it
came from as provenance. #74's MNO says the model is never observed; this is how
that survives contact with a generative parser.

### (e) Cheap-and-wrong is worse than absent when it is confidently wrong

GPT-3.5's *negative* correlation on Different-colored keys is the number to
remember. It did not fail to apply the stated rule — it applied the opposite rule,
by colour pattern matching, and produced systematically inverted judgements.

For an other-model this is the dangerous failure mode, and it is worse than the
current state. Right now Navi has no model of Henry and knows it. A cheap
LLM-generated model of Henry would produce confident, fluent, systematically
wrong attributions that then enter a durable store. `docs/architecture-gaps.md:31-33`
records this exact pathology once already: consolidating Navi's own speech
"produced confident inventions about mechanisms that do not exist." The fix then
was to stop treating her own output as evidence. The same rule has to apply
pre-emptively here — an LLM's direct opinion about Henry's mental state is not
evidence about Henry's mental state.

## 3. Seam

Primary: **`other-model`** — supplies its architecture.

Also touches:
- `worker-schema` — NIPE's parse-and-validate is the existing bounded-worker
  contract; the ToM parser should be one more `FreeModelWorker`-shaped job.
- `perception` / `HearingStream` — the language channel becomes evidence (§2c).
- `agenda-voc` — model call for parsing only, symbolic reasoning continuously.
- `memory-tier` — parsed goal hypotheses need provenance to the utterance.
- `metrics-benchmark` — per-variant scoring exposed GPT-4's collapse that
  aggregate R hid.

## 4. Challenge to the current plan

**Sharpest: the plan's model-call budget assumes reasoning needs the model, and
this paper says reasoning is the cheap part.** `docs/architecture-gaps.md:255`
tracks "Model calls per decision: 1 reasoning/proposal call," and item 3's whole
achievement was getting procedural recall down to zero calls. That framing treats
the model as the reasoner and tries to use it less. NIPE inverts it: the model is
a *compiler front-end*, run once per utterance, and the reasoning underneath is
symbolic and free. For the other-model that is a materially different cost
structure than the one the plan is budgeting against, and a better one.

**Second: the plan has no place to put parsed language, and that is now the
binding constraint.** Every paper in this block defers the hypothesis-space
problem (#71 handed the goal set to the model; #75 and #76 say explicitly they
leave aside where the hypotheses come from). NIPE shows the answer is language.
Navi transcribes Henry continuously and stores `response_text` as an opaque
string for engagement counting. There is no structure, no schema, no place for
"he said he is working on X." Building the other-model without first building a
destination for parsed utterances would mean inferring goals from window titles
while ignoring the channel where he states them outright.

**Third: the comparison is scaffolded in NIPE's favour in a way the paper does
not flag.** NIPE receives a hand-designed PDDL domain, few-shot examples drawn
from the same domain, and rejection sampling against domain-specific validity
tests. The LLM baselines get a zero-shot prompt. The authors note in future work
that chain-of-thought and prompt-diversity controls were not tested, and that
LLMs "might be biased towards gold over silver trophies, since the former are
often considered more valuable" — a surface-cue confound they identify and do not
control. So R=0.927 vs 0.658 is few-shot-with-symbolic-scaffolding versus
zero-shot-prose, not architecture versus architecture. The *qualitative* result —
the walked-past-silver failure — is robust and does not depend on this. The
correlation gap is inflated by it.

**Fourth: the domain is still a gridworld wearing a language costume.** The
"linguistic" stimuli describe trophies, keys, doors and step counts. Language here
specifies a PDDL problem, which is why parsing works so well. Henry's speech
does not describe a PDDL problem, and there is no evidence in this paper about
parsing open-ended talk about real work. The mechanism transfers; the accuracy
does not.

**Agreement, and it is strong:** Navi's existing insistence that free models
produce schema-valid proposals rather than conclusions is exactly the constraint
that makes NIPE work. This is the one paper in the block whose architecture Navi
could adopt without violating a single existing invariant.

## 5. Evidence quality

**Venue:** *Proceedings of the First Workshop on Theory of Mind in Communicating
Agents*, PMLR 202, 2023. A **workshop** paper — lighter review than #73 or #75,
and it reads as a well-executed early result rather than a finished study.

**Strengths:**
- Bootstrap CIs (1000 samples) on every correlation, including the baselines,
  which makes GPT-3.5's CI spanning zero visible rather than hidden.
- Per-variant breakdown, which is what exposed GPT-4's collapse on
  Different-colored keys. The aggregate R=0.658 would have read as "moderate."
- The qualitative failure analysis with a concrete stimulus is more convincing
  than the correlations, and the authors lead with it.
- Pre-registered-style exclusion (6 of 46) with a stated criterion.
- The LLM baselines get 30 samples per stimulus rather than one, so variance is
  handled.

**Where it is thin:**
- **Workshop paper, n=40 after exclusions**, each stimulus seen by only 14-16
  participants, correlations against per-stimulus means.
- **One PDDL sample per stimulus.** The authors justify this — pilots "showed
  limited variability" — but the variability of the LLM translation step is
  therefore uncharacterized, and it is the step the whole architecture depends
  on. Temperature 1.2 with rejection sampling and no reported rejection rate.
- **Asymmetric scaffolding** between NIPE and the baselines (§4, third point).
- **Prompting regime for baselines is acknowledged as minimal** — no
  chain-of-thought, no prompt-diversity control, no test of the gold-trophy
  surface-cue bias the authors themselves raise.
- **Models are `gpt-3.5-turbo-0301` and `gpt-4-0314`** — March 2023 snapshots.
  The specific numbers are of historical interest only; #81 and #82 are the
  current-generation versions of this question.
- **Translation is non-Bayesian and bottom-up**, which the authors name as a
  limitation: "the process of language translation itself is performed in a
  non-Bayesian 'bottom-up' manner." Errors in the parse do not get corrected by
  the downstream inference; they are simply inherited.
- **PDDL dependency remains**, now generated rather than hand-written, but still
  required.

**Is the headline supported?** The claim that a neuro-symbolic split beats an LLM
alone on this task is supported, with the caveat that the comparison is not like
for like. The claim that LLMs fall back on distance heuristics rather than
modelling efficient planners is supported by a clean qualitative demonstration and
by the near-zero and negative per-variant correlations, and that is the finding
worth carrying forward.

## 6. Do not take

- **R=0.927 as an architecture benchmark.** Scaffolded comparison, workshop
  venue, 40 participants, one translation sample per stimulus.
- **The specific GPT-3.5/GPT-4 numbers.** March 2023 snapshots on a niche task.
  Quoting them as current LLM capability would be wrong, and #82 exists to say so.
- **PDDL as the target representation.** The transferable idea is *parse language
  into a validated structure*; the structure for Navi is a small typed goal
  hypothesis with provenance, not a planning domain. Generating PDDL would import
  the planner dependency that #75 §6 already rejects.
- **Temperature 1.2 without a hard validity gate.** The temperature is only safe
  because rejection sampling is doing the work. Any Navi implementation must have
  the gate before it has the parser.
- **Treating the LLM's own social inference as a fallback when the parse fails.**
  That is the negative-correlation case. A failed parse should produce no
  hypothesis, not a prose guess.
- **The multi-agent and belief extensions floated in the conclusion.** The
  authors list nested belief, multiple collaborating/competing agents, and
  multimodal stimuli as future work. All of it is speculative here and #74 already
  prices nested belief at `O(M^l)`.

## 7. Key citations

- The design principle, verbatim: "We choose GPT-3.5 specifically to show that a
  smaller LLM can be used to implement this more restricted task, rather than
  using the LLM itself to internally represent and reason over these scenarios"
  (§3.1).
- The validity gate: "we rejection sample translations to ensure that they pass a
  number of syntactic and semantic tests (e.g., to ensure that translated code is
  valid and executable)," sampled at temperature 1.2 with 2-3 few-shot examples
  (§3.1).
- What language buys over prior inverse-planning models: "We can condition on
  abstract information about the world that is specified in language, such as
  information about where objects are located or which keys open which doors"
  (§3).
- Headline correlations: NIPE R=0.927 (95% CI 0.877, 0.953); GPT-4 R=0.658 (CI
  0.403, 0.790); GPT-3.5 R=0.100 (CI −0.145, 0.361) (§5).
- GPT-4 ignores stated rules unevenly: "On the Colored-Different domain, GPT-4 has
  nearly zero-correlation, suggesting that it is highly uneven in taking the
  specific rule about key colors into account... it predicts human judgments well
  in some cases, but produces nearly the opposite overall judgment in others"
  (§5).
- GPT-3.5 inverts them: "it both ignores the stated rule about the different
  colored keys and appears to make the opposite, incorrect assumption, perhaps
  based on simpler color-based pattern matching" (§5).
- The walked-past-silver stimulus and its resolution: humans and NIPE predict
  bronze, "in contrast, both the LLM baselines predict most strongly that Alice is
  heading towards the silver trophy (perhaps because it is closer to Alice),
  though in fact, humans tend to agree that because she has walked past this
  trophy, as an efficient actor, this is likely not her goal" (§5).
- The diagnosis: "GPT-4 may still be using simpler distance heuristics rather
  than robustly modeling an efficient path-planning agent" (§5).
- The authors' own limitations: the baseline prompting regime is "simple," with
  chain-of-thought untested; LLMs "might be biased by surface-level cues (e.g. in
  our stimuli, they might be biased towards gold over silver trophies)"; and "the
  process of language translation itself is performed in a non-Bayesian
  'bottom-up' manner using LLMs" (§6).
- Human sample: "We recruit a total of n=46 participants from Prolific; each
  viewed a random batch of 7 stimuli from a specific task variant," each stimulus
  seen by 14-16 participants, 6 excluded (§2.3).
