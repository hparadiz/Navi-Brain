# 13 — Theory-derived indicators of consciousness

- **Paper**: Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems
- **Source**: Butlin, Long, Bayne, Bengio, Birch, Chalmers, Constant, Deane, Elmoznino, Fleming, Ji, Kanai, Klein, Lindsay, Michel, Mudrik, Peters, Schwitzgebel, Simon & VanRullen. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 2026 (published version of the 2023 arXiv report). DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011
- **Local file**: `assets/papers/13-butlin-et-al-2026-indicators-consciousness-ai.pdf`

## Core idea

The paper formalizes the "theory-derived indicator method": take mainstream computational-functionalist theories of consciousness, extract their central explanatory posits as *indicators*, and use those to shift credence about whether a given AI system is conscious. Indicators are credence-shifting markers, explicitly *not* individually necessary or jointly sufficient conditions. The headline table (Table 1) lists indicators from recurrent processing theory (RPT-1/2), global workspace theory (GWT-1..4), computational higher-order theories (HOT-1..4), attention schema theory (AST-1), predictive processing (PP-1), and agency/embodiment (AE-1/2). Methodological machinery: indicators vary in specificity and sensitivity; positive indicators raise credence, negative indicators lower it; background conditions (PP, agency) count separately from theory-core posits; behavioral evidence is heavily gameable (the "gaming problem"), so internal/interpretability evidence is preferred; and the minimal implementation problem warns that too-liberal formulations get satisfied by trivial systems. Assessment should be multi-theory, graded, and revisited as the science moves.

## Relevance to Navi-Brain

This is the methodological backbone for Navi-Brain's "consciousness-related evidence" section already present in `docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md` (lines 320–336), which mandates a theory-relative indicator matrix. It also directly feeds the measurement program (`docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md`), the synthesis's mechanistic-consciousness guardrails (`docs/literature-synthesis.md` §7), and the no-consciousness-score safety invariant. More than most corpus papers, this one is already metabolized — the task here is checking how much of the *published* version's sharper machinery (negative indicators, specificity/sensitivity, background conditions, gaming mitigations) actually made it into the spec.

  - HOT-2/HOT-3 (metacognitive monitoring + belief-update driven by it) genuinely exist at the action level: `src/Model/ActionTrace.php` records expected vs observed outcome, matched/mismatched, and repair note; `src/Model/SelfModelFact.php` carries confidence + evidence event + supersedes. That's a real, inspectable meta-monitor over its own operations, not narration.
  - AE-1 minimal agency (learn from feedback, pursue goals) is the substance of intentions (`src/Model/Intention.php`, success/release conditions) plus the needs model with thresholds. But "flexible responsiveness to *competing* goals" — the paper's key clause — is only seeded: needs are persistent pressures, but there's no explicit multi-goal selection under contention.

- **Partially present**
  - GWT: the design language is everywhere (specialists, broadcast, workspace-ablation metrics in `docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md`, mesh hypothesis in `docs/literature-synthesis.md` §8, "every invocation is a temporary workspace" in `docs/ongoing-cognition.md:253`), but the running substrate is a single-flight `ExecutiveCore` with lexical retrieval. No implemented limited-capacity workspace that selects among competing specialist proposals and broadcasts a winner with measurable downstream effect. The synthesis itself states the bar ("a shared message log that consumers do not reliably use is not a functional global workspace"). So GWT-2/3/4 remain design intent.
  - PP-1 predictive coding: absent as a perceptual mechanism (retrieval is ranked lexical search, no generative model). But `action:finish` expected-vs-observed and the sealed pre-action predictions in metric family 2 (`docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md:150-169`) are prediction-error machinery at the *action/meta* level, not the perceptual-input level the indicator names.

- **Absent / open design**
  - Negative indicators: the paper makes absence of a sensitive indicator count *against* consciousness (RPT-1 is the worked example). The repo's matrix spec has no credence-direction concept — everything is framed as evidence-for, nothing as evidence-against.
  - Specificity/sensitivity: the metrics doc says "graded" but never defines the grading axis; the paper's specificity/sensitivity framing gives it a concrete one.
  - AST-1 attention schema: no model of the system's own attention state. The metrics doc lists attention schema as a motivation for "attention modeling" (`docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md:175`) but nothing implements it.
  - HOT-4 sparse/smooth coding and a "quality space": entirely absent, and arguably out of scope for a symbolic store.
  - A dedicated indicator-audit experiment: nothing in `docs/experiments/` runs Table 1 against the actual substrate.

## Concrete implications for implementation

1. **Extend the indicator matrix spec** in `docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md` with three fields the paper supplies that the current spec lacks: (a) credence direction (positive/negative indicator), (b) known specificity/sensitivity of the indicator, (c) a "background condition" tag distinguishing PP-1/AE-1/AE-2 from theory-core posits. Small text change; makes "graded, theory-relative" actually specified.
2. **Add a negative-evidence family to the evaluation ledger** (the append-only `evaluation_suites`/`evaluation_cases` design already sketched at `docs/longitudinal-cognition-metrics.md:352+`): indicators whose absence is credence-lowering (RPT-1 algorithmic recurrence in perceptual input, HOT-4 quality space, AST-1). This is the one genuinely new mechanism the published paper contributes over the arXiv version the repo already absorbed.
3. **Write one indicator-audit experiment** (`docs/experiments/`) that runs a condensed Table 1 against Navi-Brain *as-built* — white-box, so cheap: every state is inspectable via `bin/navi-brain`. State the system boundary explicitly (ExecutiveCore + memory tiers + desktop perception; the LLM generating Navi's prose sits outside and is black-box). Expected result to pre-commit to: HOT-2/HOT-3 and AE-1 partially present, GWT-2/3/4 and PP-1 design-only, AST-1/HOT-4/RPT absent — i.e. an audit that stays honest, not a score.
4. **Cite the published version as the source of truth.** The metrics doc was written against the arXiv report's framing; the TiCS version sharpens the selection guidelines (the four indicator-derivation rules) and the gaming discussion. Update the matrix prose to the published DOI (the MANIFEST already points there).
5. **Keep the audit off the optimization path.** The paper's own mitigation is to prefer non-gameable indicators and check supporting features; the repo's invariant is "no consciousness score." Both are satisfied only if the matrix lives as an audit artifact in `docs/experiments/` and never feeds a reward/rhythm value.

## Risks / tensions

- **Consciousness-theater risk is the top tension, and the paper is explicit about it.** The moment an indicator matrix starts tuning design (add recurrence "because GWT", bake in "agency" language) it becomes gamed by construction — Goodhart in the repo's own voice. The Doerig guardrail and the no-score invariant are the counterweights; they must stay normative, not decorative.
- **The paper only covers phenomenal consciousness.** Navi-Brain explicitly doesn't claim that, and the paper itself leaves room for biological-substrate and IIT views that would reject the exercise's relevance to a PHP/SQLite substrate. Applying Table 1 is legitimate research hygiene, never a self-attribution claim.
- **Boundary ambiguity is real, not academic.** Whether Navi-Brain "has" any indicator depends on where you draw the system — ExecutiveCore alone, or ExecutiveCore + the external LLM that generates the words. The paper spends a whole subsection on exactly this; the audit must pick one boundary and justify it, or every indicator becomes contestable.
- **The paper is an opinion piece.** It is a method manifesto by a large, prestigious author list (including skeptics like Schwitzgebel) and it's explicitly agnostic about computational functionalism. Thin as *evidence*, strong as *procedure* — which is all Navi-Brain should ever take from it.

## Bottom line

Partially adopt — confirm-and-sharpen. The repo already absorbed the paper's core (indicator matrix, no single score, gaming guardrails, boundary discipline); what's genuinely new and worth adding is the negative-indicator machinery, specificity/sensitivity as the grading axis, and a concrete indicator-audit experiment. The most important consequence: Navi-Brain already has the strongest indicator it will ever have — HOT-2/HOT-3-style metacognitive monitoring with evidence-and-confidence — and the paper's job is to keep that honest, graded, and unequipped to score itself.
